# Earlier work on the same question in other groups

Claude, 27 November 2026, at Fable's and Barry's request. Fable observed that the article cites Strauss and Sadler, Marshall, Hanley, Silvestro et al. 2015 and CladeDate for its method, but none of the closer relatives listed in `docs/precedents.md` (line 135). The task was to read the three papers named there, search four further families of earlier work, and cite only what was read.

## Method

- **Search.** Five Codex `sol` jobs ran in parallel at high effort with web search: one for the three named papers, and one for each family. The families were (a) origin estimates from fossil sampling and a diversification model; (b) calibration densities from fossil sampling, and dating with fossils in the tree; (c) the mirror problem of dating an extinction from a sighting record; and (d) tests of absence-based maxima in other groups. Each job verified identity, read what was open and stopped at six candidates.
- **DOI check.** Claude checked all 26 DOIs against the Crossref REST API, since Codex could not reach it. All resolve to the expected work.
- **Description check.** Five Claude Sonnet 5.5 reader agents checked every description against its source, statement by statement, without bypassing any paywall.
- **Read levels.** "Read" gives each agent's level: Codex, then Claude. Only papers that Claude's check confirmed in full are cited, and each is cited only for what that check confirmed. Codex Sol then checked the article sentences and the cited rows against the sources: no errors in the article, five imprecisions there and seven in the table (one table error, the CI 4 scope for Marjanović & Laurin), all adopted.
- **Closed literature.** Papers that neither agent could open in full are listed in `docs/closed-literature.csv` and described only by title here.

## Does any paper undercut a claim or offer a method that would change a result?

None undercuts a claim. The reviewed studies leave F3's conditional limits and finding-rate requirements intact. Some offer alternatives that could give a different number for this record if they could be shown to apply to it. Nothing is changed here; this is reported for discussion.

- **Coverage under declining sampling (Rivadeneira et al., as reported by Codex).** Extinction-time limits lose coverage when sampling declines toward the unobserved endpoint. F3 already states every steady-rate limit as conditional and runs its own coverage check under a declining history (C34).
- **External preservation benchmarks (Foote et al.).** Foote et al. take the preservation rate from contemporaneous relatives, and both they and Tavaré et al. caution against transferring such rates across groups, regions or intervals. F3 takes the rate from the focal record's later window. That caution bears on how F3's later-rate benchmark is read.
- **Rock-based recovery (Marjanović & Laurin).** They weighted recovery by rock area times modelled diversity, and found rock area alone fitted poorly. F3's C59 checks a different proxy, North American non-marine column area, and finds that proportionality to it predicts too few late-Silurian finds. A diversity-weighted recovery history supported for this record could change the limits. No study here supplies one.
- **Non-uniform endpoint estimators (CladeDate).** CladeDate's uniform-recovery formula reproduces C27's 428.84 Ma with the same cutoff, oldest age and count (Codex's check). Its non-uniform estimators could give a different bound, but the papers do not establish their assumptions for this record.
- **Recognisability (Matschiner et al.).** They handle it by restricting direct calibrations to identifiable clades. F3 models a delay. Both treat recognisability as part of sampling.

- **Foote et al.'s conclusion.** The related-work paragraph gave Foote et al.'s ten-fold figure without what they concluded from it. It now adds that, with that benchmark, they concluded that many modern orders were unlikely to be much older than their oldest fossils, and that in F3 each outside measure either fails its late-Silurian check in at least one inventory or falls too little (Results (*g*), C59), so F3 states the requirement and leaves its plausibility open. The sentence makes no comparison of size between Foote et al.'s requirement and F3's, because the two differ in rate (per lineage against the whole record), criterion (50% against 5%) and benchmark (other mammals against the record's own later window).
- **Clark & Donoghue 2025.** Cited in the Conclusion at "Testing alternative defensible maxima then shows how much each assumption moves the timescale". Fable read the full text in the Europe PMC open copy (PMC12115813). Its Discussion reports that accepting older equivocal records in the crown-angiosperm maximum gives correspondingly older crown ages, and that the differences between calibrations exceed those from clock models, taxon sampling or partitioning. The eight-reference limit in Fable's prompt was Fable's own; the journal sets none.
