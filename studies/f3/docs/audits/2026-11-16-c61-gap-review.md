## Derivation and numerical check

Conditional on the exact oldest age \(a_0\), the other \(n\) independent draws
are truncated to \([c,a_0]\). Put \(G=a_0-b\), \(L=b-c\), and let finding have
relative rate \(\rho\) in the older stretch and one in the later window. Then

\[
p=\frac{\rho G}{\rho G+L},\qquad
X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p),\qquad
\rho_U=\frac{L}{G}\frac{p_U}{1-p_U},
\]

where \(P_{p_U}(X\le k)=0.05\). I computed the binomial CDF by directly summing
its polynomial terms and inverted it by bisection, without SciPy or the
production root-finder. For zero counts this independently agrees with the
closed form \(p_U=1-0.05^{1/n}\).

The shortest admitted gap gives the weakest ratio bound because \(p_U\) and
\(L\) remain fixed and \(\rho_U\) is inversely proportional to \(G\). In these
three cases the uncertain-age count is also fixed: its minimum and maximum are
identical. The two cutoff-straddling units have younger supports and cannot
enter any of the stretches; including them would increase the later count and
tighten these benchmarks. No alternative admissible age assignment weakens the
reported shortest-gap values under this model.

For the regional Homerian case, the upper rate ratio is 1.604960102388. Its
reciprocal of 0.623068 is therefore no evidence for a reduction. Even the
longest-gap reciprocal, 0.990519, remains below one.

## Interpretation and repairs recommended

1. The arithmetic is correct. The measures are relative integrated finding
   rates, conditional on the other units being independent and on a level
   later rate. They are not population-abundance ratios or fossilisation
   probabilities. A varying rate within a stretch with the same integrated
   intensity is indistinguishable from the stated average; neither zero-count
   stretch establishes continuously low finding throughout it.
2. The gaps and edges were selected after seeing the record. The nominal
   one-sided 95% inversion does not supply selection-calibrated historical
   lower bounds. Results (i)'s repeated statements that finding "was at least"
   a factor lower should identify them as conditional gap-model benchmarks.
   The disclosure of selection is necessary, but does not itself calibrate
   the numerical bound. The independent fixed-history shape tests address a
   different question and do not calibrate these selected rate-ratio limits.
3. Table S19 is described as exhaustive but presently has no condition row for
   the new gap/Homerian measures. Add their conditional-oldest, independent-
   draws, level-later-rate and post-selection assumptions, with a pointer to
   S5.11. The main Conditions post hoc list may name the gaps alongside the
   existing selected scenarios if that list remains enumerative.
4. Table S18's heading "Longest gap" contains factors rather than durations;
   "Factor at longest gap" would make the comparison unambiguous. Its gap
   column gives the shortest guaranteed empty interval; the full allowed gap
   duration is correctly supplied in the neighbouring column.
