# M3's fossil basis: the Irish Homerian *Cooksonia* and *C. barrandei*

28 October 2026. Claude Opus 5.5, for F3 (`docs/f3-shape.md` v3, M3) and Q9.

**Question.** Hooper et al. (2026) set a soft maximum of 431.2 Ma on crown Tracheophyta from the first appearance of *Cooksonia*. Does *Cooksonia barrandei* (Libertín et al. 2018), dated older, bear on that basis?

**Sources read in full:**
- Hooper et al. (2026), Supplementary Table 4, C_4 and C_24 (`data/external/deposits/hooper2026-spore-evolution-esm/`);
- Edwards & Feehan (1980);
- Libertín et al. (2018) and its supplementary text (`library/closed/`).

## What the sources say

| | Irish material (Hooper's basis) | *C. barrandei* |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Fragmentary compressions. One specimen shows dichotomous branching with two terminal sporangia. The others are sporangia on very short unbranched axes, which "do not strictly conform to the generic diagnosis of *Cooksonia*". More than one morphospecies. | One robust holotype (D 552a,b): an isotomously branched axis with overtopping and terminal lentil-shaped sporangia, 3–5 mm across, with in situ trilete spores (*Aneurospora* type, some in tetrads, 10–15 µm). |
| Anatomy | No tracheids seen. The authors "cannot be absolutely certain that they were vascular plants". | None reported. |
| Age | *Monograptus ludensis* Zone, the highest Wenlock graptolite zone (late Homerian); graptolites in the same strata. | Middle Sheinwoodian, *M. belophorus* Biozone, c. 432 Ma. The fertile holotype came from a misfiled nineteenth-century Barrande box labelled *Fucoides*. Its horizon is inferred from three things: a *M. flemingii* on the same slab, *M. belophorus* with other specimens in the box, and shared lithology and fauna. The relocated layer at the Loděnice road cut yielded sterile cooksonioid fragments within the *M. belophorus* Biozone. |
| Hooper et al.'s treatment | "The oldest unequivocal record of total group Tracheophyta is *Cooksonia*". Its affinity rests on tracheids in younger *C. pertoni* and on stomata, branching sporophytes and sterome in *Cooksonia*. The maximum is the Homerian base, 430.5 ± 0.7 Ma, giving 431.2 Ma. | Not mentioned in Supplementary Table 4. |

## Adjudication (Claude Opus 5.5)

1. **On characters, *C. barrandei* qualifies at least as well as the Irish material.** Hooper et al. assign total-group status to *Cooksonia* at genus level: the tracheids are in Lochkovian *C. pertoni*, not in the Irish specimens. Both the Irish fossils and *C. barrandei* show the observed characters that matter here, a branching sporophyte with terminal sporangia. *C. barrandei* adds in situ trilete spores and is far better preserved. Neither shows tracheids. On the specimens alone, both are polysporangiophyte morphotypes. *Cooksonia* is treated as a morphotype in this project (`taxa/cooksonia.md`). The total-group placement of either rests on the same inference.
2. **On age, *C. barrandei* is older but less directly dated.** Its middle-Sheinwoodian assignment is a reasonable correlation, not an in-place observation of fertile material. The repository's main reading keeps the authors' Sheinwoodian. A new alternative (`I-barrandei-wenlock`, live) carries "Wenlock, horizon inferred" for sensitivity.
3. **Consequence for M3.** If Hooper et al.'s own rule (maximum = the oldest possible age of the oldest total-group record) is applied to the main reading, the oldest record is *C. barrandei*, not the Irish material. The maximum would then move to the oldest possible age of the middle-Sheinwoodian *M. belophorus* Biozone. No numerical age for that biozone was found in the sources read. Its outer limit is the Sheinwoodian base, 432.9 ± 1.2 Ma in `data/timescale.csv`, against 431.2 Ma. The shift is at most about 3 Myr. It matters because the published crown-tracheophyte posterior, 432–428 Ma, lies against the maximum (primer §5.3). How much it moves the posterior is a Q9 question, and cannot be read off the calibration (F3-R6).
4. **This is not a contradiction of a younger crown.** A stem polysporangiophyte older than the crown maximum is compatible with that maximum (F3-R4). The point is narrower. The fossil-absence argument Hooper et al. use, that no total-group record is older than the Homerian, is not true of the published record on their own criteria. So the absence the maximum rests on begins at least about 1–3 Myr earlier than they state.
