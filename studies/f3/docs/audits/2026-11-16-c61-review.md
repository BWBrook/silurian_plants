## Repairs required before acceptance

### 1. Separate the endpoint result from a required clock maximum

Main Results (i), Discussion and Conclusion infer that a clock maximum drawn
from the branched record would lie at 434.6 Ma or older. A conditional upper
confidence limit on a recoverable category's start is not itself a required
95% tail point for a crown-calibration prior. The result also does not exclude
a start at 431.2 Ma. The existing caution about *C. barrandei*'s age range is
correct, but does not repair that inferential conversion.

The endpoint can inform a proposed maximum if that mapping and its model are
stated. Make the comparison explicitly conditional on this endpoint method;
do not present it as a requirement on every way to construct a clock maximum.

Retain the positive result: **applying this steady-rate endpoint method to the
declared branched record gives an older upper limit than 431.2 Ma under each
existing counting rule.** Describe what that says about this particular
absence model; do not turn it into a generally required calibration maximum.
Carry the distinction into the Abstract, Results, Discussion, Conclusion and
C61's claim/limit, with a C61 entry in the interpretation specification first.

The source attribution also needs narrowing. Hooper C_24 keys its maximum to
Irish *Cooksonia* interpreted as unequivocal total-group Tracheophyta, discussing
several characters and using tracheid evidence from younger *C. pertoni*.
It does not state an absence of every branched plant. Our B1 category is
broader than that taxonomic claim. It is a useful test of the broader
branched-body record relevant to the rationale, rather than exactly the
source's fossil category. Replace the attributed shorthand “no branched
plants, so no crown ancestor” accordingly. Branching and conducting tissue
must remain separate from demonstrated crown placement.

Source locator: the held, ignored
`data/external/local/deposits/hooper2026-spore-evolution-esm/41477_2026_2432_MOESM1_ESM.pdf`,
Supplementary Table 4, C_24. No source text is reproduced here.

### 2. “434.6 Ma or older at every count” is too broad

The existing `reviewed_bounds.csv` contains this counterexample, independently
recomputed with `c = 393.47`, `A = 432.9` and
`T = c + (A-c) exp(-log(0.05)/n)`:

| B1 main, Emsian cutoff, steady history | Guaranteed count | Limit (Ma) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Collection | 101 | 434.08703921 |
| Locality and age | 69 | 434.64961522 |
| Locality | 67 | 434.70301910 |
| Region and stage | 29 | 437.19097923 |

Thus all four existing rules remain older than 431.2 Ma, but their range is
434.09–437.19 Ma. The specific 434.6 lower endpoint applies to the 69-unit
treatment and reductions from that count, with the oldest possible age held
fixed. Say that if it is the intended scope. Repair the same assertion in C61,
the Abstract, Results, Discussion, Conclusion and the new decision-log entry.
This finding does not require rerunning the underlying analyses.
