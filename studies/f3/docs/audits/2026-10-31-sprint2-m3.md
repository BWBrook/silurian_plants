# Sprint 2: M3 denominator assessment

31 October 2026. Codex. Read-only assessment against source-roster commit `d17d033cc2bb7f083a61e31228d30919f2406beb`. Derived outputs are integrated in `data/units/sprint2/m3/`; the independent assessment made no model or source-table edits. No network acquisition or contact took place.

**Decision: the bounded held-source roster does not admit an M3/E3 numerical absence design.** It supplies occurrences, preservation context and search leads, but no complete, independently delimited inventory of searched compression-fossil material with E3-positive and E3-negative reporting and comparable reference opportunities. This is a limitation of the assembled evidence, not evidence that older E3 bodies were absent. No empirical E3 detection rate, compatibility bound or crown-tracheophyte maximum was calculated.

## What the databases actually contain

The verified PBDB plant extract has 3,704 occurrence rows attached to 1,302 collections and a 523-row reference table. Its frame is selected through plant records. Of the 1,302 collection rows, 53 name a local section, 76 a local bed, 251 populate collection coverage, 92 collection size and 121 rock censused. Populated fields do not establish a complete E3 search: PBDB collection 10594, the Irish locality, explicitly says `some macrofossils`, has no rock-censused or collection-size entry, and no local bed or section identifier. Its formation remains Hollyford, whereas the fuller primary report places the plant-bearing locality in the Cloncannon Formation (Edwards et al. 1983, pp.20–21). Keep the primary identification and database value separately, rather than silently overwriting provenance.

There are real measured-effort leads in the plant-selected extract: 121 rows populate rock censused, but 108 cite PBDB reference 6895, the SUNY-Binghamton Paleobiology Collection, and concern Devonian material. The remaining rows include animal/algal palaeoecological collections and other Devonian descriptions. This metadata is inventoried in `pbdb_rock_censused_metadata.csv`; it was not followed into a fourth additional primary-source reading or admitted as a common Silurian compression-search frame.

The locally held all-taxon PBDB raw pull contains 27,830 unique collection IDs citing 3,115 primary-reference IDs. Its query retained location, stratigraphy, palaeolocation and environment, but **does not contain** collection methods, coverage, size or rock-censused fields. It does have 5,520 populated local-section fields and 5,161 local-bed fields. These help locate primary descriptions; they cannot turn omitted plants into searched negatives or 27,830 collections into trials. This October 28 query and the October 23 plant snapshot also differ in date and time-window selection and were not treated as a single complete sample frame.

The GBDB extract has 3,099 plant-filter taxon-list rows (410 core) from 146 sections, not 3,099 specimens or independent searches. Its plant-section table has 2,389 section × formation-entry × unit rows, including nine rows with blank unit ID; there are 2,380 nonblank unit-ID labels. It holds all units of plant-selected sections. `Facies` and `Environment` are blank in all 2,389 rows; 1,829 have free-text Details, and 2,380 have thickness. Lithological descriptions can support prospective facies selection after review but provide no declared megafossil-search scope.

The separate all-window GBDB summary covers 3,217 sections and sums to 22,000 window units. The 3,154 sections with zero recorded core-plant rows are database omissions under the extraction filter, **not E3 non-detections**. Formation-entry ages retain their source vintage and should not be treated as modern ICS point ages. The 2,129 age-less candidate rows and 340 unmatched fossils-workbook rows remain separate, unresolved leads.

Exact counts, record grains and admission gaps are in `database_grain_coverage.csv`; field population counts are in `field_coverage.csv`.

## Primary evidence and material identity

**Irish Cooksonia, Edwards & Feehan (1980), pp.41–42.** The paper describes plant compressions in buff siltstone and explicitly reports fossils absent from interlaminated grey-green mudstone. This is real source-level qualitative non-detection language and should be retained. It supplies neither the number/identity of searched mudstone beds or slabs nor the amount or method of search. The lithology yielding plants differs from that reported fossil-free, so comparable preservation cannot be assumed. The 2,500 m succession and approximately 900 m ludensis sequence are geological thicknesses, not amounts searched. Failed searches for tracheids concern E4 and preservation, not absence of E3.

**Libertín et al. (2018), Nature Plants and supplement.** The reporting summary (PDF p.4, printed reporting-summary p.1) gives 50 samples of sterile and fertile axes and one carrying in situ spores. These are already selected plant specimens/samples, not 50 rock searches; one in situ-spore specimen out of 50 is not an E3 encounter rate. The supplement (PDF pp.2–3) identifies the historical Fucoides-labelled museum box and the inferred belophorus horizon; the fertile holotype is historical, while relocated layer 4 yielded new sterile fragments. The section and its six numbered lithological units do not report complete searches of all units. Its source/acquisition qualification remains: the held supplement matches its registered SHA-256, but the original download route is unproven.

Exactly three additional held sources were selected under `selection-criteria.md`, before their detailed extraction:

1. **Edwards, Feehan & Smith (1983), pp.20–21 and 32–34.** The fuller locality account records further plant collection and laboratory methods, including that axes barely visible dry became visible under ethanol. Mudstone samples from within a few metres of the plant horizon were processed for palynology. It gives no complete megafossil slab/bed inventory or unsuccessful-search count. Its additional material overlaps the same locality and programme as the 1980 report; publication count cannot supply independence.
2. **Libertín et al. (2018), Fossil Imprint, pp.327–330, Fig.1.** Approximately 50 historical cooksonioids include the previously described type; three further fertile specimens are described, and eleven new sterile fragments were collected from layer 4. The approximately 50 should not be added to the original 50; the additional descriptions do not become independent field trials. The eleven field fragments are material from one layer, with incomplete E3-diagnostic morphology, not eleven independent negatives. Fig.1 distinguishes six units and gives 0.5–1 m for layer 4; it contains no all-unit search/negative ledger.
3. **Strullu-Derrien et al. (2010), pp.831–833, Fig.3; pp.835–837, Fig.7.** Châteaupanne has seven currently exposed places and a map of fourteen positions, some removed. The described macrofossils came from localities 11, 12 and 13; palynological samples from 11, 13 and 14, with five preparations; petrographic samples are C2, C8 and C9. These are distinct units and methods. Fig.3’s plant symbols identify reported plant levels, but their omission at other logs does not declare complete E3-negative searches. This is a useful younger preservation/protocol lead, but is not a qualified reference frame for M3.

`m3_source_denominator_assessment.csv` gives the file and page evidence for every source. `primary_material_inventory.csv` records each potentially misleading quantity and why it is not an E3 trial count. The assessment concerns sampling/reporting; it does not re-adjudicate the repository’s anatomical or age readings. In particular, preserve the main Sheinwoodian *C. barrandei* reading and its wider horizon alternative; a stem morphotype predating a crown maximum is not, by itself, a contradiction of the crown date.

## What would admit a design

A bounded follow-up would specify a list of sections and lithological search strata independently of E3 recovery; enumerate every exposed/processed material block and exclude inaccessible or unsearched material explicitly; record area, rock volume or another defensible search-effort measure with protocol and failures; report E3 detected, explicit non-detection or unresolved morphology for every eligible unit; and preserve the link between collecting visits, fragments, slabs, horizons, sections and publications. Older and reference material need comparable reporting and preservation classes, independently supported age assignments and adequate reference support with complete detection/non-detection reporting in each class; an all-positive reference requires explicit boundary-rate handling. Museum re-examination, field collection and preparation methods should not silently share a detection rate.

The most direct routes in this roster are a field ledger for the Irish siltstone/mudstone sequence or the Loděnice section, plus a defensible reference sample frame. The publications alone cannot reconstruct those ledgers. The detailed Châteaupanne logs can guide methods and facies coding but do not justify transplanting its detection rate, nor any spore productivity or palynological rate, to Silurian compression fossils.

`prospective_scenarios.csv` is a transparent **hypothetical design illustration only**. If an independent, eligible material block has a known detection probability p under an explicitly stated lineage/persistence scenario, and every block is completely searched/reported, then P(no detections in n blocks)=(1-p)^n. To make that probability at most 0.05 requires 299, 59, 29, 14 or 5 independent blocks at p=0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 or 0.50 respectively. None of those p values is estimated or supported by these holdings. Multiple specimens from one bed do not create those independent blocks; when dependence or rate transport is unresolved, this calculation supplies no empirical sampling requirement. It is a simple event-detection calculation, not the qualified F3 pipeline or a probability that a clock maximum is true.

## Reproduce and integrate

Run `python3 data/units/sprint2/m3/reproduce.py`. It verifies 17 input file hashes, recomputes database grain/coverage and conditional illustration tables, and writes output hashes to `receipt.json`. The source-assessment and material-inventory CSVs are manual transcriptions with page locators, not discoveries regenerated by the script. Outputs stay in `data/units/sprint2/m3/`.

The 17 checks comprise the used PBDB/GBDB extracts and GBDB provenance/field files, the locally held all-taxon PBDB raw pull, and six primary PDFs. All match their held registers. Remote EPRP raw snapshots and GBDB workbooks were not re-opened; the verification claim for their local derivatives is therefore explicitly limited to the committed manifests. Copyrighted source text and rendered PDF pages remain in local scratch; only derived tables, the reproducer, criteria, receipt and this paraphrased assessment are integrated.
