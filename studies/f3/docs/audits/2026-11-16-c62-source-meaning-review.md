# C62 source and meaning review

Codex, 16 November 2026. Bounded review of `da3f432`: the new estimand, Discussion, Conditions and Table S19, checked against C43 and the held primary sources. Numbers, bindings, reproduction and C61 row invariance belong to the parent review. No product, ledger or code was edited here.

## Source anchor: passes, with its existing scope

The held `library/closed/rubinstein2019-open_firststudy.pdf` is Rubinstein and Vajda (2019), *Baltica cradle of early land plants?*, DOI `10.1080/11035897.2019.1636860`. Printed p. 188 names definite triletes including *Aneurospora* at 43.1 m and places these occurrences below the Kinnekulle K-bentonite, citing its 454.41 ± 0.17 Ma age. This supports C43 and the nominal 454.41 Ma minimum anchor used in C62. It does not depend on the disputed 41.1 m specimen or the possible 48.2 m specimen.

The paper treats the spores as evidence of land plants, while explicitly leaving their higher taxonomic assignment uncertain (p. 181). Thus land plants before that age is supported under the adopted reading; neither crown Embryophyta nor crown Tracheophyta follows from the trilete mark alone. Broad land-plant continuity into the later interval is a reasonable lineage inference. It does not establish survival of the same local populations, the same producer lineage, the same body plan or the same recoverable-body category throughout the span.

## Material meaning problems

1. **The Discussion dropped the Oman exclusion.** It says that in the main reading no land-plant body is older than 432.9 Ma. Canonical main B0 includes the accepted Oman fragments at Katian ages, as the estimands file and Table 2 state. The no-Oman qualification survives in the new estimand and supplement, but must also be attached to the Discussion's zero-count interval and to the corresponding ledger wording.

2. **The zero-count precedent is an exclusion sensitivity, not the canonical observed record.** The 13.4-fold quantity is the model requirement for removing Oman and treating the resulting 21.5 Myr span as empty. The actual admitted record has a positive body find inside that span. Consequently, the assertion that a requirement of this size has been met once in this record is stronger than the evidence establishes. Even in a genuinely empty interval, the factor is a compatibility threshold under the declared rate, independent-count and recognisability assumptions, rather than a measurement of the historical rate ratio or delay. The caveats about post-selection and the factor's algebraic origin do not turn that threshold into an observed biological quantity.

   The useful interpretation can be retained: spores independently establish early plant presence, and the body record remains sparse despite it. The accepted Oman-to-next-body gap provides the more direct precedent for long body-record sparsity, with C61's own interval, counts and conditional requirements. If C62 is retained as calculated, identify it as the no-Oman sensitivity and avoid declaring its exact requirement historically demonstrated.

3. **Oman weakens a universal diagnostic-delay explanation without separating all causes.** The held `library/closed/al2003d_firststudy.pdf`, Wellman et al. (2003), DOI `10.1038/nature01884`, pp. 282–284, describes spore masses with preserved presumed sporangial covering. Under the adopted Katian reading this is a recognisable B0 body inside the interval. It therefore rules out a universal absence of recognisable land-plant bodies until 432.9 Ma. It does not demonstrate that later members retained equally diagnosable or preservable bodies, nor separate encounter, preservation, searching and taxon-specific recognisability. The Discussion's cautious direction toward lower finding is defensible if it makes this distinction; the estimand's exclusive contrast between finding and recognisability is too categorical.

## Transfer to vascular ancestors

The numerical comparison to C48 can describe similar conditional requirements for different admitted records. It cannot establish that the biological or taphonomic mechanisms needed for older vascular ancestors actually occurred. C62 concerns bodies of any land plant; the trilete evidence does not supply the anatomy, architecture or preservational properties of the producers. Wellman et al. infer very small parent plants from small Oman sporangia (pp. 283–284), but no whole plant or conducting tissue is preserved. That does not establish that all early trilete producers were small and unlignified. Treat those features as possible differences or an explicit interpretation, not observed properties of the whole pre-432.9 Ma group.

The existing caveat that the two factors derive from interval lengths and later counts is valuable and should stay. So should the distinction between a broad land-plant record and the particular crown-vascular ancestor. No additional inventory work is needed to make those qualifications.

Finally, the anchor comes from a different fossil channel; this does not establish statistical independence of all source selection, or make it independent of every selected fossil find. Saying that it comes from the spore record rather than the selected oldest body find states the useful distinction directly. The explicit post-selection qualification should remain.

## Disposition

The primary-source anchor is sound. The broad observation of early plant presence alongside a sparse body record is useful. The canonical/exclusion distinction and the claim that the numerical requirement has already been realised need repair before C62 is supported in its current form.
