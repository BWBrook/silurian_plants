# Earliest triletes: recommendation for adjudication

Codex, 5 November 2026. This is a bounded comparison of Borenshult-1, the Upper
Ordovician Turkish record, Zbrza and Qusaiba-1. It uses published sources only. Private
author correspondence was neither opened nor used.

## Recommendation for adjudication

The four records should not be reduced to one acceptance score. Their specimen bases,
authors' identifications, age controls and possible failure processes are different and are
not exchangeable.

- **Qusaiba-1 remains the secure comparator.** It has at least 19 measured specimens
  distributed among eight trilete taxa, trilete taxa reported in 22 of 37 samples, explicit
  author language of true or undoubted trilete spores, and independent age control from
  several fossil groups and glacial stratigraphy. The exact number of trilete specimens
  observed was not reported, so neither 19 nor the plate panels is a total count.
- **Borenshult should no longer be rejected wholesale.** I propose a five-depth
  concordant subset of definite trilete-bearing depths, beginning at
  43.1 m: 43.1, 41.9, 40.35, 40.2 and 33.05 m. Figure 3 adds a sixth definite depth at
  41.1 m, but Figure 4, the results text and Figure 5 do not. Retain the six-depth
  Figure 3 reading as an explicit alternative, with 41.1 m documentary-conflicted.
  Keep 48.2 and 34.0 m possible only. Hooper et al.'s later compression concern is a
  reason for a sensitivity reading, not a demonstrated artefact in any named specimen.
- **Retain the Turkish occurrence as the describing authors' unqueried
  *Ambitisporites avitus–dilutus* morphon, but weaken its usual age label.** The original
  paper gives an Ashgillian-to-Hirnantian placement, supported by acritarchs and
  chitinozoans; it does not by itself establish a uniquely Hirnantian occurrence. Its exact
  Upper Ordovician specimen count is also unreported. Later papers' shorthand
  “Hirnantian Turkey” must not make this record appear better counted or more precisely
  dated than its original evidence supports; a later primary stratigraphic revision could
  still narrow the age.
- **Do not admit Zbrza as a true-trilete occurrence.** Its authors report rare
  *Ambitisporites*-like spores but explicitly say the irregular marks cast doubt on their
  trilete nature. The host sediment is well supported as Hirnantian; that strong age does
  not resolve the spore character. Neither the authors nor the later review demonstrate
  that the marks are artefacts.

Thus the recommendation is narrow but material: adjudication should admit the
reviewer-proposed five source-concordant Borenshult depths as author-identified
definite triletes, while retaining the authors' six-depth Figure 3 reading as an explicit
alternative with 41.1 m conflicted and the two queried depths possible. Pending a later
primary stratigraphic revision, the Turkish occurrence should retain the original
Ashgillian–Hirnantian wording; Qusaiba-1 remains unequivocal and Zbrza remains outside
strict true-trilete occurrences. A sensitivity analysis may
exclude all Borenshult specimens under Hooper et al.'s proposed compression concern, but
that is not the same evidential statement as a demonstrated false mark.

## Question, observation process and rubric

The decision is the source-attributed status to assign to each occurrence, not the
unobservable proposition that a particular Ordovician plant certainly made a functional
trilete spore. The observation process comprises rock selection, palynological extraction,
slide search, preservation and compression, recognition of a Y-shaped mark, taxonomic
assignment and stratigraphic placement. A sampled horizon is not a specimen; several
specimens from one horizon are not independent localities; and several horizons in one
core do not replicate an evolutionary origin.

The records also have different sampling units. Borenshult has 34 samples from one core;
Qusaiba-1 has 37 samples from one core; the Turkish paper combines a section and four
core wells over Upper Ordovician and Upper Silurian strata; Zbrza searched about 1400 kg
of fresh mudstone and processed six palynological samples from one exposed layer. These
effort measures help interpret non-detection and documentation, but they cannot be used
as a common denominator for specimen abundance.

Each axis is reported separately:

1. **Specimen basis:** an exact reported number, a defensible lower bound, or not
   reported. Positive horizons and plate panels are also stated, but never substituted for
   a total specimen count.
2. **Describing authors' confidence:** definite/unqueried, probable/possible, or
   explicitly doubted.
3. **Independent age control:** which evidence dates the host level independently of the
   spore identification, and the temporal resolution it actually supports.
4. **Artefact evidence:** demonstrated on the claimed specimen, proposed as a
   specimen-specific concern, or only a generic failure process. A generic possibility is
   not scored as a demonstrated false positive.
5. **Documentary consistency:** agreement or conflict among text, tables, figures and
   later readings.

There is deliberately no summed or weighted score. High age confidence cannot rescue an
ambiguous mark, and many specimens cannot repair a misdated unit.

## Source checks and exact locators

### Borenshult-1

Rubinstein & Vajda (2019), DOI `10.1080/11035897.2019.1636860`, held at
`library/closed/rubinstein2019-open_firststudy.pdf`:

- Figure 3, p. 183: distinct symbols for trilete and possible trilete spores; direct
  visual reinspection at 300 dpi confirmed definite symbols at 43.1, 41.9, 41.1,
  40.35, 40.2 and 33.05 m, and possible symbols at 48.2 and 34.0 m.
- Figure 4, p. 184: trilete taxon occurrences at 48.2, 43.1, 41.9, 40.35, 40.2,
  34.0 and 33.05 m, but none at 41.1 m. The 48.2 and 34.0 m taxa carry question
  marks. The caption states that the 300-count is a combined marine/terrestrial
  palynomorph count, not a trilete specimen inventory.
- Figure 5 and caption, p. 185: 11 selected Sandbian specimens A–K, each with depth
  and England Finder reference. Two are from 43.1 m and four from 40.2 m. Panel G
  is *Retusotriletes?* sp. at 34.0 m and panel I is *Chelinospora?* sp. at 48.2 m.
  No 41.1 m trilete is figured.
- Results, pp. 186–187: the 48.2 m report is explicitly a single probable specimen;
  the “consistently well-preserved” definite records named in text are
  *Aneurospora* at 43.1 m, *Ambitisporites* at 41.9 m, *Leiotriletes* at 40.35 m
  and *Retusotriletes* at 40.2 m.
- Materials and methods, p. 184: the independent framework uses conodonts,
  graptolites and chitinozoans plus bentonite constraints; the text gives
  454.41 ± 0.17 Ma for the correlated Kinnekulle K-bentonite.

Hooper et al. (2026), DOI `10.1038/s41477-026-02432-3`, main-text PDF p. 3,
calibration paragraph (`library/closed/hooper2026_firststudy.pdf`), states only that
the authors are concerned the marks in the figured specimens “might” be compression
artefacts. Supplementary Table 4, crown-Embryophyta entry, pp. 52–54 of
`data/external/local/deposits/hooper2026-spore-evolution-esm/41477_2026_2432_MOESM1_ESM.pdf`,
calls the 48.2 m *Chelinospora?* specimen probable and Qusaiba's triletes unequivocal.
This supports separate possible/definite and sensitivity readings; it does not report a
re-examination that demonstrated compression in any specimen.

### South-eastern Turkey

Steemans, Le Hérissé & Bozdogan (1996), DOI
`10.1016/0034-6667(95)00119-0`, held at
`library/closed/al1996_macbook20261030.pdf`. The library register records a complete
42-page publisher-layout copy with
SHA-256 `116d57d6f80fd3fa33d0a228418554331d37f65122d8cdb9bd3e1fe0a4e9f773`;
its poor text layer required visual checking of the relevant pages.

- Systematic palynology, p. 47: the unqueried *Ambitisporites avitus* morphon
  includes *A. avitus–dilutus*; nine specimens measured, with distribution spanning
  named Late Ordovician samples and the Late Silurian Dadas samples. This pooled
  count cannot be assigned wholly to the Ordovician claim.
- Figure 3, p. 39, and distribution/age discussion, p. 71: the Upper Ordovician
  occurrence is tied to samples B2, H2 and GH6–GH7; the authors describe specimens
  in the basal morphon-bearing zone as rare and small.
- Ordovician age discussion, p. 40: acritarch and chitinozoan evidence permits the
  stratigraphic position and oldest *Ambitisporites* occurrence to be “more
  Ashgillian to Hirnantian”. Page 71 calls the biozone base Ashgillian on acritarch and
  chitinozoan data. The source therefore does not justify a uniquely Hirnantian label.

Wellman et al. (2015), p. 122, later describes the Turkish record as very rare
laevigate triletes from the Hirnantian. This is a useful later synthesis, but it does not
itself document a new primary stratigraphic revision or supply the missing specimen count.
No later primary revision of the Turkish sample ages was identified in this bounded audit.

### Zbrza

Salamon et al. (2018), DOI `10.1111/nph.15091`, main paper
`library/closed/salamon2018.pdf` and supplement `library/closed/salamon2018_SI.pdf`:

- Main paper, pp. 1305–1306, Figure 2g,h and caption: rare compressed spores from
  slide ZbrzaA resemble *A. avitus–dilutus*, but their irregular marks “cast doubts”
  on trilete nature. The two panels do not establish the total number of specimens.
- Supplement, p. 2: about 1400 kg of freshly excavated mudstone produced only a few
  putative plant macrofossils; six palynological samples were processed. This is search
  effort, not a spore count.
- Supplement, pp. 3–4: abundant, well-preserved palynomorphs come from the
  plant-bearing layer. Common *Desmochitina typica* precludes Silurian, while
  *Spinachitina oulebsiri* and abundant *Euconochitina moussegoudaensis* support
  Hirnantian. The older acritarch component is explicitly mixed or recycled.

Wellman, Cascales-Miñana & Servais (2022), DOI `10.1144/SP532-2022-92`,
`library/open/W4306698490.pdf`, PDF p. 4, places Salamon et al. among pre-Silurian
claims lacking sufficient unequivocal land-plant characters. PDF p. 6 explains the
generic process by which torn permanent tetrads can acquire false trilete marks. It does
not state that this process was demonstrated in the Zbrza spores.

### Qusaiba-1

Wellman, Steemans & Miller (2015), DOI
`10.1016/j.revpalbo.2014.09.003`,
`library/closed/wellman2015_firststudy.pdf`:

- Systematics, pp. 117–118: measured trilete specimens are *Ambitisporites avitus*
  (3), *Aneurospora* sp. A (1), *Chelinospora prisca* (10), *Chelinospora* sp. A
  (1), *Leiotriletes* sp. A (1), *Synorisporites?* sp. A (1), *Synorisporites* sp. B
  (1) and *Synorisporites?* sp. C (1), giving a documented lower bound of 19.
  Several entries say “measured” rather than “observed”, so the sum is not a total.
- Table 2, p. 116: direct transcription gives 22 of 37 sampled depths with at least one
  trilete taxon and 15 without a trilete taxon. `data/units/qusaiba_inventory.csv`
  reproduces this 22/15 split. Table 2 prints `m`, whereas the core log and Plate IV
  caption use the same numeric values in feet.
- Age dating, p. 122: all 37 samples were rich; independent ages come from
  chitinozoans and acritarchs, graptolites in the upper units, and Hirnantian glacial
  lithostratigraphy. The authors call the hilate and trilete spores undoubted and state
  that they occur below the Hirnantian.
- Assemblage and interpretation, pp. 122–124: eight trilete taxa are present, the
  naturally separating hilate/trilete component is less than 1% of the spore
  assemblage, and the authors repeatedly call the spores true. Abundance below 1% is
  an assemblage fraction, not a count of trilete specimens.

## Checks, uncertainties and next decision

- DOI identities for all six cited papers were checked against Crossref on
  5 November 2026; returned titles and years matched the held sources and manifests.
- Borenshult Figures 3–5 were independently re-rendered and visually inspected for this
  audit. The result agrees with the 4 November figure audit but does not depend on
  private correspondence.
- The Turkish scan is readable but its text layer is poor. Exact Upper Ordovician
  specimen count remains unavailable in the paper; the nine measured specimens are
  pooled across Ordovician and Silurian material.
- No specimen was physically re-examined. “Definite” and “true” are the describing
  authors' published identifications, not independent microscopy by this audit.
- The next decision is whether the strict occurrence dataset should adopt the
  reviewer-proposed five-depth concordant Borenshult subset, while retaining the
  six-depth Figure 3 reading and wholesale compression-exclusion reading as explicit
  alternatives. The evidence rubric recommends the five-depth subset. It does not
  recommend promoting Zbrza or narrowing Turkey to Hirnantian without a later primary
  stratigraphic revision.
