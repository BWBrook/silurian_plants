## Geographic occurrence and finding frequency

Late-Silurian crown fossils occur in six named palaeoregions in the reviewed inventory and three even among database-backed finds. The observations establish a minimum geographical extent by that time. The changing frequency of records could reflect the plants, where they were preserved, which beds were searched or which occurrences entered a compilation. A count of region–stage cells supplies neither dispersal speed nor local population density. [Occurrence units](../../models/loop3/out/inventory_symmetry_units.csv) · [Regional counts](../../models/loop3/out/inventory_symmetry_stage2_tests.csv)

I would drop “8–12 times more Devonian than Silurian” as a shared result. Of 59 database-backed finds, 5–7 can be late Silurian, giving Devonian/Silurian count ratios of 7.43–10.80. Capel’s main reading gives 7–8 of 109 and ratios of 12.63–14.57 **for species endpoints**. The two intervals also span 26.15 and 7.08 Myr. A higher raw count in the longer interval can occur at a steady rate. In the main text the three to six represented regions and the dependence of the frequency estimate on curation carry the science. ## What the coarse ages allow

The 21 units added to the 38 guaranteed S2a localities all carry the same 393.47–419.62 Ma database range. I constructed two complete assignments for S2b’s 59 finds, using ages within every original range. In one, just two restored units lie younger than 400 Ma (finite-sample KS *p* = 0.08267). In the other all 21 do (*p* = 0.23844). Both assignments have an oldest age of 425.0 Ma within the 426.7 Ma envelope. Putting the finds at their recorded midpoints gives *p* = 5.93 × 10⁻²⁰. These examples show precisely what the 10 Myr age-shape test admits. They supply no estimate of when an individual fossil lived. [Complete assignments](2026-11-23-inventory-age-examples.csv) · [Reproduction script](2026-11-23-inventory-age-examples.py)

Two localities illustrate the importance of age resolution. Morris and Edwards place the Newton Dingle plant bed (PBDB 25242) in the late Lochkovian. The collection note for La Gileppe (34965) gives upper Lochkovian or lower Pragian. Using the full outer stages puts their youngest possible ages at 413.02 and 410.62 Ma respectively. Narrowing only those two supports lowers S2b’s best *p* from 0.23844 to 0.06987. This checks the effect of a conservative stage restriction. The finer positions within those stages come from stratigraphy. Capel dates its Lerbekk entry at Røragen to the early Emsian. The Helderberg Group correlation at Milesburg and the Callander and Young Inlet locality names would need collection-to-horizon evidence for narrower dates. [Morris and Edwards, §2.2, p. 29](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.revpalbo.2014.09.006) · [PBDB collection notes](../../../../data/eprp/record/pbdb_plant_collections.csv) · [Capel row 1174](../../models/loop3/out/capel_s3_rows.csv)

S1’s Devonian dates contribute to its shape rejection. Keeping the 13 Silurian-only ranges and all three crossing ranges, and widening only the 53 Devonian-only ranges to their epoch, changes the best *p* from 0.00425 to 0.10496. This is a diagnostic of where S1’s age information comes from. For the 25 Myr history, the original S0 crown results are 0.03522, 0.04916, 0.04916 and 0.06713. The last already passes. On S1 all four pass. The main branched inventory, the land-plant bodies including Oman and the inclusive branched inventory with Zbrza each change from failure in all four S0 cases to a pass in all four S1 cases. Those reversals establish inventory sensitivity. [Shape results](../../models/loop3/out/inventory_symmetry_stage2_shape.csv) · [Reproduction script](2026-11-23-inventory-age-examples.py)

Keep three distinct meanings in the estimands. C49 asks whether any assignment of ages in range fits a specified whole-window shape. C60 asks where finds fall within that window. A 10 Myr exponential time constant implies a 27.74-fold rise from 426.7 to 393.47 Ma. That exceeds both of S2b’s upper regional benchmarks for an exponential rise (18.10 and 12.54), even though the C49 locality-age test admits the history. C57’s early rises end at the oldest find and assume a steady later rate. An increase in finding through the Devonian remains possible. Its timescale and biological cause remain unresolved.
