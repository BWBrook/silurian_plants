# M3 record identity: the Homerian *Cooksonia* behind the crown-tracheophyte maximum

Claude Opus 5.5, 30 October 2026, for the trait pilot (`docs/pilot-traits.md`, P3b record
identity) and Q2. Sources read in full or at the cited pages; page numbers are printed pages
unless marked PDF.
- Hooper et al. 2026, Supplementary Table 4, PDF pp. 16–17 (C_4) and 23–24 (C_24).
- Edwards and Feehan 1980.
- Edwards, Feehan and Smith 1983.
- Gonez and Gerrienne 2010a.
- Garza et al. 2024.

This is an adjudication of the record, not a new analysis.

## What the calibration claims

Hooper et al. use *Cooksonia* cf. *pertoni*, TCD 22944–22956, from the Cloncannon Formation at
the top of Borrisnoe Mountain, Devil's Bit range, Co. Tipperary, twice:
- **C_4, the minimum for crown Embryophyta, 426.9 Ma.** The top of the *Colonograptus ludensis*
  Biozone, 427.4 ± 0.5 Ma.
- **C_24, the soft maximum for crown Tracheophyta, 431.2 Ma.** The base of the Homerian,
  430.5 ± 0.7 Ma.

Both rest on the same phylogenetic justification. *Cooksonia* is placed in total-group
Tracheophyta because younger specimens of *C. pertoni* have tracheids, and because *Cooksonia*
has stomata, branching sporophytes and sterome.
- C_4 calls stomata and branching sporophytes total-group synapomorphies.
- C_24 calls them crown-group synapomorphies and sterome a total-group character.

The two entries are inconsistent on this point.

## What the Irish specimens preserve (O)

| Character | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| C3, branched with several sporangia | **direct, if the terminal expansions are sporangia.** TCD 22946d has a terminal expansion on each branch of a dichotomy, and TCD 22946g has two near a branch point. No spores were isolated from any expansion, so the authors state these "can only be assumed to be sporangia" | Edwards et al. 1983, pp. 21, 23; Edwards and Feehan 1980, Fig. 1 |
| C1, trilete spores in situ | **unknown.** Film pulls of the sporangia yielded no spores. *Ambitisporites*-type trilete spores occur dispersed in the same sediments, not in situ | Edwards et al. 1983, pp. 25, 29–32 |
| C4, tracheids | **unknown.** No tracheids, and no well-preserved cellular detail, in fertile or sterile axes. The authors judged *Cooksonia*'s vascular status "conjectural" for fertile axes. They explicitly allowed that the Irish fossils might share *Cooksonia*'s form without conducting tissue, the level of organisation called rhyniophytoid | Edwards et al. 1983, pp. 20, 33; Edwards and Feehan 1980, p. 42 |
| Stomata, sterome | **not reported** in either description (text searched) | Edwards and Feehan 1980; Edwards et al. 1983 |

**Taxonomy.** Gonez and Gerrienne (2010a) accept the Irish sporangia as *Cooksonia* by sporangial
form, and note that the material "was not demonstrated to be vascularized". Their emended
diagnosis of *Cooksonia* includes stomata, a peripheral sterome and a central strand of C-type
tracheids, none of which the Irish material shows.

**Placement is disputed.** *C. pertoni* has been placed in two ways:
- **On the stem of the eutracheophytes, outside the crown.** Kenrick et al. 2012 and Edwards and
  Kenrick 2015, as cited by `taxa/cooksonia.md`.
- **As sister to the Lycophytina, inside the crown.** Gonez and Gerrienne's (2010a) cladistic
  analyses resolve *C. pertoni* with *C. paranensis* there, and find the genus *sensu* Lang
  polyphyletic. Hooper et al.'s own ancestral-state trees also put *Cooksonia* on the lycophyte
  line.

**Age.**
- Edwards and Feehan (1980) place the beds in the *M. ludensis* Zone, the highest Wenlock, and
  Hooper et al. follow them.
- Garza et al. (2024) date the plant-bearing siltstone (sample 22MSS, linked to TCD 22944) to a
  detrital-zircon maximum depositional age of 426 ± 2 Ma, which they class as Gorstian–Homerian.
- Garza et al. also report that Cope (1959) assigned the Cloncannon Formation to the lower Ludlow
  (*M. tumescens* Zone). They attribute the plants to what they call the Hollyford Formation.

## Assessment (I)

1. **The record's identity is a transfer.** The Homerian specimens show branching with terminal
   expansions, and nothing else that places them. Total-group tracheophyte status, and the stomata,
   sterome and tracheids the calibration cites, come from Lochkovian *C. pertoni*. Under the pilot's
   evidence levels that is a **transfer**, and the original authors' rhyniophytoid caveat contests it.
   - In the pilot's audited specimens, C3 without tracheids occurs in *Aglaophyton*.
   - Branching therefore does not by itself mark a tracheophyte, let alone the crown.
2. **Which node?** If the Irish fossils are *C. pertoni*-like members of the lycophyte line, as
   Gonez and Gerrienne's and Hooper et al.'s placements imply, they are crown tracheophytes. A crown member's first
   appearance can bound the crown's age only under the absence premise F3 tests. It also has to be
   the oldest crown member. *C. barrandei* (middle Sheinwoodian by correlation; Libertín et al. 2018)
   is older than the 431.2 Ma maximum. On the stem-eutracheophyte placement, or if the Irish fossils
   are stem polysporangiophytes without tracheids, they bear on the total group only, as the
   calibration assumes.
3. **Age.** The 426 ± 2 Ma depositional maximum is consistent with the latest Homerian. It also
   allows a Gorstian age, which an earlier stratigraphic scheme preferred. The 431.2 Ma maximum
   takes the base of the Homerian; it does not use the ages of these beds.
4. **Hooper et al.'s own premise.** For the spore maximum they write that, because the trilete
   spore is ancestral, its "first appearance must pre-date the origin of crown-Embryophyta" (SI
   PDF p. 16). That holds for the trait's origin, not for its first fossil occurrence. The step from
   one to the other is the absence premise F3 tests. The same paragraph says the spores "can be
   used to minimally constrain this node", apparently a slip for maximally.

**Status for the pilot:**
- M3 record identity: **contested**, resting on transfer.
- P3a (C3 at the crown-tracheophyte node): unaffected.
- The M3 node statement must carry both readings, total-group or crown, and the *C. barrandei*
  precedence.

**What would settle it.** Anatomy (tracheids, sterome, stomata) or in situ spores from the Irish
specimens TCD 22944–22956, or from *C. barrandei*. A specialist re-examination of the Trinity
College Dublin material is worth flagging (contact is Barry's decision).
