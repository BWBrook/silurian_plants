# Science audit of reconstruction steps 1–2

Codex, 10 November 2026, reviewed against `1fe577c`. **This is a review note, not reconstruction
step 3.** Parent Codex owns edits to the draft files; this note preserves an independent reading.
No model, ledger, generated product or frozen manuscript was changed for this audit.

## Decision

The three assessments and the recording–diagnosis trade-off can carry the paper. The present
one-page version needs scientific repairs before its hypotheses and main message are adopted.
The existing numerical results suffice; another analysis is unnecessary for this reconstruction.

Four changes are material:

1. Present the three assessments as research questions assembled during the study, rather than
   prospective H1–H3. The record explicitly identifies the body analysis as a revision after the pilot.
2. State the Libyan result as a model-dependent upper compatibility limit on sustained local encounter,
   rather than an observation that producers were less abundant.
3. Describe 15–23 Myr as minimum **assumed** diagnostic delays at the constant-history bound, alongside
   the zero-delay recording contrast. These are alternative edges of a rate–delay trade-off.
4. Replace the global young-origin message. Equal earlier/later recording is neither necessary nor
   sufficient for the claimed conclusion, even before translating fossil-category endpoints to nodes.

## The main-message objection has a numerical counterexample

Claude's current sentence is:

> The fossil record supports a young origin of land plants only if the earliest plants were fossilised
> and found as often as later ones.

For the existing primary B2 step construction, $A=426.7$, $c=393.47$ and $n=48$. The extra exposure
needed at the 0.05 comparison contour is

$$E=(A-c)(0.05^{-1/n}-1)=2.140005949\ \mathrm{Myr}.$$

At earlier/later intensity $r=0.5$, the contour is

$$T=A+E/r=430.980012\ \mathrm{Ma},$$

still younger than the 431.2 Ma maximum. Thus even the selected model permits a twofold earlier
recording reduction while retaining a body-record bound younger than that maximum. Equality of rates
is not a necessary condition. Conversely, constant recording with coarser unit rules gives 432.38 or
434.05 Ma (C30), so rate equality alone is insufficient. The category-to-node link and diagnostic delay
remain separate assumptions. Murzuq is local; M1 lacks a quantitative frame. They cannot jointly supply
the claimed global young-origin support.

This is a substantive disagreement with the working message, not a disagreement with Barry's positive,
plain-language prose rule. Preserve Claude's version and this reason beside the proposed replacement.

A narrow positive alternative for the strongest quantitative result is:

> Older vascular-plant origins fit the body fossils through lower early recording rates or a later
> appearance of identifying features.

A broader alternative, whose detail belongs in the results, is:

> Fossil limits on plant origins depend on where plants lived, how their remains were recorded, and when
> identifying features appeared.

Both avoid treating this study as independent support for a clock. The second is broader but less
specific; the first foregrounds the result the frontier actually quantifies.

## Hypothesis provenance and scientific form

`studies/f3/docs/loop3-design.md` opens by saying that it describes the implemented analysis after source
corrections and is **not a preregistration before seeing the pilot**. Its extrapolation grid was added
after seeing the steep-decline shape results. `studies/f3/docs/plan-f3.md` explicitly distinguishes estimator
acceptance criteria from predictions about which timescale is correct. The declared fixed-history
order-statistic formula predicts narrowing with increasing sample size, but it does not prospectively
predict proximity to a particular clock. That proximity also depends on the admitted oldest fossil,
cutoff and unit count; some of those fossils calibrate the clocks themselves.

H1 is an organising source-audit question. H2 concerns the scope of an observation model and the
delivery–intensity identification limit. H3 is a retrospective quantitative question. They can structure
Introduction and Results without a false prospective history:

1. What evidence and assumptions support each maximum?
2. What histories of sustained trilete encounter do the Libyan cuttings permit?
3. What recording contrast or assumed diagnostic delay would make older vascular-plant ages compatible
   with the body record?

The statement that spore absence is **more informative than body absence** is not tested by this study;
the channels have different frames, targets and mappings. Spore durability motivates the investigation
but does not establish this comparative result.

## Exact checks for the two questioned numerical paraphrases

### Murzuq, C24

Selected `studies/f3/models/out/m2_results.csv` by `design=LY-MZ-CU`, `primary=True`,
`variant=allocation=stage;cluster_k=2.0;material_type=chart-code`:

| Fixed delivery rho | H0c at 459.1 Ma | H0 at 459.1 Ma |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.0910 | 0.1835 |
| 0.1 | 0.9103 | 0.9207 |

The constant sustained-path result concerns $\rho f$ near 0.091. Under the more general H0 class the
exposure-weighted result differs. Relative producer abundance requires additional proportional and
comparable detection assumptions (`studies/f3/docs/f3-shape.md`, layer 2): production, transport, preservation,
preparation and reporting intervene. Equal delivery alone cannot establish historical abundance.

Compact replacement:

> Under constant sustained input and equal delivery, the model puts the upper limit on earlier trilete
> encounter at about a tenth of the later rate. A tenfold delivery reduction raises that limit to near
> the reference level.

The assumptions paragraph must retain the age mixture, reference-purity and well-effect scope. The
10/18 temporal split matters in the full text/display because most older samples postdate 459.1 Ma.

### Body-record rate and delay, C33/C48

`studies/f3/models/loop3/out/frontier.csv`, `reading=main`, `unit_treatment=primary`, `delay=0`, gives later/earlier
ratios 8.0841 at 444 Ma, 9.4860 at 447 Ma and 11.8224 at 452 Ma. These are flat-plus-step compatibility
contours at the data-selected oldest possible B2 age, not a fitted earlier rate.

`delay_sensitivity.csv`, `category=B2`, `history=flat`, gives recoverability bound 428.84000595 Ma.
Subtracting it from candidate ages 444 and 452 gives 15.15999405 and 23.15999405 Myr. They are the minimum
**assumed** offsets needed at that bound; longer delays are also possible, and rate reduction and delay
can be combined.

Compact replacement:

> With zero assumed diagnostic delay, the retrospective step model accommodates ages of 444–452 Ma at
> earlier recording rates about 8–12 times lower. Under constant recording, assumed delays of at least
> 15–23 Myr provide an alternative route; intermediate combinations trade off rate and delay.

## Consequences for steps 1a and 1b

- Replace “have never been tested” with the specific gap this audit establishes; a universal novelty
  claim requires a literature-wide argument.
- Remove “the oldest fails” where the finding is an unquantified link. Lack of quantification does
  not itself falsify the published maximum.
- Replace the assertion that both younger maxima “hold if recording was roughly constant”. Murzuq
  provides a local sustained-encounter constraint, not a dated global absence; the B2 bound crosses
  the maximum under other unit rules.
- Remove “H3…prediction made in advance…and it held”. Preserve the retrospective provenance.
- In the abstract, identify zero delay at the 8–12 factor and keep the primary/coarse-unit sensitivity
  at useful precision. The coarser 432–434 Ma range changes the 431.2 Ma comparison, so it has higher
  priority than repeating the Libyan counts when expanding towards 200 words.
- Treat the recording contrast as one of several unresolved links. Measuring it is a later candidate
  investigation, not an analysis required to complete this paper or a guarantee of choosing a clock.
