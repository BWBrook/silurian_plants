## I2: define soft maxima without a prevalence claim

Replace “Clock analyses usually express maxima as soft bounds that leave a small probability for
older ages” with:

> A soft maximum permits older ages through a specified tail of the calibration prior.

Yang and Rannala's original method explicitly permits probability outside calibration bounds. It is
the direct primary source for this definition. It does not establish how frequently current analyses
use soft maxima. The word *prior* also separates a chosen calibration tail from the posterior
probability of an older divergence after the molecular analysis.

Parham et al. support the following sentence about explicit justification. Their discussion of
maximum ages calls for arguments grounded in the relevant geological, ecological, geographical and
taphonomic record, and recommends soft, liberal maxima. Their historical statements about practice
do not establish prevalence in 2026. These two sources cover I2's added methodological context;
reading all six calibration candidates is unnecessary unless the prose makes further claims.

## D3: Wilkinson is distinct but optional

Wilkinson et al. fit a branching-and-sampling model to fossil species counts, their geological
distribution and extant diversity, then use the fossil-derived age distribution as a prior in a
molecular dating analysis. This is a substantive precedent for process-based fossil information
entering a molecular clock. It is broader than choosing a soft-bound tail and differs from the
present conditional bounds on recording categories.

D3 already has a clear comparative purpose: the nearest plant application, a method producing
calibration densities, and the recalibration that motivates this study. Wilkinson adds no necessary
support to its current sentences. Omit the candidate from this paragraph. Retain it only if the
authors want to explain the additional methodological option above, with a sentence doing that
work; an extra name alone would dilute the comparison. This recommendation does not treat
Wilkinson and CladeDate as interchangeable, or imply that this study assessed either method's
assumptions.

## Reference depth and material paragraph issues

The 52–75 references in comparator papers can prompt a coverage check. They supply neither a
minimum nor evidence that 48 is inadequate. Prioritise primary calibration evidence, the estimator
methods actually used, the closest empirical comparisons, and sources supporting biological and
geological premises. Add candidates when they fill those needs; preserve their unread status until
their relevant content has been assessed. No source-count target is needed.

Three points merit attention before expansion:

- **D2 overstates attribution.** “The analysis sizes these routes” suggests separate estimates of
  rarity, distribution, tissue properties and searching. It quantifies their possible combined
  recording contrast. Make that the paragraph's one message: these processes could change
  recording, while the frontier quantifies the aggregate contrast a candidate age requires.
- **I1 starts with outputs before defining the tool.** A new reader receives six ranges before
  learning how DNA differences and fossil calibrations enter a molecular clock. Give that mechanism
  briefly and identify which study owns each range. Retaining every interval in this opening is an
  editorial choice; their ownership and meaning are essential.
- **D5 promises estimability too strongly.** Documented searched beds and effort could help
  constrain early recording under a specified model. They do not alone identify a biological
  recording rate. Read the proposed Macrostrat source before claiming that it supplies the required
  search-effort information; this review has not assessed that source.

The separation of the spore and body explanations in D1, and the distinction between divergence,
character acquisition and first recording in I3, remain sound at this planning level. The adopted
closing message remains unchanged.
