## Media summary (100 words)

DNA clocks disagree about when living land plants shared an ancestor. We examined three fossil age
limits behind those dates. Libyan wells constrain sustained local spore input; older marine rocks
provide a qualitative preservation argument. Statistical limits from plant bodies change with how
finds are counted. Older ancestors shared by clubmosses, ferns and seed plants fit if early
fossils were recorded several times less often or diagnostic bodies appeared later. These are
conditional scenarios, leaving biological plausibility open. Clock builders should state the assumptions
linking fossils to ancestors and test how alternative limits change their estimated ages using
published evidence.
