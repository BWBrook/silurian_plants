# Older than their fossils? What the Ordovician–Silurian record can test about the maxima that divide land-plant molecular clocks

<div id="authors"></div>

## Abstract

Molecular clocks place the origin of crown land plants anywhere from about 515 to 450 million years
ago (Ma), and of crown vascular plants from about 452 to 428 Ma. With the same sequence data, much of
that spread follows from fossil calibrations, and above all from maximum ages: claims that a lineage
would have left recoverable fossils earlier had it existed. We asked what the sampled Ordovician–Silurian
record can actually test about three such maxima: 515.5 and 459.1 Ma for crown land plants, and 431.2 Ma
for crown vascular plants. None can be tested as a pure absence. The 515.5 Ma maximum rests on the
affinity of Cambrian spore-like fossils, which none of the sources we examined accepts as land-plant,
not on sampled absence. The 459.1 Ma value is the base of the Sandbian stage, set by the oldest possible
trilete spore, and does not depend on whether that specimen is doubtful. One Libyan basin supplies the only
qualified quantitative test of it: [28]{.C01} older well cuttings without trilete spores against [51 of 59]{.C02}
younger cuttings with them. If older material delivered spores as well as younger and producer intensity
was constant, Late Ordovician trilete encounter was below about a tenth of the younger level; the test
cannot separate rare producers from poor delivery, and its verdict depends on assumed contamination and
dating. For vascular plants we analysed [48]{.C25} dated crown-member locality–age units. If recovery was
constant, or tracked collecting effort, crown vascular-plant bodies entered the recoverable record no
earlier than about [429]{.C27} Ma (95% bound), consistent with the young clock. In one illustrative scenario in
which recovery before the Ludlow was a tenth of later levels, the bound moves to [448]{.C32} Ma, overlapping
older clock estimates. The record within its observed range cannot distinguish these cases. Applied to
all land-plant bodies, the same calculation shows how much a single record decides: macrofossils and
mesofossils with axes give bounds [21–24]{.C28} Myr younger than the certain land-plant
spores, but one Katian assemblage of spore-filled sporangial fragments brings them within about [3]{.C40} Myr,
under recovery histories that then fit the sequence poorly. Under plausible assumptions the evidence is
compatible with an older, sparsely recorded early flora; it does not require one.

## 1. Why fossil maxima decide the age of land plants

When did plants colonise the land, and how fast did the vascular plants that came to dominate it
diversify? Molecular clocks give strikingly different answers (Fig. [1](#fig-problem)). @morris2018TimescaleEarlyLand placed
crown Embryophyta (the last common ancestor of living land plants and all its descendants) at 515–474 Ma
across seven topologies, and @harris2022DivergentEvolutionaryTrajectories at 515–493 Ma. @hooper2026ReconstructingSporeEvolution, using the same molecular dataset as Harris et al. but
recalibrated fossils, obtained 457–450 Ma: a crown-group mean 55 Myr younger. For crown Tracheophyta
(living vascular plants) the estimates are 451–419 Ma (Morris et al.), 452–447 Ma (Harris et al.) and
432–428 Ma (Hooper et al.). @su2021LargescalePhylogenomicAnalyses obtained still older crown-embryophyte ages with a
Proterozoic maximum. These are disagreeing results, not one uncertain estimate.

The difference matters biologically. If crown land plants are Cambrian, they existed for about 80 Myr
before the first recognisable land-plant body fossil, from the Sheinwoodian, about 432 Ma [@libertin2018SporophytesPolysporangiateLand]. If they are Late Ordovician, that gap shrinks to as little as 19 Myr and the oldest bodies sit close
to the root of the living radiation. The two readings imply different histories of terrestrial
productivity, weathering and the assembly of the plant body plan [@kenrick2012TimelineTerrestrializationConsequences; @strother2021FossilRecordLand; @donoghue2021EvolutionaryEmergenceLand].

Fossils enter clocks as minima and maxima. A minimum rests on a dated fossil whose placement can be
argued. A maximum rests on what was not found: older rocks that should have yielded the diagnostic
fossil if the lineage had existed. Hooper et al.'s recalibration shows how much rides on that argument.
Where Morris et al. set the crown land-plant maximum at 515.5 Ma from Cambrian cryptospores, Hooper et al.
set it at 459.1 Ma from the oldest possible trilete spores, and they set 431.2 Ma for crown vascular plants. The use
of absence-based maxima is itself contested [@hedges2018AccurateTimetreesRequire; @su2021LargescalePhylogenomicAnalyses].

A maximum makes two claims that are easily run together (Fig. [2](#fig-mapping)). The first concerns recovery:
that sampled rocks of the right age, facies and preparation would have yielded the fossil if its
producers were present at some stated abundance. The second concerns biology: that the producing
lineage made the diagnostic spore or body from its origin, so the first recoverable fossil can stand
in for the node. Early land plants strain both. Land-plant spores appear about 40 Myr before land-plant
bodies [@wellman2000MicrofossilRecordEarly]. Terrestrial deposits are scarce before the latest Silurian, at least in
western Europe [@kenrick2012TimelineTerrestrializationConsequences]. The oldest parent plants known in detail are minute, and
bryophyte-grade plants lack lignin and preserve poorly [@edwards2014CryptosporesCryptophytesReveal; @morris2018TimescaleEarlyLand].

We therefore asked what each of the three maxima would need from the sampled record, and then tested
what the record allows. The work had three parts:

1. **An audit** of the observations behind each maximum, keeping the specimen, its identification, the
   sampling evidence, the persistence of the diagnostic trait and the node it bears on as separate
   links.
2. **An absence test** wherever an older-empty, younger-positive spore comparison could be built from
   documented samples.
3. **A positive-record analysis** of dated body fossils, which estimates how far before the oldest find
   the recoverable record probably began, under declared recovery histories.

The third part needs no inventory of searched but empty rock; it substitutes an assumption about how
recovery behaved before the first find, and we vary that assumption explicitly.

## 2. Evidence, its geography and its independence

Three bodies of evidence carry the tests (Fig. [3](#fig-evidence)).

**Murzuq Basin cuttings.** @abuhmida2013PalynologicalAnalysisOrdovician reported palynology from Ordovician to lower Silurian
wells in southwest Libya. We transcribed all [164]{.C38} appendix samples and their chart and prose reports,
and used the chart-coded cuttings of five wells as the primary frame. Three wells supply [28]{.C01} cuttings
older than the Llandovery, none reporting trilete spores, and all five supply [59]{.C02} Llandovery reference cuttings, [51]{.C02} of which report them. Northern Africa lay near the Ordovician South Pole, within the area
of Hirnantian glaciation [@cocks2021OrdovicianPalaeogeographyClimate; @scotese2021PhanerozoicPaleotemperaturesEarths], so this frame samples one
high-latitude basin.

**QSIM-801.** @vecoli2017MiddleOrdovicianCryptospores tabulate [99]{.C11} cryptospore occurrences in [17 of 20]{.C11} samples from a
Saudi Arabian borehole assigned to the late Dapingian–early Darriwilian, and report no trilete spores
in the studied sequence. The material and basin are independent of Murzuq.

**Dated body fossils.** We assembled the accepted land-plant body occurrences from 445 to 393 Ma that could
be dated and linked to a locality. Land-plant bodies were declared as any accepted macro- or mesofossil,
sterile axes included. The inventory as built took macrofossil names from PBDB and curated records with
branched axes, tracheids or crown-member features; it therefore omitted spore-filled sporangial
fragments recovered from palynological preparations, of which one Katian assemblage, from Oman, falls in
range. We analyse it as a declared sensitivity (§3.3). Sources were the Paleobiology Database (PBDB) names, classified
by AI with cited sources and audited, together with our curated set of the oldest records and their
competing readings. The audit also covered a separate screen of all [556]{.C38} older non-spore PBDB rows. The
counting unit is a locality–age proxy, so several collections, taxa or papers from one horizon count once.
The main reading yields [77]{.C25} land-plant body units, [72]{.C25} of polysporangiophytes (branched sporophytes with
several sporangia) and [50]{.C25} of crown vascular-plant members (the lycophyte line or euphyllophytes). Of these,
[75, 70 and 48]{.C25} are certainly older than the end of the Emsian ([393.47]{.C25} Ma).

The body record is geographically concentrated where it matters most. Of the [48]{.C25} crown vascular-plant units, only [16]{.C26} have age support older than the Silurian–Devonian boundary, and those come from eight
regions. The two oldest-possible clusters are Yea, Victoria, two horizons whose age is disputed between Gorstian and Early Devonian [@earp2024NotanopliidBrachiopodsAssociated], and the
Ludfordian of Bathurst Island, Arctic Canada [@kotyk2002MorphologicallyComplexPlant]. Silurian land-plant bodies more generally come mainly from Laurentia ([11 of 34]{.C37} wholly Silurian units) and peri-Gondwanan Europe ([8]{.C37}).
Laurentia supplies [29 of the 77]{.C37} land-plant units, most of them Devonian.

Three comparisons are independent of one another in material: Murzuq, QSIM and the body record. Within
each, independence is limited. Murzuq's 87 cuttings come from five wells, and its older cuttings from three. Body units share regions and
collectors, and their proxy for a horizon is not a verified bed. None is independent of the clocks
in the way a validation would need. The oldest *Cooksonia* and Ludlow *Zosterophyllum* are themselves
calibrations, and the oldest trilete spores set a maximum. Agreement between a body-record bound and a
clock therefore tests whether an absence-based maximum is reasonable; it does not validate a clock
posterior.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Crown land plants before 515.5 Ma: a question of affinity, not absence

The 515.5 Ma maximum of @morris2018TimescaleEarlyLand rests on Cambrian cryptospore-like packets and polyads.
Of the [three]{.C15} sources we examined, none accepts them as land-plant spores. @strother2016SystematicsEvolutionarySignificance placed the
Tennessee taxa in Charophyta, @taylor2024UltrastructureCambrianCryptospores read the packets as pre-embryophytic, and
@wang2022PalynologicalAssemblageCambrian favoured ancestral aeroterrestrial algae while calling an embryophyte affinity unlikely.
More decisively, the material is Stage 4, [514.5–506.5]{.C16} Ma on our timescale, and so younger than the maximum
itself. Even accepting it would neither contradict 515.5 Ma nor establish crown membership. No assessed
source documents productive sampled material older than 515.5 Ma in which land-plant spores would have
been reported. This maximum cannot be tested from the sampled record; it is a judgement about affinity.

### 3.2 Crown land plants before 459.1 Ma: one quantitative test, conditional on recovery

The 459.1 Ma maximum of @hooper2026ReconstructingSporeEvolution rests on the oldest possible trilete spores, from the
Borenshult-1 core in southern Sweden [@rubinstein2019BalticaCradleEarly]. Their Supplementary Table 4 keys it to a queried
*Chelinospora?* specimen at 48.2 m and takes the number from the stage, not the level: the GTS2012 base of
the Sandbian plus its uncertainty, [458.4 + 0.7 = 459.1]{.C42} Ma. The describing authors plot that specimen as a
possible trilete only. They plot definite triletes from 43.1 m upwards, still in the Sandbian and below
the [454.41]{.C43} Ma Kinnekulle K-bentonite (Supplement S2.4). The stage-base rule gives the same maximum from
either level, so the value does not rest on the doubtful specimen. Accepting the definite triletes could
move a minimum, not this maximum, and only with a further argument that their producers were crown
members, which a trilete mark alone does not supply. Borenshult cannot test the maximum itself: only
three Darriwilian samples lie below its first definite trilete, too few under any of the three readings
of its figures ([]{.C44}).

Hooper et al.'s ancestral-state reconstructions make a proximal trilete aperture modal at the
embryophyte crown (aperture and dispersal unit were reconstructed separately; Supplement S6), so a crown
older than 459.1 Ma would imply undetected trilete producers. Murzuq is the only frame we found in which
that implication can be confronted with documented older and younger samples.

We modelled each cutting's chance of reporting triletes as an encounter process sharing a well effect,
with older encounter intensity expressed as a fraction *f* of the Llandovery reference. An assumed
delivery-and-recovery ratio *ρ* covers differences in transport, preservation and preparation between
older and younger material. Procedures were checked by simulation on a declared grid and admitted only
where coverage reached 0.93 (Supplement S2).

The result is clear in form and conditional in substance (Table 1). What the zeros constrain is
delivered, recovered encounter. Under the constant history the product *ρf* stays at [0.091]{.C24} across
delivery scenarios; under the exposure-weighted history it runs from [0.184]{.C24} at equal delivery to [0.092]{.C24}
at a tenth. If older material delivered and preserved spores as well as the Llandovery reference, trilete
producers at constant intensity from 459.1 Ma must have been below a tenth of their Llandovery intensity,
and below about [0.18]{.C24} of it under the exposure-weighted history. This bounds sustained intensity in one
basin, not the intensity at every time in the Late Ordovician. If delivery was a tenth as good,
producers at nearly the reference intensity are allowed under either history. Intervals of producers at a tenth or a
hundredth of reference intensity are unbounded under every delivery scenario. The record therefore
rejects abundant, well-delivered Late Ordovician trilete producers in this basin, not sparse or poorly
delivered ones, and it cannot tell those two apart.

The verdict also moves with the auxiliary assumptions. Removing one well at a time spans exposure-weighted limits of [0.185–0.365]{.C07}. Shared within-stage age positions can remove all exposure from the [459.1–462.6]{.C09} Ma window. With no contaminant transfer to older cuttings, a reduction of more than about [4.4%]{.C08} in the indigenous share of reference intensity crosses the short window's comparison threshold. At that window's simulated boundary,
[20 of 1,000]{.C06} screen-passing simulations missed the truth, and those 20 were among only [60]{.C06} that made a
claim. The comparison is real and useful, but it is one basin, it sits at a glacial pole, and it depends
on preservation and recovery.

QSIM-801 points the same way without reaching the same standard. Treating each of the [99]{.C11} occurrences
as an independent unit on which a trilete would have been detected, a trilete-to-cryptospore ratio that
stayed above [0.031]{.C12} in every sample is incompatible with the record at equal effective recovery, and one
above [0.307]{.C12} at a tenth of it (as shares of all spores, [0.030]{.C12} and [0.235]{.C12}). This bounds the
minimum ratio across samples, not an average or the pooled assemblage.
The source does not document the search protocol needed to make those units independent and
target-detectable, so we report this as an exploratory, model-conditional calculation, not a second
qualified test (Supplement S3)[]{.C12}.

### 3.3 Crown vascular plants: what the body record allows

No assessed source supplies a roster of searched Silurian beds with comparable effort for the 431.2 Ma
maximum. The Irish late-Wenlock sections report fossil-free mudstone qualitatively, without an enumerated roster [@edwards1980RecordsCooksoniatypeSporangia], and the historical Barrandian axes are selected specimens, not search trials. We therefore turned to the positive record.

**Method.** For a fixed recovery history *w*(*t*), the relative rate at which an existing lineage
contributes dated, recoverable occurrences, and *n* independent units older than a fixed younger cutoff *c*, the
one-sided 95% older bound *T* on the start of the recoverable record solves
$U(T) = U(A)\,0.05^{-1/n}$. Here *A* is the oldest unit's age and $U(a)=\int_c^a w(s)\,ds$. With constant
*w* this is the classical confidence interval on a stratigraphic range [@strauss1989ClassicalConfidenceIntervals; @marshall1990ConfidenceIntervalsStratigraphic], and the absolute rate cancels. Interval-dated units enter conservatively: the oldest possible
age and the smallest number certainly older than the cutoff. We report the bound for six histories:
constant recovery; recovery proportional to PBDB land and marginal-marine collections per Myr, with and
without collections that match the plant-filtered extract; and recovery declining exponentially into
the past on 25 and 10 Myr timescales, the last also combined with plant-excluded collection density
(Supplement S5).

**The land-plant check, and how much one record decides.** Land-plant cryptospores are certainly
present by [458.2]{.C28} Ma, so the same calculation for land-plant bodies can be set against a known minimum
for the lineage. For the inventory as built ([75]{.C25} units, oldest the Sheinwoodian *Cooksonia barrandei*),
the four histories that give a bound place the start of the recoverable body record at
[434.5–437.2]{.C28} Ma, [21–24]{.C28} Myr after the spores (Fig. [4](#fig-bodies), top). That inventory omits the Katian
spore-filled sporangial fragments from the Ghaba-1 borehole, Oman [@wellman2003FragmentsEarliestLand], which the declared
definition of a land-plant body admits. Adding that one unit moves the oldest body to [452.8]{.C40} Ma and the
bounds to [455.2–456.6]{.C40} Ma under constant and effort-tracking recovery, [1.6–3.0]{.C40} Myr after the spores; a
25 Myr decline gives [465.2]{.C40} Ma, older than them (Table 2). Under the inclusive reading without Oman, the
Hirnantian Zbrza axes give [447.2]{.C29} Ma under constant recovery.

Neither inventory measures a lag. With the Oman unit included, every declared history fits the sequence
poorly (constant recovery: nominal *p* = 5.5 × 10⁻¹² at midpoint ages[]{.C41}), because the Katian assemblage is
followed by more than 12 Myr with no other body unit. Without it, constant recovery fits only marginally
(*p* = [0.0041–0.062]{.C41} across age allocations). The separation between spores and bodies is therefore
conditional on the inventory and the recovery history. What the check does show is that a bound of this
kind is set by the oldest one or two admitted units: whether a single fragment assemblage counts as a
body changes the answer by about 20 Myr. The crown vascular-plant bound below rests in the same way on its
oldest units, Yea and Bathurst Island.

**Crown vascular plants.** For the [48]{.C25} crown-member units the bounds are tightly grouped under the
histories tied to the observed record: [428.8]{.C27} Ma for constant recovery, [428.9]{.C27} Ma for collection density and [428.5]{.C27} Ma with plant-matched collections removed (Table 2). These fall within Hooper et al.'s
432–428 Ma interval and are younger than their 431.2 Ma maximum. A 25 Myr decline into the past gives [431.6]{.C27} Ma. A 10 Myr decline gives no bound within the [469.4]{.C27} Ma limit of the effort history.

Across the declared data sensitivities the constant-recovery bound for crown vascular plants runs from
[427.2]{.C30} to [434.05]{.C30} Ma (Supplement Tables S4–S6), and residual dependence among units could widen it further.
Counting individual collections instead of locality proxies gives [428.0]{.C30} Ma, and restoring wide-age records [428.1]{.C30} Ma. Leaving out each region in turn gives [427.2–430.5]{.C30} Ma: the youngest without the
Australian–Antarctic records (including Yea), the oldest without Laurentia. Ending the window at the Silurian–Devonian boundary, which
leaves [13]{.C30} mainly Silurian units, gives [428.5]{.C30} Ma, so the result is not an artefact of the dense Devonian
record. The bound grows when effective density falls. Collapsing units to region–stage cells ([20]{.C30} units) gives [432.1]{.C30} Ma, and the curated oldest-record set alone ([15]{.C30} units) gives [434.05]{.C30} Ma.

**What the record can and cannot test.** The observed age sequence discriminates against some recovery
histories, but it does not select one. A steep (10 Myr) decline into the past fits the crown-member
sequence poorly under every age treatment: nominal goodness-of-fit *p* of about 10⁻¹¹ to 10⁻⁶ across
independent age allocations and 3 × 10⁻¹³ at midpoint ages[]{.C31}. Constant recovery looks adequate only when
ages are drawn independently within their ranges (*p* = [0.073–0.765]{.C31}); at midpoint ages its *p* is [0.0085]{.C31},
and with every age placed at a shared quantile of its range it spans 3 × 10⁻⁹ to [0.0165]{.C31}. No probability law
for ages within reported ranges was declared, so no treatment is preferred, and these nominal exact-age
tests are not a combined test for interval-dated fossils (Supplement Table S7). The 25 Myr decline and
plant-excluded density also fit poorly. The unbounded and 431.6 Ma cases are therefore not equally
supported alternatives, but neither is constant recovery shown to be adequate. The observed sequence says
nothing about recovery before the oldest possible crown-member find. Suppose crown vascular plants
existed, but their bodies were recovered at a tenth of the later rate before the Ludlow ([426.7]{.C32} Ma). The
fitted records would look exactly the same, and the bound would move to [448.1]{.C32} Ma; at a hundredth there
is no bound. These are illustrative scenarios chosen after the analysis, not fitted reductions or
thresholds, but they show where the answer is decided: in an interval the body record cannot see.

Finally, a recoverability endpoint is not a crown date. Mapping one to the other needs an assumed
delay between crown origin and the recoverable presence of crown-diagnostic bodies. With constant
recovery, reaching the young end of Harris et al.'s interval ([447]{.C33} Ma) needs an assumed delay of at least
about [18]{.C33} Myr at the 95% endpoint bound; a [20]{.C33} Myr delay maps [428.8]{.C33} Ma to [448.8]{.C33} Ma. These are minimum
offsets, not fitted delays, and the record does not estimate the delay.

Simulations at the real unit counts confirm that the calculation behaves as stated when its
assumptions hold (exact-age coverage [0.941–0.958]{.C34}) and show how it fails when they do not. Duplicated
horizons treated as independent cut coverage to about [0.78]{.C34} (pairs) or [0.53]{.C34} (quartets). Fitting constant
recovery to a true 10 Myr decline gives [0.03–0.24]{.C34}. An ignored 10–20 Myr origin-to-body delay can give
zero coverage of the lineage age (Supplement S5.6, Table S11).

## 4. What follows for early land-plant evolution

### 4.1 An older, sparsely recorded flora is allowed, not required

Taken together, the three tests support a specific, conditional conclusion. Where the record can be
interrogated, it rules out early land plants that were both abundant and recoverable before the
younger dates: no well-delivered, Llandovery-level trilete producers in Late Ordovician Murzuq, and no
crown vascular-plant bodies recovered at the later Silurian–Devonian rate before about [429]{.C27} Ma. It does not
rule out early land plants that were rare, small or poorly preserved. Murzuq allows trilete producers at
a tenth of their later intensity, or at full intensity with poor delivery. The body record allows crown vascular plants that were present but recovered at
a tenth of later rates, or that lacked crown-diagnostic bodies, before the Ludlow.

Whether those allowances are plausible is a biological question, and the land-plant check shows why the
record cannot settle it directly. Counting macrofossils and mesofossils with axes, the recoverable body
record begins about 20 Myr after land-plant spores are certain; counting one Katian assemblage of
sporangial fragments, the gap all but disappears, under histories the sequence then fits poorly[]{.C40}. A long
interval in which land plants left spores but few recognisable bodies is consistent with the record, and
is not special pleading, but the record measures its length neither for land plants as a whole nor for
crown vascular plants. Recoverable bodies need size, cuticle and lignified tissue,
and those traits evolved along the lineages being dated. Terrestrial deposits are scarce before the latest Silurian
[@kenrick2012TimelineTerrestrializationConsequences], and the oldest unequivocal body fossil is preserved in marine sediments
[@libertin2018SporophytesPolysporangiateLand]. In our judgement, a tenfold lower pre-Ludlow recovery is therefore not extreme,
but neither is it demonstrated.

The young clock of Hooper et al. is thus consistent with the record under the simplest recovery
assumptions, and its 431.2 Ma maximum is conservative relative to our bounds under those assumptions.
Harris et al.'s interval, and the Morris et al. means near [444]{.C33} Ma, need one of two things: a long early
phase in which crown vascular plants were rare or poorly recoverable as bodies, or crown-diagnostic body
features that arose at least about [15–23]{.C33} Myr after the crown node (minimum offsets at the constant-recovery
bound). The record within its observed range cannot discriminate between these possibilities and the young
clock. The disagreement among clocks is thus largely a disagreement about recovery and trait history in an
interval that leaves few bodies, and fossil maxima set there should carry that uncertainty explicitly
instead of hiding it in a soft-bound tail probability.

### 4.3 What would sharpen the answer

Four kinds of evidence would most improve these inferences. Any author could use them, and none is
requested here.

1. Documented searched-but-empty Silurian non-marine beds with stated effort, which would turn the body
   record's untestable pre-Ludlow recovery into an estimable quantity.
2. A second older-empty, younger-positive spore sequence outside polar Gondwana, with counted target and
   control grains.
3. Finer, independent dating of the Yea and Bathurst Island horizons, which set the crown-member bound.
4. Anatomical demonstration of tracheids in the oldest polysporangiate bodies, which would replace an
   assumed character-to-node link with an observed one.

## 5. Conclusions

The fossil maxima that most divide land-plant clocks cannot be justified or rejected by absence alone.
One cannot be tested at all from the sampled record. One has a single qualified test, in one polar
basin, and that test excludes abundant, well-delivered early producers but not sparse or poorly delivered ones. The third is consistent with
the positive body record under representative recovery, but becomes compatible with much older crown
ages if recovery before the Ludlow was low. Whether land-plant bodies became recoverable about 20 Myr after the lineage
or almost at once turns on whether one Katian fragment assemblage counts as a body[]{.C40}: such bounds rest
heavily on the oldest admitted records. An older, sparsely recorded early flora is therefore a live
possibility under assumptions that the record itself cannot test. Clock analyses should treat these
maxima as conditional on stated recovery and character assumptions, and propagate them as such.

## Methods in brief

All ages use the ICS v2024/12 stage bases held in the project timescale. Literature dated to GTS2012 was
converted at stage level, with source-specific statements retained. Each analysis declares a named
reading of disputed identifications (main or inclusive). An explicit non-detection is scoped to the
material examined; a missing report becomes a zero only after a full reporting audit. The Murzuq encounter
model, its simulation-based qualification and the contamination identification result are in
