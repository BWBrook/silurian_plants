![How the body-fossil limit links fossils to ancestors, shown with the main crown vascular-plant finds. (*a*) The finding rate is steady after the oldest find; before it, the rate is unchanged, halved, cut to a tenth, or rises exponentially at the limiting rate for 48 finds, doubling every [1.48]{.C57 .V123} Myr: that rise keeps the summed earlier finding within the equivalent of [2.1]{.C57 .V112} Myr at the later rate, so it fits an ancestor of any age. It is a requirement for fitting. (*b*) Dots show the [48]{.C25} finds certainly inside the window, at the oldest ends of their age ranges and stacked for visibility. For an assumed finding-rate history, the oldest find *A* and the count *n* give the 95% confidence limit *T* on the start of the recognisable record. For finds dated by age ranges, *A* is the oldest possible age of the oldest find and *n* counts only finds certainly inside the window. In the scenarios chosen after seeing the data, halving the earlier finding rate gives a compatibility endpoint of [431.0]{.C53 .V108} Ma, and cutting it to a tenth gives [448.1]{.C32} Ma; the steady-rate 95% confidence limit is [428.8]{.C27 .V003} Ma. An assumed delay *d* before recognisable bodies links the start of the record to a candidate ancestor age *t*; the arrow adds a [20]{.C33} Myr delay to the steady-rate limit, giving [448.8]{.C33} Ma.](../figures/fig2_bound.png){#fig-bound width=100%}

Dots mark the main count of [48]{.C25} finds certainly inside the window ([428.8]{.C27 .V003} Ma), region–stage cells ([19]{.C30}; [432.4]{.C30} Ma) and the curated oldest records ([15]{.C30}; [434.05]{.C30} Ma). The curve crosses the 431.2 Ma maximum (dashed) at [23.6]{.C55 .V138} finds, about half the main count. (*b*) How many times less often fossils would have to have been found before the oldest find than after it, for each candidate ancestor age *t*. The finding rate is steady after *A*, the oldest possible age of the oldest find. Where a curve rises above one, an oldest find no older than *A* has probability 0.05 given the count, the rule behind the steady-rate limit, with the record starting at *t* − *d*. Main curves use *n* = [48]{.C25} and assumed delays *d* of zero, ten and twenty Myr before recognisable bodies; zero-delay comparison curves count by region and stage (*n* = [19]{.C30}) or only the curated oldest records (*n* = [15]{.C30}). Shading marks the published clock intervals; Morris's younger interval continues outside this panel (figure 1). Thin lines show three outside measures of how many times scarcer something was before *A* than after it, for an ancestor at *t* with zero delay: area of North American columns holding non-marine units, PBDB land and marginal-marine collections, and the share of indexed Palynodata studies reporting trilete spores[]{.C56}; they are rough guides without confidence status and compare only with the zero-delay curves. Combinations with *t* − *d* $\le$ *A* are omitted; flat portions at one show ages a steady finding rate already allows.
