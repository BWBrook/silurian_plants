Molecular clocks date the origin of living land plants anywhere from the Cambrian to the Late
Ordovician, largely through their fossil maximum ages. We tested the three maxima that divide recent
clocks (515.5, 459.1 and 431.2 Ma), separating each fossil's identity and age from the sampling
opportunity before it and the link between its character and the crown group. The oldest maximum rests
on a preservation argument the available material cannot quantify. Spore-barren older cuttings from one
Libyan basin limit trilete-spore delivery only locally. Under constant recording, vascular-plant bodies
give a 95% older bound near 429 Ma on their record's onset; crown ages of 444–452 Ma need earlier bodies
recorded 8–12 times less often, or a delay before diagnostic bodies appeared. Admitting Ordovician
sporangial fragments from Oman moves the broader land-plant bound 20 Myr older. No maximum is a hard
limit: each assumes a recording history clocks should state.
