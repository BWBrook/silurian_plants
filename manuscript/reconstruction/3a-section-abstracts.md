## Introduction

Molecular clocks date ancestors from DNA differences and fossil age limits. For the ancestor
shared by living land plants, recent clocks give 515–474 Ma (Morris et al. 2018), 515–493 Ma (Harris et
al. 2022) and 457–450 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026), the last recalibrating Harris's DNA data. For living
vascular plants (ferns, seed plants and their relatives) they give 451–419, 452–447 and 432–428 Ma. The dates order plant character
assembly and frame hypotheses on early terrestrial weathering. They date a shared
ancestor; extinct lineages may have occupied land earlier.

A fossil minimum shows that a lineage existed by a given age. A maximum adds an argument that older
rocks would probably have preserved and yielded recognisable remains had the lineage existed.
Connecting any fossil to an ancestor spans two intervals: from divergence to the first recognisable
character, and from that character to its first recorded fossil (Marshall 2019). Early land plants
lengthen both. Dispersed spores precede branched bodies by tens of millions of years, terrestrial
deposits are scarce before the latest Silurian, and the oldest well-known parent plants are minute.

We examine three maxima: 515.5 and 459.1 Ma for living land plants, and 431.2 Ma for living vascular
plants. Each question carries an expectation from established theory.

1. What evidence and links to the ancestor underlie each maximum? *Expectation:* an absence
   argument is as strong as its documented sampling opportunity.
2. What sustained older spore encounter does one Libyan basin allow? *Expectation from encounter
   models:* zeros exclude abundant, well-delivered producers and allow sparse or poorly delivered
   ones.
3. Which earlier recording rates or delays permit older vascular-plant ancestors? *Expectation from
   range-confidence theory:* a well-sampled record bounds its start near the oldest find; lower earlier
   recording moves the bound older.

We apply these established principles to the dividing maxima and size each required assumption.

## Material and methods

**Timescale and readings.** Ages use ICS v2024/12 stage bases, converting GTS2012 literature at stage
level. Analyses declare their reading of disputed identifications.

**Calibration audit.** For each maximum we traced the calibration fossil, age rule, recovery route and
published identity readings to the original descriptions.

**Libyan spores.** We transcribed Abuhmida's (2013) [164]{.C38} Murzuq Basin samples. The primary frame holds chart-coded well cuttings (drilled rock chips) from five wells:
[28]{.C01} pre-Llandovery and [59]{.C02} Llandovery reference cuttings. Each cutting reports
trilete spores through an encounter process with a shared well effect. Older intensity is a fraction *f* of
the reference; a delivery ratio *ρ* covers transport, preservation and preparation. Ages are uniform within stages. Procedures are qualified at
simulated coverage of at least 0.93 on a declared scenario grid.

**Body record.** We assembled dated land-plant body fossils of 453–393 Ma from audited Paleobiology
Database records and a curated set of the oldest, with competing readings. Units are locality–ages, so each horizon counts once. Of three nested categories, crown vascular-plant members (lycophyte line or
euphyllophytes) supply [48]{.C25} units.

For *n* units older than the end of the Emsian, the one-sided 95% older bound *T* on the start of the
recognisable record solves *U*(*T*) = *U*(*A*)·0.05^−1/*n*^, where *A* is the oldest unit's age and *U*
integrates a fixed relative recording rate *w*. Constant *w* gives the classical stratigraphic
confidence interval. Interval-dated units enter at their oldest possible age. Six histories vary *w*:
constant, tracking collection density (two variants), declining into the past over 25 or 10 Myr, and
that decline combined with density. A step model lowers *w* before the oldest possible find, [426.7]{.C32} Ma; the
reduction reaching each candidate age is its recording contrast. An assumed delay maps record start to
ancestor age. Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests, searched across admissible age allocations, check each
history's fit.

## Results

**Calibration evidence.** The 515.5 Ma maximum lies below Cambrian spore-like packets that all
[three]{.C15} examined sources assign to algae or pre-land-plant lineages. Its absence argument uses
older marine algal cysts as a preservation control; the assembled sources leave spore delivery and
search effort unquantified. The 459.1 Ma maximum is the Sandbian stage base plus its uncertainty, keyed
to Swedish trilete spores; the definite triletes give the same value as the doubtful specimen. The
431.2 Ma maximum applies the same rule to Irish *Cooksonia*. The older Czech *C. barrandei* carries the
same characters and gives [434.1]{.C47} Ma under that rule.

**Libyan spores.** Triletes appear in [51 of 59]{.C02} reference cuttings and zero of [28]{.C01} older
cuttings. With equal delivery, constant producer intensity from 459.1 Ma reaches at most
[0.091]{.C24} of the reference ([0.184]{.C24} under exposure weighting). With a tenth of the delivery,
intensity near the reference fits ([0.910]{.C24}). Most exposure comes from [18]{.C45} cuttings wholly
younger than 459.1 Ma.

**Vascular-plant bodies.** The [48]{.C25} units give bounds of [428.8]{.C27 .V003} Ma under constant
recording, [428.5]{.C27 .V010}–[428.9]{.C27 .V009} Ma tracking collection density and
[431.6]{.C27 .V011} Ma under a 25 Myr decline; a 10 Myr decline leaves the start open. Coarser or
curated units give [432.4–434.05]{.C30} Ma; stage-boundary shifts give
[427.31]{.C50 .V062}–[430.42]{.C50 .V063} Ma. Simulations reproduce nominal coverage
([0.941–0.958]{.C34}) when the assumptions hold. Halving the earlier rate gives 431.0 Ma. Candidate
ancestors at 444–452 Ma need earlier recording [8]{.C48 .V054}–[12]{.C48 .V055} times lower, or
[2.4]{.C48 .V056}–[4.5]{.C48 .V057} times under coarser units; at constant recording they need
recognisable bodies [15–23]{.C33} Myr after the ancestor. The 10 Myr decline fails the shape diagnostic
under every admissible age allocation[]{.C49}.

**Land-plant bodies.** Adding Ordovician sporangial fragments from Oman moves the constant-rate bound
from [434.5]{.C28} to [455.2]{.C40 .V022} Ma; with them, every recording history fits the age sequence
poorly[]{.C49}.

## Discussion

The record limits two specific histories: sustained, well-delivered trilete-spore encounter in
one polar basin, and the start of the recognisable vascular-plant body record under later recording
rates. Older ancestors fit both if early plants were recorded several times less often, or acquired
recognisable bodies later. Routes include rarer or restricted producers, scarce
terrestrial deposition before the latest Silurian, small bodies with few diagnostic tissues, and
limited searching. Size, cuticle and lignin raise preservation and recognition, and evolved along the
dated lineages.

Silvestro et al. (2015) estimated the origin of vascular plants at 433.5 Ma from genus occurrences with
a fitted preservation rate. Their posterior targets the first genus lineage; our bounds target a
declared fossil category under named recording histories, and the two share data. CladeDate converts
fossil times into calibration densities; we report what such densities assume.

**Conditions.** The spore limits treat cuttings as independent given a well effect; weighting cuttings
by well–formation block raises the constant-history limit to about [0.4]{.C51 .V081}. Body units are
independent, with age ranges containing true ages. The step was placed after inspecting the record. Mapping bodies to the ancestor assumes crown membership
and a specified delay. A trilete aperture present at the land-plant ancestor persists to the
vascular-plant ancestor with probability [0.977]{.C19} under Hooper et al.'s fitted models.

Four kinds of evidence would narrow these histories: searched Silurian non-marine beds with
stated effort, a second older-empty spore sequence outside polar Gondwana, finer dates for the oldest
vascular-plant horizons, and characters placing the oldest branched sporophytes.

Clock builders can declare each maximum's character, geographical and recording assumptions and justify
its soft-bound tail against them; the separation transfers to other absence-based maxima. Older dates
for living land plants fit the fossil record if early plants were found several times less often, or
recognisable features evolved later.
