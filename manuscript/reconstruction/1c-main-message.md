# Reconstruction step 1c: main message (25 words or fewer)

## Reconciled proposal (Claude, after Codex's check), 10 November 2026

> Older dates for living land plants fit the fossil record if early plants were found several times less often, or recognisable features evolved later.

Word count: 24.

**What it takes from Codex's version:**
- the positive framing as compatibility;
- the two routes, recording and a delay in characters;
- dropping "only if as often". Claude concedes that point: halving the earlier rate gives 430.98 Ma, still
  younger than 431.2 Ma.

**What it adds:**
- **The size of the requirement ("several times").** Codex's "less often" is true of any contrast,
  however small, and gives the reader nothing to weigh. The ranges behind "several times":
  - vascular bodies need 8–12-fold under the primary units with zero delay (C48), or 2.4–4.5-fold
    under coarser units;
  - Libyan spore encounter fits at about a tenth of the younger level with equal delivery (C24).
- **The target ("dates for living land plants").** "Plant origins" lets a new reader take the message
  to be about the first plants on land, which 1a and Codex both separate from the crown node.

**Remaining tension, for Barry.** "Several times" compresses three quantities onto one scale: the body
contrasts, the unit-dependent range and the spore encounter limit. Codex may judge that too
compressed. The fallback is Codex's wording, with the size of the requirement moved into the abstract.

## Codex proposal, 10 November 2026

> The fossil record allows older plant origins when early remains were found and identified less often, or distinctive body features appeared later.

Word count: 22. This is a positive statement of compatibility under alternative recording and
character histories. The quantitative requirements come from vascular-plant bodies; the spore result
retains its local scope and the oldest maximum its qualitative rationale. “Found and identified less
often” describes the frequency of recorded occurrences, incorporating abundance, geographic range,
preservation, delivery, recognition and collecting. It describes a scenario, rather than a measured
historical contrast.

## Claude's version retained for Barry and Claude

The wording Barry had accepted as a draft before this scientific check:

> The fossil record supports a young origin of land plants only if the earliest plants were fossilised and found as often as later ones.

**Codex disagrees with its scientific implication.** It makes equal recording a necessary condition,
applies one condition to three different targets, and converts a conditional body-record comparison
into support for a young origin. The existing primary step model gives a bound of 430.980012 Ma when
early recording is half the later rate, still younger than the 431.2 Ma maximum. Under equal recording,
alternative unit treatments give 432–434 Ma. The rate equality therefore supplies neither the stated
necessity nor a general node-age conclusion. See `codex-science-check.md` for selectors and arithmetic.

Earlier wording rejected by Barry as unclear and built on a negation:

> Fossil maxima dividing land-plant clocks are not hard limits; the young ones hold only if early plants were recorded as often as later ones.

## Prose rules for the rebuild (Barry, chat, 10 November 2026)

“Don't assume anything of your reader. Also, main messages with a '... are not x' negation is bad form.
You really overuse negations within positive statements throughout. Constraints, assumptions and
limitations are better packaged up in a specific paragraph or section rather than being injected
repeatedly throughout the text. This is a general writing problem that will need fixing when we get back
to the prose.”

1. State findings positively. Keep the result's object accurate: local encounter, a body-record bound
   or a requirement of a declared scenario.
2. Gather detailed conditions and sensitivities in one dedicated section. Name the base treatment in
   Methods so its results remain interpretable when read on their own.
3. Define terms at first use, or replace them with familiar words. An ancestor shared by living plants
   and the first occupation of land are separate events.
