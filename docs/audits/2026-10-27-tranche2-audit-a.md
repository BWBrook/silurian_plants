## 5. The four specific checks

**(a) Hedeia/Yarravia synonymy.** Formalised by McSweeney, Shimeta & Buckeridge (2021), "Yarravia oblonga Lang & Cookson, 1935 emended, from the Lower Devonian of Victoria, Australia", Alcheringa 45(3): 299-314 (thesis ch.7). Evidence: specimen NMV P236587 from Eglinton Cutting near Alexandra (collected by Clem Earp, reported by Earp 1999 as H. corymbosa) has about 20 fructifications on one slab, most in the well-preserved "Hedeia" state (secondary branches terminated by sporangia or sterile axes) and one in the poorly preserved "Yarravia" state that looks synangial; so "both forms of preservation were found occurring on the same specimen". Yarravia (23 March 1935) has priority over Hedeia (15 July 1935); H. corymbosa and H. sinica were transferred as new combinations. Hueber (1983, p.74) had earlier proposed the idea without taking nomenclatural action. Both cards are correct. Minor addition for both: the synonymy is nomenclatural plus morphological, and the authors say Y. subsphaerica may need its own genus.

**(b) Pluricaulis biformis.** Not validly published. It is an informal manuscript name from Tims's unpublished 1980 Melbourne PhD thesis. The 2020 Alcheringa paper does not use it (it erects Parazosterophyllum and Gippslandites). The 2022 MMV paper names it only in the introduction as one of Tims's three informal zosterophyll names and does not treat it. McSweeney's thesis (title page January 2022; deposited 24 November 2024) says it, C. tylosus and Y. aggregatus "need to be formally described". My Crossref/OpenAlex searches found no later use. Card is correct; it should say the thesis text dates from January 2022.

**(c) Baragwanathia tracheids.** Neither card is fully right and they are inconsistent for the same plant. Evidence: (1) Hueber 1983 (abstract) describes B. abitibiensis (Emsian, Ontario) with flattened xylem strands and "probable anatomical similarities" to B. longifolia; later reviews (via Semantic Scholar/Edwards 2003 snippets) cite that paper for G-type tracheids in coalified Baragwanathia compressions, so the detailed wall evidence is from Ontario material, not Victorian. (2) Hueber 1992 (read in full) gives B. longifolia a "protostelic, exarch, lobed" vascular strand with single-vein leaf traces and says xylem strands are what survive in Baragwanathia compressions. (3) A secondary source attributes to Lang & Cookson 1935 (pl. 31) annular-thickened metaxylem tracheids in the Victorian plant, seen by reflected light, film pulls and ground sections; I could not read the primary. (4) Tims & Chambers 1984 and McSweeney et al. 2021 found no cellular structure in the Wilson Creek and Limestone Road material they studied, and SEM of Yarravia found no tracheids. Verdict: the longifolia card's state "tracheids" is defensible (xylem strand present; Lang & Cookson reportedly saw annular thickenings), with its caveat "wall type not verified" correct. The flora card's "no tracheid wall type has been seen in Victorian material" is true only of the Tims/McSweeney collections and ignores Lang & Cookson's original material, so "unknown" for the assemblage is over-cautious. Recommend one coding for B. longifolia: tracheids, wall type annular (Lang & Cookson, secondary), G-type by inference from B. abitibiensis (Hueber 1983), confidence low; reword the flora card to "in the Tims/McSweeney material examined".

**(d) Honegger et al. 2018 and Welsh Borderland Prototaxites.** The paper does report Welsh Borderland charcoalified material: "Prídolí and Lochkovian charcoalified samples (Welsh Borderland)" in the abstract; methods name Ludford Corner (Pridoli) and North Brown Clee Hill (Lochkovian). But the only Prototaxites figured from there is Prototaxites sp. HD(L)171/03, Lochkovian. The Pridoli specimen (LL03/02, Ludford Lane, Shropshire) is an unnamed apothecial fragment of a presumed pezizomycete, not Prototaxites. The paper's own introduction gives the genus range as "Silurian (Ludlow) to ... Famennian" (citing Hueber 2001; Taylor et al. 2015). So the card's "earliest firm material ... charcoalified Pridoli fragments from the Welsh Borderland (Honegger et al. 2018)" is MISATTRIBUTED. Correct to: Honegger et al. 2018 document Lochkovian charcoalified Prototaxites sp.; the Pridoli material in that paper is a pezizomycete apothecium; the earliest Prototaxites they cite is Ludlow. A GCR-based guide (Scottish Geology Trust, citing Cleal & Thomas 1995) puts Prototaxites hicksii at Pen-y-Glog, Corwen, in the early Wenlock; I could not confirm this from a primary paper, but range.first for the genus should be rechecked against Hueber 2001. Likely source of the Pridoli idea: Edwards, Axe & Honegger 2013 (Nematothallus revisited, cited as ref 33 in Honegger) covers Pridoli-Lochkovian Welsh Borderland nematophytes; not checked.

## 6. Summary

Counts across the 44 claims tabulated for the four audited cards: SUPPORTED 35, OVERSTATED/IMPRECISE 5 (P8, Y8, H6, R4, R11), UNVERIFIABLE 4 (P7, Y11, H10, R12), MISATTRIBUTED 0, WRONG 0. Specific checks: (d) adds one MISATTRIBUTED claim (Prototaxites card); (c) adds one IMPRECISE/inconsistent coding (Baragwanathia flora card).

Patterns:
- Quotations, numbers and dimensions are accurate; "as summarised by" attributions held up (Hueber, Banks, Edwards & Edwards, Hao & Xue, Parry/Mark). The cards flag what they did not read, and the unverifiable items are mostly paywalled or blocked full texts.
- Errors cluster in (i) age attributions to named papers where the papers hedge differently across versions (McSweeney 2021 Alcheringa vs MMV; 2022 dating Ghin Ghin Road only); (ii) carrying facts across a synonymy without keeping the species straight (Eglinton Cutting height under corymbosa); (iii) cross-card compression of a source claim (Honegger Pridoli); (iv) dropped hedges and one contested figure stated as general fact (Boyce & Nelsen "perhaps"; hot-spring duration).
- Minor bookkeeping: the thesis is cited as 2024 but dated January 2022; inconsistent citation years (Kerp 2017/2018); the Shenzhen Code is now superseded.
- Fitness as draft context: the four audited cards are reliable enough to use as context, with the five IMPRECISE items corrected. The Rhynie card is the strongest (every number checked matched). The Prototaxites card needs the range.first/Honegger correction before reuse, and the two Baragwanathia cards need one agreed coding. Expect similar errors in other cards that carry an age or an attribution across papers; sample those first.
