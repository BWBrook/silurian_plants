## Samphire: the original is no longer missing

Balme's section, printed pp.22–26/PDF pp.26–30 in the held 43-page report,
explicitly covers six received samples. Samples 1–3 are Jurassic cuttings;
samples 4–5 are Permian cores. Sample 6 is a graptolite-bearing Lower Ordovician
black shale from core 4. The section gives no depth for sample 6: the corrected
4090–4100 ft interval and Floian interpretation come from the 2021 paper, not
from a new measurement in Balme. The 2021 supplement also distinguishes its
newly examined cores 6 and 8 and documents the three-foot correction to the
well-completion report. The records remain separate.

Balme directly reports rich spinose hystrichosphaerids and well-preserved organic
entities after maceration. He reports absence of definite spores while allowing
small, smooth spherical bodies to be alete spores. The bracket identifying
"definite spores" with trilete spores is supplied by Strother & Foster (2021).
It is not in Balme. The original therefore verifies preservation and qualified
observation language, but does not establish an E2-negative search under the
modern target definition. Unknown E2 ascertainment is retained.

The source record, candidates, frame inventory and core-4 evidence provenance
are reconciled. All six original entries enter the evidence inventory; the five
younger entries remain excluded from an early-Palaeozoic reference comparison.
The legacy interface stays at 49 material units and 294 outcomes, with seven
candidate frames and 282 inventory entries. No event/reading outcome was changed.

## Saudi and Paraguay: reporting belongs to each event

Strother et al. (1996), Fig.2 p.56, supplies fourteen counted Tayma-4 depths and
reports mean palynomorph count 154. It does not certify the full processed set,
including RA cuttings. The 2015 methods, p.98, explicitly revisit the earlier
material; described specimens come from TAYM-4 and additional preparations were
examined. Preparation is HF/HCl acid maceration without oxidation. These are
not two independent inventories. The Hanadir E1 non-detection cannot be reused
as an E2 zero: cryptospore dyads/tetrads are the reported target occurrences.
The source's algal/embryophytic distinctions apply to particular forms, not a
blanket reversal of every E2 report.

For Qusaiba, `tools/sprint2.py qusaiba` extracts the 21 dyad/tetrad taxon rows
of Table 2, p.116/PDF6: **226 marks across 37 columns, every column positive**.
This deliberately omits ambiguous monads and does not infer E2 from E1. An
independent raster check verified all 777 cells and every core/depth join.
Methods p.112 and results p.122 document marine recovery in all samples.
The 2009 account and 2015 acritarch companion retain the previously reconciled
preparation, depth-unit and duplicate-material qualifications. No extra sample
or negative follows from the companion's additional depths.

The source's literal metre labels remain in `qusaiba_e2_reports.csv`; the
independently supported feet interpretation stays in `qusaiba_inventory.csv`.
The oldest material is Katian under the competing within-Katian datings; the
deepest column already carries multiple cryptospore reports. Its 37/37 result
does not give a finite interior Poisson-rate MLE, and cannot be copied as a
known detection probability. This does not make the complete reference set
intrinsically unusable; it requires boundary-aware inference and qualification,
alongside an independently justified older comparison.

Steemans & Pereira (2002), pp.409–411, states 53 Paraguay samples and abundant
marine palynomorphs, but poorly preserved miospores. Its borehole summaries
include six very poor RD-116 samples and distinguish envelope-enclosed forms
from naked forms. No enclosed forms in Asunción-1 does not mean no E2; a naked
cryptospore is reported in its lowest sample. The held range charts and Grahn
companion do not identify all 53 samples with E2 outcomes. This sprint closes
the bounded reassessment with that precise gap; it does not repeat a search
for the same absent log or await outside replies.

## What the temporal gaps do and do not imply

Shandong's Stage 4 maps to 514.5–506.5 Ma in `data/timescale.csv`, wholly younger
than 515.5 Ma. Tennessee's source estimates include a broad Series 2/Rome
assignment; neither a uniform 521–506.5 Ma age nor a secure pre-515.5 sample is
an observed datum. Argentina and Samphire are much younger still.

**Being younger than 515.5 Ma is not itself a reason to reject an absence
design.** Properly searched younger negatives could test an origin at 515.5 Ma
if the hypothesised lineage continued producing detectable E2 through those
sampled intervals. Conversely, a claim about intensity before 515.5 Ma allowing
any subsequent history needs exposure before that date. These are different
hypotheses. Here source-admission defects prevent the first test, and no
securely dated admitted exposure supports the second. No uniform-age fill,
continuous absence record, or persistence assumption is invented to bridge it.
