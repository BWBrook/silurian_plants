## First study: F3 with a bounded biological pilot

Retain the adopted F3 question: what can sampled fossil absences exclude under explicit
sampling and biological assumptions? `f3-shape.md` governs its statistics. Add a small
pilot on observed trait combinations and supported orderings, drawing on the same
specimen evidence. This is the intended biological capstone, not a requirement to date
every innovation before the paper can be completed.

Keep three levels distinct:

1. **Observed chronology.** Earliest secure specimens for explicitly defined characters,
   with age evidence, alternative identifications and source locators.
2. **Supported combinations and partial ordering.** Which traits occur together, which
   observed sequences survive age and character uncertainty, and which evolutionary
   orderings depend on published phylogenetic placements. Observed occurrence order
   alone is not evolutionary acquisition order. Allow unresolved pairs and repeated
   origins; do not impose a single ladder.
3. **Conditional origin intervals.** Joint inference of ages, phylogeny, character gains
   and losses, and preservation/sampling. Defer this to a follow-up study with its own
   design, recovery tests and decision before model code starts.

The pilot should select a few characters with direct relevance to F3, provisionally
trilete-spore production, branching with multiple sporangia, and demonstrable tracheids
(E1, E3, E4; producer links E6). These are candidate choices, not an adopted matrix or
findings. It should test explicit propositions about co-occurrence and ordering,
identify pivotal specimens, and state which additional observations could resolve
ambiguity. It may compare published placements; it does not authorise a new phylogenetic
reanalysis, joint clock model or F1/F2 mechanism simulation.

## Evidence and inference discipline

- Spores preserved in a plant's own sporangia supply a direct producer link; a dispersed
  spore morphotype does not uniquely identify a whole plant. Keep spore production,
  branching architecture, vascular anatomy and crown-group assignment separate.
- Record observed absence separately from unpreserved or unreported anatomy. A trait
  combination can be informative without providing a narrow absolute origin date.
- Retain the finest defensible dating constraints and their basis: stratigraphic stage,
  biozone, horizon correlation or radiometric evidence, including shared constraints.
  The current F3 implementation integrates uniform within-range ages conditional on
  fixed chart boundaries, with separate boundary-shift scenarios. That is a modelling
  assumption, not an empirical probability distribution established for every unit.
  The evidence-only age register has not adopted real-data densities.
- The observed first appearance and an inferred evolutionary origin are different
  quantities. An ancient rare or geographically restricted history can be indistinguishable
  from a younger origin. Better dating alone does not resolve that identifiability problem.
- New fossils are one route forward. Re-reading existing specimens and sources, better
  dating, reliable negative observations and explicit sampling opportunities can also
  change what can be inferred.

## Contribution and publication boundary

The prospective contribution is a reproducible account of which chronological claims
the early land-plant record supports, which require particular assumptions, and which
remain unresolved. A precise limit or a robust partial ordering can be substantive;
“the record is incomplete” alone is not the intended result.

Novelty remains a claim to test against the closest precedents, not an established
priority claim. Preservation-aware origin inference, fossil-absence testing and plant
morphological-evolution analysis already exist (`precedents.md`). F3's specific opportunity
is the material-level audit of the absences behind early land-plant maximum-age arguments,
coupled to tested conditional bounds and alternative fossil readings. The pilot must add
a biological inference, not merely another descriptive trait table.

Build reusable evidence now, but do not make the first study depend on the full joint
model. Reconsider aggregation only if a bounded feasibility analysis demonstrates a
recoverable biological conclusion from substantially the same evidence that survives
reasonable alternative assumptions. Merely obtaining a numerical interval is insufficient.
