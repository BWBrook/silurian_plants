# Rejected, reassigned and disputed early land-plant records

This note collects records, Ordovician to Early Devonian in scope, that were or still are offered as evidence for the origin and early history of land plants but have been rejected, reassigned or remain disputed. The observation model planned for the record-timing deliverable (does the fossil record support the Hooper et al. 2026 timescale?) needs these false positives and contested claims, not only the accepted records, to set rates of misidentification, misdating and contamination. The list is not exhaustive: a record is here only if its reassessment, or the dispute over it, was read in this task, and each entry says how much of each source was read.

Compiled 2026-10-28 by a Claude Sonnet 5.5 subagent; revised the same day after a cross-family audit (Codex gpt-6.1-sol), each finding checked against its source. Status: draft; not yet checked by a second agent or by Barry. Ages are ICS stage names from `data/timescale.csv`; older regional stage names are kept as written and not mapped. Entry numbers follow document order.

**Classes.** *Rejected*: the plant identification is abandoned in the literature read and no defence of it was found; *provisional* marks a rejection that rests on one study or one statement. *Reassigned*: the name or the specimens persist, but with a different identification or age; the replacement identity may still be open. *Disputed (live)*: the identification, affinity or age is contested, or no independent assessment was found. **Error types:** misidentified non-plant; contamination or intrusion; misdating; misidentified plant group; unresolved dispute.

**Limits for modelling.** This is a selected set of resolved and unresolved cases. It has no denominator of records examined, it includes dependent records (the Prague, Gotland and South African cases each share one reassessment), and it mixes demonstrated errors with live disputes and with transfers between plant groups. It cannot by itself estimate error rates.

## Rejected

### 1. *Boiophyton pragense* Obrhel 1959

- **Claimed:** a land plant from the Middle Ordovician of the Prague Basin, Czech Republic. Obrhel (1959) published it as a land-plant find in the Central Bohemian Ordovician (title), with the genus on p. 536; the type species is *B. pragense* (Maletz 2020, who writes the authority as Obrhel in the entry and Obrehl in running text). The original spelling of the species name was not checked.
- **Reassessment:** Kenrick, Kvaček & Bengtson (1999). New evidence on internal structure shows that the putative plant is a dendroid graptolite. Other heavily coalified branched axes from associated strata were less clear, and scanning electron microscopy of their surfaces suggests collagenous stipes of gorgonacean octocorals (the abstract says "suggests"; the second fossil is not named there).
- **Current interpretation and age:** Maletz (2020, Treatise Online) retains *Boiophyton* Obrhel 1959 as a large, bushy, erect callograptid dendroid. The holotype, NM-D418 from Praha-Vokovice, is Darriwilian. The genus ranges from the Darriwilian (*Corymbograptus retroflexus* Biozone) to the Gorstian (Czech Republic, Sweden). Maletz uses the case as the demonstration that callograptids are easily misidentified as land plants in poor, flattened material. He also lists *Boiophyton*, *Bowerophylloides* and *Saxonia* together as taxa first described from small, largely unbranched fragments as land plants, and notes that fusellar construction has not yet been shown in this material (see entries 8 and 9). I read that as a caution on the graptolite assignment; the animal reading itself stands.
- **Error type:** misidentified non-plant (flattened colonial animal read as an axial plant).
- **Read status:** Kenrick et al. abstract only (Crossref and Palaeontological Association page); the internal-structure evidence is not in the abstract. Maletz's entry read in full. Obrhel 1959 not seen.
- **Sources:** Kenrick et al. 1999; Maletz 2020; Obrhel 1959.

### 2. *Psilophyton*? *hedei* Halle 1920

- **Claimed:** a probable land plant of the Silurian of Gotland (title of Halle 1920).
- **Reassessment:** Lundblad (1972) redescribed it as a dendroid graptolite. Maletz (2020) captions the holotype (S-010022, Hemse Marl, Silurian Ludlow, Hablingbo, Gotland) as now identified as a specimen of *Boiophyton aculeatus* (Počta 1894), citing Kenrick et al. (1999, fig. 3D).
- **Current interpretation and age:** callograptid dendroid, Gorstian (Maletz 2020).
- **Error type:** misidentified non-plant. It removes a claimed Silurian vascular plant ("Psilophyton") from the Gorstian record.
- **Read status:** Maletz's text and captions only; Halle and Lundblad not seen, and the Kenrick et al. figure was not seen.
- **Sources:** Halle 1920; Lundblad 1972; Maletz 2020.

### 3. *Musciphyton* and *Hepaticaephyton* Kozłowski & Greguss 1959

- **Claimed:** Ordovician land plants simpler than the Psilophyta, 2 to 8 mm high, a moss-like monopodial type (*Musciphyton*) and a hepatic-like dichotomous type with conducting elements (*Hepaticaephyton*), the "Propsilophyta". Extracted by acid from limestone erratic boulders carried to Poland by Pleistocene glaciers from Scandinavia and the Baltic, found near Zakroczym on the Vistula. The boulder with *Musciphyton* is a lithographic-type Baltic limestone ("Ostseekalk") assigned to the Saunja horizon of the middle Estonian Upper Ordovician by lithology, because its index graptolite was not found in that boulder; the other boulder is dated only as Ordovician (Kozłowski & Greguss 1959).
- **Reassessment:** Pacyna & Barbacka (2022) state that these alleged fossils are now believed to be degraded roots of living plants that penetrated the erratic boulders. They cite no primary reassessment, and I did not find one.
- **Current interpretation and age:** modern roots, so no Ordovician plant. **Class: rejected, provisional.** The rejection rests on one uncited statement in a 2022 review; I found no primary reassessment, so "consensus" is not claimed.
- **Error type:** contamination or intrusion.
- **My reading, not a published reassessment:** the 1959 text itself describes stems that looked almost like recent plants once the calcite was dissolved, quartz and glauconite grains cemented onto them, and regularly arranged root hairs on subterranean parts, which fits modern roots.
- **Read status:** Kozłowski & Greguss read in full (text; plates not seen). Pacyna & Barbacka: the relevant section.
- **Sources:** Kozłowski & Greguss 1959; Pacyna & Barbacka 2022.

### 4. *Promissum pulchrum* Kovács-Endrődy 1986 (Soom Shale, South Africa)

- **Claimed:** spiny axes read as the remains of a creeping land plant, either the earliest known vascular plant or an ancestor of the tracheophytes (Theron & Kovács-Endrődy 1986; Kovács-Endrődy 1986, whose title places the flora in the Lower Silurian Cedarberg Formation, Table Mountain Group; as relayed by Chesselet 1992).
- **Reassessment:** Theron, Rickards & Aldridge (1990) proposed a different interpretation from morphological and chemical characters, a giant conodont, from bedding-plane assemblages (title: a new giant Ashgill conodont); later titles describe the apparatus of this Upper Ordovician conodont and a giant conodont with preserved muscle tissue (Aldridge et al. 1995; Gabbott et al. 1995; titles and Crossref metadata only). Chesselet (1992) summarises the fossil as now thought to be a giant conodont. Salamon et al. (2018) cite the conodont work to exclude conodont affinity for the Zbrza fragments, which they call smooth and heavily coalified (entry 11).
- **Current interpretation and age:** giant conodont, Late Ordovician. Chesselet (1992) places the Soom Shale Member in the late Ashgillian, with an age bracket of Upper Ordovician to Lower Silurian from invertebrates and spore tetrads and a late Ordovician age from chitinozoans; Theron et al. favour Ordovician over Silurian for *P. pulchrum*. The Ashgill is not mapped to an ICS stage here.
- **Error type:** misidentified non-plant.
- **Read status:** Chesselet's abstract, introduction and geological setting read in full; the conodont papers and the original plant descriptions not read.
- **Sources:** Chesselet 1992; Theron et al. 1990; Theron & Kovács-Endrődy 1986; Kovács-Endrődy 1986; Aldridge et al. 1995; Gabbott et al. 1995.

### 5. *Eohostimella parva* Kovács 1986, now *Siphonacis parva* (Soom Shale, South Africa)

- **Claimed:** a non-vascular Palaeozoic plant, an indicator fossil for the *Promissum* assemblages and assigned to *Eohostimella* Schopf et al. 1966, the genus of *E. heathana*, an upright, hollow, dichotomously branched problematic plant from the Lower Silurian of northern Maine that Schopf et al. suggested might be an incipient land plant (all as summarised by Chesselet 1992).
- **Reassessment:** Chesselet (1992) found smooth-walled tubes and pores leading into tapering tubes in the "axes"; this distinctive internal structure invalidates assignment to *Eohostimella* (her abstract), and she also notes that the spinose appendages are regularly arranged, not irregularly as in *Eohostimella*. She erected the genus *Siphonacis* for it, with *S. parva* (Kovács) Chesselet 1990 n. comb. She concludes the fossil is not of plant origin and is probably a disarticulated soft-bodied marine animal, with the affinity unknown and a possible link to *Promissum* left as a hypothesis for investigation. The reassessment concerns *E. parva* only; the status of *E. heathana* is not addressed.
- **Current interpretation and age:** not a plant; probably a soft-bodied marine animal; late Ashgillian (Soom Shale Member; see entry 4). **Class: rejected, provisional,** because it rests on one detailed study and later assessments were not sought.
- **Error type:** misidentified non-plant (replacement identity unknown).
- **Read status:** Chesselet's abstract, introduction, geological setting, morphology discussion and conclusion read; the original description not seen.
- **Sources:** Chesselet 1992.

## Reassigned

### 6. *Protolepidodendron* Krejčí and the records assigned to it

*Protolepidodendron* has not become an invalid name, but its older usage needs to be sorted record by record. The type species is Bohemian and Middle Devonian, and no secure Silurian exemplar of the genus was found.

- ***P. scharianum* (also written *scharyanum*), Bohemian type: misdating.** Krejčí's account of the plants (1879, printed 1880) is titled as land-plant remains in the Bohemian Silurian formation, and Stur's paper on the flora of Barrande's étage H-h1 is titled as a Silurian flora (both titles as given in the reference list of Jurina 2009). The plants are now treated as Middle Devonian, from the Srbsko beds of the Givetian (other works in Jurina's reference list are titled as Srbsko beds (Givet) and as plants from the Givetian of the Czech Republic). Jurina (2009) is the dedicated study of the history of study and of the holotype. Her reference list also has a conference abstract on the holotype and the problem of authorship. I could not retrieve the abstract of Jurina (2009); only title, metadata and reference list were read.
- **Belgian "P. scharianum" (Middle Devonian): misidentified plant group.** Fairon-Demaret (1981) re-studied five specimens from Oé and one from Ronquières and showed that they belong to *Leclercqia*. The two genera are separated by the leaf: once-bifurcate in *P. scharianum* according to Stur (as relayed by Fairon-Demaret 1981), five unequal segments in different planes in *Leclercqia complexa*; the five segments are seldom visible in specimens exposed by splitting rock, so the confusion can be avoided only by studying leaves with suitable techniques (Fairon-Demaret 1980, method; 1981). Oé is middle Couvinian, probably close to the base of the Eifelian, and Ronquières is upper Givetian (Fairon-Demaret 1981): both Middle Devonian. Only the abstract and body of the 1981 paper were read; the 1980 paper's abstract could not be retrieved.
- **Chinese "P. scharyanum", North Xinjiang (Middle Devonian): misidentified plant group.** Xu & Wang (2008) show that the leaf is three-dimensional with one central abaxial and four lateral adaxial segments, which does not fit the once-bifurcate leaf of *Protolepidodendron*, and transfer the specimens to *Leclercqia* cf. *L. complexa*, the first unequivocal *Leclercqia* in China on complete leaf morphology. The title gives the Middle Devonian. Read from the published abstract (via Crossref); the full text was not read.
- **Early Devonian "P. wahnbachense" Kräusel & Weyland 1932: misidentified plant group.** Fairon-Demaret (1972) described fertile leaves of Belgian Lower Devonian material under this name (two sporangia per leaf, attached near the fork). She later made the Belgian specimens from the Lower Siegenian of Estinnes-au-Mont the new genus *Estinnophyton* (*E. gracile*), discussing lycophyte and Sphenophyllales affinities (1978), and moved the German plant from the Siegenian of the Wahnbachtal to *Estinnophyton wahnbachense* comb. nov. (1979). The Siegenian is a regional Early Devonian stage. Only the 1972 abstract and the 1978 Crossref abstract were read; the two-sporangia description is the 1972 reading, and the leaf features on which the 1979 combination rests were not read.
- **Other Early Devonian occurrences.** The Palaeobiology Database (queried 2026-10-28) holds 14 occurrences identified as *Protolepidodendron*: 13 Middle or Late Devonian and one Emsian to Eifelian (*Protolepidodendron* sp., Pimenteira Formation, Brazil). Their primary sources were not examined and their identifications were not re-assessed. Each Early Devonian record needs checking individually against the leaf features above.
- **Current interpretation:** the genus survives for the Bohemian Givetian type and close relatives, and the Belgian and Xinjiang specimens belong to *Leclercqia* (see `taxa/leclercqia.md`).
- **Sources:** Jurina 2009; Xu & Wang 2008; Fairon-Demaret 1972, 1978, 1979, 1980, 1981; Krejčí 1880; Stur 1882.

### 7. Nematophytes (*Prototaxites*, *Nematothallus*, *Nematasketum*)

The taxa and the hypotheses about them need separating, because rejecting a plant reading has not produced one agreed replacement.

- **Claimed:** plants (an alga or an early land plant); Hueber's (2001) title traces the history of *Prototaxites* as rotted wood, then alga, then fungus (the earlier readings were not read).
- **Reassessment, by taxon and hypothesis:**
  - Wellman et al. (2022) report that the Silurian to Devonian nematophytes have recently been shown to have fungal, and possibly lichen, affinities, and that no convincing nematophyte has been reported from the Ordovician.
  - *Nematasketum*: Edwards & Axe (2012) argue a fungal affinity (title; abstract not read).
  - *Prototaxites taiti* (Rhynie chert): assigned to the Ascomycota as a basal member by Honegger et al. (2017; title only). Loron et al. (2026) report that *P. taiti* is chemically distinct from contemporaneous Fungi and structurally distinct from all known Fungi, call the ascomycete assignment contentious, and state that the two remaining viable hypotheses are a fungus or an entirely extinct eukaryotic lineage, which they favour. Their evidence concerns *P. taiti* only, and they do not give one replacement identity for every nematophyte.
- **Current interpretation:** not a plant on any hypothesis read (fungus, lichen, extinct eukaryotic lineage); the replacement identity is unresolved and may differ between taxa.
- **Error type:** misidentified plant group.
- **Read status:** Wellman et al. (one paragraph); Loron et al. (introduction and abstract-level statements); titles and Crossref metadata for the rest. For Ordovician claims see entry 12.
- **Sources:** Wellman et al. 2022; Loron et al. 2026; Honegger et al. 2017; Edwards & Axe 2012; Hueber 2001. See `taxa/prototaxites.md` and `taxa/nematothallus.md`.

## Disputed (live)

### 8. *Bowerophylloides mendozaensis* Edwards, Morel, Poiré & Cingolani 2001

- **Claimed:** a land plant from the Devonian Villavicencio Formation, Mendoza Province, Argentina (title of Edwards et al. 2001; Maletz gives the genus authority and p. 7).
- **Dispute:** Kraft & Kvaček (2017, p. 185) suggested a possible animal, as relayed by Maletz (2020), who places the genus among the callograptid dendroids that were first described as plants: unbranched stipe fragments with radiating tubular extensions, with no fusellar construction shown (see entry 1). The affinity is disputed, not reassigned.
- **Current interpretation and age:** possibly a dendroid graptolite; Devonian, possibly Lochkovian (Maletz 2020).
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute (possible non-plant).
- **Read status:** only Maletz's statements. Edwards et al. and Kraft & Kvaček not seen.
- **Sources:** Edwards et al. 2001; Kraft & Kvaček 2017; Maletz 2020.

### 9. *Saxonia microphylla* Roselt 1962 (Ludlow, Germany)

- **Claimed:** a possible land plant from the Ludlow of Saxony (title of Roselt 1962, which Maletz cites for the genus at p. 323); the holotype is from Ölsnitz, Vogtland, Germany. Maletz describes robust, dichotomously branching stems 0.5 to 0.7 cm wide with slender projecting tubes.
- **Dispute:** Maletz (2020) treats *Saxonia* with *Boiophyton* and *Bowerophylloides* among taxa first described as land plants from small, largely unbranched fragments, shows complex stipes and isolated tubular extensions that may be thecal tubes, and notes that fusellar construction has not been shown, so inclusion in the Graptolithina is not demonstrated. Placement among the dendroids is his; the affinity is uncertain and probably animal.
- **Current interpretation and age:** uncertain, probably animal; Ludlow (Maletz 2020).
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute (possible non-plant).
- **Read status:** only Maletz's taxonomic section; Roselt 1962 not seen.
- **Sources:** Roselt 1962; Maletz 2020.

### 10. Cambrian to Tremadocian "cryptospores" (irregular polyads)

- **Claimed:** irregular clusters of dyads and other polyads from the Cambrian and Tremadocian, included with the cryptospores by some authors and so counted as the earliest land-plant spores.
- **Dispute:** Strother & Foster (2021) describe a Tremadocian assemblage from Australia (about 480 Ma) whose spores are of intermediate morphology between confirmed land-plant spores and earlier forms of uncertain relationship (abstract). Wellman et al. (2022) report the most recent interpretation of the Cambrian to Early Ordovician palynomorphs as charophyte algal remains that may be intermediate organisms on the algal to land-plant lineage, and call the inclusion with cryptospores largely a semantic debate, because "cryptospore" has been defined either as any non-marine palynomorph or as the spore of a land plant, the second being their preference. The Hooper et al. (2026) supplementary information calls the affinity of the Cambrian palynomorphs equivocal because they lack the regular geometric arrangements of younger cryptospores.
- **Current interpretation and age:** unresolved: algal-grade or intermediate, not shown to be land-plant spores; cryptospores clearly related to land plants are accepted from the Dapingian or Darriwilian (Wellman et al. 2022).
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute over affinity (not a demonstrated misidentification).
- **Read status:** Wellman et al. (the relevant section) and the Hooper et al. SI read; Strother & Foster abstract only.
- **Sources:** Strother & Foster 2021; Wellman et al. 2022; Hooper et al. 2026. See `taxa/ordovician-cryptospores.md`.

### 11. Zbrza assemblage (Hirnantian, Poland)

- **Claimed:** Salamon et al. (2018): a putative plant macrofossil assemblage from mudstones of the Zalesie Formation at Zbrza, Holy Cross Mountains, with branching axes, terminal sporangium-like bodies, a probable stoma and rare spores with irregular trilete marks, read as the oldest polysporangiophytes, probably non-vascular.
- **Dispute:** its authors call it putative. A review places it among claims that lack sufficient characters to be assigned to land plants, a different class from claims shown to be fragments of other organisms (Wellman et al. 2022). Co-authors state that redeposition of the fragments cannot be ruled out (Trela et al. 2019). Three hypotheses are separate: taxonomic misidentification (not shown); reworking of older material, which changes the time and possibly the place the plants lived but keeps a minimum age if incorporation was genuine; and younger intrusion or contamination, which would invalidate the minimum and which no source read addresses.
- **Current interpretation and age:** unresolved. Hirnantian is the age assigned to the host rock (445.2 to 443.1 Ma, each bound +/- 0.9 Myr); it is a conditional minimum for the fragments, not a first appearance.
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute (identification uncertain; reworking and intrusion unassessed).
- **Read status:** the paper's text and figure legends read; Text S1 (age and sample detail) not read.
- **Sources:** Salamon et al. 2018; Wellman et al. 2022; Pacyna & Barbacka 2022. Full treatment in `taxa/zbrza-assemblage.md`.

### 12. Douglas Dam, Tennessee: Ordovician plants and fungi (Retallack 2019)

- **Claimed:** plant compressions in a Middle Ordovician sinkhole fill at Douglas Dam, Tennessee, assigned to the Darriwilian (c. 460 Ma), with five bryophyte clades and two fungal clades, including the peat moss *Dollyphyton boucotii*, a lichen assigned to *Prototaxites* (*P. honeggeri*) and an endomycorrhizal fungus, *Palaeoglomus strotheri* (abstract as relayed by search).
- **Dispute:** Wellman et al. (2022) list it among claims that lack sufficient characters to be unequivocally assigned to land plants. Edwards et al. (2021, p. 1453, as quoted by Wellman et al.) give it as an example of fragmentary material lacking diagnostic features that has led to implausible attributions. The project's *Prototaxites* card records no independent confirmation of *P. honeggeri*.
- **Current interpretation and age:** unresolved; no support outside the author's own publications in the sources read.
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute (misidentification of fragmentary organic material is the stated concern).
- **Read status:** abstract as relayed by search only; the full text could not be retrieved, and the critics' statements are read through Wellman et al.
- **Sources:** Retallack 2019; Wellman et al. 2022; Edwards et al. 2021.

### 13. Ordos Basin Sandbian vascular plants (Xu et al. 2022, preprint)

- **Claimed:** Xu et al. (2022): over 500 fragmentary mesofossils from three boreholes in the Ordos Basin, Inner Mongolia, China, including a branched axis 200 µm wide forking at about 75 degrees, stomata 14-20 x 35-47 µm, trilete spores 15-37 µm and cryptospores, and a tracheid-bearing axis fragment 372 µm wide and 687 µm long. The abstract's "bifurcated axes up to 687 µm wide" disagrees with the Results, where 687 µm is the length of the tracheid-bearing fragment. The characters occur on separate fragments, not on one reconstructed organism. The age is early Sandbian (c. 458 Ma), said to be dated through marine fossils in the upper and lower beds of the cores, that is, by bracketing and not directly. The authors place the plants as stem-group tracheophytes close to the crown, more derived than eophytes and *Aglaophyton*.
- **Dispute:** no published assessment was found, and preprint status alone shows no error. The Crossref record shows a Research Square preprint of 23 May 2022 and no later journal version; I did not establish whether it has been peer reviewed. If real, tracheid-bearing plants at c. 458 Ma would pre-date the accepted Wenlock *Cooksonia* by about 25 to 31 Myr (my arithmetic from `data/timescale.csv`). As a stem-group claim it need not contradict the crown timescale of Hooper et al. (2026), but it would conflict with the premise that the oldest accepted tracheophyte megafossils are Silurian (my inference).
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute; unassessed.
- **Read status:** the preprint's abstract, Results and Discussion read; the extended data and videos not seen.
- **Sources:** Xu et al. 2022.

### 14. *Akdalophyton caradocki* Snigirevskaya et al. 1992 (Kazakhstan)

- **Claimed:** the oldest higher-plant remains of the Middle Ordovician of Kazakhstan (title of Snigirevskaya et al. 1992 as given by Salamon et al.). Salamon et al. and Pacyna & Barbacka place it in the Sandbian of Kazakhstan, so the stage differs between sources.
- **Dispute:** Salamon et al. read it as a moss without tracheids but with apparent hydroids and helically arranged leaves, and compare its stomata with their Zbrza stoma; Pacyna & Barbacka list it among putative land plants. I found no dedicated assessment, and Wellman et al. (2022) do not name it.
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute; unassessed.
- **Read status:** the original paper not seen; descriptions come from two later papers.
- **Sources:** Snigirevskaya et al. 1992; Salamon et al. 2018; Pacyna & Barbacka 2022.

### 15. Swedish Sandbian trilete spores (Borenshult-1)

- **Claimed:** Rubinstein & Vajda (2019): trilete spores from the Sandbian of the Borenshult-1 core, Sweden, offered by some as the earliest trilete spores. Two horizons appear in the sources read and are kept apart here. (a) *Chelinospora*? sp. at 48.2 m, in the *Baltoniodus variabilis* conodont Biozone (lower Sandbian), called a probable trilete spore in the Hooper et al. supplementary information. (b) *Ambitisporites* sp. at 41.9 m, in a younger interval constrained to 455-454.41 Ma by the Kinnekulle K-bentonite (U-Pb 454.41 +/- 0.17 Ma), as recorded in `taxa/ambitisporites.md`.
- **Dispute:** Hooper et al. (2026) use (a) only as a soft maximum of 459.1 Ma for crown embryophytes, calling it the earliest possible trilete spore and the Katian Saudi *Ambitisporites avitus* the earliest unequivocal one (Supplementary Table 4). The project card records that they did not use (b) as a minimum because its trilete mark might be a compression artefact (from their main text, not re-read here). Spores torn from cryptospore tetrads often bear false trilete marks (Wellman et al. 2022); that is a general risk, not a finding on either specimen. No source read shows that either Swedish specimen is an artefact, and each needs judging separately.
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute (possible false trilete mark, not shown).
- **Read status:** Hooper et al. SI and Wellman et al. read; Rubinstein & Vajda not read.
- **Sources:** Rubinstein & Vajda 2019; Hooper et al. 2026; Wellman et al. 2022.

### 16. Argentine Dapingian cryptospores

- **Claimed:** Rubinstein et al. (2010): cryptospores from the Dapingian of north-west Argentina, dated on chitinozoans (`taxa/ordovician-cryptospores.md`), the oldest record clearly attributed to land plants by some authors (Wellman et al. 2022 give the oldest as Dapingian or Darriwilian).
- **Dispute:** the table of Wellman et al. (2022) notes that Strother et al. (2015) questioned the validity of the cryptospores described in the Argentine paper. That is a challenge to the identification of the spores; no source read challenges the Dapingian age. I did not read Strother et al.'s reasons.
- **Current interpretation and age:** unresolved; Dapingian if the spores are valid.
- **Error type:** unresolved dispute over identification (validity as cryptospores), not misdating.
- **Read status:** Wellman et al.'s table and its footnote read; Rubinstein et al. and Strother et al. not read.
- **Sources:** Rubinstein et al. 2010; Strother et al. 2015; Wellman et al. 2022.

### 17. Yea *Baragwanathia* assemblage (Victoria, Australia)

- **Claimed:** a Gorstian to Pridoli lycopsid flora (*Baragwanathia*) dated by graptolites, which would make it among the oldest lycopsids.
- **Dispute:** later work dates the beds to the Early Devonian on brachiopods, bivalves and mapping, and treats the monograptid identifications as unreliable. For the Gorstian *Bohemograptus* record the stated problem is provenance, a road-making stockpile, not the identification; the cards say Earp (2024) accepts no locality with a supported Silurian age but calls the question only partly answered. The Boola Formation gives late Lochkovian to early Pragian. The detail is in `taxa/baragwanathia-flora.md` and `taxa/baragwanathia-longifolia.md`; I did not re-read the primary sources, and Earp (2024) could not be reached because of bot protection.
- **Error type:** misdating or introduced material (unresolved for the eastern localities).
- **Read status:** from the project cards only.

## Recurring failure modes for the observation model

1. **Animal axes read as plant axes.** Graptolites (entries 1, 2, 8, 9), a conodont (entry 4), an unidentified soft-bodied animal (entry 5), possibly octocorals (entry 1) and hemichordates mimic dichotomising or spiny axes in flattened coalified material (Maletz 2020; Chesselet 1992; Salamon et al. 2018).
2. **Modern or younger material in old rock.** Roots in erratic boulders (entry 3, provisional); a road-making stockpile at Yea (entry 17); the same concern has not been excluded for fragment finds in marine mudstone (entry 11, my inference).
3. **Reworking, a separate process.** Older material redeposited into a younger bed changes the time and place of life but keeps a minimum age if incorporation is genuine (entry 11); it is different from misidentification and from younger intrusion.
4. **Look-alike genera sorted by one leaf character.** Once-forked versus five-segment leaves separate *Protolepidodendron* from *Leclercqia* (entry 6); a genus name can therefore carry several ages.
5. **Stratigraphic revision of a type locality.** Bohemian "Silurian" beds became Givetian (entry 6); graptolite and brachiopod dates disagree at Yea (entry 17).
6. **Spore artefacts and uncertain spore affinity.** Torn tetrads can give false trilete marks (entries 11, 15), irregular polyads have uncertain affinity (entry 10), and the validity of the Argentine cryptospores is questioned (entry 16).
7. **Plant versus fungus or lichen.** Nematophytes (entry 7), and a lichen and a mycorrhizal fungus in entry 12.
