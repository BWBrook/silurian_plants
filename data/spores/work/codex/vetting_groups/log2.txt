# silurian_plants: agent guidance

Exploratory, long-term programme on simulation modelling of early land-plant evolution, Late
Ordovician to Early Devonian (c. 458–393 Ma). Read `docs/charter.md` for purpose, stage and
route, and `docs/decisions.md` for what has been settled. Current stage: orientation; no
research question has been chosen, so do not start model code without a framing decision
recorded in `docs/decisions.md`.

## Layout

- `taxa/`: one information card per taxon. `taxa/_template.md` is the schema; `taxa/README.md`
  is generated.
- `docs/`: charter, decisions, primer, precedents review, candidate framings, the source register
  (`docs/sources.md`, the index of all data held and needed: update it when a source changes) and
  the spore-compilation plan.
- `data/timescale.csv`: ICS v2024/12 stage base ages, the single source of numerical ages.
- `data/traits.csv`: generated trait matrix, one row per card.
- `data/eprp/`: material extracted from Barry's EPRP programme: literature, PBDB and Macrostrat
  records, climate and palaeogeography context, and software pointers. Treat it as inputs, not
  verified facts. Read each area's README before use, and regenerate tables with its scripts
  rather than editing them. `data/eprp/local/` is git-ignored (unlicensed, no-derivatives or bulky
  material) and exists only on mac-mini; rebuild it from `deep-server` with the scripts.
- `docs/audits/`: independent checks of cards and deposits.
- `tools/cards.py`: card validation, DOI checking and index generation.

Run `check`, `index` and `matrix` after editing cards, and `dois` after adding references.

## Conventions

- Facts, numbers and citations come from sources read in the task, never from memory. Every
  DOI is verified against Crossref; never guess one. In references lists, one reference per line
  with first author, year, title and DOI, so `dois` can compare them.
- When a source cannot be read in full (paywall, blocked supplement, print only), add it to
  `docs/closed-literature.csv` with what it would settle; Barry obtains these through his
  institutional login. Never bypass access controls. PDFs he obtains go in `library/closed/`,
  which is git-ignored: copyrighted texts are never committed.
- Never send Barry's email address or other personal data to any service or API, including ones
  that ask for a contact email.
- Separate observation, interpretation and speculation; present disputes with both sides.
- Cards record ages as ICS stage names; give finer ages in `range.notes` with their source.
  Literature before 2024 mostly uses the GTS2012 Early Devonian boundaries (Lochkovian 419.2,

  `na` means not applicable. Add vocabulary to `tools/cards.py` when a real case needs it.
- A card is `draft` until a second agent or Barry has checked its facts; then `reviewed`.
- British spelling. Dense, precise prose; no filler.

# Charter

Status: **Stage 0, orientation** (opened 2026-10-26). No research question is chosen yet.

## The idea

Barry's starting point, in his words (chat, 2026-10-26): "a simulation modelling project on the
evolution of early land plants (late Ordovician to early Devonian), but it's a very nascent
thought". He frames it as "a long-term development project that might prove infeasible or
uninteresting, but is worth … at least exploring", with no hurry, and with the structure left
free to evolve.

## Why this interval

Between roughly 470 and 393 Ma, land plants went from a dispersed-spore record with no
recognisable bodies, through centimetre-scale leafless axes, to lycopsids and euphyllophytes with
leaves, roots, secondary-growth precursors and metre-scale height. Nearly every innovation that
defines vascular plants appears in that window: desiccation-tolerant spores, cuticle and
stomata, lignified conducting cells, branching sporophytes independent of the gametophyte,
apical dominance, leaves and roots. The same plants are implicated in changes to weathering,
atmospheric CO2 and O2, river form and soil. The record is unusually uneven, with abundant spores
and a few exceptional deposits (the Rhynie Chert above all) set against long gaps in
megafossils, so most explanations of the pattern remain verbal. That combination of a major
evolutionary transition, rich constraints and few quantitative tests is the case for modelling.

## What would make it worth doing

A framing earns a modelling programme if it meets all four tests:

1. **Discriminating evidence.** Competing hypotheses predict outcomes that the fossil, proxy or
   experimental record can tell apart: trait sequences, timing, size distributions, diversity
   or disparity patterns, geochemical signals.
2. **Novelty.** The model adds something the precedents (`docs/precedents.md`) do not: a
   mechanism, a coupling, a confrontation with data, or a reanalysis that changes a conclusion.
3. **Tractability.** The data can be assembled and the model run with resources on the mesh,
   without years of bespoke data collection.
4. **Interest.** Barry finds the question worth his attention.

If no framing passes tests 1 and 2 after orientation, the project is parked with a short written
account of why. That account is itself a useful output.

## Route

0. **Orientation** (current): taxon cards (`taxa/`), a period primer (`docs/primer.md`), a review
   of modelling precedents (`docs/precedents.md`) and candidate framings (`docs/framings.md`).
   Ends with a choice of framing, which is Barry's call on a recommendation from the agents.
1. **Sketch:** the smallest end-to-end model of the chosen framing, deterministic where possible.
2. **Ground:** null models, baselines and the data the model will confront (trait matrices built
   from the cards, occurrence data, proxy series).
3. **Enrich:** one mechanism at a time, each motivated by a discrepancy with data.
4. **Compare:** contrasts that discriminate between competing explanations.
5. **Synthesise:** what is robust, what depends on representation, and what is next.

Stages are a guide, not approval gates. Barry's operating principle applies: reversible work
proceeds and is shown afterwards; first public release, submission and outward-facing messages
are his decisions.

The repository is private for now. Candidate outputs, in any combination: the card collection
and trait matrix as an open dataset, a review or perspective, a modelling paper with code and
data archived, and the human–AI correspondence record published alongside.

# Dispersed-spore dataset (minimum viable version)

The project's spore channel for framing F3 (`docs/framings.md`): occurrences, first appearances
and stage-by-stage presence of cryptospores and trilete spores, Darriwilian to Emsian and
beyond. This is MVD-A of `docs/spore-compilation-plan.md`, built from open sources only. The
post-2006 literature (MVD-B) is still to add.

Rebuild with `uv run tools/spores.py build`. `uv run tools/spores.py check` validates the
crosswalks, and `uv run tools/spores.py inputs` regenerates the work lists they are built from.

## Sources

- **Palynodata 2006** (GSC Open File 5793, OGL-Canada), as extracted in `data/external/spores/`:
  14,114 spore rows in the window from 985 documents, 14,059 after exclusions. Its literature stops
  in 2006.
- **Wellman, Steemans & Vecoli 2013**, supplement (CC BY 4.0): 144 curated Ordovician–Silurian
  assemblages, 1,430 taxon records, digitised into `sources/`.

## Outputs

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `occurrences.csv` | One row per taxon record from either source: genus and taxon, location, palaeogeographic unit, spore class, ICS stage range and ages, age confidence, flags (reworked or caved, synonym list, new taxon, questionable affinity), and `usable` |
| `first_appearances.csv` | Per spore class (and class group) × palaeogeographic unit (and all units): number of sources; `fa_certain_ma`, the oldest age at which a record is certainly present (its youngest bound); `fa_possible_ma` (its oldest bound); and the records behind each |
| `presence_by_stage.csv` | Number of distinct sources per spore class × unit × ICS stage, from usable records whose range overlaps the stage |

`usable` means all of the following:
- a real spore class;
- age confidence high or medium;
- an age range of 25 Myr or less;
- not reworked, caved, from a synonym list or of questionable affinity;
- a known palaeogeographic unit.

## Crosswalks (`crosswalks/`)

| File | Maps | Made by |
|---|---|---|
| `age_names.csv` | 189 Palynodata age names (Gedinnian, Ludlovian, Caradoc, …) to ICS v2024/12 stage ranges, with confidence and source | Claude Sonnet 5.5 with literature; reviewed by Claude Opus 5.5, who narrowed Gedinnian to the Lochkovian |
| `locations.csv` | 286 Palynodata location strings to palaeogeographic units, by stated terrane rules | Codex `gpt-6.1-terra`; reviewed by Claude Opus 5.5 |
| `spore_genera.csv` | 717 genera and labels to spore classes | 80 genera by pilot consensus (Codex `gpt-6.1-sol` and Claude Sonnet 5.5, 47/47 agreement on confident calls); 637 by Codex `gpt-6.1-sol`; informal type labels reclassified by Claude Opus 5.5 |
| `wellman_series.csv`, `wellman_regions.csv` | Wellman et al.'s series headings and regional labels | Claude Opus 5.5, consistent with `age_names.csv` |
| `exclusions.csv` | Documents excluded as entry errors | as listed |

The pilot is recorded in `docs/audits/2026-10-27-codex-pilot.md`.

## Read before use

- **Rows are reports, not samples.** Palynodata rows are mentions in documents, so one assemblage
  can recur across papers. Count sources (documents or Wellman entries), not rows.
- **The oldest records need vetting.** Raw first appearances of trilete spores are driven by early
  Soviet and French reports of Ordovician triletes that later work doubts. `crosswalks/doc_vetting.csv`
  (in progress) records a verdict for every document with usable records older than the Wenlock,
  and first appearances should be read with it.
- **Ages are crosswalked, not re-dated.** Each record carries the ICS stage range implied by its
  age names. Palynodata's own numeric ages are 2006-era and are not used.
- **Spore classes are morphological.** Following Hooper et al. (2026), a trilete spore marks an
  embryophyte, not necessarily a vascular plant, and cryptospores may belong to stem-group plants.
- **Palaeogeographic units come from present-day locations** through terrane rules (Avalonia,
  Baltica, Laurentia, peri-Gondwanan Europe, Gondwanan regions, South China and others). No plate
  rotation is applied.
- **Absence of spores is not yet represented.** Documents reporting marine palynomorphs but no
  spores (sampling effort) are in `data/external/spores/` but not yet in this dataset.

## In brief

Cryptospores are sporopollenin-walled meiospores that stay joined as permanent tetrads or dyads, or occur as hilate or enveloped monads, and lack the trilete mark of vascular-plant spores (Richardson's 1988 definition, adopted by Edwards et al. 2014). They are the earliest widely accepted evidence for embryophytes: assemblages from the Middle Ordovician, and spore-filled sporangium fragments from the Late Ordovician of Oman. No whole Ordovician plant is known, and what the spores prove is now contested: bryophyte-grade early embryophytes (the traditional view), stem-group embryophytes (Hooper et al. 2026), or developmental precursors that pre-date upright sporophytes (Strother and Taylor 2024).

## Morphology and anatomy

Dispersed units are tetrahedral or planar tetrads, dyads and monads, naked or enclosed in an envelope. Qusaiba-1 taxa measure 23-62 µm; *Tetrahedraletes medinensis* is 26-42 µm (Wellman et al. 2015). The Dapingian Argentine assemblage has five genera, including *Tetrahedraletes* cf. *medinensis*, an enveloped monad and a new enveloped quadrate tetrad (Rubinstein et al. 2010).

Walls vary. Oman dyads have walls 0.72-1.01 µm thick with up to 11 parallel lamellae, each 41-58 nm; Oman tetrads are homogeneous, a difference that may reflect development or diagenesis (Wellman et al. 2003). Darriwilian Hanadir spores show homogeneous and lamellate walls (Taylor et al. 2017). Utah spores include planar sheets of dyads (Strother et al. 2017).

The only Ordovician parent-plant material is twelve fragments, 0.24-0.49 mm, from Oman: spore masses with scraps of a thin, featureless sporangial covering. One 0.30 x 0.23 mm fragment held c. 2,620 tetrads; Wellman et al. (2003) calculated c. 95,000 tetrads for a 1 mm spherical sporangium. No axes, stomata, elaters or conducting cells are known.

## Age and distribution

Darriwilian: Saudi Arabia, Bohemia, Libya, Australia and Sweden, where *T. medinensis* occurs c. 460 Ma and 15 cryptospore taxa by the lowest Sandbian, 456-457 Ma, dated by conodonts and a U-Pb bentonite (454.41 ± 0.17 Ma; Rubinstein and Vajda 2019). Katian: Oman fragments, and Qusaiba-1, where cryptospores are the commonest spores and separating (hilate or trilete) spores are under 1% (Wellman et al. 2015). Cryptospores continue through Hirnantian glacial deposits, some reworked. Few Middle Ordovician assemblages have been described, so first appearance is sampling-limited.

## Affinity and controversies

Evidence for embryophyte status: sporopollenin tetrads, absent from living algae (Strother et al. 1996); spores in sporangia (Wellman et al. 2003); decline offshore; and lamellate Oman walls matching living liverworts (Wellman et al. 2003). Counter-evidence: homogeneous Hanadir walls like those of living land-plant spores formed in a tapetal sporangium, with lamellate walls also in Cambrian forms (Taylor et al. 2017). Edwards et al. (2014) suggest that some Pridoli-Lochkovian cryptospore plants may be stem-group embryophytes or tracheophytes, and that others are bryophyte-like but not bryophytes as defined by living representatives.

Three readings now compete. (1) Traditional: bryophyte-grade first flora (Gray and Lawson 1985; Steemans et al. 2009). (2) Hooper et al. (2026), by ancestral-state estimation: the ancestral embryophyte spore was an unenveloped trilete monad from a tetrahedral tetrad, so permanent tetrads and dyads cannot be crown-embryophyte and their producers are stem-group; irregular Cambrian polyads are by consensus algal; regular polyads first appear definitively in the Darriwilian. Eophyte position (stem-embryophyte or stem-tracheophyte) is untested. (3) Strother (2023) and Strother and Taylor (2024): cryptospores record sporogenesis characters assembled before upright axial sporophytes, so "Ordovician land plants" may not exist as plants; Cambrian forms show charophyte-like karyokinesis and liverwort-like ultrastructure. Strother and Foster (2021) report a Tremadocian assemblage bridging algal and embryophyte spores.

Clocks differ by tens of millions of years: Morris et al. (2018) place crown embryophytes at 514.8-473.5 Ma, Hooper et al. (2026) at 457-450 Ma after recalibrating with trilete spores.

## Ecology and physiology

Source plants were probably millimetre-scale (sporangia of about 1 mm or less). Gray and Lawson (1985) proposed rapid colonisation by founder populations with limited genetic diversity, desiccation tolerance and short life cycles. Edwards et al. (2014) note that a permanent tetrad can found a population by selfing from one spore, a dyad only if gametophytes were bisexual. Argentine spores come from an estuarine facies, suggesting plants near rivers, not remote uplands (Rubinstein et al. 2010).

## Why it matters for modelling

- Start time: treat first land plants as a prior with two scenarios. Regular polyads secure total-group embryophytes by about 460 Ma (471-469 Ma if Argentina holds); crown embryophytes are 457-450 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026) or 515-474 Ma (Morris et al. 2018). Biogeochemical models that start non-vascular plants at 470 Ma rest on cryptospores (noted by Hooper et al.).
- Scale: millimetre to centimetre, non-vascular, probably poikilohydric by analogy with younger eophytes (Edwards et al. 2021).
- Fecundity: c. 2,600 tetrads in a sub-millimetre fragment up to a calculated 95,000 per 1 mm sporangium; units 23-62 µm.
- Niche: cosmopolitan, 30-60°S, persisting through the end-Ordovician glaciation: a cold-tolerant generalist.
- Rate: spore form changed little for 35-50 Myr (Rubinstein et al. 2010; Wellman et al. 2015), possibly masking parent change.
- Replacement: cryptospore diversity fell in the Early Silurian as trilete spores rose (Steemans et al. 2009), attributed to competition from larger plants (Wellman et al. 2015).

## Open questions

- Is the Argentine Dapingian record valid, and did land plants originate in Gondwana or Baltica?
- Are regular polyads embryophyte (stem-group) or algal-grade developmental precursors?
- Where do eophytes sit relative to crown embryophytes and tracheophytes?
- Can an Ordovician Lagerstätte reveal axes, stomata and gametophytes?
