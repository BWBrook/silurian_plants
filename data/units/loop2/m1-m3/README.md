## M1: the affinity of the Cambrian Stage 4 forms (decided)

`m1_affinity.csv` applies the adopted E2 addendum (`data/units/e2-unit-rule.md` v1) to the three held
sources that describe the oldest cryptospore-like forms. On Axis 1 they are packets, dyads, tetrads and
irregular polyads. On Axis 2 every describing source assigns charophyte, pre-embryophytic affinity:
- Strother 2016 places the taxa in Charophyta;
- Taylor & Strother 2024 interpret them as meiotic products of terrestrial charophytic algae formed
  without a sporangium;
- Wang et al. 2022 judge them "unlikely directly related to embryophytes".

**Verdict.** Under main they are not E2, which confirms the existing reading. The inclusive reading
remains a declared alternative, but no held source supports embryophyte affinity for these forms. Its
acceptance rests on morphology alone, which the addendum says does not establish E2. It also comes close
to assuming what M1 tests: the clock's prediction of embryophytes by about 515 Ma is the main reason to
read Cambrian forms as embryophytic (Taylor & Strother 2024, p.90 introduction, cite Morris et al. 2018 for
that prediction).

**Consequence for M1.** Under every evidence-supported reading, M1 remains an absence question, and no
admitted older-zero design exists (Loop 1). The occurrence route depends on an affinity that the
describing authors reject.

**Related lead, recorded and closed.** Vecoli et al. (2017, p.320) note that small tetrads in the
Argentine Dapingian assemblage were "erroneously attributed to *Tetrahedraletes medinensis* in
Rubinstein et al., 2010". This is a taxonomic reassignment, and the forms remain tetrads. The Dapingian
lies far younger than 515.5 Ma, so the lead changes no M1 or E2 outcome.
