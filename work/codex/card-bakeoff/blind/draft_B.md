## In brief

*Sengelia radicans* supplies an exemplar of inferred clonal persistence and recovery after burial. Predominant clonality is a life-history reconstruction, not a measured reproductive allocation. Its value is to test whether investment in spreading can outperform investment in stature or dispersal. [Matsunaga and Tomescu (2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5604611/).

## Morphology and anatomy

The rooting material described before formal naming is the Cottonwood Canyon lycophyte of Matsunaga and Tomescu (2016). Connections distinguish leafy shoots, root-bearing modified stems and lateral roots; HPH 356 documents attachment and vascular continuity. A model should preserve those organ identities rather than combine all below-ground tissue into one root compartment. [Matsunaga and Tomescu (2016), figs 3 and 8](https://mihaibotany.online/pdf/MatsunagaTomescu2016.pdf).

## Age and distribution

The late Lochkovian or Pragian assignment comes from the **abstract only** of Noetinger et al. (2021); its diagnostic palynomorph ranges were not independently checked here. The older mid–late Emsian assignment concerns the separate Beartooth Butte locality, not this occurrence. Neither formation-wide ages nor records of congeners should extend this species' range. [Noetinger et al. (2021)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/spp2.1395); [archived methods](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.zcrjdfnb2).

## Affinity and controversies

Root identity deserves qualification: endogenous origin is unresolved, root caps are tentative, and root hairs were not observed. The authors favour roots over a novel appendage on combined morphological and functional evidence; missing hairs could reflect preservation or biological absence. Resolving apical anatomy would strengthen homology claims. [Matsunaga and Tomescu (2016), discussion](https://mihaibotany.online/pdf/MatsunagaTomescu2016.pdf).

## Ecology and physiology

Caruso and Tomescu (2012), using the older identification *Drepanophycus devonicus*, inferred living, partly submerged plants from encrusted in situ stems. Their microconchid evidence establishes aquatic exposure but supplies no calibrated inundation duration. The later reconstruction favours terrestrial plants periodically flooded. Treat hydroperiod as a variable to test. [Caruso and Tomescu (2012)](https://mihaibotany.online/pdf/Caruso%202012%20Lethaia.pdf); [Matsunaga and Tomescu (2017), growth environment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5604611/).

## Why it matters for modelling

A useful experiment would vary burial frequency and depth, lateral extension costs, bud survival and investment in elevated reproductive structures. Compare occupied area, persistence and recovery time as well as dispersal success. This is a proposed comparison, not evidence that spreading was universally superior.

Use a separate observation model for preserved stems and reproductive structures. Seasonal fertility, selective preservation and collection could also influence their apparent ratio; these alternatives should compete with the clonal-dominance interpretation. Recurring vegetation above flood deposits does not by itself distinguish surviving clones from recolonisation.

## Model transfer

Chosen to contrast: **clonal-growth**, **height** and **reproductive-strategy**, testing local spreading and persistence against investment in stature and propagule dispersal.

- **Can transfer:** organ dimensions from the Cottonwood Canyon material subsequently named *S. radicans*: root-bearing axes 2–8 mm wide, mean 4.4 mm across 452 measurements; lateral roots 0.4–0.7 mm wide, reaching 20 mm laterally with up to five branching orders, documented from 615 root tufts on 310 axes. These constrain geometry, not growth rates. [Matsunaga and Tomescu (2016), results](https://mihaibotany.online/pdf/MatsunagaTomescu2016.pdf).
- **Can transfer:** reproductive observations from the pooled Cottonwood Canyon *S. radicans* collection: 33 sporangia on 20 stem fragments; sporangia 2.3–3.7 mm across. USNM 598158 illustrates attachment. These are fossil sample statistics, not individual lifetime fecundity. [Matsunaga and Tomescu (2017), fig. 6 and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5604611/).
- **Can transfer:** the architectural option of pairing shoot extension with substrate exploration, illustrated by FMNH PP15959 at Cottonwood Canyon. It motivates separate costs for anchorage, absorption and shoot production. [Matsunaga and Tomescu (2016), fig. 7](https://mihaibotany.online/pdf/MatsunagaTomescu2016.pdf).
- **Cannot transfer:** annual branching, clonal spread, bud activation, survival after burial, generation time or sexual allocation as measured parameters. Vary them in sensitivity analyses; the reconstruction does not identify their values.
- **Cannot transfer:** compressed widths as mechanical diameters; fossil fragment lengths as stature; sporangium counts as spore output; sediment thickness as elapsed time. Each requires additional geometry, biological information or chronology.
- **Cannot transfer:** genotype boundaries, kin recognition or the identity of successive populations as the same clone. Test alternative spatial and recovery mechanisms before invoking those explanations. Traits of *S. devonica*, *S. minor* or other lycopsids require explicit proxy attribution using `from:`; none supplies a numerical proxy here.

## Open questions

Can regrowth through sediment be distinguished from recruitment onto it? What fraction of mats belonged to connected individuals? Can reproductive sampling be standardised across horizons? Priority evidence comprises connected shoot–root systems across burial surfaces, better sporangial contents and apical anatomy, and the full palynological age argument.
