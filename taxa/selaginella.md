## In brief

*Selaginella*, the spike mosses, is the only genus in the card set with living heterospory. All species make large megaspores and small microspores. The gametophytes grow inside the spore walls and depend on the spore contents and, for the microgametophyte in some species, perhaps on the parent sporophyte. The embryo emerges from the megaspore, with its foot embedded in the megagametophyte, after 3-6 weeks in the three species timed. Renzaglia and Whittier (2013) describe the emerged sporophyte as photosynthetic and independent, but no source read measured when dependence ends. The genus was added for one reason: an Early Devonian plant whose spore sizes grade and have been read as intermediate heterospory (*Omniastrobus dawsonii*, card in preparation) needs a living heterosporous comparison. *Selaginella* supplies endpoint measurements; no source read describes an intermediate state in the living genus. It is a Carboniferous-or-older crown group of about 750 species, so it is a derived heterosporous lineage, not an ancestral one.

## Morphology and anatomy

The sporophyte is herbaceous with microphyll leaves carrying a basal ligule, dichotomous branching and rooting through rhizophores. In *S. kraussiana*, branching is anisotomous, with a dominant major branch and a minor one, and angle meristems at each fork form rhizophores or angle shoots (Spencer et al. 2023). Leaves are spiral and isophyllous in some clades and four-ranked and anisophyllous in others; rhizophores are ventral in one superclade and absent in *S. selaginoides* and its ally (Kang et al. 2024; Zhou and Zhang 2023).

Reproduction is by strobili. In *S. moellendorffii* each microphyll of a strobilus bears a mega- or a microsporangium (Banks et al. 2011; page extractor). In *S. apoda* the small dorsal leaves become small dorsal sporophylls bearing microsporangia, and the large ventral leaves become large ventral sporophylls bearing micro- or megasporangia (Schulz et al. 2010; abstract only; the full text, not read, gives timings for megaspore opening, archegonium development, fertilisation and first branching).

Spore size is strongly dimorphic. Species values from Hilger et al. (2005), whose megaspores open at a trilete mark: *S. pilifera* megaspores 0.3-0.4 mm in diameter, *S. martensii* 0.2-0.35 mm, *S. kraussiana* 0.4-1.1 mm long. Across the genus, megaspores are 150-1500 micrometres and microspores 15-50 micrometres, with megaspores of 500-1000 micrometres often in subgenus Gymnogynum and 200-400 micrometres in Heterostachys and Pulviniella (Zhou and Zhang 2023, a compilation of unpaired ranges, not itemised by species in the passage read). The number of surviving megaspores per megasporangium is variable in *Selaginella*, and more work is needed (Kinosian 2026).

The sequenced *S. moellendorffii* genome is small, a 212.6 Mb assembly of two haplotypes of about 106 Mb, and shows no sign of an ancient whole-genome duplication (Banks et al. 2011; page extractor). Basic chromosome numbers are likely x = 9 or 10 in the genus, with 8 in the Heterostachys clade, and the *S. sanguinolenta* clade has 30 (Kang et al. 2024).

## Age and distribution

Cosmopolitan, about 750 species (Kang et al. 2024); Zhou and Zhang (2023) say over 750. Fossils begin in the Carboniferous (see the range note); the Isoetales record is older (the earliest isoetalean *Lepidosigillaria* is Late Devonian, used by Kang et al. 2024 to calibrate the Isoetaceae-Selaginellaceae split at 370 Ma); Schmidt et al. (2022) name no Devonian *Selaginella*. Three molecular frameworks give different dates and are kept apart here.
- **Klaus et al. (2017; abstract read).** rbcL and ITS from 200 species. *Selaginella* originated about 383 Ma (Devonian), the two main species-rich lineages diverged about 318 Ma, and major divergences within them fell in the Late Permian and Early Triassic. The audit reports a crown of 373 Ma in their Table 1 beside the 383 Ma stem; I could not check that table.
- **Kang et al. (2024; full text read).** 347 nuclear genes from 46 accessions, with two fossil calibrations: Isoetaceae-Selaginellaceae at 370 Ma, and *S. selaginoides* from the rest at about 304 Ma (*S. suissei*). The crown age is therefore set by that fossil. Inside it they date the split of the two main superclades at about 268 Ma (nuclear genes) or 272.63 Ma (95% HPD 289-248, plastomes), and the allopolyploid origin of the *S. sanguinolenta* clade at about 248 Ma (269-215), Early Triassic. They reject the earlier use of *S. resimus* to date that clade's split (330-350 Ma in Klaus et al., as they describe it) because *S. resimus* is isophyllous and the clade anisophyllous, so fossil placement drives the disagreement.
- **Testo et al. (2018; full text read).** The homosporous and heterosporous lycophyte lineages diverged 403.3 Ma (95% HPD 394-414), and lycophytes split from euphyllophytes at 431.5 Ma (426-440).
These studies use different calibrations and timescales and cannot be merged into a joint interval.

## Affinity and controversies

*Selaginella* is the sister of *Isoetes* within the heterosporous lycophytes; Lycopodiales are their sister (Zhou and Zhang 2023). Heterospory evolved independently in the lycophytes (Selaginellales and Isoetales), in one fern order and in the seed plants (Kinosian 2026, after Bateman and DiMichele 1994). On the genus itself:
- **One genus or many.** Zhou and Zhang (2023) split *Selaginella* into seven subfamilies and 19 genera, with *Selaginella* conserved with a new type; this card keeps the broad genus.
- **Hybrid origin of one clade.** Kang et al. (2024) conclude that the *S. sanguinolenta* clade is an allopolyploid between ancestors of two superclades, supported by gene-flow tests, species networks, plastome phylogeny, rhizophore and spore morphology, chromosome counts and Ks distributions. The 'earliest allopolyploid in tracheophytes' claim is theirs.
- **Rhizophore.** It is unclear whether the rhizophore was ancestral in Selaginellales; the common ancestor may have had a small root system like that of *S. selaginoides*, which roots from a basal swelling (Wyman et al. 2025).
- **Dating.** The calibration choices and the fossil placements differ between studies (above); the 'originated in the Carboniferous' statement in Kang et al. follows from their *S. suissei* calibration.
- **Authority.** P.Beauv. follows the type-species combination in Zhou and Zhang (2023); the genus authority was not checked against a nomenclatural database.

## Ecology and physiology

**Life cycle.** Spores are retained in strobili. Micro- and megagametophytes remain inside the spore wall and are highly dependent on the sporophyte; mature spores are shed with reduced gametophytes inside and fertilise quickly (Sorojsrisom et al. 2022, a general statement for heterosporous pteridophytes; Meissner 2021 for *Selaginella*, citing Lyon and Slagg). In some *Selaginella* species microgametophyte development begins in the microsporangium, and nutrients are suggested to pass from the sporangial tapetum (Slagg, cited by Meissner 2021; not read). The megagametophyte's archegonium supports the young sporophyte (Meissner 2021).

**Embryo (Hilger et al. 2005, full text read).** Megaspores from greenhouse plants of *S. pilifera*, *S. martensii* and *S. kraussiana* were sown with microspores on wet filter paper at 23-30 degrees C. Megaspore germination (wall rupture) began after 3-5 days in *S. pilifera* and 5-8 days in the other two. The first embryos emerged from the megagametophyte three weeks after germination in *S. pilifera*, four in *S. martensii* and six in *S. kraussiana*. The megagametophyte stays inside the spore-wall remnants, 6-7 cell layers thick; the shoot breaches it first and the root emerges opposite; the foot remains embedded. In *S. martensii*, the one species studied by electron microscopy, a placental space fills with collapsed gametophyte cells, neither generation forms transfer cells or wall ingrowths, and the sporophytic epidermis of the placental region has enlarged cells apparently adapted to take up nutrients from the gametophyte. *Isoetes boliviensis* shows a similar junction, unlike homosporous *Lycopodium*. Times are first emergence after germination under these conditions, not fertilisation-to-independence durations. Renzaglia and Whittier (2013; read through a page extractor) say young heterosporous sporophytes become photosynthetic and independent at emergence, but Hilger et al. did not measure the end of dependence.

**Fungi.** Isoetaceae and Selaginellaceae appear to carry only Glomeromycotina, after a loss of Mucoromycotina, but only three of 688 *Selaginella* species had been analysed molecularly (Rimington et al. 2020), so a Mucoromycotina partner is not excluded. The survey lists *S. kraussiana*, *S. martensii*, *S. moellendorffii* and *S. selaginoides* as Glomeromycotina hosts; which of them have molecular identification I did not check. It also records dark septate endophytes in ten lycophyte species, with no evidence that they are mutualists, and some non-symbiotic reports, so status varies by species.

**Rooting and desiccation.** Rhizophores are the root-bearing organs of most of the genus (Zhou and Zhang 2023). Desiccation tolerance evolved within the genus: in genome work on the close relatives *S. sellowii* (resurrection) and *S. silvestris* (sensitive), tolerance involves expanded stress-protection gene families, contracted senescence-related genes, priming of protective transcripts and flavonoid accumulation in unstressed tolerant plants (Alejo-Jacuinde et al. 2024). No water-loss thresholds are given here.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Living heterospory as an end member.** The size ranges (megaspore 150-1500 micrometres; microspore 15-50 micrometres, unpaired across species) and the species values (megaspores 0.2-1.1 mm in three species; Hilger et al. 2005) give the size dimorphism of a committed heterosporous lineage. A fossil whose spore sizes grade can be compared with these endpoints. Endpoint data can constrain a model of the transition, but no source read documents an intermediate living plant.
- **Separate variables.** Spore provisioning, gametophyte autonomy, embryo development and the end of matrotrophy are different quantities. In *Selaginella* the gametophyte is endosporic and loses autonomy; the embryo is nourished through a placenta without transfer cells, with a 3-6 week emergence in three species; and the end of dependence is unmeasured.
- **Embryo support across the card set.** Embryo matrotrophy is described as years in *Lycopodium obscurum*, months in green-gametophyte *Lycopodiella* ([Lycopodiella inundata](lycopodiella-inundata.md)), and of unmeasured length in [Ceratopteris richardii](ceratopteris-richardii.md), against a 3-6 week emergence from the megaspore in *Selaginella*. The measures differ and are not one scale (inference).
- **Timing of heterospory.** If heterospory is a shared origin of Selaginellales and Isoetales, it arose on the stem lineage after the homosporous-heterosporous split (403.3 Ma, 95% HPD 394-414; Testo et al. 2018) and before the Selaginellales-Isoetales divergence, which Kang et al. calibrate at 370 Ma and Klaus et al. place at about 383 Ma (inference; the dates come from different studies and timescales and give no joint interval).
- **Record against clock.** The Selaginellales fossil record starts in the Carboniferous and the Isoetales record in the Late Devonian, while molecular dates place the genus's origin at about 383 Ma (Klaus et al.) within a heterosporous lineage dated to 403.3 Ma. No Devonian Selaginellales fossil was named, which makes it a test case for an observation model of the fossil record.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** reproductive-strategy, sporophyte-independence and record-timing, because *Selaginella* is the living heterosporous end member whose endospory and megaspore size reduce gametophyte autonomy, and whose fossil record trails its molecular age.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Endpoint spore sizes: megaspore diameters of 0.3-0.4 mm in *S. pilifera*, 0.2-0.35 mm in *S. martensii* and 0.4-1.1 mm length in *S. kraussiana* (Hilger et al. 2005), and the genus-wide unpaired ranges of megaspores 150-1500 micrometres and microspores 15-50 micrometres (Zhou and Zhang 2023, species not itemised), with 500-1000 micrometres in subgenus Gymnogynum and 200-400 in Heterostachys and Pulviniella.
  - Embryo timing, as first emergence after megaspore germination on wet filter paper at 23-30 degrees C: 3 weeks in *S. pilifera*, 4 in *S. martensii*, 6 in *S. kraussiana* (Hilger et al. 2005).
  - Placental structure in *S. martensii*: a placental space with collapsed gametophyte cells, no transfer cells, enlarged sporophytic epidermal cells (Hilger et al. 2005); a similar junction in *Isoetes boliviensis*, unlike homosporous *Lycopodium* (same source).
  - Strobilus organisation: one sporangium per sporophyll, mega- or micro- (*S. moellendorffii*; Banks et al. 2011), with large ventral sporophylls bearing either kind in *S. apoda* (Schulz et al. 2010), and a Late Carboniferous *S. suissei* cone with apical microsporangia and basal megasporangia (Zeiller 1906, via Kang et al. 2024).
  - Chronology as alternative scenarios, not one ordering: Klaus et al. 2017 (origin about 383 Ma; two main lineages about 318 Ma), Kang et al. 2024 (calibrated crown about 304 Ma; superclades about 268 Ma), Testo et al. 2018 (heterosporous lineage 403.3 Ma).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - An intermediate organism: no source read gives a within-plant or within-sporangium spore-size gradient for living *Selaginella*, and the genus-wide ranges are unpaired compilations across species, not specimen-level distributions. The living genus can set endpoints, not demonstrate intermediate heterospory.
  - Emergence time as a dependence duration: first emergence after germination was timed, not the end of nutritional dependence (Hilger et al. 2005), and the statement that the sporophyte is independent at emergence is relayed from Renzaglia and Whittier (2013) without measurement.
  - Megaspore reserves or provisioning as a quantity: provisioning is inferred here from size; no reserve measurement was read. The tapetal nourishment of microgametophytes is suggested for some species and not read first-hand.
  - A Devonian value for ligules, rhizophores or strobilus organisation: the genus is a crown group of Carboniferous or greater age, and the rhizophore homology is unresolved.
  - Desiccation tolerance as a genus trait: it varies between close relatives (*S. sellowii*, *S. silvestris*).
  - Genome size, chromosome numbers or the absence of whole-genome duplication to a Devonian plant: these are modern traits of the sequenced *S. moellendorffii* genome and of the genus (Banks et al. 2011; Kang et al. 2024; x = 8 also occurs in Heterostachys), against about 9.6 Gb in the homosporous fern *Ceratopteris richardii* (Marchant et al. 2022).
  - The crown age as an independent measure: it is set by a fossil calibration (Kang et al. 2024), and the fossil placement is itself disputed.
  - Mycorrhizal status: only three of 688 species have molecular data (Rimington et al. 2020).

## Open questions

Whether any Early Devonian plant shows partial heterospory with a gradient that a living lineage can be compared with (see the *Omniastrobus* card); within-plant megaspore size distributions and survivorship across *Selaginella* species; the ages and stratigraphic positions of *S. resimus*, *S. labutae* and *S. suissei* from their original descriptions (Rowe 1988, Bek et al. 2009 and Zeiller 1906 were not read); the crown and stem ages in Table 1 of Klaus et al. (2017) and their calibration settings; Kang et al.'s calibration settings in their supplement; the original timings for *S. apoda* gametophyte and embryo stages (Schulz et al. 2010, abstract only); the megaspore wall and 'wick' literature on tapetal provisioning (not read); the end of nutritional dependence of the sporophyte; and whether a Devonian *Selaginella*-line fossil exists, given a molecular origin at about 383 Ma within a heterosporous lineage dated to 403.3 Ma.
