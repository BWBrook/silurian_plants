## In brief

*Chara* and its relatives are the stoneworts: macroscopic, haplontic, oogamous algae of fresh and brackish water whose only diploid cell is the zygote, held in an oogonium wall of five spiral sterile cells that calcifies into the fossil gyrogonite. They matter for life-cycle theory as the macroscopic end-member of the haplontic condition from which Bower-type interpolation starts, and as living analogues of the Rhynie alga *Palaeonitella*. They are no longer thought to be the closest algal relatives of land plants, and the genus *Chara* proper is Late Cretaceous to Recent, so it stands in for the class, not for Silurian-Devonian species.

## Morphology and anatomy

Main axes alternate nodes of small uninucleate cells with internodes that are single multinucleate cells, generally 1 to 4 cm and up to 15 cm, and carry whorled branchlets; some species are corticated and calcified. The oogonium (up to 1,400 µm) is wrapped by five spiral cells and fertilised by a small biflagellate spermatozoid; sporostine and the calcified spiral cells form a thick resistant envelope (Guerlesquin and Feist 2005). Fossils are mostly calcified fructifications; uncalcified thalli are rare, with silicified Rhynie *Palaeonitella* (uncorticated whorls) the best known. Palaeozoic gyrogonites had 5 to 12 (Moellerinales) or 8 to 13 (Eocharaceae) enveloping cells, fixed at five in the Porocharaceae and all post-Palaeozoic families; sinistral coiling appears in the Devonian (Feist et al. 2005). The *C. braunii* genome has a land-plant-like phragmoplast, xylan synthases, expanded families for ROS tolerance, LysM receptor kinases and transcription factors, and plant-like storage and stress-protection proteins expressed in the zygote (Nishiyama et al. 2018).

## Age and distribution

Oldest certain charophytes: Ludlow of Europe, all from Baltica; the late Silurian also has Sycidiales; *Trochiliscus* appears in the Early Devonian and the oldest calcified Charales, *Eochara wickendenii*, in the Middle Devonian (Feist et al. 2005; Feist and Guerlesquin 2005). Martín-Closas (2003) reports a Devonian diversification, linked by Tappan to oligotrophic freshwaters once soils retained nutrients, and a late Palaeozoic turnover as swamp forests darkened and acidified lakes. Palaeozoic fructifications often occur in marine rocks; Martín-Closas argues they were transported, perhaps from supratidal ponds, because vegetative remains occur only in freshwater beds, whereas the Treatise allows shallow low-salinity marine habitats.

## Affinity and controversies

Karol et al. (2001) put the Charales closest to land plants, with the Coleochaetales beyond; genome-scale trees now put the Zygnematophyceae there (Wickett et al. 2014; Bierenbroodspot et al. 2024). Among Palaeozoic forms the Moellerinaceae are central; the Sycidiales are an extinct branch with a utricle, perhaps protecting the zygote from desiccation; the Charales came from utricle-free relatives (Feist et al. 2005). *Palaeonitella* thalli resemble Nitelleae and its oogonia Chareae, consistent with Nitelleae at the base of the Characeae (Kelman et al. 2003). The Treatise counts seven extant genera and over 400 microspecies; Bierenbroodspot et al. (2024) give six genera and 395 to 450 species.

## Ecology and physiology

After fertilisation in water, meiosis occurs at the first division of the zygote; after dormancy the egg germinates into a haploid protonema; the diploid phase is the egg, so the cycle is monogenetic haplophasic. An alternative reading put meiosis in the diploid thallus before gametes, but chromosome studies confirmed a haploid thallus (Guerlesquin and Feist 2005). Characeae are regarded as annual (thalli can persist under special conditions), often bear over 100 gametangia per individual, and the resistant zygote can wait from a few months to several years; germination potential persists for years in some species (Feist and Guerlesquin 2005). Oospores and gyrogonites survive desiccation; thalli rarely do. Bulbils and nodes spread clones; parthenogenesis is known in one living species. *C. braunii* occupies shallow temporary wetlands, is annual, and germinates in late spring and early autumn; in culture germination ranged from 3.3% (untreated, room temperature) to 66.7% (gibberellic acid plus red light), and desiccation, stratification, gibberellic acid or red light alone gave none in strains S276 and NIES-1604 (Holzhausen et al. 2022).

## Why it matters for modelling

(1) Haplont end-member: the diploid phase is one dormant cell, so diploid fitness reduces to dormancy survival and germination. Scott and Rescan (2017) name *Chara* as a haplontic alga; in the modifier models they review, haplonty is favoured when deleterious mutations are partially dominant. (2) Zygote-bank parameters: dormancy of months to years, over 100 gametangia per plant, annual turnover, fertilisation in open water (a water-column problem, not a water-film one). (3) A dated zygote-protection series: sterile-cell envelope from the Ludlow, utricle in the Sycidiales, enveloping cells reduced to five; Martín-Closas (2003) cites work that helically coiled gyrogonites with fewer cells resist internal pressure during zygote maturation better. (4) Observation model: gyrogonites are an unusually complete record of freshwater green algae, and *Palaeonitella* grew beside the Rhynie gametophytes, whose spores germinated on wet *Palaeonitella* mats ([Rhynie Chert gametophytes](rhynie-chert-gametophytes.md)). (5) Characeae retreat under competition from seed plants (Feist and Guerlesquin 2005), a possible competition term for later vegetation (my inference). The comparison does not supply matrotrophy: the zygote falls to the bottom with its reserves and envelope, and nothing read shows post-fertilisation maternal transfer.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast a haplontic life cycle with an interpolated multicellular sporophyte, using a macroscopic haploid thallus whose diploid investment is confined to the resistant zygote.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Haplontic architecture in Chara: represent the green thallus as haploid and the diploid phase as the oospore; species and study sites are unspecified (Guerlesquin and Feist 2005; Scott and Rescan 2017).
  - Culture germination in C. braunii strains S276 and NIES-1604 ranged from 3.3% untreated at room temperature to 66.7% with gibberellic acid plus red light; retain treatment dependence rather than a field germination rate (Holzhausen et al. 2022).
  - Annual turnover and late-spring and early-autumn germination are candidate seasonal relationships for living C. braunii in shallow temporary wetlands; field sites and specimens are unspecified (Holzhausen et al. 2022).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Post-fertilisation maternal nutrient transfer cannot be assigned: this card reports a provisioned, detached zygote and no demonstrated continuing maternal supply.
  - The Silurian first appearance cannot date the genus Chara: those fossils belong to other genera and extinct orders, whereas Chara itself is much younger.
  - Nitella branchlet bifurcations cannot define Chara branching: the front matter combines different genera, and Charoideae branchlets are not dichotomous.
  - Internode lengths cannot supply plant height: they measure giant cells, and this card explicitly lacks extracted whole-plant height.

## Open questions

Which Ludlow stage holds the oldest certain charophyte, and is the Anticosti form one? How secure is the Pragian Charales assignment against the Treatise's Middle Devonian start? Did Palaeozoic charophytes live in fresh or marginal-marine water? Does the gyrogonite record covary with embryophyte spread?
