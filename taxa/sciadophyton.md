## In brief

Sciadophyton is the best-known fossil interpreted as the gametophyte of an early polysporangiophyte, found as compression rosettes in Early Devonian rocks of Germany, Belgium, Wales and France, and reported from Canada and China. Remy et al. (1980) read the radiating axes with terminal cups bearing gametangia as gametophytes; reviews since accept this, but no sporophyte has been connected to it, and Capel et al. (2022) place their French specimen under 'Gametophyte incertae sedis'. Its use for this programme is as a large, conducting gametophyte to set beside the Rhynie gametophytes, with the uncertainty about its sporophyte as part of the result.

## Morphology and anatomy

The German material shows rosettes of up to 30 leafless axes up to 10 cm long radiating from a central region (Remy et al. 1980, as given by Gerrienne & Gonez 2011). Axes are 2.0-2.6 mm wide and over 9 cm long, bifurcate infrequently, and each ends in a shallow cup 3.5-15.0 mm wide with a weakly lobed margin; the upper surface of the cup carries oval to circular bodies 0.20-0.55 mm wide, smaller and denser at the margins and larger and less dense towards the middle (Kenrick 2017). In partly mineralised specimens the axis holds a vascular strand of simple, helically thickened S-type cells (Kenrick et al. 1991, as given by Kenrick 2017). Cellular detail of the cups and of the putative gametangia is not preserved (Taylor et al. 2005; Kenrick 2017). An independent audit reports that Kenrick, Remy & Crane (1991), in its abstract, describe spongy helical thickenings and a microporate lining in the strand cells; I could not read it, and an S-type label does not by itself give conductivity or mechanical properties.

Observed: radiating axes, cups, bodies on cup surfaces, and in some specimens a conducting strand. Interpreted: the cups as gametangiophores and the bodies as gametangia. Taylor et al. (2005) note that the bodies come in two sizes and that it is unknown whether these are developmental stages or antheridia and archegonia on one cup (a bisexual gametophyte). It is also unresolved whether the axes of a rosette arose from one protocorm or from tightly packed independent plants whose bases are hidden; on the first reading each gametophyte would carry far more gametangiophores than the Rhynie gametophytes, and 'Sciadophyton' is a form genus often with several growth forms mixed on one bedding plane (Taylor et al. 2005).

Sample USTL 3686 from Rebreuve carries several poorly preserved specimens on one bedding plane; Capel et al. (2022) describe the best preserved, which has four axes 3.2-10 mm long and at most 0.3 mm wide radiating from an elongate centre 2 x 0.5 mm; the number of axes is unknown, and no cups, conducting strand or gametangia are preserved. They identified it by rosette geometry alone and named it Sciadophyton sp.; Danzé-Corsin (1956) had called it S. cf. laxum.

## Age and distribution

The genus is Early Devonian in all sources I read. Germany: Siegenian rosettes and stands (Remy et al. 1980a, b), with Emsian Calyculiphyton, a similar cup-bearing gametophyte of Germany and Canada (Remy et al. 1991, via Gerrienne & Gonez 2011; Kenrick 2017). Belgium: Stockmans (1940) recorded S. laxum, including at Estinnes-au-Mont, an early Emsian locality for Capel et al. (2022). Wales: the Senni Beds (Croft & Lang 1942, cited by Capel et al. 2022). France: Rebreuve, which Capel et al. (2022) place in the early Emsian from regional correlation and associated plants, calling the age equivocal because no direct date exists, whereas earlier workers gave Taunusian (about Pragian) or Pragian to Emsian. Canada and China are listed by Gerrienne & Gonez (2011) without detail; they also list Brazil, citing Gerrienne et al. (2001), but the 2001 paper is reported by an independent audit to compare unnamed circular structures with Sciadophyton cups and not to identify Sciadophyton, so I do not count it. The records I can place are Laurussian (Germany, Belgium, Wales, France).

## Affinity and controversies

Gametophyte. Remy et al. (1980a) interpreted Sciadophyton as the gametophyte of an early land plant, and Gerrienne & Gonez (2011) call it the first plant described as a gametophyte. Kenrick (2017) judges the case compelling because the cups match the Rhynie gametangiophores in shape, size and position and the bodies resemble antheridia, even though gametangium histology is missing; Taylor et al. (2005) agree while stressing the lack of structural detail. I found no modern source arguing that Sciadophyton rosettes are sporophytes; the earlier treatment of the genus by Steinmann and by Kräusel & Weyland (1930) I could not read.

Which sporophyte. Three partners have been proposed, none shown.

- Stockmansella langii, a paratracheophyte: Schweitzer (1983) suggested the two were the sporophyte and gametophyte of one plant from their frequent association (via Gerrienne & Gonez 2011).
- Huvenia kleui, another paratracheophyte: Kenrick et al. (1991) suggested this from association and the shared S-type central strand (via Gerrienne & Gonez 2011).
- Zosterophyllum rhenanum or Drepanophycus spinaeformis, lycophytes: Schweitzer (1983, 1987) and Remy & Hass (1991c). Gerrienne & Gonez (2011) call this unsupported beyond occasional association, because the strands of Zosterophyllum and Drepanophycus have G-type cells and not the S-type of Sciadophyton. Capel et al. (2022) list all three taxa as cases where similar structures have been read as gametophytes of different lineages. Taken together, the form genus may include gametophytes of more than one lineage; Remy et al. (1992) asked in a title whether it is the only representative of an Early Devonian body plan. Kenrick (2017) states that the sporophyte of Sciadophyton remains speculative. These matches rest on association and tissue similarity, the same class of evidence as the Rhynie gametophyte-sporophyte matches (see [the Rhynie chert gametophytes](rhynie-chert-gametophytes.md)), and differ from the direct connections reported for [Sporogonites](sporogonites.md) and the Brazilian Cooksonia paranensis cluster, which are themselves tentative.

Rosette architecture is ambiguous between generations. Schultka (2003) described German Early Devonian 'Cooksonia' rosettes of dichotomous axes on a central disc ending in lobed structures read as sporangia; Gerrienne et al. (2006) suggested these terminal structures might be gametangiophores and the whole a Sciadophyton-type gametophyte (via Gerrienne & Gonez 2011). A conference abstract (Capel et al. 2026, not peer-reviewed so far as I can tell) reports a Cooksonia gametophyte in connection with sporophytes at Impofu Dam, South Africa, as an isotomously branched axial system ending in several archegoniophores, it does not name Sciadophyton and I have not seen its figures.

Evidence that would settle it: a Sciadophyton rosette organically connected to, or growing from a spore of, a named sporophyte; gametangium histology; spores germinating on the rosette bases.

## Ecology and physiology

Large (up to about 10 cm), leafless, with a conducting strand in some axes and cups bearing gametangia, Sciadophyton was probably an autotrophic, erect, free-standing gametophyte, as the Rhynie gametophytes were (see [the Rhynie chert gametophytes](rhynie-chert-gametophytes.md)); this follows from form and has not been tested for it, as no sporophyte is attached. Fertilisation presumably needed a water film, as in the Rhynie gametophytes, but I read no direct evidence. Stands of rosettes on single bedding planes (Taylor et al. 2005; Remy et al. 1980b, title) suggest gregarious growth. The sexual condition, the lifespan and the nutrition of the gametophyte are unknown.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Gametophyte size.** Axes of 2.0-2.6 mm width and up to about 10 cm length, with cups of 3.5-15 mm, make Sciadophyton much larger than the Rhynie gametophytes of 0.4-2 cm in the Rhynie card. It extends the upper range of Early Devonian gametophyte size, but because the sporophyte is unknown no gametophyte-to-sporophyte ratio can be computed, and none of the candidate partners has a size attached here.
- **Gametophyte conduction.** Some axes had an S-type strand, as Remyophyton does among the Rhynie gametophytes; the cups themselves have no preserved anatomy.
- **Independence.** With no attached sporophyte, the fossil gives no direct test of sporophyte independence; it bounds the gametophyte side only (size, gametangiophore count).
- **Output per gametophyte** depends on whether a rosette is one individual (then up to 30 cups, each with gametangia of unknown number) or a stand of many.
- **Record.** No Silurian gametophyte is known (see the Rhynie gametophyte card); the sources I read do not fix the oldest Sciadophyton stage within the Early Devonian, so the card's Lochkovian lower limit is a bound, not a record.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** sporophyte-independence, from the gametophyte side: a large, conducting, cup-bearing gametophyte with no attached or identified sporophyte, to set against the Rhynie gametophytes, whose sporophyte matches rest on tissue similarity and co-occurrence, and against [Sporogonites](sporogonites.md), where a gametophyte-sporophyte connection is reported but contested.

- **Can transfer:**
  - German Sciadophyton, up to 30 axes up to 10 cm long radiating from a central part (Remy et al. 1980, via Gerrienne & Gonez 2011; a secondary account, the primary not read).
  - German Sciadophyton, as described by Kenrick (2017): axes 2.0-2.6 mm wide and over 9 cm long; terminal cups 3.5-15.0 mm wide carrying bodies 0.20-0.55 mm wide, smaller and denser at the margin. Use as gametophyte-organ size and layout for the genus as a form-genus concept; the specimens, localities and species behind each figure are not identified in the sources I read, so treat them as separate observations.
  - Some partly mineralised German axes: a strand of S-type conducting cells (Kenrick et al. 1991, via Kenrick 2017 and Gerrienne & Gonez 2011), as a trait of some axes and not of every specimen.
  - Frequent association in the same beds with Stockmansella langii and Huvenia kleui (Schweitzer 1983; Kenrick et al. 1991, via Gerrienne & Gonez 2011), as co-occurrence only.
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - A sporophyte partner, sporophyte size or the gametophyte-to-sporophyte ratio: the partners proposed are unsupported or unproven, and the same morphology has been tied to different lineages.
  - Sporophyte independence or dependence: no sporophyte is attached to any Sciadophyton rosette, so the fossil bounds the gametophyte side only.
  - Standing height, original diameter or physiological parameters: lengths and widths are of flattened compression axes in a form genus, and an S-type label gives no conductivity, mechanical or water-use values.
  - Gametangium number per cup, the sex of gametophytes, or per-individual gametangiophore output: the bodies are of two sizes of unknown meaning, and one protocorm against packed individuals is unresolved.
  - The Rebreuve specimen for any size or anatomy: it has axes at most 0.3 mm wide, no cups or strand, and was identified by rosette geometry alone, so its values are outside the trait bounds as well as the transfer list.
  - A Brazilian occurrence, a Lochkovian record, or stage-level timing within the Early Devonian, or that the German, Belgian, Welsh and French records are one species.
  - Rhynie-style ontogeny, spore germination or fungal symbiosis: I found none documented for Sciadophyton in the sources I read.

## Open questions

Is there any Sciadophyton rosette attached to a sporophyte, and is the form genus polyphyletic? Is a rosette one gametophyte or a packed stand? Are the two sizes of bodies antheridia and archegonia? Can the Rebreuve specimen be separated from rosette-like fragments of other taxa? What stage are the German Siegenian beds on the ICS scale, and is any record Lochkovian? Is the Brazilian structure in Gerrienne et al. (2001) a Sciadophyton, and does Remy et al. (1993) record stomata in Sciadophyton itself? What did Steinmann and Kräusel & Weyland originally take the rosettes to be?
