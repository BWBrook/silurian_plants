## In brief

The Baragwanathia floras are the compression assemblages of the Melbourne Trough in central Victoria, named for the long-leaved lycopsid [Baragwanathia longifolia](baragwanathia-longifolia.md) and also holding zosterophylls, rhyniophytoids, a trimerophyte-grade plant and bryophyte-like fossils. Lang & Cookson (1935) called them Silurian on graptolites; Garratt (1978) divided the Yea district into a Lower Plant Assemblage (LPA), argued Ludlow, and an Upper Plant Assemblage (UPA), Early Devonian. Brachiopod evidence and revised mapping now put most of the Yea beds in the Early Devonian (Earp 2024), though some eastern LPA localities remain undated. The floras are the principal Early Devonian record of vascular-plant diversity in eastern Gondwana, and survive only as transported fragments.

## Morphology and anatomy

By my tally from Table 1.1 of McSweeney (2024), about 16 formally named species in 11 genera are known: *Baragwanathia longifolia*; the zosterophylls *Zosterophyllum australianum*, *Z. ramosum*, *Parazosterophyllum timsiae*, *Gippslandites minutus* and *Taungurungia garrattii* (see [zosterophylls](zosterophylls.md)); the rhyniophytoids [Salopella](salopella.md) (three species), [Yarravia](yarravia.md) (three, including the former [Hedeia](hedeia.md)), *Uskiella spargens* and *Cooksonia* cf. *paranensis*; the trimerophyte-grade *Dawsonites subarcuatus* (see [Dawsonites](dawsonites.md)); and the non-vascular *Sporogonites chapmanii*. Unnamed forms include zosterophylls with bilateral symmetry (cf. *Z. fertile* and cf. *Zosterophyllum* sp. A; McSweeney et al. 2022) and Tims's unpublished names, among them [Pluricaulis biformis](pluricaulis-biformis.md).

Tims & Chambers (1984) list for Limestone Road (LPA) *Salopella*, *Hedeia*, one definite and two probable zosterophylls, *B. longifolia* and another lycophyte; Ghin Ghin Road adds *Parazosterophyllum* (McSweeney et al. 2020). The Wilson Creek Shale at Frenchmans Spur has *Salopella australis*, *S. caespitosa*, *Yarravia oblonga*, zosterophylls, *B. longifolia* and *Dawsonites*. The assemblages share genera; what separates them is the age argued for them. Preservation is poor: compressions and impressions, with no cell detail or spores from maceration (Tims & Chambers 1984).

## Age and distribution

Elles identified a graptolite with the type flora as an early Ludlow species (Lang & Cookson 1935; Cookson 1935: 'not younger than Lower Ludlow'). Jaeger (1966) redescribed it as *Monograptus thomasi* with *M. aequabilis notoaequabilis*, which indicated the Pragian; Lenz (2013), as summarised by McSweeney (2024), puts *Uncinatograptus thomasi* in the yukonensis Biozone, upper Pragian to lower Emsian. Garratt (1978) found two assemblages about 1,700 m apart, the upper Early Devonian and the lower Ludlow. Edwards et al. (1979) objected that the shelly fauna indicated the Early Devonian, and Hueber (1983) that Baragwanathia's complexity matched the Early to Middle Devonian, with no precursors in well-dated Silurian sediments. Garratt & Rickards (1984) restated the Ludlow age with a lag of about 10 Myr between the assemblages; Garratt & Rickards (1987) and Rickards & Garratt (1990) added Přídolí graptolites from the Humevale Formation at Ghin Ghin and Cheviot, above the Rice's Hill Sandstone Member; Rickards (2000) argued for the Gorstian.

Two revisions followed. Edwards et al. (1997) and VandenBerg's unpublished 2008 map (as cited by McSweeney et al. 2022) reassign much of the Yea area to the Norton Gully Sandstone and Wilson Creek Shale, and McSweeney (2024, chapter 9, in review at the time) concludes that the beds are Pragian-Emsian, with Ghin Ghin Road older than Limestone Road. Earp (2024) uses brachiopod biozones and the Welch et al. (2011) mapping: the Broadford Formation at Yea is Pridoli-Lochkovian, mostly Early Devonian; the two Gorstian graptolite localities appear to lie in Early Devonian units; the graptolite identifications are judged unreliable, one Gorstian record being from a stockpile; localities west of the anticline are Pragian-Emsian. Brachiopods and plants almost never occur together, so no LPA locality is directly dated, and those east of the anticline, including Limestone Road and Ghin Ghin Road, remain unresolved, with the evidence not supporting Ludlow either. The revisions differ in stratigraphic framework and agree that no Silurian age is demonstrated. Elsewhere, Baragwanathia abitibiensis is Emsian (Ontario; Hueber 1983) and the Silurian candidate is [B. brevifolioides](baragwanathia-brevifolioides.md), described as B. brevifolia by Kraft & Kvaček (2017). Pšenička et al. (2021) still treat the Victorian assemblage as Ludfordian, at about 10 degrees south.

## Affinity and controversies

Garratt et al. (1984) and Rickards (2000) read larger UPA Baragwanathia as evolution over the Silurian-Devonian interval; McSweeney (2024) found no difference in axis width or microphyll length between the assemblages and suggests that smaller Limestone Road axes reflect size sorting. Kotyk et al. (2002) reviewed the LPA as another advanced late Silurian flora while concluding that Bathurst Island gave the best evidence of Silurian complexity; the comparison lapses if the LPA is Early Devonian. Hao & Gensel (1998) proposed a north-east Gondwana unit linking Victoria and South China, sharing Zosterophyllum australianum, Z. ramosum, Yarravia and a poorly preserved Baragwanathia sp.; McSweeney (2024) finds more genera in common with regions outside South China and wants more evidence, while Capel et al. (2023) recover a Pragian-Emsian north-east Gondwana phytochorion.

## Ecology and physiology

All material is transported. The Wilson Creek Shale is black, deep-water and anoxic; the Norton Gully Sandstone and Broadford Formation carry turbidites, the latter a submarine fan; the Humevale Siltstone is distal-shelf mudstone (as summarised by McSweeney et al. 2020, 2022; Earp 2024). Palaeocurrents indicate land to the west and south-west, and to the east for Walhalla (McSweeney et al. 2021).

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Date as a parameter.** A Gorstian LPA (426.7-425.0 Ma) would place microphylls and axes over 20 mm wide 5-14 Myr before an Early Devonian one (my arithmetic, ICS bounds). Use Lochkovian as baseline, a Pridoli sensitivity, and the Gorstian only as an extreme.
- **Size jump.** The [Cooksonia](cooksonia.md) card gives Silurian axes of 0.2-1.5 mm; Baragwanathia axes over 20 mm are more than an order of magnitude wider. If the date is Early Devonian this is an Early Devonian size step, not a Silurian anomaly (F2).
- **Timescale.** Crown tracheophytes at 432-428 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026) leave 1-7 Myr before a Gorstian LPA and over 8 Myr before a Lochkovian one (my arithmetic).
- **Observation filter (F3, F4).** Transported marine compressions give size and gross form, not physiology, and cannot be tied to dispersed spores; the age of plant beds is indirect.
- **Palaeolatitude (F4).** Reconstructions give about 10 or 30 degrees south, and the PBDB gplates model moves the Victorian localities from 16 to 32 degrees south between its Ludlow and Pragian-Middle Devonian assignments (model output, not data).
- **Diversity.** About 11 genera against 28 in the Posongchong flora ([Eophyllophyton](eophyllophyton-bellum.md) card); the records are probably not sampled comparably (my inference).

## Open questions

Can the eastern Yea localities be dated by in-place graptolites, conodonts, brachiopods or U-Pb on tuffs (my suggestion)? Do LPA and UPA Baragwanathia differ? Will Tims's undescribed zosterophylls be described? Is the Mudgee (New South Wales) Baragwanathia, cited by McSweeney as a personal communication, real?
