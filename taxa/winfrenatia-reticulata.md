## In brief

Winfrenatia reticulata is a crustose, layered fungus-cyanobacteria association silicified in the Rhynie chert, described in 1995-1997 as the oldest terrestrial lichen. It is a rare, direct view of a cyanobacterium-based symbiosis on early land, but later work has weakened the lichen label: re-examination suggests a more complex, three-partner consortium, and Berbee et al. (2020) say a stable, mutualistic fungus-photobiont relationship cannot be inferred from the morphology. It also anchors a wider debate: were lichens or cyanobacterial crusts a land cover before plants, and when did true lichens appear?

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed (Taylor et al. 1997): the thallus consists of superimposed layers of aseptate hyphae with numerous uniform depressions on its upper surface. Hyphae extend into the base of each depression and form a three-dimensional net; each net space encloses a coccoid cyanobacterium with a thick sheath. The cells divide in three planes, forming clusters of up to about 64 individuals, and the fungus appears to parasitise the photobiont as new cells form. Reproduction was by endospores and soredia. Reported dimensions are not extracted here.

Reinterpretation (Karatygin et al. 2009, from their abstract). The main component of the thallus is a filamentous Nostocales-type cyanobacterium, and structures read as hyphae are probably its hollow sheaths. Mycobiont hyphae occur at the base of the thallus and associate with a coccoid cyanobacterium, making the organism three-part: one fungus and two cyanobacteria. I could not read the paper beyond its abstract.

## Age and distribution

Rhynie chert only, Pragian (see range notes). Rhynie preserves a hot-spring wetland with seven small vascular plant species, arthropods, algae, oomycetes, fungi and cyanobacteria (Berbee et al. 2020). The Rhynie cyanobacteria include Hapalosiphonaceae (Nostocales), showing morphologically complex cyanobacteria adapted to land by 407 Ma (Strullu-Derrien et al. 2023).

## Affinity and controversies

- Taylor et al. (1997): a cyanolichen; mycobiont closest to the Zygomycetes, photobiont like Gloeocapsa or Chroococcidiopsis.
- Karatygin et al. (2009): a three-partner thallus dominated by filamentous cyanobacteria, though still called a lichen.
- Honegger et al. (2013) call it enigmatic and note it lacks the stratified anatomy of the Lochkovian lichens they describe (Cyanolichenomycites and Chlorolichenomycites, about 415 Ma, internally stratified, tentatively Pezizomycotina).
- Berbee et al. (2020): morphologically unlike extant lichens, a loose consortium; lichen in the broad sense at most. They regard the Lochkovian charcoalified thalli as the earliest compelling lichens.

Evidence that would settle it: a stable spatial correlation of fungal hyphae with photobiont cells across many specimens, chemical markers of the fungal partner, and better size and abundance data.

## Early lichens and pre-plant land cover

- Ediacaran claims. Yuan et al. (2005) described lichen-like fossils in the marine Doushantuo Formation (551-635 Ma); Retallack (2013) read some Ediacaran fossils as lichen-like soil-crust organisms. Berbee et al. (2020) call the Doushantuo evidence less than compelling and note that Dickinsonia steroids point to an animal.
- Phylogenetic constraint. Time-calibrated phylogenies of ascomycetes and chlorophyte algae put fungal lichenisation after the earliest tracheophytes, with no unambiguous earlier fossils (Nelsen et al. 2020).
- Minority view. Retallack (2022) argues for an Ordovician-Silurian 'age of lichens' with branched, lichen-like nematophyte canopies; this conflicts with Nelsen et al. and is not independently corroborated.
- What is secure: microbial (cyanobacterial) land cover has older, mostly geochemical support (extensive soil-forming cover by about 850 Ma, aeroterrestrial algae in Cambro-Ordovician palynomorphs; Wellman & Strother 2015), and coalified microbial mats consistent with soil crusts appear by about 449 Ma (Berbee et al. 2020).

## Ecology and physiology

Photosynthetic carbon fixation by the cyanobacteria with fungal support is the inferred economy; Karatygin et al. suggest the filamentous partner could add nitrogen fixation, which is an inference. Taylor et al. propose the symbiosis suited periodically desiccated surfaces.

## Why it matters for modelling

- Pre-plant cover: model the early land surface as microbial crusts and cryptogamic ground cover, not bare rock. Porada et al. (2016) simulate a potential weathering flux of 2.8 km3 of rock per year by Late Ordovician non-vascular vegetation, about three times today's global chemical weathering flux and highly CO2-sensitive; this is a model output to test, not data.
- Soil formation: lichen- and liverwort-dominated cryptogamic covers form thin soils whose depth is limited by organism size and growth form, aggregated by cyanobacteria and fungi, and are better early-Devonian analogues than moss covers (Mitchell et al. 2016).
- Nitrogen and carbon inputs from cyanobacterial consortia: Winfrenatia gives a Rhynie-scale existence proof, but no rates.
- Timing: use 415 Ma (Lochkovian) for the first compelling lichens; treat earlier lichen cover as a hypothesis with the Nelsen et al. phylogenetic result against it.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast preserved fungal–cyanobacterial association with assumed mutualistic nutrition and early lichen cover, because Rhynie material records spatial association without establishing nutrient-exchange rates.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Spatial association in Rhynie W. reticulata thin sections: Taylor et al. (1997) report three-dimensional nets enclosing sheathed coccoid cyanobacteria; use as their anatomical interpretation.
  - Reproductive structures in Rhynie W. reticulata material were reported as endospores and soredia (Taylor et al. 1997); encode that interpretation without specifying production or dispersal rates.
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Stable mutualistic carbon-for-nutrients exchange cannot be assigned to W. reticulata from morphology; Berbee et al. (2020) explicitly reject that inference.
  - Nitrogen-fixation capacity or rate cannot be treated as observed in W. reticulata; the proposed contribution of its filamentous partner is an inference.
  - Weathering rates cannot be taken from Porada et al. (2016) as measurements of W. reticulata: their Late Ordovician non-vascular vegetation flux is a simulation output.
  - The first compelling lichen occurrence cannot be assigned to Winfrenatia; Berbee et al. (2020) instead identify Lochkovian charcoalified thalli.

## Open questions

- Was the Winfrenatia fungus-photobiont relationship mutualistic, parasitic or neutral?
- How abundant was it at Rhynie?
- Did any lichen-grade symbiosis predate vascular plants?
