## In brief

Hedeia is Cookson's (1935) name for Victorian plants whose naked axes end in flat-topped, corymb-like fructifications of elongate sporangia. It was long treated as a separate genus from Yarravia, and was placed variously in the trimerophyte grade, among advanced rhyniophytes and, most recently, as a possible progymnosperm precursor. McSweeney et al. (2021) show that Hedeia and [Yarravia](yarravia.md) are preservation states of one plant and sink Hedeia, so a card is kept here only because the name is in the literature, in datasets and in the affinity debate. For modelling, treat the two cards as one taxon.

## Morphology and anatomy

The 1935 protologue was not available; I rely on McSweeney et al. (2021), as reproduced in McSweeney (2024). Cookson described a fructification as a secondary branch system, in some cases sterile, with large elongate to oval sporangia terminating its subdivisions and all tips at about one level. Y. (=Hedeia) corymbosa fructifications are 10-20 mm wide and 10-25 mm high, equidimensional or slightly taller, with sporangia 1.0-2.0 by 5.0-9.0 mm, the central ones longer than the lateral ones, and long intervals between dichotomies, so that some sporangia sit in the distal half. Cookson (1949) showed radial construction in a cast from Lilydale. Y. sinica is smaller, with sporangia 0.3-0.5 by 2.0-3.0 mm on an inverted-umbrella fructification. No spores, cuticle or tracheids are known.

## Age and distribution

Cookson (1935) described H. corymbosa from the 'Alexandra Sandstone' near Alexandra, in a paper on Silurian plant remains; the unit is now the Norton Gully Sandstone, taken as Pragian-Emsian. Tims & Chambers (1984) listed Hedeia spp. from the Wilson Creek Shale at Frenchmans Spur and Hedeia sp. from the Lower Plant Assemblage at Limestone Road, and argued that if Hedeia-type organisation had evolved from Salopella-type, this must predate the Ludlow. That argument lapses if the Yea beds are Early Devonian (Earp 2024 supports this for the western localities only). Tims's manuscript name Hedeia gracilis (1980 thesis) turned out to be Y. oblonga (McSweeney et al. 2021).

Outside Victoria, McSweeney et al. (2021) cite Cookson's (1937) suggestion that a specimen from Warrentinna, Tasmania, resembled the base of a H. corymbosa fructification (with a narrower axis) and Talent & Banks's (1967) listing from the Warrentinna and Scamander siltstones. H. sinica (Hao & Gensel 1998) is the only other formally named Hedeia species recognised in that account, from Yunnan. The PBDB also lists H. parvula (Yurina 1969, Central Kazakhstan), which I could not verify; the PBDB accepts all Hedeia entries as Yarravia. Capel et al. (2023) count Yarravia (with Hedeia) among taxa shared between Australia and South China in the Pragian-Emsian.

## Affinity and controversies

Banks (1975), Hueber (1983), Edwards & Edwards (1986), Hao & Gensel (1998) and Hao & Xue (2013) are summarised by McSweeney et al. (2021): trimerophyte grade (Banks); an advanced rhyniophyte, possibly a poorly preserved Hedeia behind Yarravia (Hueber); a rhyniophytoid for lack of vascular evidence (Edwards & Edwards); agreement with Hueber but noting that dichotomies are not synchronous (Hao & Gensel); and Progymnospermopsida with Polythecophyton, on the lateral fertile branches resembling Aneurophytales (Hao & Xue), although cambium and pitting are apparently absent. McSweeney et al. find simple branching, which they take to exclude the trimerophyte grade, and propose heterobathmy. None of these placements can be tested without anatomy, and the progymnosperm suggestion would need the very tissues that are missing.

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing direct. The material is transported and gives no information on habit, water relations or life cycle.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Naming.** Use Yarravia in the programme's records; keep Hedeia only as a search key. The PBDB already accepts Hedeia entries as Yarravia, whereas literature compilations such as Capel et al. (2023) mark the combination as recent.
- **Traits.** Fructification size 10-25 mm and sporangia 5-9 mm long give the larger end of the compound-fertile-unit range; the sporangium count per fructification is not stated for Y. corymbosa in the source read.
- **Geography (F4).** The only formally described species outside Victoria is Y. sinica; Tasmanian and Kazakh records are unverified. Without spores, shared genus-level presence cannot be turned into a dispersal constraint, and convergence is an open alternative (McSweeney et al. 2021).
- **Record (F3).** Hedeia cannot be used as evidence for a Silurian origin of compound fertile units unless the Yea beds are shown to be Silurian.

## Open questions

Does the Tasmanian material belong here? Is H. parvula the same plant, and from what level? Is any in situ spore or tracheid evidence recoverable from the Alexandra or Yunnan material?
