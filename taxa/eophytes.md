## In brief

Eophytes are the plants known to have made permanent cryptospores (dyads and tetrads that never separate). Edwards et al. (2014) coined "cryptophytes" for all such plants. Edwards et al. (2021a) then erected the subclass Eophytidae for those with forking, striated, leafless axes, rare stomata, terminal valvate sporangia and leptoid-like conducting cells. The parent plants are minute charcoalified fragments from the Pridoli and Lochkovian of the Welsh Borderland, which means the spore record (from about 460 Ma) outruns the body record by some 40 Myr. Their placement, on the embryophyte stem, the tracheophyte stem or elsewhere, decides what cryptospores can date.

## Morphology and anatomy

The core members in Edwards et al. (2021a) are *Fusiformitheca* (a replacement name for *Fusitheca* Wellman, Edwards & Axe 1998), "cf. *Salopella*" in part, *Grisellatheca* and *Ficoiditheca*. *Partitatheca* and *Culullitheca* are "possible" members, because they rest on sporangia with little subtending axis. *Lenticulatheca* (discoidal sporangia whose dyads separate into monads) is excluded.

- *Culullitheca richardsonii*: beaker-shaped sporangia on an unbranched axis, permanent dyads (Wellman et al. 1998).
- *Fusitheca fanningiae*: fusiform sporangia on an isotomously branching axis, permanent dyads (Wellman et al. 1998).
- *Grisellatheca salopensis*: tetrads and a putative elater at a forking apex (Edwards et al. 1999).
- *Ficoiditheca aenigma*: fig-shaped sporangia on branching, stomatiferous axes, with dyads unmatched in dispersed assemblages (Morris et al. 2012).
- *Partitatheca*: four-valved sporangia with sculptured dyads (see the [Partitatheca](partitatheca.md) card).

Anatomy comes from SEM of split charcoal. Central cells have thick, globule-bearing walls, occasional strands and plasmodesmata-sized pores, and no water-conducting cells (Edwards et al. 2021a). Edwards et al. (2022) describe a range of branching in smooth axes and report about 20 species with terminal valvate sporangia; I saw that count only as a search-tool summary of the paper's highlights. Thalloid fossils built of transfer cells are proposed as gametophytes (Edwards et al. 2021b). [*Aberlemnia*](aberlemnia-caledonica.md) has no in situ spores and is not an eophyte on present evidence.

## Age and distribution

Spores first: definitive tetrads and dyads from the Darriwilian, with an older disputed Argentine record ([Ordovician cryptospore assemblages](ordovician-cryptospores.md)). Cryptospores dominated assemblages for about 60 Myr and fell abruptly near 410 Ma (Edwards et al. 2021a), though five cryptospore species still occur in a late Lochkovian or Pragian Wyoming palynoflora (Noetinger et al. 2021). Global dispersed diversity, as summarised by Pšenička et al. (2021): a Rhuddanian peak of 17 genera and 33 species, a trough in the Sheinwoodian (9 and 9), then a stable 14 genera and 25 species on average from the Homerian to the Pridoli, while trilete spores rose from 3 genera and 4 species in the Hirnantian-Sheinwoodian to 35 and 129 in the Pridoli. In the Pridoli, cryptospore taxa restricted to one climatic belt were commonest in the cool belt and the tropical belt was poorest (Bek et al. 2024).

Bodies appear only in the Pridoli and Lochkovian of Shropshire, among *Cooksonia*-grade plants. Morris et al. (2011) hypothesised that discoidal *Lenticulatheca* (dyads separating into monads) and trilete-producing *Paracooksonia* are close relatives that differ in meiotic cytokinesis. Cryptospore plants were then replaced, with zosterophylls displacing them locally in the late Lochkovian (Edwards et al. 2014; Edwards and Richardson 2004).

## Affinity and controversies

Edwards and Kenrick (2015) drew cryptophytes as a grade potentially spanning stem-group embryophytes, hornworts, mosses, liverworts and vascular plants. Edwards et al. (2021a) called the position speculative: the stem of the tracheophytes or of the liverwort-moss clade (as recorded in the Partitatheca card), or within polysporangiophytes in the original sense of Kenrick and Crane. Wellman (2026, abstract) argues from cryptospore distribution that eophytes may be the most basal stem-group embryophytes, sister to bryophytes plus tracheophytes. Clark et al. (2023) scored eophytes among 160 fossils; the sentence I read groups them with stem-tracheophyte branches. Hooper et al. (2026) ran *Partitatheca* and *Lenticulatheca* as stem-embryophytes and as stem-tracheophytes, noting that no cladistic test exists. Hooper et al. count *Lenticulatheca* as an eophyte, whereas Edwards et al. (2021a) exclude it, and Pšenička et al. (2021) list *Fusiformitheca* among rhyniophytoids. Wellman et al. (1998) had attributed the dyads to small rhyniophytoid plants and argued that a bifurcating axis weighs against bryophyte affinity. Strother and Taylor (2024) go further and doubt that Ordovician spores imply plants at all.

Do not confuse the group with the "Eophytic Flora" of Cleal and Cascales-Miñana (2014), the zosterophyll-dominated Early Devonian flora.

## Ecology and physiology

Small size, thick-walled epidermis and leptoid-like cells suggest poikilohydric plants (Edwards et al. 2021a). Edwards et al. (2022) suggest sporophytes may have been matrotrophic, nourished by autotrophic gametophytes that are still unidentified. Permanent dyads allow selfing only if gametophytes were bisexual (Edwards et al. 2014).

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Scale.** Axes at most 1-2 mm wide and a few millimetres to about 2 cm tall (an inference): sub-tracheophyte hydraulics, with growth in wet pulses.
- **Life cycle.** A candidate for gametophyte-dependent sporophytes, which makes them an anchor for F1. Treat photosynthetic autonomy as a parameter, not a trait.
- **Meiosis.** Sporogenesis gave dyads or tetrads and never single spores, so models of mating, selfing and dispersal need unit-sized propagules (23-62 um dispersed units; about 2,600 tetrads in a 0.3 mm Ordovician sporangium fragment, by the Ordovician card).
- **Timeline.** Cryptospores dominated for about 60 Myr, then collapsed near 410 Ma while trilete producers rose; a displacement model needs both the cryptospore decline and the zosterophyll rise.
- **Calibration risk.** The same fossils give a different crown-embryophyte age depending on whether eophytes sit on the embryophyte or tracheophyte stem (Hooper et al. 2026).

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast permanent cryptospore dispersal units and leptoid-like food conduction with tracheophyte reproduction and hydraulics, using charcoalified bodies while separating them from the much older dispersed-spore record.

- **Can transfer:**
  - For Fusitheca fanningiae, now Fusiformitheca, in Lower Devonian Welsh Borderland charcoalified material, represent fusiform sporangia, isotomously branching axes and permanent dyad dispersal units (Wellman et al. 1998).
  - For Grisellatheca salopensis among Welsh Borderland charcoalified mesofossils, represent tetrads at a forking apex; retain the elater as putative rather than functional machinery (Edwards et al. 1999).
  - For Ficoiditheca aenigma in Lochkovian Welsh Borderland material, represent fig-shaped sporangia, branching stomatiferous axes and dyads; its dispersed-spore counterpart is unidentified (Morris et al. 2012).
  - For eophyte split-charcoal specimens from the Welsh Borderland, species unspecified, distinguish thick-walled central cells from absent water-conducting cells; this constrains anatomical states rather than measured hydraulic rates (Edwards et al. 2021a).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Matrotrophy and gametophyte dependence cannot be fixed as observed life-cycle states: proposed gametophytes are unattached and sporophyte nourishment remains hypothetical.
  - The 2 cm height maximum cannot be treated as measured stature: no whole plant exists and the estimate is inferred from fragments.
  - Ordovician cryptospore occurrence cannot establish an Ordovician eophyte body plan or apply its propagule measurements to a named body taxon: the producers are unknown.
  - Cryptospore-bearing fossils cannot impose one crown-embryophyte calibration: alternative stem placements change the inferred age and lack a cladistic test.
  - Permanent dyads cannot establish selfing without an independently supported bisexual gametophyte.

## Open questions

Cladistic placement, with attached axes and the gametophyte; whether Ordovician producers had the same sporophyte; the last Devonian occurrence; and whether the Wellman new eophyte taxon changes the diagnosis.
