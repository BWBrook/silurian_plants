## In brief

*Ambitisporites* is a form genus for small, smooth, subtriangular trilete spores with an equatorial thickening. It is the commonest and simplest of the earliest trilete spores, recorded from the Katian of Saudi Arabia and, more doubtfully, the Sandbian of Sweden, and abundant only from the Silurian. Trilete spores form when tetrads break apart. They were long read as the earliest trace of the vascular-plant lineage; Hooper et al. (2026) now infer that the ancestral embryophyte spore was trilete, so the genus marks total-group embryophytes, not tracheophytes. In situ examples occur in Pridoli-Lochkovian *Cooksonia*.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed: a Y-shaped trilete suture on the proximal face, a laevigate to finely microgranulate surface, subtriangular outline and an equatorial thickening (crassitude) (Libertín et al. 2022). Some Saudi specimens show a faint "ghostly" distal ornament (Wellman et al. 2015). *A. avitus* sensu Steemans et al. (1996) measures 31-44 µm; the other true trilete taxa in the same core span 21-38 µm. Ordovician forms are lumped as the *A. avitus-dilutus* morphon (Steemans et al. 1996). Wall ultrastructure of Ordovician specimens is unknown, and Habgood et al. (2002) caution that spores of such simple form may come from several taxa. True trilete spores must be told from monads that merely separated from permanent tetrads, such as *Imperfectotriletes*, which lack a functional suture (Wellman et al. 2015).

## Age and distribution

Katian to Hirnantian: Qusaiba-1 yields eight trilete and two hilate taxa, six of the trilete forms ornamented, although ornamented trilete spores were previously unknown before the Wenlock (Steemans et al. 2009; Wellman et al. 2015). Naturally separating (hilate and trilete) spores are under 1% of the Qusaiba-1 spore count. Hirnantian: rare laevigate trilete spores in Turkey (Steemans et al. 1996). Sandbian: *Ambitisporites* sp. at 41.9 m in the Borenshult-1 core with *Aneurospora*, *Leiotriletes* and *Retusotriletes*, between 455 and 454.41 Ma, smooth and less diverse than the Saudi forms (Rubinstein and Vajda 2019). Type material is Lower Silurian, from Libya (Hoffmeister 1959).

Sporophyte megafossils lag the spores: *Cooksonia barrandei* (middle Sheinwoodian) is the oldest unequivocal one (Libertín et al. 2018) and *C.* cf. *pertoni* has a minimum age of 426.9 Ma (Morris et al. 2018; Hooper et al. 2026), about 23 Myr after the minimum age of the Saudi spores (449.65 Ma; my arithmetic).

## Affinity and controversies

The older convention: trilete spores come mainly from vascular plants (Gray and Lawson 1985), because only c. 2.5% of living bryophyte genera make them, many with non-functional marks, and tetrad dissociation was judged a derived state, adopted by polysporangiophytes or by hornworts plus polysporangiophytes (Wellman et al. 2015). Rubinstein and Vajda (2019) accepted that trilete spores do not unequivocally mark vascular plants. Salamon et al. (2018) describe Hirnantian Polish plants with *Ambitisporites*-like spores whose marks are irregular, as in bryophyte spores, and read the plants as probably non-vascular polysporangiophytes.

The new result: Hooper et al. (2026) reconstruct the ancestral embryophyte spore as an unenveloped trilete monad from a tetrahedral tetrad, robust across models and trees. Trilete spores then date the embryophyte crown, not the tracheophyte crown, and permanent tetrads and dyads are stem-group. They bound crown embryophytes at 459.1-426.9 Ma (maximum from the Swedish spores, minimum from *Cooksonia* cf. *pertoni*), use the Katian *A. avitus* (449.65 Ma) as a minimum for the older split between Zygnematophyceae and embryophytes, and recover crown embryophytes at 457-450 Ma and crown tracheophytes at 432-428 Ma, coincident with the first *Cooksonia*.

Direct links to *Cooksonia* are good: *Ambitisporites* occurs in situ in Welsh Borderland *C. pertoni* subsp. *pertoni*, *C. banksii* (assigned to *A. avitus*) and *C. hemisphaerica* (Habgood et al. 2002; Edwards et al. 2014, Table 2), and in *C. bohemica* from the Pridoli of Bohemia (Libertín et al. 2022). Pridoli *Cooksonia* has a simple conducting strand (Edwards et al. 1992; Wellman et al. 2015). The Ordovician spores remain unlinked to any plant.

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing is known directly. Ordovician spores arrive in marine shelf sediments by wind or water, and Saudi Arabia lay at 30-60°S (Wellman et al. 2015). Wellman et al. (2015) place the origin of the trilete lineage in southern high-latitude Gondwana, Rubinstein and Vajda (2019) in Baltica; the data cannot separate them.

## Why it matters for modelling

- Do not use trilete spores as a vascular-plant marker in Ordovician-Silurian vegetation or weathering models; on Hooper et al. (2026) they mark total-group embryophytes, and tracheophyte origin sits near 432-428 Ma. The older reading (Steemans et al. 2009) put a vascular-lineage origin near 450 Ma; keep it as a sensitivity case.
- Clock constraint: trilete spores give a soft maximum on the embryophyte crown (459.1 Ma); Morris et al. (2018) allowed crown tracheophytes 450.8-419.3 Ma, Hooper et al. (2026) 432-428 Ma.
- Abundance: separating spores were under 1% of Katian-Hirnantian spores in Saudi Arabia, so their producers were a minor component of the spore rain; the share rises only in the Silurian.
- Lag: c. 23 Myr from the Katian spores to the first Cooksonia megafossils; Hooper et al. (2026) cut the clock-versus-fossil mismatch to as little as 19 Myr.

## Open questions

- Are the Sandbian Swedish specimens true trilete spores?
- Does Ordovician *Ambitisporites* have tracheophyte-type or bryophyte-type wall ultrastructure?
- Which plants made Ordovician *Ambitisporites*, and are the Polish Hirnantian macrofossils related?
- Did the trilete lineage arise in Gondwana or Baltica?
