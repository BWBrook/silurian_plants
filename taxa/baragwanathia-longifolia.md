## In brief

Baragwanathia longifolia is the type species of a genus of large, long-leaved, microphyllous lycopsids from the Melbourne Trough of Victoria. Lang & Cookson (1935) dated the flora to the Ludlow from an associated graptolite, which made it the earliest lycopsid and a plant far in advance of contemporary Silurian floras. Jaeger (1966) moved the type flora into the Devonian, but a Silurian age was maintained for an older, lower assemblage near Yea until at least 2000. Brachiopod biostratigraphy and revised mapping now put the Yea beds in the Pridoli-Lochkovian, mostly Early Devonian (Earp 2024). Elsewhere, Baragwanathia-grade lycopsids are recorded only from the Pridoli (Bohemia, graptolite-dated; Kazakhstan, tentative), so this card is mainly a test of how the fossil record constrains the timing of microphyll evolution.

## Morphology and anatomy

Primary descriptions (Lang & Cookson 1935; Hueber 1983) were not available in full text for this card; the account below uses abstracts and secondary summaries and says so where it matters. The type diagnosis describes stems of considerable size clothed with spirally arranged, long, simple leaves of uniform width, a large central xylem cylinder with stellate primary xylem, and sporangia among the leaf insertions in certain zones of ordinary shoots, possibly on the adaxial side near leaf bases. Secondary summaries describe the leaves as single-veined microphylls, in contrast to the unvascularised enations of Asteroxylon; I could not verify the venation in a primary source.

Preservation is poor. Tims & Chambers (1984) describe compressions and impressions in the Wilson Creek Shale whose mineral remains were fragmented by folding; cellulose-acetate peels and maceration gave no cellular structure or in situ spores. Epidermis and cuticle are therefore documented only in the Emsian congener B. abitibiensis (Ontario), which also shows flattened xylem strands and pyritised leaves (Hueber 1983). The Bohemian B. brevifolioides is diagnosed by slender isotomous axes with short-to-moderate linear microphylls that have a median midrib and form a dense tuft enfolding the apex (Kraft & Kvaček 2021).

## Age and distribution

Elles identified the associated graptolite as Monograptus uncinatus, an early Ludlow species, which gave Lang & Cookson's flora its Silurian age (as reported by Tims & Chambers 1984). Jaeger (1966) described it as a new species, M. thomasi, found M. aequabilis (a basal Lochkovian index) in plant beds 1,500 ft below the M. thomasi horizon, and concluded that the earliest adequately dated Baragwanathia was basal Lochkovian. Garratt (1978) distinguished two Yea assemblages separated by about 1,700 m of virtually unfossiliferous strata: an Early Devonian upper one and a lower one claimed as Ludlow. Hueber (1983) disputed this on the grounds that Silurian sediments of Wales, Czechoslovakia, New York and Podolia show no precursors. Garratt et al. (1984), Garratt & Rickards (1987; Monograptus cf. prognatus, Pridoli) and Rickards (2000; Gorstian) defended Silurian ages, and Kotyk et al. (2002) and Kenrick et al. (2012) accepted them.

Recent work reverses much of this. McSweeney et al. (2022) call the Ludlow-to-Pragian age 'contentious' and adopt Pragian-Emsian for Ghin Ghin Road; Earp (2024) finds the Yea Broadford Formation Pridoli-Lochkovian with most of it Early Devonian, treats the monograptid identifications as unreliable, and notes that a Gorstian Bohemograptus record came from a road-making stockpile. Earp did not identify any Yea plant locality with a supported Silurian age, but calls the question only partially answered: several localities, including the two Gorstian-graptolite sites, lack brachiopod control, and one is more likely Pridoli. The secure Silurian record of the genus is B. brevifolioides from the Pridoli Neocolonograptus ultimus Biozone of Karlštejn, Czech Republic (Kraft & Kvaček 2017, 2021), and a Baragwanathia sp. from the Pridoli of Kazakhstan that a later compilation marks with a query (Pšenička et al. 2021; Bek et al. 2024). Pšenička et al. (2021) still list the Victorian assemblage as Ludfordian, so the literature is not uniform.

## Affinity and controversies

Baragwanathia is a drepanophycalean lycopsid close to Drepanophycus. Hueber (1992) called it 'questionably the earliest' lycopsid and thought Drepanophycus the earliest recognisable one. Tims & Chambers (1984) asked whether the flora evolved in isolation in the Melbourne Trough; the Early Devonian date removes the problem, because B. longifolia then sits among other Lochkovian-Pragian lycopsids. Unresolved points are the monophyly of the genus (Australia, Ontario, Bohemia) and the position within Drepanophycales.

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing direct is known about growth habitat, because the fossils are transported into marine mudstones. Pšenička et al. (2021) note that Baragwanathia has been interpreted as characteristic of warm, humid zones, but B. brevifolioides occurs at higher latitude in peri-Gondwana. Rooting, spores and gametophytes of B. longifolia are unrecorded in the sources read.

## Why it matters for modelling

The date is a free parameter with consequences. A Gorstian B. longifolia (426.7-425.0 Ma in the project timescale) would place microphylls, stellate xylem and large stems about 5-14 Myr before a Lochkovian record (419.6-413.0 Ma); an Early Devonian date makes the genus unremarkable. The conservative model setting is a first lycopsid occurrence in the Pridoli (422.7-419.6 Ma, B. brevifolioides) and B. longifolia from the Lochkovian, with a Gorstian scenario as a sensitivity run. Molecular clocks place crown tracheophytes at 432-428 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026), so the lycophyte lineage predates every lycopsid body fossil and the fossil record sets only minimum ages.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Test the timing of a microphyllous plant with stellate xylem, using the Victorian record's reported anatomy alongside its disputed age and limited primary-source verification.

- **Can transfer:**

  - B. longifolia, Victorian type material: provisionally represent sporangia among leaf insertions in zones of ordinary shoots; retain the uncertainty over their adaxial position (Lang & Cookson 1935, type diagnosis quoted second-hand).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not use the reported 10-50 cm stem fragments or inferred length to c. 2 m as verified stature measurements; they come from an unverified museum search-result summary.
  - Do not transfer stomatal or epidermal details from Emsian B. abitibiensis to B. longifolia; the latter lacks the corresponding documented anatomy.
  - Do not assign isotomous branching to B. longifolia from B. brevifolioides; branching is verified only for the Bohemian congener in this comparison.
  - Do not treat homospory as observed; it is assumed without verified reproductive evidence.
  - Do not fix the Yea occurrence in the Silurian or equate the conservative Lochkovian setting with a demonstrated first appearance.

## Open questions

Do any Yea Lower Plant Assemblage beds have a defensible Silurian age (in-place graptolites, conodonts, brachiopods bracketing the plant horizons)? Is B. brevifolioides congeneric with B. longifolia? A secondary summary reports marine epibionts on the Bohemian holotype; this should be checked in Kraft & Kvaček (2017). Quantitative morphology (stem and leaf dimensions, branching, roots, spores) of B. longifolia needs re-measurement from the Lang & Cookson and Hueber material.
