## In brief

*Parka decipiens* is a cutinised, flat thallus a few centimetres across whose upper surface carries many small discoid structures read as sporangia, holding alete spore-like bodies. It is common in Lochkovian lacustrine and distal alluvial beds of the Old Red Sandstone. It has been read as an animal egg mass, a plant fruiting body, a green alga, a possible liverwort and a link between charophytes and bryophytes. Nothing in the sources read fixes its placement or the ploidy of its spores, so for the life-cycle question (F1) it is a flagged problematicum, not a source of parameters.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed, from 56 specimens (Niklas 1976): a dorsiventral thallus with a thick, many-layered ventral cell layer, dorsal sporangia arising from the margin and from the tissue beneath, and a disc-like holdfast. Growth simulations and latex replicas led Niklas to infer lateral proliferation of cells within a pseudoparenchyma of dichotomising filaments; this is a reconstruction. Cleal and Thomas (1995) call it a cutinised, encrusting alga whose thallus was covered in large sporangia. Martín-Closas (2003) gives 2 to 3 cm across, against 1 to 2 mm for living *Coleochaete* (a derived 10-30-fold linear contrast).

Spores (Hemsley 1989, SEM and TEM): a two-layered wall, one layer lamellate, with the lamellae attributed to deposition by a tapetum, and some similarity to the lamellate exine of liverworts. Niklas's chemical comparison found similarity to *Pachytheca* (as summarised by Cleal and Thomas 1995), but Abbott et al. (1998, abstract only) found *Prototaxites* and *Pachytheca* chemically distinct from *Parka* by pyrolysis-GC-MS, FTIR and solid-state 13C NMR.

## Age and distribution

PBDB (`data/eprp/record/pbdb_plant_occurrences.csv`) holds two *Parka* occurrences, both UK and Lochkovian. Turin Hill, near Forfar, is the best-known locality and the basis of much of the early debate; Carmyllie and Targrove Quarry also yield it (Cleal and Thomas 1995). At Turin Hill it occurs with *Zosterophyllum myretonianum* ([Zosterophyllum](zosterophyllum.md)), *Cooksonia caledonica* ([Aberlemnia caledonica](aberlemnia-caledonica.md)) and *Prototaxites* ([Prototaxites](prototaxites.md)); the Zosterophyllum Zone flora there is early Gedinnian by palynology and fish (as summarised by Cleal and Thomas 1995).

## Affinity and controversies

Earlier readings, as listed by Cleal and Thomas (1995), were animal origin, seeds of a rush or bur-reed, the fruiting body of another plant, and eurypterid egg packets (Marston 1870, for Targrove specimens). Later: a thalloid plant on a holdfast (Niklas 1976); a green alga resembling *Coleochaete* in ontogeny (Niklas 1976); possible liverwort affinity (Neuber 1979, as summarised by Cleal and Thomas 1995); an intermediate between algae and bryophytes (Hemsley 1989, 1990, as summarised by them). Hemsley (1990) argued the alete spores could be haploid or diploid, and that diploid early spores would support the interpolation theory of the sporophyte; the spores may lie between algal oospores and the trilete haploid spores of bryophytes. Hemsley (1994) derives the sporophyte and its sporangial contents from a charophyte zygospore.

Martín-Closas (2003) calls it the only fossil tentatively related to Coleochaetales, but with a thallus far larger than any living *Coleochaete*. A new result bears on the analogy: complex discoid *Coleochaete* thalli with perpendicular cell divisions form a group only 66.5 ± 17.8 Myr old (Bierenbroodspot et al. 2025; abstract read, full text not). If that holds, a Lochkovian discoid thallus cannot be an ancestor of that group, and any resemblance is convergence or reflects stem-group forms (my inference). Kenrick (2018) lists *Parka* among Silurian-Devonian fossils of unclear affinity that are potentially relevant to life-cycle evolution, without discussing it.

## Ecology and physiology

The lacustrine setting and the cutinised, holdfast-bearing thallus fit an organism of shallow water margins (my inference); Niklas inferred that it probably spent at least part of its life out of water (as summarised by Cleal and Thomas 1995). Reproduction is by spore-like bodies. No gametangia, zygotes, embryos or fertilisation structures are known, and no evidence of matrotrophy.

## Why it matters for modelling

It does not constrain F1 parameters; it constrains how far to trust the algal-to-plant analogy. (1) If its spores were diploid (Hemsley 1990), *Parka* would be a multicellular diploid spore producer without vascular tissue, a natural experiment in "interpolation"; if haploid, it is a thallus bearing sporangium-like structures with no counterpart in living streptophyte algae. The two readings predict different alternation structure, so a model can carry "Parka-like thallus, ploidy unknown" as a scenario, not a fitted trait. (2) It shows a centimetre-scale, cutinised, non-axial spore producer coexisting with *Zosterophyllum* and *Cooksonia* in Lochkovian freshwater settings, so the plant community was not only axial polysporangiophytes. (3) For the F3 observation model, alete spores from such thalli would enter Lochkovian dispersed assemblages as non-trilete monads and cannot yet be separated from other alete forms (my inference). (4) Martín-Closas (2003) takes *Parka* as possible evidence of high diversity among fossil taxa in land-plant ancestry; whether it left descendants is unknown, so a model should not assume every early thalloid spore producer lies on the line to embryophytes.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast a non-axial, spore-bearing thallus with axial plants and alternative life-cycle scenarios using a Lochkovian organism whose ploidy and affinity remain unresolved.

- **Can transfer:**
  - P. decipiens, Old Red Sandstone material: thalli 2 to 3 cm across constrain horizontal size, without supplying height; the review does not identify measured specimens/site in this card (Martín-Closas 2003).
  - P. decipiens, 56 Scottish Lower Old Red Sandstone specimens: dorsiventral thallus, many-layered ventral tissue, dorsal reproductive structures and disc-like holdfast provide observed architecture (Niklas 1976).
  - P. decipiens spores examined by SEM and TEM have a two-layered wall with one lamellate layer; use observed wall architecture, with specimen/site unspecified (Hemsley 1989).
  - P. decipiens at Turin Hill co-occurs with Zosterophyllum myretonianum, Cooksonia caledonica and Prototaxites; represent mixed thalloid and axial community membership (Cleal and Thomas 1995).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not fit haploid/diploid dominance, matrotrophic transfer or an established alternation of generations: spore and thallus ploidy are undetermined and reproductive life-cycle structures are unknown.
  - Do not use about 35 um spore size or about 35,000 spores per sporangium as verified parameters: both come from an unattributed web summary.
  - Do not treat dichotomising filaments and lateral cell proliferation as directly observed growth rules: they were reconstructed from simulations and latex replicas.
  - Do not assign Coleochaete physiology, ancestry or life history from thallus resemblance: the affinity is tentative.
  - Do not infer measured terrestrial water relations from cutinisation or the lacustrine setting: submerged versus emergent growth is unresolved.

## Open questions

What is the ploidy of the spores and the thallus? Are the discoid structures sporangia, gametangia or zygote-like bodies (a UCMP page says oval structures may be zygotes or sporangia)? Does the wall contain sporopollenin, as in the *Coleochaete* zygote wall (Delwiche et al. 1989)? Is there any Pridoli record, and is the United States record real? Is the *Pachytheca* link, rejected chemically by Abbott et al. (1998), still arguable on growth form?
