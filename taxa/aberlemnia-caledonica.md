## In brief

*Aberlemnia caledonica* is a small, naked, dichotomously branching plant with terminal, flattened, circular to reniform sporangia. Edwards (1970) described it as *Cooksonia caledonica* from the Lower Old Red Sandstone of Angus; Gonez and Gerrienne (2010b) moved it to a new genus when they restricted *Cooksonia* to forms with trumpet-shaped sporangia. It is a typical cooksonioid: a morphology known from impressions, with no spores, stomata or anatomy, so its vascular status is inferred by association. It matters as a documented size and branching template for the earliest polysporangiophytes and for the Silurian-Devonian spread of this grade across Laurussia and Gondwana.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed in the type material (Edwards 1970): an incomplete plant at least 6.7 cm high; naked axes 0.4-1.8 mm wide (mean 0.65 mm), branching at 55-60 degrees with four orders and distally narrowing diameter; a slight pseudomonopodial tendency without inequality of axes; terminal sporangia 1.3-3.0 mm wide and somewhat shorter than wide (globose forms 1.3-2.2 mm wide, 1.2-2.0 mm high; oval forms 1.8-3.0 mm wide), some reniform; the axis widens below the sporangium; a faint border sometimes present. No central strand was seen. The whole-plant reconstruction in Edwards (1970) is explicitly conjectural, assuming the observed branching repeats.

Interpreted later: Gonez and Gerrienne (2010b) diagnose isotomous, occasionally trichotomous axes whose distal segments each end in one laterally flattened sporangium of two equal valves, joined proximally, opening along a subdistal line (as relayed by the Plant Fossil Names Registry and Drovandi et al. 2024; I could not access the paper). Edwards (1970) noted one specimen that might be a two-valved dehisced sporangium but kept the single-sporangium reading. Pridoli Argentine specimens: axes 0.94 mm, sporangia 2.1 mm wide by 1.75 mm high (Drovandi et al. 2024).

## Age and distribution

Type: Aberlemno Quarry, Dittonian, with *Zosterophyllum myretonianum*, *Prototaxites* and *Parka* (Edwards 1970). Later: late Lochkovian floras of the Paraná Basin, Brazil, which lay near the South Pole (Milagres et al. 2018; the species is identified in their figure captions, and I did not read their text); and the Rinconada Formation, Argentina, in a matrix whose graptolites are early Pridoli, in a unit dated Wenlock to Early Devonian by different authors (Drovandi et al. 2024). Morel et al. (1995) described Ludlow plants "resembling" *C. caledonica* from Bolivia at 50-60°S. The genus is Pridoli by other species: *A. krizii* in Bohemia (Libertín et al. 2024) and *A. junggaria* in West Junggar (Liu et al. 2024).

## Affinity and controversies

Gonez and Gerrienne (2010a) redefined *Cooksonia* with apomorphies after an allometric study of over 100 specimens of *C. paranensis*, which separated true variation from growth stage. The sporangial shape range in *A. caledonica* should be tested the same way. Libertín et al. (2022) kept the Pridoli *C. bohemica* in *Cooksonia*, arguing its sporangia lack a valve slit, so genus limits are contested. Liu et al. (2024) place *Aberlemnia* in Rhyniopsida; Drovandi et al. (2024) call the Argentine flora basal tracheophytes. No anatomy supports either for *A. caledonica*. I could not read Libertín et al. (2024), the genus review.

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing is known directly. Axes of a few millimetres and no foliage imply a determinate, leafless plant with a few terminal sporangia. The type slab is covered with parallel axes, suggesting current alignment, and the associated fish and eurypterid fragments suggest aquatic deposition. Argentine fossils were transported by turbidity currents into the sea (Drovandi et al. 2024).

## Why it matters for modelling

- Size: centimetre-scale height (at least 6.7 cm), axes 0.4-1.8 mm, sporangia 1.3-3.0 mm wide, about three times the mean axis width.
- Architecture: determinate, isotomous, four branching orders; if regular, up to 16 terminal sporangia (my arithmetic), which bounds fecundity per plant.
- Range: Laurussia and southern Gondwana (50-60°S and near the pole), so a cold-tolerant, wide-dispersing grade; the Pridoli Rinconada record fits the South Laurussian-Northwest Gondwana realm of Drovandi et al. (2024).
- Cooksonioids radiated away from the Rheic Ocean region over the Silurian-Devonian transition (Libertín et al. 2022). Hooper et al. (2026) treat *Cooksonia* as a stem-tracheophyte and date crown tracheophytes to 432-428 Ma, coinciding with its first appearance; *Aberlemnia* is younger, and whether it is stem- or crown-tracheophyte is untested.
- Do not give it tracheids, stomata or spores in a model without a stated assumption.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast centimetre-scale, repeatedly branching reproductive architecture with anatomically better-known plants, using measured type fragments while retaining uncertainty over vascular status and Silurian occurrence.

- **Can transfer:**
  - A. caledonica, Aberlemno type material: use at least 6.7 cm as preserved height and 0.4-1.8 mm as axis width, with mean 0.65 mm (Edwards 1970).
  - A. caledonica, Aberlemno type material: represent four observed branching orders, branching at 55-60 degrees and distal axis narrowing (Edwards 1970).
  - A. caledonica, Aberlemno type material: use terminal sporangia 1.3-3.0 mm wide, somewhat shorter than wide, as reproductive geometry (Edwards 1970).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not assign observed tracheids, stomata or spore characters to A. caledonica; this card explicitly requires assumptions for these traits.
  - Do not treat 16 terminal sporangia as measured fecundity; this is arithmetic conditional on regular repetition of the branching pattern.
  - Do not use the Rinconada occurrence as an unqualified Pridoli first appearance; the enclosing unit has conflicting age assignments.
  - Do not parameterise growth habitat or cold tolerance from depositional setting and geographical distribution alone; the growth habitat is unknown.

## Open questions

- Do sporangia contain spores, and of which type?
- Is *Aberlemnia* monophyletic, and is *A. bohemica* separable?
- Does the Argentine Pridoli record survive revised dating of the Rinconada mélange?
- Is the sporangial shape range ontogenetic?
