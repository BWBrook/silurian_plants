## In brief

Tortilicaulis offaeus is a charcoalified mesofossil from the Lochkovian of the Welsh Borderland: a branching stem a tenth of a millimetre wide ending in a fusiform sporangium of under a millimetre, with twisted epidermal cells that led earlier workers to compare it with bryophytes. Morris et al. (2024) scanned five specimens by synchrotron tomography and report elongate cells with regularly spaced internal wall thickenings, which they interpret as simple annular tracheids, so the plant looks bryophyte-like on its surface and is vascular inside. The anatomy is from this species only; the type species, T. transwalliensis, is known from compressions without anatomy, and the evidence is clear in one specimen and weaker in the rest.

## Morphology and anatomy

Stems are smooth and naked, isotomously branched at wide angles, with elongate epidermal cells twisted around the stem axis, most strongly beneath the sporangium, and stomata with collapsed guard cells. Sporangia are fusiform with a rounded tip, 0.25-0.96 mm long, with epidermal cells twisted 20-45 degrees from the long axis, thick outer walls and thin inner walls, and a dehiscence line into two valves along modified boundary cells (Morris et al. 2024; Morris et al. 2012 for the valves).

Observed: elongate, deeper-seated cells with regularly spaced transverse internal thickenings, damaged in places by pyrite crystals and by compression. Interpreted by the authors: true secondary-wall thickenings, hence tracheids, and not charring artefacts, because the cells lie deeper than the superficial cells that showed horizontal intracellular structures in earlier SEM work (Edwards et al. 1994), which Edwards & Axe (2004) read as residual cytoplasm. The wall pattern (G, S or P type) and any lignification are not shown. The abstract and introduction call the evidence demonstrative ('unequivocal'); the results admit that two of five specimens do not show it and a third shows one cell, so the conclusion leans on HD743/01 and on agreement across the others.

## Age and distribution

All five specimens come from one early to middle Lochkovian siltstone on North Brown Clee Hill; two were collected in 1995 and three in 2012 (Morris et al. 2024). The type species T. transwalliensis extends the genus to the Pridoli (Freshwater East, Pembrokeshire, compressions; Edwards 1979) and to a lower Lochkovian compression assemblage at Targrove Quarry near Ludlow (Fanning et al. 1992). Other genus-level records (the PBDB lists a 'cf. Tortilicaulis' from Argentina) were not examined. For the group's Silurian record see [the rhyniophytoids](rhyniophytoids.md).

## Affinity and controversies

Edwards (1979) described the type species with twisted, unbranched stems under sporangia with twisted cells; its affinity could not be settled without anatomy, and she compared it with the possible bryophyte Sporogonites (see [Sporogonites](sporogonites.md)) and with the twisted, ephemeral seta of the liverwort Pellia. Later authors added the moss Takakia (Renzaglia et al. 1997; Jia et al. 2003, as cited by Morris et al. 2024). The genus was then cited as non-vascular and as evidence for Late Silurian bryophytes (Pant & Bhowmik 1998; Yang et al. 2004; Banerjee & Dutta 2014; Ligrone et al. 2012, who list T. transwalliensis among the best-known fossil bryophyte candidates), though Edwards had noted that tracheids are rarely preserved in such compressions. Isotomous branching and trilete monad spores in Targrove T. transwalliensis (Fanning et al. 1992) already ruled out a Takakia relationship, because Takakia sporophytes have a columella, a calyptra and unbranched stems (Morris et al. 2024). Morris et al. (2024) cite the cladistic analysis of Kenrick & Crane (1997) for placing Tortilicaulis among the horneophytes, the earliest-diverging polysporangiophytes in the tracheophyte stem group (the reference they list is the authors' Nature review; the full analysis may be in their 1997 Smithsonian monograph, which I did not see), and note that three characters scored unknown or absent in that analysis are now known to be present (stomata, tracheid thickenings, valved dehiscence).

Morris et al. (2024) conclude that T. offaeus is an early-diverging tracheophyte and leave its stem-group or crown-group position to later cladistic analysis. They compare its twisted, bivalved, elongate sporangia and Apiculiretusispora-type spores with Psilophyton, as Gerrienne (1997) did, but note that the unique shared feature, a mode of sporangial dehiscence, is common in early land plants with elongate sporangia, and that Gerrienne's hypothesis that Tortilicaulis-type plants were ancestral to the trimerophytes remains unproven. Their title and abstract speak of Tortilicaulis as a genus; the anatomy is from T. offaeus. Whether T. transwalliensis is also vascular is untested: its epidermal twisting is similar, but its size and spore size (43-49 um) differ and its anatomy is unknown.

## Ecology and physiology

Stomata on stems and trilete spores point to a homoiohydric sporophyte (Morris et al. 2024), now with a conducting cell file in the axis. Twisted stems and sporangium cells in the type species suggested to Edwards (1979) that the stems lacked rigidity, and twisting is associated with valved dehiscence in T. offaeus. The plants were tiny: stems about 0.1 mm wide bearing sporangia up to about 1 mm, fragments of at most a few millimetres. Nothing is known of their rooting, gametophyte or nutrition. Boyce (2008, via the group card) argued from axis width that the narrowest early axes could not have been photosynthetically autonomous; at 0.06-0.17 mm these stems sit at the narrow end of the group card's range, so dependence is a model-based alternative here, not an observed life history.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Conducting cells in thin axes.** In HD743/01 a stem 0.12-0.16 mm wide holds cells 7.5-12.5 um wide with annular-type thickenings. Cooksonia in [the group card](rhyniophytoids.md) has axes of 0.2-1.5 mm, so these stems are narrower; the cell widths are those of twisted, compressed cells, not lumen or conduit geometry.
- **Appearance does not predict anatomy.** Twisted cells and a bryophyte-like look kept the genus among non-vascular candidates from 1979 until the 2024 anatomy, which reversed that for one species. A model that scores bryophyte-grade from gross form alone should carry that uncertainty; see [Sporogonites](sporogonites.md).
- **Record timing.** The finding is Lochkovian. It does not move the first tracheids earlier, because Whitcliffian (late Ludlow) Cooksonia-type stems with annular thickenings (Edwards & Davies 1976; Morris et al. 2024; their attachment to Cooksonia sporangia is uncertain, see the Cooksonia card) and Lochkovian charcoalified C. pertoni (Edwards et al. 1992) are already known. It removes T. offaeus from the Silurian-Lochkovian list of non-vascular, bryophyte-like sporophytes, but the Pridoli type species is untested.
- **Size.** Sporangia of 0.25-0.96 mm and spores of 12-20 um are small against the 1-9 mm sporangia of the group card.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** water-transport and record-timing: a stem 0.1 mm wide that looks bryophyte-like but contains cells with tracheid-like thickenings, in a Lochkovian charcoal assemblage where Ludlow Cooksonia-type stems and Lochkovian C. pertoni already carry tracheids.

- **Can transfer:**
  - HD743/01 (Brown Clee Hill): conducting cells 40-120 um long and 7.5-12.5 um wide with thickenings 1-1.7 um thick and 1.4-2.5 um apart, in a stem 0.12-0.16 mm wide; use as an observed small-axis example of a tracheid-bearing stem, not as a minimum, and with cell dimensions treated as approximate because the cells are twisted and compressed (Morris et al. 2024).
  - Stem widths by specimen: HD248/03 0.11 mm; HD270/01 0.115-0.17 mm; HD743/01 0.12-0.16 mm; HD(L)138/06 0.095-0.15 mm; HD(L)138/05 is not readable (printed 0.55 um) (Morris et al. 2024).
  - Isotomous branching: HD743/01 (about 125 degrees, at least two dichotomies 0.42-1 mm apart), HD270/01 (144 degrees), HD(L)138/05 and HD(L)138/06 (Morris et al. 2024).
  - Sporangia by specimen: 0.25 x 0.18 mm (HD(L)138/05); 0.37 x 0.26 mm, bifurcating (HD(L)138/06); about 0.76 x 0.35 mm (HD743/01); 0.76 x 0.44 mm (HD270/01); 0.96 x 0.37 mm (HD248/03); fusiform, valved (Morris et al. 2024).
  - Stomata on stems in HD743/01 and NMW 99.11G.1; trilete retusoid monads of about 20 um (HD743/01) and 12-15 um (HD(L)138/05), with separating tetrads in HD248/03 and HD743/01, assigned to Apiculiretusispora with the caveat on proximal ornament given above (Morris et al. 2024).
  - A Lochkovian, early to middle, occurrence of a vascular plant with these traits, dated by sporomorph biozone (Richardson & McGregor 1986, as cited by Morris et al. 2024).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - The tracheid wall pattern: G, S or P class is undetermined, and 'annular' is the authors' reading of thickenings that are clear in one specimen, undeterminable or single-cell in two, and not clearly demonstrated in two.
  - The anatomy and size values to the type species T. transwalliensis or to the genus as a whole: the type species has no demonstrated vascular tissue, larger stems (0.1-0.4 mm), larger sporangia and 43-49 um spores, and its Pridoli record cannot date tracheids. Shared morphology (twisted cells, isotomous branching, trilete monad spores, fusiform sporangia) is documented for both species, but it does not establish the type species' vascular status.
  - Whole-plant height, rooting, gametophyte or life cycle: the longest specimen is 2.9 mm of stem.
  - Hydraulic conductance, lumen diameter, lignification, water-use values or any conducting-file geometry from cell width alone: lumens, end walls and lignin are not given.
  - Dimensions as biological limits or population distributions: the material is charcoalified and partly pyritised, with cell walls shrunken, collapsed or homogenised (Edwards & Axe 2004, as cited by Morris et al. 2024), and the five specimens were selected for completeness, preservation and little pyrite.
  - Stem or crown position within tracheophytes: unresolved, and the horneophyte placement cited by Morris et al. (2024) used character scores they now revise.
  - Autonomy or independence of the sporophyte: not observed. Boyce's (2008) size-based dependence argument, as reported in the group card, is a model-based alternative that these narrow stems would fall under; it is not an observed life history.

## Open questions

Do independent specimens or scans of T. offaeus reproduce the thickenings, and what are their wall-pattern class and pitting? Does T. transwalliensis, in the Pridoli, have tracheids, which would move the genus's vascular record earlier? Where in the tracheophyte tree does the plant sit, and is its twisting a functional trait tied to valved dehiscence? Is a gametophyte or a larger plant of the same species preserved anywhere in the charcoalified assemblage?
