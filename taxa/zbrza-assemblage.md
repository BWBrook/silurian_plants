## In brief

Zbrza is one Hirnantian mudstone horizon in the southern Holy Cross Mountains, Poland, that yielded a handful of millimetre-scale coalified axes and sporangium-like bodies, one probable stoma and rare spores with irregular trilete marks. Salamon et al. (2018) read them as putative polysporangiophytes, probably non-vascular, which would pre-date the oldest accepted plant macrofossils (Wenlock *Cooksonia*) by about 10 to 18 Myr (my arithmetic from the whole-stage bounds in `data/timescale.csv`, each +/- 0.9 Myr; the authors say c. 15 Myr). The authors' case rests on gross form compared with *Cooksonia*, one probable stoma on an unattributed axis, spores with irregular trilete marks, and the exclusion of graptolite, octocoral and conodont alternatives. A later review lists it among pre-Silurian claims that lack sufficient characters to be assigned to land plants, a different class from claims shown to be fragments of other organisms (Wellman et al. 2022). This card holds the record, the dispute and the limits on its use; it describes no plant and offers no whole-plant reconstruction.

The paper reports no spores inside the sporangium-like bodies, no conducting cells and no cuticle on the figured axes. The authors describe the fossil genus *Hostinella* as including vegetative isotomously branched axes; placing axes there says nothing about the parent plant (my reading).

**Interpreted** (the authors): the bodies are sporangia, the axes belong to polysporangiophytes, and the one stoma shows land-plant status for the assemblage as a whole. The authors report three different sporangial morphologies on the three fertile fragments and read that disparity as unsurprising given the diversity of cryptospore producers. **My inference:** the stoma is the only character the authors call unambiguous, and it is linked to the other fragments by co-occurrence, not by attachment. Because the three fertile fragments differ, the axes, the stoma and the dispersed spores need not belong to one species or even one clade; the assemblage is a set of fragments, and a measurement on one fragment is not a trait of a taxon.

## Age and distribution

Single locality: Zbrza, in the Zbrza Anticline, southern Holy Cross Mountains, central Poland. The Zalesie Formation is a Hirnantian regression succession of mudstones, shales and marls with sandstone interbeds, recording sea-level fall forced by Gondwanan glaciation (Trela et al. 2019). The Holy Cross Mountains lay at about 30 to 40 degrees S near the south-western margin of Baltica, and icebergs from Gondwana reached the south-western shelf of Baltica in the Hirnantian, probably including the Holy Cross region (Trela et al. 2019, citing Porębski et al. 2019).

Age basis (see range notes): fauna and microfossils date the host rock to the Hirnantian. The palynological evidence in the paper's Text S1 and the primary stratigraphic papers (Kielan 1959; Temple 1965; Masiak et al. 2003; Trela & Szczepanik 2009) were not read; Trela & Szczepanik's abstract, as returned by search, reports two acritarch assemblages, the upper with redeposited Furongian to Lower Ordovician and Middle Ordovician peri-Gondwanan taxa such as *Frankea*, probably transported from Avalonia during its collision with Baltica. Trela et al. (2019), co-authors of the Zbrza paper, give two routes for the reworked acritarchs (redeposition at the Avalonia-Baltica collision zone, or iceberg transport from Gondwana) and conclude that the plant fragments may likewise be redeposited and older than the Hirnantian.

**Interpretation (mine, flagged):** reworking and contamination are different processes with different effects. Reworking of older material changes the time and, on the routes proposed for the acritarchs, possibly the place the plants lived, but it leaves the fragments in the rock by the Hirnantian, so a minimum age survives if incorporation into the sediment was genuine. Younger material that entered later (contamination, root intrusion, weathering, processing) would invalidate a minimum age. No source read reports evidence for or against younger intrusion at Zbrza. Neither process removes the possibility that the fragments are not plants, which is a separate question of taxonomic identification.

## Affinity and controversies

**The claim** (Salamon et al. 2018). The assemblage documents the oldest polysporangiophyte macrofossils, probably non-vascular, and so extends the polysporangiophyte record by c. 15 Myr. The authors compare the sporangia with *Cooksonia*, and with the Triassic liverwort *Naiadita*; compare the stoma with that of *Akdalophyton caradocki* (Kazakhstan; itself a claimed Ordovician plant, no tracheids, read as a moss); and read the similarity to Silurian and Devonian forms as a very low evolutionary rate. They exclude graptolites and octocorals because the fragments lack appendage-bearing axes and a laminated collagenous substance, and conodonts because the fragments are smooth and heavily coalified. They note that invertebrates and hemichordates can produce axes that resemble land-plant axes, citing Kenrick et al. (1999), and that spores with irregular trilete-like folds are known from some Ordovician forms and from mosses and hornworts.

**Counter-arguments and caveats, with sources:**

- The authors call the assemblage putative, and the trilete marks of their spores irregular, "which casts doubts" on the spores' trilete nature (Salamon et al. 2018).
- Wellman, Cascales-Miñana & Servais (2022) discount pre-Silurian plant megafossil claims in two classes: those proved to be fragments of other organisms (they cite Kenrick et al. 1999) and those that lack sufficient characters to be assigned to land plants. They place Zbrza, with Naugolnykh 2019 and Retallack 2019, in the second class, and judge it doubtful that any claimed pre-Silurian plant megafossil has enough unequivocal characters. No source shows Zbrza to be a non-plant. Cascales-Miñana is an author of both papers. I found no paper dedicated to refuting Zbrza (a title scan of the papers citing Salamon et al. in OpenAlex and Europe PMC, 28 October 2026), so this is a one-sentence judgement in a review, not a rebuttal.
- Pacyna & Barbacka (2022) call the remains rather poorly preserved and say further research is needed to confirm the discovery and document the finds.
- Trela et al. (2019), who include four of the paper's authors, state that redeposition of the fragments cannot be ruled out.
- Hooper et al. (2026) do not use Zbrza: its Supplementary Information (searched as text; main text not re-read) cites neither Salamon et al. nor any Polish record, and bases its calibrations on spores from Saudi Arabia and Sweden and on Wenlock *Cooksonia*.
- Polish precedent: Kozłowski & Greguss (1959) described Ordovician land plants from erratic boulders in Poland, now thought to be roots of living plants (Pacyna & Barbacka 2022, without citation); see `docs/rejected-records.md`.

**My assessment (interpretation, flagged):** the defence is more than shape. It also offers one probable stoma, which the authors call an unambiguous land-plant character, and it excludes graptolite, octocoral and conodont readings by the absence of their features. Its weak points are that the stoma lies on an unattributed dispersed axis, that no spores are in situ, that the trilete marks are irregular, that the fertile fragments differ from one another, and that the exclusions rest on features absent from small fragments. Dichotomising millimetre axes have been misread as plants before (Kenrick et al. 1999 for *Boiophyton*, abstract only), and sporangium shapes close to *Cooksonia* are what the authors read as slow evolution and also what younger *Cooksonia*-type material, such as the Upper Silurian flora described from the same mountains (Bodzioch et al. 2003), would show if it had entered the sample. No source states a non-plant or contamination reading for Zbrza, and I do not claim that either holds.

**What would settle it:**

- Spores in situ inside a sporangium-like body, with wall structure.
- A stoma, cuticle or conducting cells on the same axis as a sporangium-like body.
- More fertile specimens from fresh core with recorded depth, and thermal maturity of the fragments compared with the host-rock organic matter, which would separate Hirnantian material from younger intrusions (my suggestion).
- Independent palynology of the same residue, and the sampling and processing detail in Text S1.
- An independent group's re-examination of the deposited specimens (GIUS 2-3675/1-9).

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing can be inferred. The fragments are coalified debris in marine shelf mudstone, the source environment is unknown, and the Hirnantian setting was glaciated and low in sea level (Trela et al. 2019).

## Why it matters for modelling

The record is the only refereed claim of pre-Silurian polysporangiophyte macrofossils in the sources read; an unrefereed Sandbian claim with tracheids is listed in `docs/rejected-records.md`. It sits in the gap between the Katian trilete spores and the first accepted sporophyte megafossils. Hooper et al. (2026) take 449.65 Ma (Katian *Ambitisporites avitus*) as the minimum age of the algae-embryophyte split, the first accepted *Cooksonia* (Homerian) as the soft maximum for crown tracheophytes at 431.2 Ma, and recover crown embryophytes at 457-450 Ma and crown tracheophytes at 432-428 Ma (as given in the task brief and `docs/primer.md` §5.3; calibrations from Supplementary Table 4).

- If Zbrza were real, a Hirnantian polysporangiophyte would not conflict with crown embryophytes at 457-450 Ma. If it were accepted as stem-group tracheophyte it would undercut the logic of that soft maximum, which rests on the first total-group tracheophyte (my inference from the justification in Supplementary Table 4, not stated by Hooper et al.).
- It is a case of a disputed identification, not a demonstrated false positive: Wellman et al. (2022) class it as insufficiently diagnostic. Four distinct processes can each produce a spurious or misdated record and need separate observation processes: (i) taxonomic misidentification of non-plant material; (ii) reworking of older material, which loses the time and place of life but keeps a minimum age; (iii) younger intrusion or contamination, which invalidates the minimum age; (iv) spore-mark artefacts, since torn permanent tetrads give false trilete marks (Wellman et al. 2022). No source shows that any of these applies at Zbrza.
- If real, it would show what a sparse, fragmentary Hirnantian macrofossil record looks like when the plants were polysporangiate: a few specimens in one marine horizon (my reading).

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** `record-timing`. Zbrza is a claimed polysporangiophyte macrofossil record earlier than the accepted Wenlock record by about 10 to 18 Myr (an envelope from whole-stage bounds, each +/- 0.9 Myr, not a measured gap), and it is disputed. It is one of several pre-Silurian macrofossil claims (see `docs/rejected-records.md`) and the only refereed polysporangiate one in the sources read, so it tests how an observation model treats a flagged first-appearance claim.

- **Can transfer:**
  - One disputed occurrence claim: Zalesie Formation, Zbrza, Poland, Hirnantian (445.2 to 443.1 Ma on ICS v2024/12, each bound +/- 0.9 Myr), carried with a confidence class of *disputed, low*. That class is an ordinal judgement from the evidence read in this card, not a probability and not a measured quantity. **Proposed treatment (my suggestion; no source evaluates it):** keep it out of the base data set and include it in a sensitivity scenario in which it is true, reporting the shift in the posterior for the first appearance of polysporangiophytes.
  - A conditional minimum age. It holds only if the fragments entered the sediment when it formed or are older material reworked into it; younger intrusion would invalidate it. The usable number is the youngest bound of the stage, 443.1 +/- 0.9 Ma, unless the horizon is placed more finely (no finer placement was found), so 445.2 Ma is not a hard minimum. The condition and the propagation of the stage uncertainty must travel with the record.
  - A documented example of a record classed as insufficiently diagnostic (Wellman et al. 2022), to be set beside the resolved cases in `docs/rejected-records.md`. It cannot set a misidentification rate: a rate needs a set of records with resolved outcomes and a denominator of comparable records, and one unresolved assemblage supplies neither.
  - Specimen-level observations as observations of fragments: the sizes in the table above, each tied to its figured specimen. They are not trait values for a taxon, because the three fertile fragments differ in sporangium form and the axes, stoma and spores need not belong to one species.
  - The depositional setting as a sampling frame: marine shelf mudstone at about 30 to 40 degrees S on the south-western margin of Baltica, so the fragments are transported or allochthonous debris (Trela et al. 2019).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - A confident first appearance of polysporangiophytes, tracheophytes, embryophytes, stomata, sporangia or trilete spores. The Hirnantian date is a conditional minimum for the fragments, not the date of an origin.
  - Any trait value for a taxon. Every trait in the front matter is deliberately `unknown` and the size ranges are null, so that `data/traits.csv` does not pass sizes, branching, spores or stomata into the model. The fragments are up to 3.2 mm long, the affinity is unresolved, the stoma is not attached to a fertile axis, and the spores are not in situ and have irregular marks.
  - A whole-plant reconstruction, a height, a habit, or the habitat of the source plants. The depositional setting is known; where the plants grew is not. The comparisons with *Cooksonia*, *Naiadita* and *Akdalophyton* rest on gross form and size with no anatomy or in situ spores, and the last of these is itself a disputed claim.
  - The time and place of the plants' life. If the fragments are reworked, as co-authors say cannot be excluded, they could be older and, on the routes proposed for the reworked acritarchs (my extrapolation), could come from elsewhere; reworking is a different process from taxonomic misidentification and from younger contamination, and the model should treat the three separately.

## Open questions

- Are the sporangium-like bodies sporangia, and are the axes parts of the same organism?
- Do the three different fertile morphologies, the axes, the stoma and the dispersed spores belong to one taxon, several land-plant taxa, or a mixture that includes non-plants?
- Does the stoma belong to a land plant that also bore the sporangium-like bodies?
- Did the fragments live in the Hirnantian, come from older reworked rock, or enter the sample later? These are separate hypotheses with different consequences for the time and place of life and for the minimum age.
- What do Text S1 and the primary stratigraphic papers give for the age basis, and is any radiometric date available?
- Have other Hirnantian marine mudstones been processed for fragments of this size, and with what result?
