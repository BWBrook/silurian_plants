## In brief

*Ulva* (sea lettuce) is the standard exemplar of isomorphic alternation of generations: free-living, macroscopic, similar-looking haploid and diploid thalli, with no matrotrophy, retained zygote or embryo described. It is a chlorophyte, separated from the streptophyte line that gave land plants by more than 900 Myr, so its biphasic cycle evolved independently of the embryophyte one. It is useful for the generic ecology and population genetics of haploid-diploid cycles and for life-cycle plasticity, and it cannot say how or why land plants made the sporophyte dominant.

## Morphology and anatomy

Blades two cells thick, or tubes, of uninucleate cells (Wichard et al. 2015); the two generations are often indistinguishable, especially when immature (Bessho 2024). Gametophytes carry one of two mating types and release biflagellate gametes; diploid sporophytes arise from gamete fusion, and haploid gametophytes from meiotically formed zoids or from unmated gametes. Unmated gametes can develop parthenogenetically into clonal haploid thalli, and diploid parthenosporophytes can arise by spontaneous diploidisation and release zoids of one mating type (Wichard et al. 2015). As I read Coelho and Umen (2021), *Ulva* has repeat-rich, non-recombining sex-determining regions like heteromorphic sex chromosomes. The *U. mutabilis* genome is 98.5 Mbp with 12,924 protein-coding genes; morphogenesis depends on bacterial signals (De Clerck et al. 2018).

## Age and distribution

Cosmopolitan on coasts. No fossil record in the sources read, and the clock-based dates given in the range note place the origin of the green-seaweed clades hundreds of millions of years before land plants. Ulvales and Ulotrichales are sister groups in the UUOI clade (Hou et al. 2022); see [Ulothrix](ulothrix.md).

## Affinity and controversies

Not in doubt as a chlorophyte. The Chlorophyta-Streptophyta split is more than 900 Ma (a TimeTree mean of 976.3 Ma for *Ulva* versus *Klebsormidium*, as cited by Holzinger and Karsten 2013); Bierenbroodspot et al. (2024) date the origin of Streptophyta to about 1 Ga. Leliaert et al. (2012) note that the old split of the Ulvales (isomorphic alternation) from the Ulotrichales (small unicellular diploid stage) rests on life history, and molecular trees give poor or no support for it. Marine Ulvophyceae mostly alternate generations, whereas most freshwater greens are haplontic; the streptophyte ancestor of land plants was haplontic (Leliaert et al. 2012; Niklas and Kutschera 2009; Kenrick 2018).

## Ecology and physiology

Intertidal *Ulva* survives daily drying: in desiccated *Ulva* sp. linear electron flow stops and PSI-driven cyclic flow persists and recovers faster than PSII on rewetting (Gao et al. 2011). Marine green macroalgae lack polyols and use sucrose, proline, glycine betaine or DMSP (Holzinger and Karsten 2013). Reproduction is controlled by sporulation and swarming inhibitors in *U. mutabilis* and by photoperiod in *U. pseudocurvata* (weekly summer peaks); generation time of *U. mutabilis* is 3 to 5 weeks (Wichard et al. 2015). In a natural *U. pertusa* population in the Seto Inland Sea followed for more than 3 years, phase dominance alternated after 11 to 20 months with no seasonal pattern, and the sex ratio stayed near 1:1 (Hiraoka and Yoshida 2010).

## Why it matters for modelling

What the comparison is good for. (1) A test-bed for biphasic models (reviewed by Mable and Otto 1998): Hughes and Otto (1999) show that ecological differences between phases can maintain biphasic cycles even when adults look alike (parameterised on the red alga *Gracilaria*, not *Ulva*); Scott and Rescan (2017) add intrinsic fitness differences and mutation masking; Bessho (2024) shows the phase ratio is set by four demographic forces and persistence needs R0 > 1. Field ratios with phase dominance swapping every 1 to 2 years (Hiraoka and Yoshida 2010) are a target for such models. (2) Life-cycle plasticity: parthenogenesis and diploidisation let a lineage move toward either phase, so a model should allow facultative asexual propagation. (3) A contrast with Rhynie ([Rhynie Chert gametophytes](rhynie-chert-gametophytes.md)), where gametophytes are about a tenth the sporophyte's size: in *Ulva* the generations are equal in size, so any phase difference must be physiological.

What it is not good for. Embryophyte-specific constraints are absent: fertilisation by swimming gametes released into seawater, no zygote retention or maternal provisioning described, a free-living diploid thallus, no sporopollenin spore wall, no cuticle. Its isomorphy is independent of land plants, so it cannot arbitrate between the antithetic and homologous theories; at most it shows isomorphic cycles can be stable. Do not use *Ulva* rates for early land plants.

## Open questions

Do the phases of *Ulva* differ in fitness in the field, and by how much? Is the haploid-diploid transition in *Ulva* controlled by paired homeodomain genes, as Bowman et al. (2016) report for chlorophyte algae and fungi generally? What would a fossil Ulvales look like?
