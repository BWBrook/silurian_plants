## In brief

*Omniastrobus dawsonii* is a barinophyte-like lycophyte from the Emsian Campbellton Formation of New Brunswick, known from strobili of two rows of lateral sporangia preserved as impressions with carbonised spore masses. Its sporangia differ in average spore size along a continuous gradient, from about 70-150 µm to more than 200 µm, and some mix both size classes; the authors read this as a possible intermediate step towards heterospory and call it the oldest lycophyte that is not strictly homosporous (Bonacorsi et al. 2021). It puts spore-size differentiation inside the study interval, as a continuous state between homospory and heterospory rather than a switch. **Read status:** only the abstract of the formal description was available; every fact below that rests on it is marked, and the paper is listed as partly read.

## Morphology and anatomy

**From the abstract (Bonacorsi et al. 2021).** The specimens are impressions of strobili containing numerous carbonised spore masses. The strobili resemble those of the Barinophytales and consist of two rows of lateral sporangia; spores were isolated by standard palaeobotanical maceration and their size distributions measured. The abstract gives no strobilus or sporangium dimensions, no counts of strobili, sporangia or spores, no specimen numbers, and nothing on vegetative organs, so the whole-plant form is unknown to me. Claisse et al. (2025), who scored the genus in their zosterophyll matrix, treat its sporangia as lenticular and vertically flattened, the feature that unites it with *Barinophyton* and *Protobarinophyton*.

Bek and Goswami (2022) and Bek (2025) share one author and are not independent readings. Bek (2025) says he rewrote all in situ spore descriptions in current terminology, and he cites the material as "Bonacorsi et al. 2020, pp. 199-205", a page range that lies in the 2021 International Journal of Plant Sciences paper (198-209), not in the 2020 letter (R388-R389). His sculpture and morphotype assignments are therefore readings of the paper, not measurements.

Press coverage of the 2020 letter also says that some sporangia held only large or only small spores, others only intermediate-sized spores and others the whole range. That is a journalist's summary of material I could not read, and it agrees with the abstract's gradient plus bimodal mixtures. Whether mean spore size varies systematically with position along a strobilus, between strobili, or between plants is not stated in anything I read.

## Age and distribution

Known only from the Campbellton Formation in the sources read; it crops out along the Bay of Chaleur-Restigouche River shoreline of northern New Brunswick (Miller et al. 2012; Kennedy 2011). The authors date the plant as early Emsian (abstract; basis not given). The formation is about 1 km thick and overlies the Val d'Amour volcanics; rhyolite near the top of the latter gave 407.4 ± 0.8 Ma (Wilson et al. 2004, 2005, read via Wellman 2018, Kennedy 2011 and Miller et al. 2012; the original was not read), which Wellman and Kennedy call late Pragian on the older scales they used. On data/timescale.csv it lies in the Emsian (410.62-393.47 Ma).

The spore stratigraphy, from Wellman (2018) who resampled nine sections and reassessed McGregor's earlier unpublished correlations, gives a different age by bed:

- **Late Pragian to possibly earliest Emsian:** the lower eastern section III (Kennedy and Gibling's numbering), around plant localities M and N, the type localities of *Sawdonia acanthotheca* and *Zosterophyllum divaricatum*. The *polygonalis-emsiensis* zone (PE) applies, and *Dictyotriletes subgranifer* may narrow it to late Pragian.
- **Early to mid Emsian:** the western belt (sections I and II, including the Atholville fish and plant beds), the roadcut contact section with the Val d'Amour Formation (early Emsian), and the lower eastern section IV, all in the *annulatus-sextantii* zone (AS); the associated Oppel zone suggests a more restricted early Emsian. Miller et al. (2012) give early to early late Emsian for this zone, and Kennedy (2011), following McGregor, mid Emsian for the basal beds.
- **Late Emsian to possibly earliest Eifelian:** the upper eastern section IV (plant localities B and I), in the *douglastownense-eurypterota* zone.

Wellman's own summary is Pragian to late Emsian [or possibly earliest Eifelian] for the Val d'Amour and Campbellton formations together, and he notes that the boundary between them is hard to place because sedimentary intercalations and volcanism persist in the Val d'Amour Formation. Kennedy (2011), working from the older correlation, inferred a hiatus of about 3-5 Myr between the rhyolite and the basal Campbellton strata ("suggesting a hiatus of approximately 3-5 Ma").

Net: "Campbellton Formation" does not date the specimens, because the beds span late Pragian to late Emsian. "Early Emsian" is the authors' assignment on a basis I could not read, for a horizon I could not locate. It fits the western belt, the contact section and the lower section IV beds, but not the lower eastern section III beds or the upper section IV beds. The independent sources read cannot confirm it for the specimen horizon. The plant-rich beds are in the eastern exposures (Maple Green, Point La Nim, Pin Sec Point; Miller et al. 2012), but the chips' provenance within them is unknown to me. Bek (2025) and Leslie's dataset date the genus 2020, the year of the Current Biology letter; Crossref dates the formal description to 2021, and I could not tell whether the letter used the name.

## Affinity and controversies

*Placement.* The authors compare the strobili with the Barinophytales and differ from other barinophytes in having a gradient rather than discrete size classes (abstract). Claisse et al. (2025), whose zosterophyll analysis included *Omniastrobus* among 15 genera new to phylogenetic work, recover a Barinophytaceae clade of *Barinophyton*, *Protobarinophyton* and *Omniastrobus*, grouped with *Bathurstia*, in the full-sampling parsimony consensus (a basal polytomy among the large-clade lineages) and in the reduced-sampling parsimony tree (sister to the Sawdoniaceae-lycopsid clade); the Bayesian tree for the reduced sampling supports the Barinophytaceae + *Bathurstia* clade but places the remaining Lycophytina differently. The clade's taxon-stability score of below 1 means only that its divergence-node rank agreed between their parsimony and Bayesian trees, which share just 43.6% (17 of 39) of their Lycophytina nodes; it is not a measure of support for the placement. Kenrick and Crane (1997, via Claisse et al.) recognised the Barinophytaceae within Zosterophyllopsida by heterospory and sporangiferous appendages, and Cascales-Miñana and Meyer-Berthaud (2014, via Claisse et al.) defined a Zosterophyllopsida sensu lato that includes them. The abstract and secondary sources describe only strobili, so the placement rests on reproductive characters; I found no vegetative evidence that informed it. See [Zosterophylls](zosterophylls.md) for the grade.

*Reading of the spore-size gradient.* Two readings are on the record and I found no published rebuttal; the third item below is my own list of points the sources do not address.

- **Authors (abstract):** the plant "may show an intermediate step in the emergence of heterospory". The hedge is theirs. They add that it is coeval with the earliest previously known heterosporous plant (not named in the abstract), that it is the oldest lycophyte that is not strictly homosporous, and that its spore-size distribution is unlike nearly all other plants but resembles high spore-size variation in a few unrelated Palaeozoic taxa, which may mean that this is a common early step.
- **Stronger reading (Bek and Goswami 2022; Bek 2025):** intrasporangial heterospory, with micro- and megaspores that are morphologically alike and differ only in diameter. Their argument is that Bonacorsi et al. found equal maturity in all spores, which excludes developmental stage as the cause and leaves different biological function. Bek calls it probably the first heterosporangiate plant and an early stage in which male and female spores are not yet morphologically distinct. This is Bek's gloss, not the authors' wording.
- **Untested alternatives:** that size variation reflects sporangium-to-sporangium developmental or nutritional variation without sexual function; and that the gradient is partly variation among plants or strobili rather than within one organism. Maturity was apparently addressed (Bek's report); function and sex are not addressed in the sources read, and no gametophyte is reported.

*Other early two-sized plants in the same basin.* *Chaleuria cirrosa*, an incertae sedis, possibly progymnosperm-grade plant listed from the Campbellton Formation (Andrews et al. 1974, via Kennedy 2011 and Bek and Goswami 2022), has two size classes in one sporangium, small 30-48 µm and large 60-156 µm, with each sporangium dominated by one type; the larger class falls below the usual megaspore threshold. Wellman (2018) lists *Chaleuria* among the plant assemblages of the early to mid Emsian *annulatus-sextantii* zone (sections C to F) and finds its spore *Camarozonotriletes sextantii* locally abundant. Kennedy (2011), writing before *Omniastrobus*, called it the earliest known heterospory. Leslie's dataset codes *Chaleuria* as heterosporous and *Omniastrobus* as intermediate, the only Early Devonian taxon so coded. Barinophyte relatives disagree on state: Leslie's dataset codes *Krithodeophyton croftii* (55-68 µm) homosporous, whereas Bek (2025) puts it with *Omniastrobus* in an early stage of heterospory.

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing is known of the plant's habit or habitat. The inference from the lithology is limited to the Campbellton basin: a coastal-deltaic and lacustrine-fluvial mosaic with wetlands (Kennedy 2011). Kennedy, following Bateman and DiMichele (1994), wrote that both life cycles need moist conditions and dense stands for fertilisation and that the heterosporous cycle is more closely restricted to wet, possibly semi-aquatic settings; that is a general claim about heterospory, not a test on *Omniastrobus*. Leslie and Bonacorsi (2022, abstract) argue that spores of 100-200 µm are a barrier to evolving heterospory because large spores disperse poorly while small spores cannot house a functional gametophyte inside the wall, and that the barrier was lower in the Devonian as peat-forming wetlands spread. The pooled size range of *Omniastrobus* spans that 100-200 µm window (observation). The barrier argument concerns the combination of large spores with gametophytes that mature inside the spore wall (abstract), and the authors' dataset codes heterospory and endospory as separate states (README), so a taxon whose spores span the window shows one component and not the functional transition. Whether its mixed sporangia show the barrier being crossed is the authors' hypothesis, not a finding, and a single snapshot gives no direction of change. Gametophyte development, spore germination and the sex of the larger spores are not reported in the sources read; "male" and "female" function is inferred by analogy with later heterosporous plants.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Spore size as a distribution, not a switch.** The state reported is a distribution of mean spore size across sporangia within strobili, with a bimodal subset. A model that scores heterospory as binary loses the state this plant exists to test.
- **A bounded timing signal.** On the authors' dating, spore-size differentiation in a lycophyte is documented by the Emsian (410.62-393.47 Ma), so the origin of non-homosporous reproduction in the lycophyte stem grade is at least that old. A minimum age only, with the specimen horizon unconfirmed (see Age and distribution); Silurian and Early Devonian spore compilations are dispersed and cannot show per-sporangium variation.
- **Dispersed-spore limit.** Bek (2025) assigns both size sets to *Apiculiretusispora*, a genus abundant in all the Val d'Amour and Campbellton samples Wellman (2018) studied. A dispersed assemblage can show a wide or bimodal size range within such spores, but cannot show that the sizes co-occurred in one sporangium or came from one parent, so spore-size compilations from dispersed assemblages (see docs/spore-compilation-plan.md) cannot test intrasporangial size differentiation.
- **Within-lineage trend, weakly.** Leslie's dataset, compiled from the primary sources I did not read, gives later barinophytes discrete classes with a large gap: *Barinophyton richardsonii* 48-62 and 220-250 µm, *B. citrulliforme* 33-48.5 and 700-900 µm, *B. cf. obscurum* 47-65 and 480-600 µm, *Protobarinophyton pennsylvanicum* 30-42.5 and 410-560 µm (all Late Devonian in the dataset). A continuum in the Emsian and discrete classes later is the pattern expected if differentiation progressed within the lineage, but it rests on one Emsian plant and one sampled horizon and cannot yet bear a rate. My tabulation of the same dataset (not the authors' analysis) shows no clear rise in iso- or microspore size through the Devonian among homosporous entries: median size midpoints of 50.5 µm in the Early Devonian (n = 57), 64 µm in the Middle (n = 11) and 52.5 µm in the Late (n = 9), and none of the 57 Early Devonian entries reaches 100 µm (maximum 80 µm). The auditing model reports that the full text of Leslie and Bonacorsi finds no clear temporal increase in spore diameter; I could not read it.
- **Stage framework.** Bek and Goswami (2022) cite Stewart and Rothwell (1993) for five stages from homospory to heterospory: fewer spores in some sporangia, larger remaining spores, constant content, dioecious gametophytes, endosporic gametophytes. *Omniastrobus* is evidence only on spore size; spore number per sporangium, gametophyte sex and endospory are not reported in the sources read.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** reproductive-strategy. It tests whether spore-size differentiation, a precondition for sexual specialisation of spores, was present in an Emsian free-sporing lycophyte, and whether the spore sizes at that time formed a continuum or discrete classes spanning the 100-200 µm window. This is a pattern at one time; it supplies no sequence of change.

- **Can transfer:**
  - *Spore sizes across the sporangia of the strobili:* a continuous gradient in average spore size from about 70-150 µm to above 200 µm, with some bimodal sporangia; Campbellton Formation, New Brunswick, F. M. Hueber's collection (specimen numbers, and chip, strobilus and sporangium counts, not available; the abstract does not show that the gradient spans a single strobilus) (Bonacorsi et al. 2021, abstract).
  - *Pooled spore diameter of 65-280 µm, as a range only* (Bek and Goswami 2022; Bek 2025; the authors' dataset, Leslie 2021). The 156 µm centre, the 200-280 µm large class, trilete form and equal maturity rest on Bek's reading alone and need the paper.
  - *Strobilus architecture:* two rows of lateral sporangia (abstract), lenticular sporangia (Claisse et al. 2025); a Barinophytaceae member in Lycophytina.
  - *Stage assignment:* Emsian (410.62-393.47 Ma) on the authors' dating, with "early Emsian" as their stated age. The independent stratigraphy read does not confirm it for the specimen horizon, because the formation's beds range from late Pragian to late Emsian (Wellman 2018).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - *Functional heterospory:* sex, germination, gametophyte size and endospory are not reported in the sources read; the abstract's evidence is spore size, and micro- and megaspore labels are inference. Large spores and endosporic gametophytes are separate components in Leslie and Bonacorsi's framework.
  - *Spore number or mass per sporangium, sporangium size, sporangia per strobilus, spore output per plant:* none is in the abstract; they await the full text.
  - *Any whole-plant trait:* no vegetative information appears in the abstract or the secondary sources, and whether the full text gives any is unknown. Take no height, axis width, branching, leaf, stomatal, xylem, rooting, habitat or habit value from *Omniastrobus*, and none from other barinophytes without a `from:` marker and a source.
  - *Prevalence, variance and rate:* one taxon at one formation; no population, per-plant or between-strobilus variance is available in the sources read.
  - *A trajectory:* a single undated-within-the-formation snapshot gives no direction or rate of change in spore size.
  - *The later barinophyte size classes* (*Barinophyton*, *Protobarinophyton*), which are other species from other sites and stages, and *Chaleuria cirrosa*'s classes, which belong to a plant of different affinity.
  - *Dispersed-spore proxies for intrasporangial differentiation:* both size sets fall in the *Apiculiretusispora* type, so a dispersed record can show the size range but not co-occurrence in one sporangium or a common parent.

## Open questions

- Full text: strobilus and sporangium dimensions, numbers of specimens, strobili, sporangia and spores measured per sporangium, whether the gradient follows position along the strobilus, and the evidence for equal spore maturity and for the sculpture.
- The collection locality, horizon (which section and spore zone of Wellman 2018) and facies within the Campbellton Formation, and the basis of "early Emsian".
- Whether spore number per sporangium falls as spore size rises, which would test the first stages of the Stewart and Rothwell sequence.
- The vegetative plant, anatomy and habitat; whether any gametophyte or germinated spore exists.
- Whether *Chaleuria* (in the early to mid Emsian *annulatus-sextantii* assemblages of Wellman 2018; under section IV in Kennedy's Table 4.3) and *Omniastrobus* come from the same horizons, which would fix their relative timing within the formation.
