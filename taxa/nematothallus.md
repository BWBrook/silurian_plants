## In brief

Nematothallus is a form genus for thin, cuticle-like sheets and the stratified tube-built thalli beneath them, common in Late Silurian and earliest Devonian assemblages. It belongs to the nematophytes, the group built around Prototaxites. Cuticles and banded tubes from nematophytes make up a large share of the dispersed organic debris of the time, so they matter for what the Silurian land surface actually carried before vascular plants were abundant. What they were remains contested: lichenised or non-lichenised fungi, marine red algae, or something else.

## Morphology and anatomy

Lang (1937) erected Nematothallus and the class Nematophytales for cuticles with underlying complexes of tubes from the Downtonian; because Prototaxites occurred in the same Pridoli and Lochkovian rocks he treated both as land plants (Edwards et al. 2012). Strother (1988) described three species from the Bloomsburg Formation (Ludlow) as carbonised thalli of flattened tubular elements and showed cuticles and axes of ?Prototaxites attached to Nematothallus. Strother (1993) summarised the Late Silurian and lowermost Devonian record as often dominated by fragments of two kinds: large three-dimensional axes (the Prototaxites group) and smaller thalloid incrustations (Nematothallus).

The best anatomy comes from three-dimensional charcoalified material. In N. williamii (Lochkovian, Welsh Borderland) the thallus has three zones: a superficial cortex that produced the cuticles, a palisade zone of septate parallel tubes presumed to be hyphae, and a basal zone of randomly interwoven hyphae; forms with circular incisions in the cortex or separated thickened cortical cells probably represent a developmental sequence (Edwards et al. 2013). Upper Ludlow charcoal from the Downton Castle Sandstone adds a three-layered Tristratothallus and a type with polysporic asci that Edwards et al. (2018) read as part of a pezizomycete apothecium. Banded tubes, a conspicuous element of Silurian to Early Devonian palynological residues, have homogeneous walls with no preserved ultrastructure and variable thickenings (Taylor & Wellman 2009).

## Age and distribution

Late Silurian to earliest Devonian (see range notes), on Laurentia (Pennsylvania), Baltica (Gotland) and Avalonia (Welsh Borderland). Dating is by spore biozone, not radiometric: the Ditton Group coprolite locality is early Lochkovian on the micrornatus-newportensis zone (Edwards et al. 2012).

## Affinity and controversies

Monophyly has never been demonstrated. Strother (1988, 1993) set the group apart from both embryophytes and algae in an informal group, the Paraphyta.

- Fungus, possibly lichenised. Edwards et al. (2013) argue that stratified organisation and septate tubes point to lichenised fungi, with limited chemistry favouring a fungal over an embryophyte signal, but no photobiont has been found. Edwards & Axe (2012) found cord-like construction in Nematasketum, a close ally of Prototaxites. Honegger et al. (2013) describe true internally stratified lichens from the same region and age, which makes lichen-grade fungi plausible but does not link them to Nematothallus.
- Red alga. For the Gotland cuticle type Smith & Butterfield (2013) erect Nematothallopsis and argue it was an extinct coralline-like rhodophyte: close-packed palisade filaments under a cuticle with a pseudocellular inner pattern, and apertures ringed by multicellular filaments like conceptacles. They question the terrestrial habit of cuticular nematophytes in general. Edwards et al. (2013) conclude that the stratified organisation sets nematophytes apart from algae and bryophytes.
- Plant-like chemistry. Infrared spectra of Rhynie nematophytes cluster with plants (Loron et al. 2023), whereas Loron et al. (2026) show P. taiti is chemically unlike fungi. These are tests on Rhynie taxa, not on Nematothallus itself.

## Ecology and physiology

Presumed heterotrophs of cryptogamic ground cover. Edwards et al. (2013) say the group enhanced chemical weathering and added to early terrestrial biodiversity. A small number (fewer than 50) of distinctive Lochkovian coprolites, from a locality with many spore-rich ones, contain comminuted Nematasketum tubes with rarer Nematothallus and Cosmochlaina cuticles; the producers were selective feeders, probably millipedes (Edwards et al. 2012). Later coprolites show digested hyphae from fungal mats and a cluster attached under a Nematothallus fragment, implying chitinase-bearing consumers (Edwards et al. 2020).

## Why it matters for modelling

- Land cover and carbon: nematophyte fragments often dominate Late Silurian dispersed assemblages (Strother 1993), so any model of pre-vascular organic input should include a non-embryophyte heterotrophic or lichenised component, not just plant litter.
- Trophic link: documented fungivory by early arthropods is a constraint on detritivore parameters; at the one site studied, nematophyte-feeding left under 50 distinctive coprolites against many spore-rich ones, so it was selective and not the dominant diet in that sample.
- Polyphyly is an input uncertainty: the same cuticle form could represent fungi, algae or lichens with different carbon sources and habitats. Assign them to a functional group, not a taxonomic one.
- Weathering and soil: the claim of enhanced chemical weathering is the only quantitative-style statement and is unmeasured for this genus.

## Open questions

- Which cuticle types belong to land fungi and which to marine algae?
- Is there a photobiont, and are Nematothallus thalli lichens?
- Do banded tubes and cuticles belong to the same organism?
- What are the Ordovician records, and how reliable?
