## In brief

*Palaeostigma* is a morphogenus for Devonian lycopsid-type stem impressions with leaf scars, known from South Africa and Brazil and usually found alongside *Haplostigma* in the Gondwanan flora of high southern palaeolatitudes. Kräusel & Dolianiti (1957) named it, with *P. sewardi* as type species, when these beds were thought to be Lower Devonian; the dated records are Middle Devonian. It is a thin, low-confidence record, useful mainly for two points: the early "South Gondwanan" plant province partly rests on misdated assemblages, and by the Middle Devonian a distinct high-latitude flora with endemic stem taxa existed.

## Morphology and anatomy

I could not obtain the original description or any figure. What I could verify: Andrews's (1970) generic index lists *Palaeostigma sewardi* as a stem impression (Kräusel & Dolianiti 1957, p. 10, pls 1-2). A search-engine summary of Berry et al. (2000) says the authors described stems superficially similar to lycopsids, with randomly arranged leaf scars; I did not see this in the paper. *Haplostigma*, the genus it is paired with, has herbaceous stems up to about 20 mm wide, rarely branched, with enlarged leaf cushions in pseudosigillarioid to sigillarioid arrangement, slender non-persistent leaves and no known reproductive structures (Matsumura et al. 2024). No anatomy, spores or strobili are known for *Palaeostigma*. Species other than *P. sewardi* (*P. gracilis*, *P. robusta*) come from PBDB entries based on Anderson & Anderson (1985), which I did not read.

## Age and distribution

Authority and age need care. The type locality is given as Steytlerville, South Africa, in Andrews (1970) but the 1957 paper is about Piauí, and Matsumura et al. (2024) list *P. sewardi* among plants Kräusel & Dolianiti described from the Pimenteira Formation of the Parnaíba Basin. I could not resolve whether the type is South African or Brazilian. Piauí material is Eifelian at the eastern margin of the basin (Matsumura et al. 2024, citing Grahn et al. 2006); the Pimenteira Formation as a whole spans late Eifelian to early Famennian, with a gap over the late Givetian and early Frasnian. South African records are in the Bokkeveld Group (Waboomberg Formation, entered as Eifelian) and, per PBDB, the Witteberg Group, whose age as entered (Middle Devonian) may be too old. PBDB holds eight occurrences: seven South African (six from Anderson & Anderson 1985 in five collections, one from Oosthuizen 1984) and one Brazilian "Palaeostigma sp." (Melo 1988); stage assignments there are contributors', not evidence.

Matsumura et al. (2024) include *Palaeostigma* in their Spongiophyton-Haplostigma floral stage, late Emsian to early Frasnian, without dating *Palaeostigma* separately. Capel et al. (2023) identify *Haplostigma* usually associated with *Palaeostigma* and *Malanzania*, endemic to a South Gondwanan phytochorion, and explain that the South Gondwanan unit of Raymond et al. (1985), characterised by *Haplostigma* and *Palaeostigma*, arose from dating errors in the South African and Antarctic assemblages, which are now known to be Middle Devonian. In their Middle Devonian analysis *Palaeostigma* contributes 2.91% of the dissimilarity between phytochoria.

## Affinity and controversies

Carbonaro & Ghilardi (2016) list it under Lycopsida; I follow that for want of a better home, on the basis of stems with leaf scars. PBDB classifies the genus in Rhyniaceae through an opinion attributed to Crane et al. (2004), which I did not check; that is hard to reconcile with leaf-scar-bearing stems and looks like an error. Whether *Palaeostigma* is separable from *Haplostigma* is unresolved in the sources I read; both are impression taxa defined by leaf cushions or scars, and Capel et al. (2023) treat them as separate taxa. The Goiás record carries a question mark (Carbonaro & Ghilardi 2016). A search-engine summary says specimens labelled *Palaeostigma* have sometimes been reassigned to *Spongiophyton* in later work; I did not verify this.

## Ecology and physiology

No direct information. The beds are marine to marginal marine with transported plant debris, at high southern palaeolatitudes; the Pimenteira marine fauna mixes Malvinokaffric, Old World and Eastern American elements (Matsumura et al. 2024). *Haplostigma* is read as herbaceous; no habit can be assigned to *Palaeostigma*.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **F4 provinciality.** A South Gondwanan phytochorion with endemic stem taxa (*Haplostigma*, *Palaeostigma*, *Malanzania*) is detectable by the Middle Devonian (Capel et al. 2023). In the Early Devonian only one reliable South Gondwanan Pragian-Emsian assemblage (Argentina) exists and it shares no taxa with other assemblages (Capel et al. 2023), so Early Devonian provincial structure should be treated as unobserved, not absent.
- **Dating risk.** Records published as Lower Devonian (this genus's own title) were later revised to the Middle Devonian; use stage assignments from recent revisions, and keep first appearances for this taxon at Eifelian unless an Emsian record is demonstrated.
- **High-latitude land plants.** If the lycopsid reading holds, land plants grew at roughly 65 to 80 degrees S (palaeolatitude models disagree by up to 15 degrees) by the Eifelian-Givetian, a constraint for climate-belt and dispersal models.
- **Taxon definitions.** Morphogenera defined on leaf-scar form give weak units for diversity or first-appearance models.

## Open questions

- Read Kräusel & Dolianiti (1957) and Anderson & Anderson (1985): diagnosis, type locality (South Africa or Piauí), species limits and horizons.
- Is *Palaeostigma* distinct from *Haplostigma*, or a synonym?
- Are the Witteberg records Middle or Late Devonian?
- Is there any Emsian or older Gondwanan record?
