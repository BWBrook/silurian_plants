## In brief

The Xitun Formation near Qujing in Yunnan yields a small flora of zosterophylls from the Lochkovian, a stage poorly represented by vascular-plant fossils worldwide (Xue 2009, 2012). Its plants are leafless axes with laterally attached, stalked sporangia and K-type branching, and one uprooted specimen gives the oldest known rooting system and a measured root:shoot ratio (Hao et al. 2010). Because Laurussian and western Gondwanan floras of the same stage are dominated by isotomously branched plants with terminal sporangia, the Xitun flora is the main evidence for a distinct South China province in the Lochkovian.

## Morphology and anatomy

Composition (Xue 2009, 2012; Hao et al. 2010):

- *Xitunia spinitheca*: stalked sporangia in a helical arrangement, flattened, two unequal valves, the larger abaxial valve with long spiny appendages at its distal margin.
- *Zosterophyllum minorstachyum*: K-shaped branching at the base, small terminal spikes of helically arranged sporangia.
- *Zosterophyllum shengfengense*: tufted, aerial axes 98 mm tall, rhizome with root-like axes about 20 mm long; root:shoot 0.028, root-like axes about 3% of biomass (see [*Zosterophyllum*](zosterophyllum.md)).
- Gen. nov. A: naked axes, K-type branching with small laterals, lateral stalked sporangia.
- aff. *Huia* sp.: ovate stalked sporangia in loose spirals, reflexed adaxially; and one unnamed spike.

That is five or six taxa. No anatomy or in situ spores are reported in the abstracts I could read, and the full text of Xue (2012) was not available.

## Age and distribution

**Age.** Lochkovian, by vertebrate biostratigraphy and position; see range notes. The vertebrate fauna includes placoderms, sarcopterygians such as *Youngolepis* and acanthodian scales (Dupret & Zhu 2008; Cui et al. 2022; Li et al. 2021).

**Setting.** Qujing lies on the South China block, which Xue et al. (2018) describe as paleogeographically isolated and at low latitude; Xue (2012) calls the plant province "Northwest Gondwanan" where Cascales-Minana et al. (2018) say north-eastern Gondwanan, so the label is not stable. No latitude with a stated plate model was found; the Posongchong value (about 0.6 degrees S to 6 degrees N in PBDB) is only a proxy for the same block. PBDB has no Xitun plant record.

**Taphonomy and sampling.** The abstracts do not state the preservation mode; compression is assumed from the other Yunnan floras. The taxa rest on three papers and, for *Z. shengfengense*, on one whole uprooted specimen. Specimen counts and locality numbers were not given in the abstracts.

## Affinity and controversies

Xue (2012) argues that a zosterophyll-dominated Lochkovian South China, set against rhyniophytoid-dominated Laurussia and western Gondwana, documents phytogeographic isolation. The sampling behind that contrast is a handful of taxa against the much larger Welsh Borderland and Rhynie datasets, so province differences are partly sampling differences (my inference). The abstracts do not discuss relationships among the zosterophyll genera; see the Zosterophyllum card for the grade.

## Ecology and physiology

*Z. shengfengense* was probably semi-aquatic with efficient water use or strong uptake by the root-like axes (Hao et al. 2010). Nothing else is reported on physiology.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Belowground allocation.** One measured value: root:shoot 0.028, roots about 3% of biomass, aerial height about 10 cm (Hao et al. 2010). A model with a single parameter for this stage rests on one specimen.
- **Province-specific sampling.** Xue (2009, 2012) describe the Lochkovian as poorly represented by vascular-plant fossils; this flora and the Welsh Borderland mesofossils sample different provinces and preservation modes (compressions against charcoal). They should not be pooled without province terms.
- **Timing.** The Lochkovian is 6.6 Myr long on ICS v2024/12 and the fish place the unit late in it; use stage-level timing; the 'late Lochkovian' fish age narrows it only qualitatively.
- **Spore link missing.** With no in situ spores, the flora cannot be tied to the Posongchong and Xujiachong spore records (F4).

## Open questions

Which spores did these plants produce? How many specimens and localities underlie each taxon? Can ash or detrital zircon date the Qujing succession directly? What was the depositional setting, and did the plants grow in the basin or on its margin?
