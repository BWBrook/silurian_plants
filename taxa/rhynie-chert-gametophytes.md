## In brief

The Rhynie chert preserves the haploid generation of several early polysporangiophytes: free-living, leafless, axial plants with a conducting strand, cuticle, stomata and mycorrhizal fungi, bearing antheridia or archegonia on cups, shields or tips. Remy and Remy (1980) described them as the oldest anatomically preserved, apparently independent gametophytes, and no Silurian gametophyte is known (Pšenička et al. 2021). They show that by about 410 Ma the sporophyte was already the larger, independent plant while the gametophyte remained complex and free-living; they cannot show how that balance arose.

## Morphology and anatomy

Lyonophyton was described by Remy and Remy (1980); Langiophyton and Kidstonophyton followed in Remy and Hass's 1991 papers, and Remyophyton in Kerp et al. (2003), who also emended Lyonophyton and Langiophyton to include both sexes (Kerp 2017).

Asteroxylon, Trichopherophyton and Ventarura have no known gametophytes. Gametophytes are unisexual; antheridiophores of Lyonophyton are at least 2 cm tall and 2 mm wide with 10 to 40 antheridia up to 600 µm across (Taylor et al. 2005; Kerp 2017). Langiophyton has a cup 4 to 9 mm wide with up to 30 vascularised projections bearing archegonia.

## Age and distribution

Rhynie chert: hot-spring silica sinter in a half-graben on the Old Red Sandstone continent (Laurussia), about 25 to 28° S (Wellman 2017; Edwards et al. 2017). U-Pb on the underlying Milton of Noth Andesite gives 411.5 ± 1.3 Ma (Parry et al. 2011); 40Ar/39Ar on hydrothermal adularia gives 407.6 ± 2.2 Ma (Mark et al. 2013), favoured by Edwards et al. (2017). On the project timescale: Pragian and earliest Emsian. Plant-bearing cherts formed in low-temperature distal sinter-apron pools (Channing 2017).

## Affinity and controversies

The assignments are not demonstrated by organic continuity. Earlier readings of Rhynia axes, hemispherical projections and cut stomata as gametangia were refuted when Rhynia sporangia were found (Kerp 2017). Remy (1982) noted that the gametophytes suggest a closer relationship between bryophytes and tracheophytes than the sporophytes alone. Sciadophyton and Calyculiphyton compressions of the Early Devonian are interpreted as similar gametophytes (Remy et al. 1993; Taylor et al. 2005).

The life-cycle question is open and partly semantic. Kerp (2017) calls the generations isomorphic although the gametophyte is much smaller, with smaller cells and stomata. Kenrick and Crane (1991) argued that isomorphic alternation in protracheophytes and Rhyniaceae implies the gametophytes of extant pteridophytes are reduced. Remy et al. (1993) read the data as support for the homologous theory "to some extent" but also for the antithetic theory; Gerrienne and Gonez (2010) conclude that all fossil evidence supports the antithetic theory, with the gametophyte:sporophyte size and complexity ratio falling gradually along the land-plant tree. Gerrienne et al. (2006) hypothesised from one Cooksonia paranensis specimen that eutracheophytes are heteromorphic and sporophyte-dominant while the Rhyniopsida are isomorphic. Niklas and Kutschera (2009) instead call the oldest land plants, including Rhynia and Cooksonia, dimorphic. Ligrone et al. (2012) reconstruct the last common ancestor of land plants as an axial gametophyte bearing a simple single-sporangium sporophyte.

## Ecology and physiology

Gametophytes were free-living, apparently photosynthetic (cuticle, stomata) and mycorrhizal (arbuscules of Glomites in Lyonophyton); spores germinated on wet microbial mats; the sporophytes also cloned vegetatively, so sexual and clonal routes coexisted (Taylor et al. 2005). Sperm were flagellate and released in packets, so fertilisation needed a water film.

## Why it matters for modelling

- Linear size ratio gametophyte:sporophyte of about 0.1 in the best-matched pairs (derived from the figures above), with both generations autotrophic, stomatal and mycorrhizal.
- Dioicous gametophytes in dense stands: over 200 individuals in 1.5 × 1.5 cm (about 90 per cm², derived); of the gametangiophores identified, 27 were archegoniate and at least 69 antheridiate (Taylor et al. 2005), a count that need not equal the sex ratio.
- Sex was set by spore, sporangium or sporophyte; Taylor et al. (2005) favoured spore-level (anispory) but no spore size difference was found. About 4 × 10^5 spores per Aglaophyton sporangium, shed as aggregates, keep both sexes together.
- Post-fertilisation development (embryo to sporophyte) is essentially unobserved, so it remains a free parameter.
- Boyce (2008) argues the smallest Cooksonia axes depended on a gametophyte, so sporophyte independence was established somewhere between such axes and Rhynie-sized plants (an inference).
- Homeodomain genes regulate the haploid-to-diploid transition in chlorophyte algae and fungi and are implicated in land-plant alternation of generations (Bowman et al. 2016).

## Open questions

Who is the Langiophyton and Kidstonophyton partner outright? Do two gametophyte-sporophyte junctions in Remyophyton show embryos? What were gametophyte lifespans? Are Remyophyton sex sizes as Kerp (2017) or Taylor et al. (2005) report? Are Silurian gametophytes preserved?
