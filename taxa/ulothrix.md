## In brief

*Ulothrix* is an unbranched green filament conventionally described as a haplont: haploid filaments make zoospores and gametes, and the only diploid cell is the zygote, which undergoes meiosis. It is a chlorophyte, far from the streptophyte lineage of land plants, so it is a theory exemplar for the haplontic starting point, not an ancestor. The sources read do not support a single account: in marine *Ulothrix* the zygote settles as a unicellular stalked Codiolum stage, read as a sporophyte by Berger-Perrot (1993) and, on cytological tests made only in *Monostroma angicava*, by Horinouchi et al. (2019), and the Ulotrichales life cycle is described as controversial. I could not access a primary cytological demonstration of zygotic meiosis in *U. zonata*, so the classical haplontic account is carried here on general statements about freshwater greens, not on a source read for this genus.

## Morphology and anatomy

Unbranched filaments (Leliaert et al. 2012; Niklas and Tiffney 2023). In *U. zonata*, asexual reproduction is by quadriflagellate zoospores that disrupt the cells on release, so the filament disintegrates (Graham et al. 1985b). Hypnospores form by cleavages like those of zoosporogenesis, with 2 to 4 large or up to 32 smaller per cell; they have thick walls, stay in the parent cell and germinate into new filaments (Bullock 1978). In *U. flacca* var. *roscoffensis*, karyogamy follows gamete fusion by 8 to 24 h, flagella are shed about 8 h after fertilisation, and the quiescent zygotes become stalked sporophytes (Codiolum stages) some two days after wall formation begins (Berger-Perrot et al. 1993).

## Age and distribution

Living, in northern lakes (*U. zonata*) and, for marine species, the upper intertidal (Holzinger and Karsten 2013). See the range note for the Devonian Ulotrichales-type filaments reported by Martín-Closas (2003). Ulotrichales are sister to the Ulvales (Hou et al. 2022; see [Ulva](ulva.md)).

## Affinity and controversies

Leliaert et al. (2012) note that the Ulotrichales were separated from the Ulvales by life history, a diploid small thick-walled unicell on a stalk (Codiolum) against isomorphic multicellular alternation, and that molecular phylogenies give poor or no support for the split. So *Ulothrix* and *Ulva* are close relatives, and the contrast between haplontic and isomorphic cycles in them is a within-clade contrast (or an artefact of how the Codiolum stage is read). Horinouchi et al. (2019) state that it is unclear whether the Codiolum phase is a zygote or a sporophyte; in *Monostroma angicava* it has a single polyploid nucleus from endoreduplication, grows very large, makes numerous zoospores, and behaves as a sporophyte, so the cycle there is a heteromorphic haplo-diplontic one, and they suggest this is widespread in Ulotrichales. Most freshwater greens are haplontic with a dormant single-celled zygote (Leliaert et al. 2012); the classical account of freshwater *Ulothrix* fits that pattern, but I did not verify it. Whether the genus is natural, and whether freshwater and marine species share a cycle, is not settled here (my inference).

## Ecology and physiology

*U. zonata* favours cold, bright water. Net photosynthesis peaks at 5 °C and 1100 µE m-2 s-1 (16.8 mg O2 g-1 h-1), the optimum irradiance falls to 125 µE at 30 °C, and above 10 °C the energy balance turns unfavourable (Graham et al. 1985a). Zoospore formation is favoured near 20 °C, at 520 µE and under short or long days, and is minimal at 5 °C, 32.5 µE and 12:12 h, which explains the loss of biomass once water passes 10 °C (Graham et al. 1985b). Marine green macroalgae including *Ulothrix* lack polyols and use sucrose, proline, glycine betaine or DMSP, and the upper-intertidal belts are dried daily (Holzinger and Karsten 2013).

## Why it matters for modelling

What it is good for. (1) The pure haplont baseline: a multicellular haploid with a one-celled diploid phase, which Coelho and Umen (2021) define as the haplontic cycle (resting zygote undergoing meiosis to give haploid progeny) and which Kenrick (2018) gives as the cycle of the algal ancestors of land plants; interpolation theory is a change away from it. In the modifier models reviewed by Scott and Rescan (2017), haplonty is favoured when deleterious mutations are partially dominant or haploids are fitter. (2) Environment-cued switching between vegetative growth and asexual propagules: zoospore formation in *U. zonata* rises with temperature (5 to 20 °C) and light, and is favoured by both short and long days over a 12:12 cycle, and biomass falls above 10 °C, a template for a condition-dependent switch of mode (the sources read give no equivalent cues for gamete formation). (3) The Codiolum problem shows that "diploid zygote" and "one-celled sporophyte" overlap: if a growing, endoreduplicating, zoospore-producing diploid cell counts as a sporophyte, a minimal form of interpolation exists inside the Ulvophyceae. Homeodomain gene pairs regulate the haploid-to-diploid transition in chlorophyte algae (Bowman et al. 2016).

What it is not good for. Gametes swim in open water, the zygote is not retained or provisioned, there is no sporopollenin spore wall, and the terrestrial constraints on fertilisation and dispersal are absent. The classical haplont label may be wrong for marine species, so check the species before using *Ulothrix* as a pure haplont.

## Open questions

Is the zygote of freshwater *U. zonata* an undivided meiocyte, as the classical account has it? Do marine *Ulothrix* species share the Codiolum cycle of other Ulotrichales? Is *Ulothrix* monophyletic? Do the Devonian Ulotrichales-type filaments belong to the crown group?
