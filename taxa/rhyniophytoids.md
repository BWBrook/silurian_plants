## In brief

"Rhyniophytoid" names a morphology, not a clade: a few centimetres of naked, dichotomising axis ending in one sporangium, with trilete spores where spores are known and anatomy where it is preserved at all. These plants dominate Wenlock to Lochkovian assemblages outside the tropics, from the Sheinwoodian *Cooksonia* of Bohemia to the Lochkovian of the Welsh Borderland. Cladistic work places some in the stem group of tracheophytes and some in the lycophyte stem, so the label records ignorance about vascular tissue. See [Cooksonia](cooksonia.md) and [Aberlemnia caledonica](aberlemnia-caledonica.md).

## Morphology and anatomy

The shared plan is determinate, isotomous branching with terminal sporangia and no leaves. Edwards (1970) described *Steganotheca striata* as a bushy plant at least 5 cm high with at least ten sporangia at the tips of 0.5-0.8 mm axes. Edwards and Richardson (1974) gave *Salopella allenii* at least 24 mm high, with fusiform sporangia to 9 x 2 mm and laevigate trilete spores of 23-37 um, perhaps immature. Edwards (1979) left the type species *Tortilicaulis transwalliensis* as incertae sedis: unbranched, twisted axes 0.1-0.4 mm wide under sporangia of 1.1-3.9 mm, with no spores recovered, and both bryophyte and vascular readings possible. Later material showed isotomous branching and trilete spores in that species, and Morris et al. (2024) report stomata and tracheids with annular thickenings in five scanned specimens of the smaller charcoalified *T. offaeus* (axes 0.06-0.14 mm), so *T. offaeus* is vascular on their reading while the type species still has no anatomy (see [Tortilicaulis offaeus](tortilicaulis-offaeus.md)). Sporangium shape and spore type, not vegetative form, separate the genera; in situ spores, where known, are smooth or apiculate trilete types of the *Ambitisporites* kind (see [Ambitisporites](ambitisporites.md)). The generic list in Pšenička et al. (2021) is *Cooksonia*, *Salopella*, *Aberlemnia*, *Hedeia*, *Tortilicaulis*, *Fusiformitheca*, *Steganotheca* and *Caia*, plus sterile *Hostinella*; they call these "usually placed among rhyniophytoids". Capel et al. (2022) separate "basal eutracheophytes" (*Cooksonia*, *Eocooksonia*) from other rhyniophytoids such as *Pertonella*.

## Age and distribution

Wenlock to Pridoli assemblages of the temperate South Laurussian-north-west Gondwanan unit are rhyniophytoid-dominated, as are Lochkovian floras of the Anglo-Welsh Basin, Brazil and South Africa; zosterophylls dominate the tropical Ludlow floras of Bathurst Island (Pšenička et al. 2021; Capel et al. 2023). In the Lochkovian, the equatorial and mid-latitude phytochorion held zosterophylls and lycopsids, while rhyniophytoids and other basal eutracheophytes persisted in a southern Laurussia-west Gondwana phytochorion; they vanish from Laurussia at the end of the Lochkovian (Capel et al. 2023). Macrofossil genus diversity plateaus in the Lochkovian partly because rhyniophytoid occurrences are stratigraphically confined (Capel et al. 2022). Capel et al. (2023) call this rhyniophytoid-dominated stage the Eotracheophytic flora, followed by a zosterophyll-dominated flora that spread from the tropics. Dispersed-spore evidence from the Pridoli is read as rhyniophyte-dominated in the tropical and cool belts (Bek et al. 2024), but spore genera such as *Retusotriletes* are shared among producers.

## Affinity and controversies

Banks (1968) divided the "psilophytes" into three subdivisions, Rhyniophytina, Zosterophyllophytina and Trimerophytina. Rhyniophytina held plants with terminal sporangia, in the families Rhyniaceae (fusiform sporangia) and Cooksoniaceae (globose) (as summarised by Edwards 1970). Edwards and Edwards (1986) introduced "rhyniophytoid" for rhyniophyte-like plants lacking evidence of vascular tissue (I saw the term only through secondary citations, not the chapter). In Kenrick and Crane's (1997) analyses *Cooksonia* and *Renalia* fell in a large basal polysporangiophyte polytomy and were read as possible stem-lineage zosterophylls (Gensel and Berry 2001; see [Renalia](renalia-hueberi.md)). *Rhynia* with *Stockmansella*, *Huvenia* and *Sennicaulis* have S-type tracheids and form the narrower "rhyniopsids" or paratracheophytes (Gerrienne et al. 2006, via Ligrone et al. 2012; see [Rhynia](rhynia-gwynne-vaughanii.md)). Edwards and Kenrick (2015) state that *Cooksonia* is not monophyletic, its species sitting in the stem groups of the vascular plant and lycophyte clades. Gonez and Gerrienne (2010) restricted *Cooksonia*, and a companion paper moved *C. caledonica* to *Aberlemnia*. Pšenička et al. (2021) list *Aberlemnia* with the rhyniophytoids and also, for its bivalved sporangia opening along the distal margin, among Silurian basal lycophytes; the Aberlemnia card follows Liu et al. (2024) in placing it among the rhyniopsids. Terms now in use, narrowest last: "rhyniophyte" (Banks, any naked plant with terminal sporangia, including *Rhynia* and *Horneophyton*); "rhyniophytoid" (the same form without anatomy); "basal eutracheophyte" (*Cooksonia* sensu stricto and relatives, defined only by tracheophyte affinity; Capel et al. 2022); and "rhyniopsid" (the S-type clade). Cooksonioids often mean the *Cooksonia*-like subset. Whether Silurian rhyniophytoids are stem-tracheophytes is untested for most: Hooper et al. (2026) treat *Cooksonia* as a stem-tracheophyte and date crown tracheophytes to 432-428 Ma, at the first *Cooksonia*.

## Ecology and physiology

Edwards and Richardson (2004) proposed a ruderal strategy: life cycles completed in humid spells, with spores persisting through unfavourable ones (as summarised by Pšenička et al. 2021). Latest Pridoli-earliest Lochkovian Raglan Mudstone rhyniophytes occupied areas with temporarily raised water tables in ephemeral dryland rivers, whereas zosterophylls tracked the wetter, more stable St Maughans Formation (Morris et al. 2012). Boyce (2008) argued that the narrowest axes were too slender for photosynthetic autonomy; Libertín et al. (2018) disagreed for the 1-1.5 mm axes of *C. barrandei*.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Size.** 1-10 cm, axes 0.1-2.5 mm, sporangia 1-9 mm; one sporangium per apex, so fecundity scales with branching order (at least ten sporangia for *Steganotheca*).
- **Autonomy.** Axis width, not taxon, is the state variable for whether the sporophyte could feed itself, as in the Cooksonia card.
- **Niche.** Small, short-cycle plants of disturbed, intermittently wet ground at cool or temperate latitudes; the Welsh Borderland pattern implies loss where zosterophylls occupied stable wet ground (inference).
- **Timeline.** About 20 Myr of macrofossil record (Sheinwoodian to Lochkovian, 432.9 to 413.0 Ma on data/timescale.csv), with displacement in Laurussia by the end of the Lochkovian.
- **Bias.** Genera are form taxa. Treat the group as a morphotype pool with unknown tracheid status for most members.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast small branching systems with one terminal sporangium per apex and competing hypotheses of sporophyte autonomy, using member-specific dimensions within an anatomically heterogeneous morphotype pool.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Steganotheca striata, Capel Horeb material: at least 5 cm preserved height, at least ten terminal sporangia and 0.5-0.8 mm axes constrain a minimum fertile architecture (Edwards 1970).
  - Salopella allenii, Welsh Borderland material: sporangia to 9 x 2 mm and trilete spores of 23-37 um constrain reproductive dimensions; retain the possibility of immature spores (Edwards and Richardson 1974).
  - Cooksonia barrandei, Barrandian material: 1-1.5 mm axes provide a width case for testing photosynthetic autonomy, without coding autonomy as directly observed (Libertín et al. 2018).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not code unpreserved conducting tissue as absent or assign one hydraulic system to the grade; most members lack anatomical evidence.
  - Do not use fragment lengths or the aggregate height range as measured whole-plant stature.
  - Do not assign observed Silurian gametophyte dimensions, attachment or matrotrophy from this group: no Silurian gametophyte is known.
  - Do not model rhyniophytoids as one lineage with shared physiological parameters; the grouping combines form taxa with unresolved conducting-tissue status.

## Open questions

Which members are tracheophytes; whether the type species of *Tortilicaulis*, unlike *T. offaeus*, is vascular; how many Silurian lineages the pool hides; how long rhyniophytoids survived in the highest-latitude Gondwanan refugia.
