## In brief

Hornwort sporophytes come closer to the tracheophyte condition than those of other bryophytes in several respects: an unbranched cylinder that is photosynthetic, bears stomata, and grows from a persistent basal meristem, shedding spores progressively along its length, though still nourished by the gametophyte. *Anthoceros*, with *A. agrestis*, *A. punctatus* and *A. angustus* as the genome-sequenced species, is the type genus and the experimental model. Hornworts matter for F1 (sporophyte takeover) because their position in the tree decides whether the first embryophytes had a simple capsule or a more elaborate sporophyte, and because their stomata show what a stomata-bearing sporophyte does without active control.

## Morphology and anatomy

The gametophyte is a thalloid rosette with ventral rhizoids, mucilage canals and *Nostoc* cavities; *A. agrestis* is monoecious with embedded antheridia and archegonia. The sporophyte lacks a seta, has a foot and placenta, and splits into two valves below the apex; sporogenous tissue is produced continuously, so all stages of spore development are present along it (Frangedakis et al. 2021). Cells carry one or a few chloroplasts, with a pyrenoid in many species. About 220 species in 11 genera are recognised there (12 genera in Villarreal & Renner 2012). The three *Anthoceros* genomes are small: 116.9-132.8 Mb assembled, 24,700-25,800 protein-coding genes, no whole-genome duplication and a limited transcription-factor set (Li et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2020 sequenced *A. angustus*).

## Age and distribution

No fossil in the programme's window. Villarreal & Renner (2012) infer a hornwort crown of 303.8 Ma, with *Anthoceros* s.l. at 101.3 Ma and other genera 8.8-62.2 Ma; most extant diversity is therefore young. The paper also says the earliest reliable hornwort fossils are Late Cretaceous, which sits uneasily with its 118.56 Ma calibration. Setaphyta (liverworts plus mosses) date to 419-447 Ma (Bechteler et al. 2023) and crown bryophytes to 445-429 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026).

## Affinity and controversies

Genome analyses place hornworts sister to liverworts plus mosses with high support (Li et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2020), and bryophyte monophyly is the best-supported hypothesis (Su et al. 2021; Harris et al. 2020, 2022). Puttick et al. (2018), as summarised in `docs/primer.md`, reject all but three rooted hypotheses, which differ only in where hornworts sit. If bryophytes are monophyletic, bryophytes and tracheophytes are both highly derived from a more complex ancestor that had stomata and vasculature genes, and the stomata of liverworts were lost (Harris et al. 2020, 2022). Hooper et al. (2026) infer that stem hornworts gained cellular elaters and a persistent spore-mother-cell wall and lost the tapetum, from sporogenesis data on only three hornwort taxa.

## Ecology and physiology

Stomata open once, then stay open as the guard cells collapse and die; their role is sporophyte drying and spore release (Renzaglia et al. 2017). More than 9,000 stomata showed almost no response to ABA, darkness, desiccation or plasmolysis, unlike tracheophyte controls (Pressel et al. 2018), and density and aperture did not change between 440 and 1500 ppm CO2 in *A. punctatus* and *Phaeoceros laevis* (Field et al. 2015). Pyrenoids were gained five or six times, the oldest about 100 Ma, and lost as often, with no clear tie to low CO2 (Villarreal & Renner 2012). Cyanobionts in the thallus grow and fix CO2 more slowly but fix more N2 and release much of it to the host (Adams & Duggan 2008). Li et al. (2020) describe the sporophyte as long-lived and moderately independent of the gametophyte. In culture, *A. agrestis* germinates in about 7 days at 23 degrees C, needs 2-3 months to form a thallus competent to reproduce, produces antheridia about a month after a drop to 16 degrees C and sporophytes a month after water is added, giving 5 to over 100 sporophytes per thallus (Szovenyi et al. 2015); the whole spore-to-spore cycle can be completed within 3 months (Frangedakis et al. 2021).

## Why it matters for modelling

- **F1, the sporophyte.** A living, photosynthetic, stomata-bearing, matrotrophic sporophyte with a persistent basal meristem. If bryophytes are monophyletic, the starting sporophyte in a simulation could already be photosynthetic and stomatal, with simple capsules as the reduced state.
- **Gas exchange.** Stomata fixed open and serving drying, not regulation, are the right null for the earliest stomata-bearing sporophytes (compare the eophyte stomata in [Partitatheca](partitatheca.md)). Field et al. (2015) argue against stomatal-density CO2 proxies for such plants.
- **Time scales.** A lower bound on one generation under optimal laboratory conditions is about 3 months, with liquid water needed for fertilisation.
- **Carbon-concentrating mechanism.** Pyrenoids arose long after the Palaeozoic, and pyrenoidless *Leiosporoceros* is sister to the rest in Villarreal & Renner (2012), though ambiguous in Bechteler et al. (2023); do not give early embryophytes a CCM by analogy.
- **Nitrogen.** The Nostoc symbiosis is a candidate N source for thalloid plants; no flux per unit area was found. A cyanobacterial association is reported from Rhynie *Aglaophyton* (as relayed by Frangedakis et al. 2021).
- **Limits.** The crown age is 304 Ma and no Palaeozoic stem fossil is known, so it is an analogue for one stage of the F1 sequence, not for the tracheophyte route. Living sporophytes are unbranched.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast a photosynthetic but gametophyte-nourished sporophyte and drying-oriented stomata with greater sporophyte autonomy and active water control, using living Anthoceros physiology and culture observations.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Anthoceros sporophytes, living material with species and site unspecified: represent persistent basal growth, progressive spore shedding and continued gametophyte nourishment (Frangedakis et al. 2021).
  - A. punctatus, experimental material with site unspecified: represent unchanged stomatal density and aperture between 440 and 1500 ppm CO2 under the reported experiment (Field et al. 2015).
  - A. agrestis in culture: use germination in about 7 days at 23 degrees C and 2-3 months to reproductive thallus competence as conditional laboratory timings (Szovenyi et al. 2015).
  - A. agrestis cultured thalli under the reported temperature and watering sequence: use 5 to over 100 sporophytes per thallus as experimental reproductive output (Szovenyi et al. 2015).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not assign tracheophyte-like active stomatal regulation to these hornwort sporophytes; stomata open once and then remain open as guard cells collapse.
  - Do not give early embryophytes a pyrenoid-based carbon-concentrating mechanism by analogy with living hornworts; this card places pyrenoid origins much later.
  - Do not treat the living Anthoceros body plan as an observed Ordovician–Early Devonian ancestral condition; no Palaeozoic stem fossil is known.
  - Do not use the reported generation-time lower bound as a field generation time or a universal early-land-plant rate; it applies to optimal laboratory conditions.

## Open questions

Sporophyte growth rate, spore output per sporophyte and its fraction of carbon from its own photosynthesis; spore aperture and size; whether any Silurian or Devonian fossil belongs on the hornwort stem; how the hornwort root position changes the F1 starting conditions; and why stomata were lost twice.
