## In brief

The Zygnematophyceae, the conjugating green algae, are the closest living algal relatives of land plants. They are haplontic: vegetative cells are haploid, sex is by conjugation of non-motile gametes, and the only diploid stage is a thick-walled zygospore that undergoes meiosis on germination. They have no flagellated stages, plasmodesmata or multicellular diploid phase, and they may descend from a more complex ancestor (Hess et al. 2022), so they are poor guides to the ancestor's body but good ones to stress physiology and zygospore biology. For F1 their value is negative as well as positive: they show how much of the terrestrial toolkit pre-dates embryophytes, yet the sources read describe no provisioning of the zygote by a parent thallus.

## Morphology and anatomy

Unicellular and colonial forms (the desmids) and unbranched filaments (*Spirogyra*, *Zygnema*, *Mougeotia*). Five orders: Serritaeniales (*Mesotaenium*), Zygnematales, Spirogyrales, Spirogloeales (*Spirogloea*) and Desmidiales (Hess et al. 2022). Genomes vary hugely and do not track morphology: *Mesotaenium endlicherianum* 163 Mb and *Spirogloea muscicola* 174 Mb (Cheng et al. 2019), *Zygnema circumcarinatum* as little as 67 Mb, a congener about 360 Mb and some *Zygnema* above 2.7 Gb, *Penium* an estimated 4.7 Gb (Bierenbroodspot et al. 2024, summarising Jiao et al. 2020 and Feng et al. 2024). Cheng et al. (2019) report that GRAS and PYR/PYL/RCAR genes, which raise stress resistance in land plants, originated or expanded in the common ancestor of the clade and embryophytes and were gained by horizontal transfer from soil bacteria. Feng et al. (2024) find expanded signalling genes in *Zygnema* and cell-wall enzyme families that they date to more than 700 Ma. Over 80% of the transcription factors of the land-plant ancestor were already present in earlier algae (Bierenbroodspot et al. 2024).

## Age and distribution

Living, worldwide. Fossil record and divergence are in the range note: Carboniferous zygospores, an unexamined Devonian chert claim, and a split from embryophytes at least 449.65 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026). Bierenbroodspot et al. (2026, preprint abstract) report a deep genetic structure with an ancient class and a deep split between Spirogyrales and Desmidiales; Žárský et al. (2022) argue, as a hypothesis, that the split from embryophytes and the loss of flagella happened in Cryogenian glacial habitats.

## Affinity and controversies

Earlier trees put the Charales or Coleochaetales next to land plants on the strength of body complexity; genome-scale trees put the Zygnematophyceae there (Wickett et al. 2014; Puttick et al. 2018; One Thousand Plant Transcriptomes Initiative 2019; Cheng et al. 2019). Bierenbroodspot et al. (2024) report an unpublished study suggesting that the Spirogloeales, not the whole class, may be the closest sister. Internal branches are short, so the node is not free of doubt (repo primer). Hess et al. (2022) infer at least five origins of filaments and, as summarised by Bierenbroodspot et al. (2024), a possibly secondarily unicellular ancestor, so the clade's simplicity should not be read as ancestral. Hooper et al. (2026) cite a current consensus that Cambrian cryptospores came from non-embryophyte streptophyte algae, possibly close relatives; the sources read do not tie any spore type to this class.

## Ecology and physiology

Terrestrial lineages tolerate drying in the vegetative state; freshwater classes rely more on dormant spores (Holzinger and Pichrtová 2016). *Zygnema* pre-akinetes carry Arctic and Antarctic populations through desiccation, freezing and overwintering, where zygospores are rare; old cultures survived desiccation at 84% relative humidity, and only slowly acclimated cells survived rapid drying at 10% (as summarised by Holzinger and Pichrtová 2016). In *Z. circumcarinatum*, tolerance rose with culture age, and drying induced ROS scavenging, chaperones and aquaporins (Rippin et al. 2017). Glacier forms (*Mesotaenium*, *Ancylonema*) persist only as vegetative cells (Holzinger and Karsten 2013). The zygospore wall has a cellulosic endospore and exospore around an aromatic mesospore, helicoidal cellulose in *Spirogyra*, and ripe zygospores fill with lipid droplets; a sporopollenin-like material is proposed, which differs in chemistry and pathway from true sporopollenin (Permann and Holzinger 2024; Holzinger and Pichrtová 2016). In the desmid *Closterium*, meiosis begins in the greening zygospore and two non-sister nuclei abort (Kasai and Ichimura 1983); this is one genus, not the class.

## Why it matters for modelling

(1) Haplontic baseline in the sister group: the diploid phase is a durable single cell, and the land-plant ancestor probably had a haploid-only multicellular generation (Kenrick 2018), so interpolation starts from zygote-plus-meiosis. (2) Fertilisation by conjugation needs contact, not swimming sperm; as the class lacks flagella, land-plant flagellate sperm and water-film dependence came from earlier ancestors or were lost here, which the sources do not decide. (3) Desiccation tolerance, UV and cold tolerance and cell-wall chemistry of haploid vegetative cells were probably largely in place, with stress hubs more than 600 Myr old (Dadras et al. 2023), so a model need not make gametophyte drought tolerance a new innovation (inference); the innovations lie in protecting the diploid and its spores. (4) In *Zygnema* from harsh habitats zygospores are rare and clonal pre-akinetes carry populations, so the realised sexual fraction is a free parameter. (5) Transfer of resistant wall chemistry from zygospore to meiospore after meiosis is read as a key step (Permann and Holzinger 2024), linking to spore-based dating in F3 (Hooper et al. 2026, ancestral monoplastidic meiosis and trilete tetrads).

## Open questions

Is Spirogloeales the closest sister? Was the common ancestor flagellate, multicellular and matrotrophic? Do zygospore walls contain true sporopollenin? How old is the class and which Palaeozoic microfossils belong to it? What sexual fraction occurs in terrestrial populations?
