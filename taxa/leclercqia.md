## In brief

Leclercqia is a herbaceous lycopsid with dissected, five-tipped microphylls, sessile leaf-borne sporangia and a ligule, found on every major palaeocontinent by the late Middle Devonian. Grierson & Bonamo (1979) found a ligule on L. complexa, the earliest ligulate lycopsid, on a plant that is homosporous, against the idea that ligule and heterospory go together. Wellman et al. (2009) conclude from spore-wall ultrastructure that the ligule appeared before heterospory. It is a small herbaceous lineage that outlived the arrival of trees, an anchor for what the height race left behind.

## Morphology and anatomy

Axes dichotomise at right angles and carry spirally arranged laminar leaves divided into five segments: a long, often recurved central one and two pairs of lateral segments that may divide again (Bonamo et al. 1988; Gensel & Kasper 2005, abstract via a search summary). Sunken stomata occur on leaves, sporophylls and sporangia. Eusporangiate sporangia sit sessile on the adaxial surface of fertile leaves, possibly with a tapetum, and held well over 1,000 spores each (Bonamo et al. 1988). The ligule sits on the leaf far from its attachment to the stem, unlike any other ligulate lycopsid, yet its structure matches living ligules (Grierson & Bonamo 1979). Anatomy comes from pyrite petrifactions, with exarch and mesarch protoxylem and bordered-pitted metaxylem (Grierson 1976). Species differ in leaf form: L. andrewsii bends all five segments upward (as relayed), L. uncinata has seven tips with a distally hooked central segment (Xu et al. 2011). Without leaves, Leclercqia, Protolepidodendron and Haskinsia are nearly identical (Bonamo et al. 1988), so bare axes cannot be counted as occurrences.

## Age and distribution

The type is from the Panther Mountain Formation (Brown Mountain, New York), probably middle to late Givetian by spores (Streel 1972). Emsian L. andrewsii and L. complexa occur at Campbellton (Wellman et al. 2009; Kennedy et al. 2012); the older Wyoming material is discussed in the range notes. Queensland chert (Eifelian to Early Givetian) is the best record outside Laurussia (Meyer-Berthaud et al. 2003); single stems come from Venezuela (Berry 1994), and species from Xinjiang, Yunnan, Inner Mongolia and Washington State (Xu et al. 2011, 2020; Bai et al. 2021; Benca et al. 2014). Xu et al. (2020) reconstruct origin in Laurasia in the Early Devonian, a cosmopolitan range on every palaeocontinent and climatic zone by the late Middle Devonian, and extinction in the early Late Devonian. The project's PBDB extract has 11 occurrences, 8 in the United States.

## Affinity and controversies

Leclercqia sits in the Protolepidodendrales; its spores add a character, interradial multilamellate regions, shared with other ligulate lycopsids, within which heterosporous lycopods form a clade (Wellman et al. 2009). Species limits are fuzzy: Benca et al. (2014) found by morphometrics that L. complexa, L. andrewsii and a Washington morphotype separate about as living Lycopodium and Spinulum species do, and the New Brunswick L. complexa differs from the New York form in size and fine spore detail and lacks a preserved ligule (Gensel & Albright 2006, as relayed). Spore names conflict: Streel (1972) described the New York dispersed spore matched to the in situ one as Aneurospora cf. heterodonta (53-92 um); later work uses Acinosporites lindlarensis. I could not check whether this was reconciled.

## Ecology and physiology

Homosporous; gametophytes are unknown. Xu et al. (2011) found fertile appendages of an iridopteridalean trapped between the hooked leaves of L. uncinata and suggest a possible climbing habit; Benca et al. (2014) imagine prickly, ground-covering, scrambling mats. A PBDB comment on a Gilboa record quotes Norton et al. (1988) that mats of axes trapped small arthropods. Gilboa is now late Givetian, not the Eifelian PBDB gives.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Ligule before heterospory.** Homosporous ligulate plants at about 385 Ma, with heterospory arising later in ligulate lycopsids (Wellman et al. 2009), fix an order of innovations; the lignophyte [Archaeopteris](archaeopteris.md) was heterosporous by the Givetian, in a separate lineage.
- **Fecundity.** Over 1,000 spores of 53-92 um per sporangium, in leaf-borne sessile sporangia, give a spore-output term for small homosporous lycopsids.
- **Persistence.** A herbaceous lineage spans 28-47 Myr (Emsian or Lochkovian base to Frasnian top in data/timescale.csv; my arithmetic) through the rise of 8-30 m trees; lycopsid diversity falls in the Frasnian (Capel et al. 2022). Use it to test survival of small plants under canopy competition.
- **Spore biogeography (F4).** Acinosporites lindlarensis tracks the genus and was used to reconstruct its range (Xu et al. 2020); a cosmopolitan 53-92 um spore is a dispersal-kernel test, with Laurussia-Gondwana proximity argued from the plant (Meyer-Berthaud et al. 2003).
- **Record (F3).** The oldest fossils precede the first ligule by about 25 Myr, so the ligule date is a preservation limit, not an origin date (Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024).

## Open questions

Does the Wyoming material have a ligule and a published description? Can spore taxonomy be reconciled between New York and New Brunswick? Is the genus monophyletic? What were stem diameters, height and rooting?
