## In brief

*Pertica* is the trimerophyte-grade genus with the clearest division of labour between one upright main axis and helically arranged, repeatedly dichotomising lateral branch systems. It is known only from compressions, from about 1 m (*P. quadrifaria*) to perhaps 3 m (*P. varia*) tall. It realises the "overtopping" step of Zimmermann's telome scheme without planation or webbing, so it marks the architectural precondition for megaphylls but not a leaf.

## Morphology and anatomy

*P. quadrifaria* was erect, perhaps a metre tall, with a pseudomonopodial main axis. Side branches were arranged in a clockwise spiral from base to apex, forming four vertical ranks (tetrastichous); they dichotomised many times, with intervals between dichotomies shortening distally. Ultimate branchlets bore dense clusters of sporangia, while other side branches were sterile (Kasper & Andrews 1972). *P. dalhousii* has a central axis with spirally arranged, mostly dichotomous laterals, some ending in erect clusters of 32-128 fusiform sporangia; its spores are circular, trilete, with a detachable sculptured outer layer (Doran et al. 1978). *P. varia* is reported as taller, with fewer sporangia per cluster (secondary compilations; Granoff et al. 1976 not read).

Observed: external form, branch order and sporangial clusters. Inferred: whole-plant height, since stems are fragmentary, and the pseudomonopodial interpretation, which rests on branch geometry, not on preserved apices. Anatomy is unknown, as are axis diameters, stomata and rooting in the sources I read (Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024).

## Age and distribution

Laurentia only. The type species is Emsian-Eifelian (Trout Valley Formation; Allen & Gastaldo 2006). *P. dalhousii* is "late Lower or early Middle Devonian" (Doran et al. 1978). The genus first appears in the Emsian, later than the earliest *Psilophyton* (Pragian), and no occurrence has a radiometric date.

## Affinity and controversies

Kasper & Andrews (1972) placed *Pertica* in Banks's Trimerophytina; the group is now a paraphyletic grade (see the *Psilophyton* card). Doran et al. (1978) read the main axis versus lateral branch organisation as an early stage towards megaphyllous leaves. Tomescu & Whitewoods (2024) are more cautious: without objective criteria "leaf precursor" is a moving target, and the only leaf-defining character shared by all earliest euphyllophytes with precursor structures is regular arrangement of laterals, which *Pertica* has. Its laterals branch in three dimensions, are not planar, and determinacy is doubtful (scored as absent with a query), so it shows regular taxis alone. Cladograms placing *Pertica* below seed plants (cited in secondary summaries) were not accessible.

## Ecology and physiology

Trout Valley plants occupied coastal-estuarine settings; *Pertica* occurs in fluvial and tidal facies alongside *Psilophyton*, *Taeniocrada*, *Drepanophycus* and *Kaulangiophyton* (Allen & Gastaldo 2006). The autochthonous upright axes found at a new Trout Valley road exposure are cf. *P. forbesii* (Gastaldo 2016), and *Pertica* there is parautochthonous or allochthonous. Homosporous with trilete spores; the gametophyte is unknown.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Overtopping versus planation.** Niklas & Kerchner (1984) show by simulation that overtopped (main axis with laterals) and planated (frondlike) branching each raise photosynthetic efficiency and its ratio to bending moment. *Pertica* instantiates the first; the second does not appear in the sources until the Givetian, when euphyllophyte leaves arise, roughly 25 Myr after lycophyte leaves at about 410 Ma (Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024). A geometric advantage alone therefore cannot explain a 10-25 Myr delay between Emsian architectures and Givetian leaves; developmental (marginal meristem: Boyce & Knoll 2002) and physiological (heat-limited blades under exceptionally high CO2: Osborne et al. 2004) constraints are the usual candidates. The latter rests on the canonical view of CO2 about an order of magnitude above pre-industrial, whereas Dahl et al. (2022) estimate 525-715 ppm for 410-380 Ma. Megaphylls evolved independently several times (Galtier 2010).
- **Architecture parameters.** Four ranks, helical phyllotaxis, distal shortening of dichotomy intervals, 32-128 sporangia per cluster: usable L-system inputs.
- **Height without wood.** A 1-3 m plant with no known secondary tissue is a stringent test of hydraulic and mechanical limits; *Wilhowia* shows 1 cm axes standing without wood (Gensel 2022), but *Pertica* axis diameters are needed.
- **Stand structure.** No in-situ stands exist, so canopy height and density cannot be taken from this genus.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast overtopped, three-dimensional branching with planate leaf-bearing architecture, using differentiated main axes and fertile laterals with inferred stature and no known anatomy.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Branch geometry: P. quadrifaria compressions from the Trout Valley Formation, Maine, show a clockwise spiral of side branches forming four vertical ranks; use this species-specific arrangement (Kasper & Andrews 1972).
  - Fertile-unit construction: P. dalhousii material from the Dalhousie area, New Brunswick, bears clusters of 32-128 fusiform sporangia on dichotomous laterals (Doran et al. 1978).
  - Spore morphology: P. dalhousii from the Dalhousie area has circular trilete spores with a detachable sculptured outer layer; retain this species-specific reproductive state (Doran et al. 1978).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Measured standing-height priors cannot use the genus-wide range: P. quadrifaria stature is inferred from fragments, while the taller P. varia estimate comes from unchecked secondary compilations (Kasper & Andrews 1972; Granoff et al. 1976, not read).
  - Hydraulic conductance, wood absence and mechanical strength cannot be assigned from compression architecture: Pertica anatomy and axis diameters are unknown (Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024).
  - Planate leaves and determinate leaf growth cannot be transferred to Pertica: its laterals branch in three dimensions and determinacy is doubtful (Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024).
  - Stand density and in situ habitat cannot use Trout Valley Pertica occurrences: the upright axes are cf. P. forbesii, whereas Pertica is transported or locally displaced (Gastaldo 2016; Allen & Gastaldo 2006).

## Open questions

- True monopodial or overtopped dichotomy: are apices preserved in any specimen?
- Axis diameters and any anatomy; one permineralised *Pertica* would transform its placement.
- *P. varia* locality, unit, age and measured height.
- Whether the Trout Valley beds are Emsian or Eifelian at the *Pertica* horizons.
