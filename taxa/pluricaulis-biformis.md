## In brief

Pluricaulis biformis is a manuscript name. Tims (1980) gave it, in her unpublished University of Melbourne PhD thesis, to a [zosterophyll](zosterophylls.md) from Limestone Road, Yea, and it has not been formally published since, as far as I could establish. It therefore has no valid authorship, no designated type and no standing as a taxon. It is kept as a card to stop it entering datasets or diversity counts as a species, and to record that the Lower Plant Assemblage at Yea contains at least one zosterophyll that has never been formally described.

## Morphology and anatomy

Nothing is available to me. I could not obtain Tims's thesis, and no accessible paper describes or figures the plant. Tims & Chambers (1984) list for Limestone Road 'at least one zosterophyll (report in preparation)' and 'two other probable zosterophylls'; I infer, without confirmation, that P. biformis is among these, and that the report never appeared. Limestone Road material is preserved as fine compressions and impressions with no cellular structure or spores (Tims & Chambers 1984; McSweeney et al. 2021). The specimens presumably sit in the Museums Victoria collection, but I found no catalogue numbers.

## Age and distribution

One locality: Brackley's Cutting on Limestone Road, 2 km south-east of Yea. It is the Lower Plant Assemblage locality that Garratt (1978) and Garratt & Rickards (1984) argued to be Ludlow, and that Earp (2024) and McSweeney et al. (2021) treat as Early Devonian; see [the Baragwanathia floras](baragwanathia-flora.md). The site is heritage-listed and further collecting is forbidden (McSweeney et al. 2021), so the 1970s collections by Tims and Douglas in Museums Victoria are the only source of material.

## Affinity and controversies

**Nomenclatural status.** McSweeney, Shimeta & Buckeridge (2020, 2022) did not publish it, as the following shows.

- The 2020 paper (Alcheringa 44: 379-396) erects two taxa, *Parazosterophyllum timsiae* (from Ghin Ghin Road, Yea) and *Gippslandites minutus* (from the Boola Formation at Tyers), per its abstract. Neither is *P. biformis*. *G. minutus* is based on Tims's manuscript name *Zosterophyllum minutum*; the authors credit the epithet to her manuscript name in the etymology but publish the genus and species as their own, which is a new taxon, not a validation of Tims's name.
- The 2022 paper (Memoirs of Museum Victoria 81: 25-41) states that Tims (1980) 'described and gave informal manuscript names' to three Victorian zosterophylls, *Zosterophyllum minutum*, *Pluricaulis biformis* and *Chamaecaulon tylosus*, and says only that the first was later redescribed as *Gippslandites minutus*. It does not treat the other two.
- McSweeney's PhD thesis (RMIT, deposited 24 November 2024) lists *P. biformis*, *C. tylosus* and the rhyniophyte *Yarravicaulis aggregatus* as informal manuscript names that 'need to be formally described'. Tims's other manuscript names have fared differently: *Hedeia gracilis* proved to be *Yarravia oblonga* ([Yarravia](yarravia.md)), and *Baragwanathia subulifolia* is also still unpublished.
- Searches of Crossref and the web on 27 October 2026 found no later paper using the name.

**Code.** Under the Shenzhen Code (Turland et al. 2018), Art. 30.9, an independent non-serial thesis is not effectively published unless it carries an explicit statement of intent to publish effectively or other internal evidence, such as an ISBN or a named publisher, printer or distributor (I read the online Article 30; I have not seen the thesis, so I cannot say whether it carries such a statement, though McSweeney et al. call it unpublished). 'Nomen nudum' strictly describes a published name without a valid description; here the failure is at effective publication, not description, because Tims described the plant. I use the phrase loosely, as the shorthand for an unavailable name. In practice anyone who describes the plant may choose a new genus and epithet without regard to Tims's.

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing is known beyond the depositional setting.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Do not use it as a datum.** It has no traits, no measurements and no valid name. In first-appearance or diversity tallies it counts at most as 'zosterophyll indet., Lower Plant Assemblage'.
- **Weight on the dating dispute.** If the Lower Plant Assemblage is Early Devonian, a zosterophyll at Limestone Road is unremarkable. If it is Gorstian, it would be among the oldest Gondwanan zosterophylls, though late Ludlow (Ludfordian) zosterophylls are already known from Bathurst Island (Kotyk et al. 2002; see [Zosterophyllum](zosterophyllum.md)).
- **Observation model.** It exists only in an unpublished thesis, so literature-based databases cannot contain it: the PBDB extract in this repository has no record of it, as expected.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast an unpublished manuscript occurrence with formally described fossil records, using the Limestone Road material to test taxonomic availability and dating uncertainty rather than biological mechanisms.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Occurrence coding: retain the Limestone Road record as an indeterminate zosterophyll, without equating an identified specimen to Pluricaulis biformis; its link to the reported material remains unconfirmed (Tims & Chambers 1984; McSweeney et al. 2022).
  - Preservation filter: Limestone Road compressions lack cellular structure and spores; code these traits as unobservable rather than absent in material attributed to the manuscript taxon (Tims & Chambers 1984; McSweeney et al. 2021).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Named-species richness and first appearances cannot count Pluricaulis biformis as a formally established species: the name remains unpublished and unavailable (McSweeney et al. 2022; McSweeney 2024).
  - Cauline sporangia cannot be assigned from the zosterophyll label; they are a group expectation without an observation of Pluricaulis biformis (McSweeney et al. 2022).
  - The locality's axes up to 400 mm long cannot parameterise Pluricaulis biformis stature: the measurement is assemblage-level, with no taxon-specific description available (Tims & Chambers 1984).
  - A fixed Gorstian or precisely bracketed Early Devonian occurrence cannot be assigned: this card's interval is a revision-based window rather than a measured taxon range (Earp 2024; McSweeney et al. 2021).

## Open questions

Where are Tims's specimens, and under what registration numbers? Is *P. biformis* distinct from *Parazosterophyllum timsiae* (Ghin Ghin Road) or from the other Yea zosterophylls? Does Tims's thesis state an intent of effective publication? Will the formal description McSweeney (2024) calls for be done, and under what name?
