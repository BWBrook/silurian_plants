## In brief

Wattieza is the crown of the Gilboa tree: a leafless, tree-fern-like cladoxylopsid at least 8 m tall, with a trunk bearing large branches shed as frond-like modules, standing on the swollen, root-fringed base long known as Eospermatopteris (Stein et al. 2007). Eospermatopteris dominated the Gilboa forest (Berry 2019) and also occurs in the older Cairo forest (about 385 Ma). It is the oldest plant I found documented above the 5 m forest-tree threshold, built on a large primary body and shallow roots, a different solution from the wood and deep roots of [Archaeopteris](archaeopteris.md).

## Morphology and anatomy

The trunk is cylindrical and tapering, with a flat-bottomed base carrying many small anchoring roots; its branches stand in longitudinal ranks and were probably abscised (Stein et al. 2007). A 6 m trunk section has a 47 cm base and a scarred top; with an isolated crown it allowed the whole plant to be reconstructed (Berry 2019). Gilboa casts show longitudinal carbonaceous strands, coalified cauline xylem, arranged in one cylinder in Eospermatopteris and two in the Frasnian cladoxylopsid Xinicaulis, round a pith, linked to medullary strands and with adventitious roots from their flanks (Xu et al. 2017). Trunk width comes from a large primary body and pith with little secondary xylem in the model of Meyer-Berthaud et al. (2010). Xinicaulis (70 cm trunk; about 130 strands inferred; 17 growth increments around one strand) grew by diffuse secondary growth in parenchyma and secondary wood round each strand, with a hollow pith, a mode that "seems indeterminate" (Xu et al. 2017). Pietzschia, in contrast, reached up to 160 mm apparently by primary growth alone. Which mode Wattieza used is not settled.

## Age and distribution

Calamophyton from Lindlar (mid-Eifelian) is the oldest assemblage of tree-formed plants, with complete trunks 0.8-2.0 m long (Giesen & Berry 2013; Berry 2019). Cladoxylopsid-dominated forests then run from the Eifelian to the early Late Devonian (Berry 2019). Eospermatopteris casts occur at Cairo, Gilboa and Manorkill Falls (Stein et al. 2020); W. givetiana is a single small Belgian fragment and W. casasii a more robust Venezuelan species (Berry 2000, abstract via a search summary). Gilboa Riverside Quarry is late Givetian, about 2-3 Myr younger than Cairo (Berry 2019; Stein et al. 2020); the 6 m trunk and crown that fix the genus come from nearby South Mountain, early Frasnian (Berry 2019). The project's PBDB extract (485-383 Ma) has two Wattieza occurrences (Venezuela, Belgium) and ten Eospermatopteris collections, all in the United States; it is not a count of the genus.

## Affinity and controversies

Berry (2000) attributed Wattieza to the Cladoxylopsida; with Calamophyton, Lorophyton and Pseudosporochnus it forms the Pseudosporochnales (Berry & Fairon-Demaret 2002). Cladoxylopsids are several distinct lineages and the relationships of all Middle Devonian trees are uncertain (Stein et al. 2020). Goldring's 1924 title called the Gilboa stumps a forest of seed ferns (as cited by Stein et al. 2020); the 2007 crown showed a cladoxylopsid. Whether every Eospermatopteris cast is Wattieza rests on a few specimens plus equivalence of bases and roots. The "oldest forest" label moved from Gilboa to Cairo in 2020. Gilboa was once read as a wetland-restricted forest; Eospermatopteris also grows on the seasonally dry Cairo vertisol (Stein et al. 2020).

## Ecology and physiology

At Gilboa Eospermatopteris formed a mixed-age stand on a gleyed coastal-plain soil, probably short-lived and periodically disturbed; aneurophytalean rhizomes up to 15 cm wide ran between the trees and probably climbed into them, with one arborescent lycopsid (Stein et al. 2012; Berry 2019). Stein et al. (2020) call the tree 'weedy', relatively fast-growing and able to colonise gaps (inference). Modular branches would have made abundant litter, supporting a detritus-based arthropod fauna and carbon accumulation (Stein et al. 2007, hedged). The heavy investment in living tissue may have made these trees vulnerable to drought and cold (Meyer-Berthaud et al. 2010, hypothesis). Mintz et al. (2010) mapped woody-plant stump casts in Manorkill Formation palaeosols, with single- and multi-generation stands on aquic and seasonally well-drained soils (taxon not given in the abstract).

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Height waypoints.** Calamophyton 0.8-2.0 m (Eifelian); Pseudosporochnus about 3 m, largest trunks 13 cm wide; Wattieza at least 8 m; bases to 1 m. The FAO forest criterion cited by Berry (2019) is trees over 5 m with canopy cover over 10%. Calamophyton to Wattieza is a fourfold gain in about 9 Myr, from mid-Eifelian to early Frasnian (my arithmetic on these maxima; the 8 m plant is Frasnian-dated, the Givetian bases imply similar trees).
- **Buckling check.** Meyer-Berthaud & Decombeix (2009) quote critical height proportional to diameter^(2/3). A 130-800-fold height gain from [Cooksonia](cooksonia.md) then needs a 1,500-23,000-fold diameter gain in a solid uniform column, against an observed 130-5,000-fold (0.2-1.5 mm to 20-100 cm); a test for hollow or parenchymatous designs (my arithmetic, not a finding).
- **Roots.** 0.7-1 cm roots, radius 1-2 m, depth under 30 cm, probably replaced as the tree grew; against 6-16 cm roots to 11 m for Archaeopteris (Stein et al. 2020).
- **Contrasting strategies.** Cell walls fill 85% of Xinicaulis wood and 31% of Callixylon, so a model needs separate support, conduction and carbon-cost terms by clade.
- **Decline.** Cladoxylopsid diversity falls in the Frasnian (Capel et al. 2022).

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast tall, leafless trees supported by a broad primary body and shallow roots with woody, deeply rooted architectures, using the reconstructed Wattieza–Eospermatopteris whole plant.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Minimum reconstructed stature for the South Mountain Wattieza–Eospermatopteris whole plant, species unspecified: at least 8 m, reconstructed from a 6 m trunk section and crown (Stein et al. 2007; Berry 2019).
  - Shallow rooting geometry in New York Eospermatopteris bases attributed to Wattieza: roots 0.7-1 cm wide, radiating 1-2 m and penetrating to 30 cm (Stein et al. 2020).
  - Conducting-strand presence in Gilboa Eospermatopteris casts: coalified cauline xylem occurs with little cellular anatomy; encode strand presence without hydraulic coefficients (Xu et al. 2017).
  - Architecture of the reconstructed Gilboa Wattieza–Eospermatopteris tree: an unbranched trunk bears digitate, three-dimensional lateral branch systems in longitudinal ranks; modular shedding is the reported architectural interpretation (Stein et al. 2007).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - The 85% cell-wall fraction in Xinicaulis wood cannot set Wattieza support costs or hydraulic properties: it belongs to another cladoxylopsid.
  - A numerical growth or gap-colonisation rate cannot be derived from the description of Eospermatopteris as weedy and relatively fast-growing; Stein et al. (2020) offer an inference.
  - A maximum height of about 10 m cannot be used as a verified measurement; it comes from an unchecked press report.
  - Spore characters and reproductive output cannot be parameterised: even the statement that reproduction used spores is attributed to a press summary rather than verified reproductive material.

## Open questions
