## In brief

Late Ludlow beds on Bathurst Island, Arctic Canada, contain ten fertile plants of seven taxa, six of them zosterophylls, with spikes and sporangial clusters much larger and more elaborate than the mm-scale axes of contemporary rhyniophytoids (Kotyk et al. 2002). The authors argue this is the best evidence for substantial morphological complexity in the Silurian. Preserved lengths are modest (at most 8.1 cm), so the claim concerns fertile structure and complexity, not measured stature. The flora anchors the oldest firmly dated *Zosterophyllum* and is used in clock calibration.

## Morphology and anatomy

All determinations are from compressions and impressions (Kotyk et al. 2002):

| Taxon | Specimens | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| cf. *Bathurstia* | 3 | two-rowed spikes up to 45 x 10-11 mm on a 5 mm axis; no stalks seen |
| [*Zosterophyllum*](zosterophyllum.md) sp. | 1 | 1.0 mm axis, at least 27 reniform sporangia on stalks 1-3 mm |
| aff. *Zosterophyllum* sp. A | 1 | 2.1 mm axis, four isotomies, discoid sporangia on minute stalks |
| aff. *Zosterophyllum* sp. B | 1 | 1.9-2.4 mm axis, four terminal stalked sporangia |
| *Distichophytum* sp. | 2 | spikes 8-10 mm long, 10-14 sporangia |
| *Macivera gracilis* | 1 (holotype) | 8.1 cm, isotomous, apical clusters of 2-10 sessile sporangia, new genus |
| Rhyniophytoid | 1 | two terminal sporangia 3.0-3.2 mm across; larger than nearly all *Cooksonia* |

Sterile *Hostinella* axes about 1.2 mm wide are numerous. No cuticles, tracheids or in situ spores are reported.

## Age and distribution

**Age.** Biostratigraphic; see range notes.

**Setting.** A fine-grained flysch in a syntectonic basin, in the lower member of the Bathurst Island beds, about 100 m below the lowest large olistostrome at US383 (Kotyk et al. 2002; Kotyk 1998). Plant-bearing strata coincide with deformation, when rising highlands shed sediment into a marine basin. Palaeolatitude is low on Laurussia (Kotyk et al. 2002, citing Scotese 2001); PBDB's models give about +7 degrees (gplates) or +3 degrees (Scotese). The nearest contemporary plants, in North Greenland, were naked axes and the simple *Salopella*.

**Taphonomy.** Rapid burial of transported plants in mass-flow events, with low infaunal activity and little cleavage. Upstream beds are older, so younger material cannot have been mixed in (Kotyk et al. 2002). Source habitat is unrecorded.

**Sampling.** Five localities (seven site numbers) on one stream. Appendix A of Kotyk (1998) lists about 156 Silurian specimens (US600 alone 99; US688 is almost barren), against about 1,700 specimens from some 137 localities across the island's mainly Pragian flora. Ten fertile specimens were described. Capel et al. (2023) single out the Pragian as the one interval where sampling looks near-complete in their rarefaction analysis and attribute it to a 'monographic effect' of exhaustive collecting on the Bathurst Island marine deposits (Kotyk 1998), which is the same thesis but the younger flora.

## Affinity and controversies

Kotyk et al. (2002) call the Victorian *Baragwanathia* flora less anomalous given Bathurst Island, since both show complexity beyond rhyniophytoid-dominated Laurussian floras; see [*Baragwanathia*](baragwanathia-longifolia.md) for the dispute over that age. Most Silurian taxa here are left under cf. or aff. names. The *Zosterophyllum* specimen is, in the authors' words, perhaps a diminutive form of a Pragian Bathurst Island species, related to *Z. deciduum*, or new, so it stays *Zosterophyllum* sp. Sporangial clustering and reduced stalks are common here, and Raymond (1987) saw clustering as a possible adaptation to dry or dry and cool conditions (cited by Kotyk et al.).

## Ecology and physiology

No physiology is reported. The dry-climate adaptation hypothesis for clustered sporangia (Raymond 1987, cited by Kotyk et al.) is untested.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Calibration weight.** Recent clock work uses a Ludlow *Zosterophyllum* from here as the minimum for crown tracheophytes (see Zosterophyllum card). The fossil is a single incomplete specimen (US384-8137), so the bound rests on one fragment and on invertebrate dates.
- **Age bracket.** Ludfordian (2.3 Myr) to Pridoli (3.1 Myr), 425.0-419.62 Ma; treat the late Ludlow as most likely, not certain. A 420.7 Ma minimum is within the Pridoli, but I have not seen how it was derived.
- **Observation channel.** Plants reach the record via marine mass flows from a nearby shore, so the filter differs from fluvial or hot-spring deposits: it favours plants growing near the shore that survive transport.
- **Size and complexity.** Vegetative axes (0.7-2.4 mm) are as thin as *Cooksonia* axes (about 0.2-1.5 mm; see its card) and preserved lengths reach 8.1 cm, close to the 1-6 cm of Lang's *Cooksonia*. What differs is fertile structure: spikes up to 45 x 11 mm and at least 27 sporangia on a 1.0 mm axis. Use 'fertile complexity', not stature, as the trait.
- **Spatial heterogeneity.** A complex flora at low latitude on Laurussia while North Greenland holds simple plants implies uneven preservation or occurrence in the Ludlow.

## Open questions

Can spores, palynomorphs or an ash be recovered to date these beds independently? Are further specimens known? Did these plants live inland or along the shore? How are the unnamed taxa related to the Pragian Bathurst Island flora?
