## In brief

Zosterophylls are leafless plants whose bivalved sporangia sit laterally on the axis, in spikes or rows, often on H- or K-branched rhizomes. Banks (1968) erected them as a separate subdivision, and for decades they were the presumed ancestors of the club-moss lycopsids. The latest analysis finds them a grade, with the spiny Sawdoniaceae as sister group of the lycopsids (Claisse et al. 2025). Their late Ludlow fossils are the oldest secure crown-tracheophytes (Hooper et al. 2026). Claisse et al. call them the dominant tracheophytes at the start of the Devonian, though rhyniophytoids dominated cooler Lochkovian floras (Capel et al. 2023). The group offers measured size and life-history spread. Members: [Zosterophyllum](zosterophyllum.md), [Sawdonia ornata](sawdonia-ornata.md), [Renalia hueberi](renalia-hueberi.md) (excluded from the 2025 analysis as incertae sedis).

## Morphology and anatomy

The defining state (Lycophytina) is a laterally attached, bilaterally symmetrical, bivalved sporangium opening along its distal margin (Claisse et al. 2025). The 2025 matrix of 44 taxa (38 zosterophylls) shows the spread: sporangia irregular or in two rows, reniform to globose, in spikes (*Zosterophyllum*, *Bathurstia*) or scattered; naked axes or emergences; circinate tips in the Sawdoniaceae; H- and K-branching in rhizomes in a scattered set of taxa; xylem elliptical in the Sawdoniaceae. Huang et al. (2025) count at least 37 genera. *Zosterophyllum* axes are 0.3-5.1 mm wide and photosynthetic, with each extra millimetre of diameter adding 31 mm2 of surface per 10 mm of axis. In *Z. shengfengense* downward root-like axes of about 20 mm carried only about 3% of biomass (Hao et al. 2010).

## Age and distribution

The oldest assemblage is late Ludlow, Bathurst Island, with ten fertile plants of seven taxa (Kotyk et al. 2002). Claisse et al. (2025) infer an initial radiation by the Gorstian or earlier that produced the main lycophyte clades, little cladogenesis through the Ludfordian and Pridoli (a gap in the record), a burst of genus-level diversification in the roughly 6 Myr from the end of the Gorstian to the Lochkovian, continued branching through the Lochkovian and virtually none after the Pragian. Diversity peaks in the middle and late Pragian, then falls (Cascales-Miñana and Meyer-Berthaud 2015; I saw the abstract only, through a search summary). In genus-level data a late Pragian peak, shared with the barinophytes, is followed by decline through the Emsian and a sharp drop at the base of the Eifelian (Capel et al. 2022). Zosterophylls were the dominant tropical group of the Ludlow, appear later in temperate floras, and are absent from South Gondwana (Pšenička et al. 2021; Capel et al. 2023). South China holds many endemic Lochkovian and Pragian genera (Edwards and Li 2018; Capel et al. 2023).

## Affinity and controversies

Banks (1968, 1975) defined the group by leafless axes with lateral sporangia and exarch xylem, with six genera, and derived lycopsids from it. The sampled sets differ: 7 genera in Gensel (1992), 23 species in Kenrick and Crane (1997), 8 species in Hao and Xue (2013), 19 species in Nibbelink and Tomescu (2022) and 33 genera in Claisse et al. (2025), and the definition stretched as new genera arrived. Gensel (1992) and Kenrick and Crane (1997) put zosterophylls in a grade paraphyletic to the lycopsids, with Zosterophyllopsida a smaller clade of circinate, two-rowed forms; Hao and Xue (2013) recovered a monophyletic class excluding lycopsids (both as summarised by Claisse et al. 2025). Nibbelink and Tomescu (2022) and Claisse et al. (2025) support the grade. In Claisse et al., Lycophytina splits into a small basal clade and a large one, in which the Sawdoniaceae and lycopsids are sister groups and sister to a clade with the barinophytes; *Zosterophyllum myretonianum* sits elsewhere in a basal group, so the genus is polyphyletic. Parsimony and Bayesian trees share only 43.6% of nodes. Exarch xylem maturation, short stalks and thickened dehiscence lines, listed by Kenrick and Crane as lycophyte synapomorphies, are not supported.

## Ecology and physiology

Zosterophylls were rooted herbs of wet floodplains, probably clonal (H- and K-branched rhizomes; inference). Their stable, wetter habitats contrast with the intermittent habitats of rhyniophytoids (Morris et al. 2012). Huang et al. (2025) read the 12-31-fold difference in spike size between two coeval Pragian *Zosterophyllum* as r-like against K-like strategies, an inference from sporangial volume, not measured lifespan.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Size spread.** Whole plants 4.5-12 cm; preserved axes to 44 cm; axes 0.3-12 mm. Preserved axis length of Zosterophyllum species (not all zosterophylls) averages 74, 120 and 87 mm in the Lochkovian, Pragian and Emsian (Huang et al. 2025).
- **Reproductive allocation.** Sporangial volume per spike runs 1-864 mm3 across zosterophyllopsids and scales with axis width (Huang et al. 2025, assuming 0.6 mm sporangial thickness).
- **Belowground.** Root:shoot 0.028 from the single whole-plant specimen; rooting-axis function unproven.
- **Time series.** Cladogenesis is front-loaded (Gorstian to Lochkovian), species richness peaks in the Pragian and collapses by the Eifelian, so a model should reproduce a radiation, a plateau and a decline, with lycopsids and basal euphyllophytes as the proposed replacements (Cascales-Miñana and Meyer-Berthaud 2015).
- **Calibration.** A Ludlow zosterophyll sets crown-Tracheophyta at no younger than 420.7 Ma in Hooper et al. (2026), but they place that node at 432-428 Ma.
- **Caveat.** Zosterophyll "species" are mostly one-site compression taxa; the group has no uniform body plan.

## Open questions

Is the Pridoli gap real; which clade gave the lycopsids; spore type and anatomy for most species; whether barinophyte heterospory arose in the grade; and whether H/K-branching is ancestral for the large clade.
