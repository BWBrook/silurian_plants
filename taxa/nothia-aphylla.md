## In brief

*Nothia aphylla* is a leafless, dichotomising Rhynie chert plant, probably about 15 cm tall, that grew from branching subterranean rhizomes and bore lateral, stalked, two-valved sporangia. Its aerial axes carried stomata on small emergences, but its conducting cells have uniformly thickened walls with no tracheid-type thickenings. That combination of zosterophyll-like fertile organs and hydroid-like water transport makes it the Rhynie plant whose placement most clearly depends on which character is trusted. It is also the best-documented Rhynie plant for clonal growth, rhizome anatomy and fungal colonisation, and its probable gametophyte is *Kidstonophyton*.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed (Kerp 2017, a review of the primary descriptions; El-Saadawy & Lacey 1979 and Kerp et al. 2001 were not available to me). Rhizomes branch repeatedly, are roughly pear-shaped in section and carry a median ventral ridge of unicellular rhizoids, interrupted by transverse incisions about 100 µm wide; upright aerial axes arise from them. The aerial surface has alternating lens-shaped emergences (250-350 µm high) bearing stomata and stomata-free depressions, a double-layered epidermis with very long unthickened giant cells (to 1.6 mm), and strongly thickened short epidermal cells. Stomata are 75-105 × 70-95 µm, not sunken, with six to eight unspecialised neighbours. Sporangia are reniform, borne singly or in clusters on short adaxially recurved stalks, with walls up to five cells thick and transverse two-valved dehiscence. In situ spores reach 65 µm and are *Retusotriletes*.

The conducting strand is the contested feature. In rhizomes it is a core of 10-20 cells, 300-500 µm across, of long fusiform cells with uniformly thickened walls that Kerp (2017) says resemble fibres or hydroids; the aerial strand is poorly preserved. Aberdeen's Rhynie resource adds that the strand is endarch and crescentic to elliptical in section (web resource, not peer reviewed). Heights and diameters in this card (15 cm, 2.5 mm) come only from that resource.

## Age and distribution

Rhynie chert only: silica sinter lenses in the Dryden Flags Formation, Rhynie Basin, Aberdeenshire, deposited in an intermontane basin at about 28° S (Edwards et al. 2017). Spores give a middle Pragian to early Emsian age; the two radiometric ages (411.5 ± 1.3 Ma and 407.6 ± 2.2 Ma) differ for methodological reasons, and the andesite dated by U-Pb underlies the chert, so its link to the hydrothermal system is unproven (Edwards et al. 2017). Nothia has no recorded occurrence outside the chert in the sources read.

## Affinity and controversies

Lyon (1964) separated the plant from *Asteroxylon* (whose supposed fertile axes Kidston and Lang had described) and named it, though without a diagnosis (Aberdeen resource); El-Saadawy & Lacey (1979) supplied the first diagnosis. Edwards (2003) lists it among seven Rhynie sporophytes. Hetherington & Dolan (2017) call it a tentative lycophyte, reproducing a cladogram from Kenrick & Crane (1997) in which it is an early-diverging lycophyte, and point to a literature on its uncertain placement. Berbee & Taylor (2007) say its closest relationships may be with the zosterophylls (citing Krings et al. 2007). The conflict: sporangial position and dehiscence say zosterophyll, but a strand without thickenings is not tracheid-grade by wall anatomy. The aerial strand is poorly preserved, so the absence of thickenings rests mainly on rhizome strands. This card does not resolve it.

## Ecology and physiology

Clonal growth dominated. Daviero-Gomez et al. (2005) describe colonies made largely of clonal individuals, each a rhizome with one ventral rhizoidal ridge and one erect, photosynthetic, fertile shoot, combined with sexual reproduction; Kerp (2017) records up to four stacked rhizome horizons in sandy soil, apparently long-lived and buried alive, and reads this as a sign of some seasonality. Krings et al. (2007) found three fungal endophytes in 250 prostrate axes, with host responses (encasement layers, secondarily thickened walls separating infected tissue), and suggested the plant may have been especially susceptible to colonisation. Fungus no. 3 colonised over 95% of rhizome segments and formed vesicles but no arbuscules (Berbee & Taylor 2007's summary). Edwards et al. (1998) argue high water-use efficiency from Rhynie stomatal complexes in general (abstract), not for Nothia specifically.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Stomata without tracheids.** Nothia has stomata, emergences and probable hydroid-grade conduction. With *Aglaophyton* it separates the origin of stomata from the origin of true tracheids; a trait model should not tie them together.
- **Gas exchange.** Roth-Nebelsick & Konrad (2003), as summarised in docs/precedents.md (abstract level), ranked gas-exchange capacity Aglaophyton < Rhynia < Nothia and inferred high Early Devonian CO2. Stomatal size (75-105 µm long) and the absence of a reported density are the missing inputs.
- **Clonal versus sexual dynamics.** Clonal rhizome colonies plus a free-living gametophyte whose female form is unknown separate persistence from sexual success in F1: a sporophyte clone can persist without recruitment from spores.
- **Rooting without roots.** Bilaterally symmetric rhizoid-bearing sporophyte axes (Hetherington & Dolan 2017) are a rooting module that does not require the root meristem; relevant to nutrient-uptake terms and fungal symbiosis.
- **Fungal associations.** Three endophytes in one host, one of them in over 95% of rhizome segments, with host defence-type responses to infection (Krings et al. 2007; Berbee & Taylor 2007): a calibration point for costs as well as benefits of early fungal partnership.
- **Size.** About 15 cm and 2.5 mm axes (web-resource figures, unverified) are of the same order as *Aglaophyton* (under 20 cm; see its card), so use them only as a Rhynie-scale value.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Separate stomata from tracheid-grade conduction and clonal persistence from sexual recruitment using rhizomatous Rhynie colonies with documented fungal colonisation.

- **Can transfer:**
  - N. aphylla, Rhynie rhizomes: a conducting core of 10-20 cells, 300-500 µm across, has uniformly thickened cells; use strand geometry without assigning tracheid hydraulics (Kerp 2017).
  - N. aphylla, Rhynie aerial axes: stomata are 75-105 × 70-95 µm, unsunken, with six to eight ordinary neighbours; use geometry rather than a measured conductance (Kerp 2017).
  - N. aphylla, Rhynie sandy soils: clonal rhizomes bear a ventral rhizoidal ridge and erect fertile shoot; up to four stacked horizons constrain persistence architecture (Daviero-Gomez et al. 2005; Kerp 2017).
  - N. aphylla, 250 Rhynie prostrate axes: three fungal endophytes and host encasement and tissue-isolating wall responses constrain infection occurrence and defence relationships (Krings et al. 2007).
  - N. aphylla, Rhynie rhizome segments: fungus no. 3 colonised over 95%, producing vesicles without arbuscules; retain this separately reported prevalence (Berbee & Taylor 2007, summarising Krings et al. 2007).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not use 15 cm height and 2.5 mm diameter as verified primary measurements: they come only from an unreviewed web resource.
  - Do not convert high fungal prevalence into nutritional benefit or obligate mutualism; the documented responses include host defences against infection.
  - Do not assign Nothia-specific high water-use efficiency or fit gas exchange from stomatal size alone: the cited efficiency claim is general to Rhynie plants and density is unreported.
  - Do not assume an organically connected or complete Kidstonophyton life cycle: association rests on surface anatomy, and only antheridiate axes are known.
  - Do not assign demonstrated lignification or tracheid properties to the poorly preserved aerial strand: absence of thickenings is established mainly from rhizomes.

## Open questions

- Does the aerial strand show wall thickenings anywhere, and what do cell-wall chemistry and ultrastructure say about lignin?
- Kidstonophyton as the female or other gametophyte stages (only antheridiate axes known).
- Stomatal density per axis and sporangium, needed for gas-exchange models.
- Primary-source measurements of height and diameter, to replace the web-resource figures.
- A fossil-inclusive phylogeny that scores Nothia for conduction and sporangial characters independently.
