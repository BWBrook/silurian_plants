## In brief

*Coleochaete* is a genus of small freshwater green algae (22 recognised species) and the standard algal model for how a land-plant-like zygote could have arisen. The egg is fertilised on the haploid thallus; the zygote is retained, enlarged, provisioned and in some species enclosed by cortical cells that resemble placental transfer cells; its wall contains sporopollenin or a sporopollenin-like polymer; and after meiosis it releases 8 to 32 zoospores. The life cycle is haplontic, with no multicellular diploid phase. Phylogenomics now place the Zygnematophyceae, not the Coleochaetophyceae, as sister to land plants, and the complex discoid thalli are a young group, so *Coleochaete* is an analogy for matrotrophy and zygote investment, not an ancestor or a model of the ancestral body.

## Morphology and anatomy

Thalli range from branched filaments (*C. irregularis*) through pseudoparenchymatous (*C. soluta*) to parenchymatous discs (*C. orbicularis*, *C. scutata*) (Graham 1982), 1 to 2 mm across in living species (Martín-Closas 2003). Cells bear sheathed hairs; cytokinesis uses a phragmoplast and cells are joined by plasmodesmata (Bierenbroodspot et al. 2024). In the rbcL tree there are two primary lineages, one with fully corticated zygotes and one with naked or weakly corticated zygotes (Delwiche et al. 2002). Delwiche et al. (1989) reported lignin-like compounds in vegetative walls of sexually reproducing thalli and sporopollenin in the zygote wall; Holzinger and Pichrtová (2016) note that such early identifications used crude methods and that algal sporopollenin-like polymers (algaenans) differ chemically from true sporopollenin.

## Age and distribution

Living only, in freshwater, mostly on aquatic plants, macrophyte algae or stones; *C. scutata*, *C. nitellarum* and *C. pulvinata* are most common (Bierenbroodspot et al. 2024). There is no confident fossil; see [Parka decipiens](parka-decipiens.md) for the one disputed candidate. The complex-thallus *Coleochaete* group is only 66.5 +/- 17.8 Myr old (Bierenbroodspot et al. 2025), within a streptophyte lineage about a billion years old. As of 2024 the Coleochaetophyceae were the only major streptophyte-algal lineage without a genome.

## Affinity and controversies

The "*Coleochaete* as model" argument (Graham 1984) was made when some authors treated the Coleochaetales as sister to embryophytes (Graham 1993, as summarised by Martín-Closas 2003). Four-gene trees put the Charales next to land plants with the Coleochaetales beyond (Karol et al. 2001; Delwiche et al. 2002). Genome-scale trees put Zygnematophyceae as sister, with Coleochaetophyceae branching earlier than Charophyceae (Wickett et al. 2014; Bierenbroodspot et al. 2024); `docs/primer.md` notes comparable quartet support for a Coleochaetales-Zygnematophyceae grouping in the 1KP analysis. So zygote retention and transfer-cell-like structures either arose independently in the Coleochaetophyceae or were lost in lineages closer to embryophytes; the sources read do not decide. Haig and Wilczek's (2006) statement that the closest algal relatives retain the zygote on the parent predates the Zygnematophyceae result.

The zygote's fate is not settled: its divisions "are not fully understood", read by Allen (1905) as meioses then mitoses, while Hopkins and McBride's (1976) Feulgen data show oospore DNA levels of 2C to 8C (as I read the abstract) and Haig and Wilczek (2006) call them compatible with two replications and three reductive divisions. The resulting 8 to 32-celled body is not considered homologous to the embryophyte sporophyte. Bierenbroodspot et al. (2025) show complex discoid thalli arose recently, which also weakens the view that discs recapitulate an ancestral land-plant body.

## Ecology and physiology

Vegetative cells can become zoospores. Not all eggs become mature zygospores; disintegrating eggs, possibly aborted zygotes, were common in one study cited by Haig and Wilczek (2006). Larger zygospores of *C. pulvinata* gave up to 32 zoospores against 8 for small ones (Oltmanns 1898, as summarised by Haig and Wilczek 2006). Placental transfer-cell-like wall ingrowths occur in the cells covering the zygote in *C. orbicularis*, and their presence across species correlated with zygote-to-plant area ratios (Graham and Wilcox 1983); meiospores sit in callose chamber walls like embryophyte "special walls" (Graham and Taylor 1986). Aeroterrestrial cultures of two species survived air-drying for a week and still produced zoospores, and cells stayed intact and green after drying for up to several months (Graham et al. 2012).

## Why it matters for modelling

Haig (2015) gives the most model-ready account: optimise the number of zygotes per thallus and zoospores per zygote (3 to 5 rounds of division, 8 to 32 zoospores). Zygotic division pays once zygote size passes a threshold, but selection usually favours more zygotes before that threshold; fewer, larger zygotes are favoured by multiple paternity, low fecundity and accessory costs of matrotrophy (cortical cells, unfertilised oogonia). He proposes that unpredictable fertilisation on land raised those accessory costs and so favoured large zygotes that divided to form diploid sporophytes. That is a ready parameter set for the F1 engine: zygote size, clutch size, maternal investment, fertilisation probability and unfertilised-oogonia cost against moisture. The postzygotic body is a spore-producing unit of at most 32 cells, so it tests the amplification mechanism, not sporophyte size or independence; vegetative desiccation tolerance (weeks to months) gives a haploid baseline. Fossil support for a matrotrophic interface exists later: transfer cells in thalloid Lochkovian fossils, possibly eophyte gametophytes (Edwards et al. 2021; see [Eophytes](eophytes.md)). Do not project the 1 to 2 mm discoid body back onto Palaeozoic ancestors.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast maternal zygote investment and reproductive amplification with sporophyte independence, using retained zygotes on haploid thalli without a multicellular diploid generation.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Zygote size and offspring yield covary in C. pulvinata: larger zygospores produced up to 32 zoospores versus 8 from small ones; specimens and sites are unspecified (Oltmanns 1898, as summarised by Haig and Wilczek 2006).
  - Represent transfer-cell-like wall ingrowths as an anatomical interface in zygote-covering cells of C. orbicularis, without assigning a nutrient flux; specimens and sites are unspecified (Graham and Wilcox 1983).
  - Aeroterrestrial cultures of two unnamed Coleochaete species survived air-drying for a week and still produced zoospores; use this as a culture-specific desiccation baseline, with strain and site identities unavailable (Graham et al. 2012).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - The postzygotic cell body cannot be coded as an embryophyte sporophyte: its homology is rejected in this card and its nuclear divisions remain unresolved.
  - A measured maternal nutrient-transfer rate cannot be assigned from wall ingrowths: this card leaves actual transfer unconfirmed.
  - Modern discoid thallus dimensions cannot reconstruct Palaeozoic ancestral bodies: complex discoid thalli arose recently.
  - Green, intact cells after months of drying cannot establish months-long reproductive survival: demonstrated zoospore production follows the week-long treatment.

## Open questions

What is the nuclear cycle of the zygote? Is nutrient transfer across the putative transfer cells demonstrated? Does the corticated-zygote lineage coincide with the young complex-thallus group? What would a *Coleochaete* genome show about zygote-stage expression? Could *Parka* be a Palaeozoic stem coleochaetophyte?
