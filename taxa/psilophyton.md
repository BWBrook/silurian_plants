## In brief

*Psilophyton* is a leafless, upright plant with three-dimensionally branching axes, terminal sporangia and tracheids with bordered pits. It is the best-characterised genus of the "trimerophyte" grade at the base of the euphyllophyte clade (Colston et al. 2023), with at least 14 species from the Pragian to the early Eifelian (Doran & Tomescu 2025). Species reportedly differ in how strongly a main axis dominates the laterals, which makes the genus a natural testbed for the onset of apical dominance without leaves.

## Morphology and anatomy

Dawson's 1859 type material was a composite; Hueber & Banks (1967) emended the genus around connected fertile material. Species come from compressions and from permineralised axes (calcareous pebbles at Gaspé, silicified tuff at Atholville).

In *P. crenulatum* vegetative branches are 2 mm to 8.3 cm long and 2-7 times dichotomous; fertile units end dichotomous axes, divide 5-7 times in three dimensions, hang pendulously and bear sporangia 3-5 x 1-2 mm containing spores of 48-102 µm (Doran 1980). Spines are multicellular, up to 6 mm. Example axes are 3-5 mm wide with growing tips of about 1.5 mm (Doran & Tomescu 2025); the centrarch vascular strand reaches 1 mm. *P. dawsonii* tracheids are P-type, with scalariform to circular bordered, multiaperturate pits (Hartman & Banks 1980). Trant & Gensel (1985) describe five anatomical patterns at branch points and show that axis and stele diameters can be uncorrelated.

Architecture is the contested point. Compilations describe *P. princeps* and *P. forbesii* as having a clearly differentiated main stem with spiral laterals (I could not check the primary descriptions), whereas Tomescu & Whitewoods (2024) score branches of all orders in the genus as irregular in arrangement and radially symmetrical.

## Age and distribution

The Pragian record is *P. primitivum* from the Posongchong Formation, dated by spores to the middle to late Pragian (Cascales-Miñana et al. 2017). *P. crenulatum* at Atholville is earliest Emsian. Other species come from early Emsian Belgium, the Emsian Battery Point Formation at Gaspé, and the Emsian-Eifelian Trout Valley Formation. Stage assignments rest on spores and associated fauna; absolute ages are poorly constrained (see range notes).

## Affinity and controversies

Banks proposed the subdivision Trimerophytina in 1968 (reviewed in Banks 1975) to hold *Psilophyton* and relatives. It is now treated as a paraphyletic grade or "plexus": fossil-inclusive analyses find the seed-free euphyllophyte lineages (psilotopsids, equisetopsids, living ferns) unrelated and separated by leafless extinct lineages (Rothwell & Nixon 2006; Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024). Emsian actinostelic plants with regularly arranged laterals (*Kenrickia*, *Gothanophyton*, *Wilhowia*) belong to the plexus leading to lignophytes and seed plants (Toledo et al. 2021; Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024; Gensel 2022 instead treats *Wilhowia* as a unique lineage fitting no recognised euphyllophyte group), in contrast to *Psilophyton*, whose branches have irregular taxis; whether *Psilophyton*-type architecture is ancestral to theirs is untested. No analysis samples Devonian euphyllophytes evenly, and the monophyly of the 14+ species is untested.

## Ecology and physiology

Trimerophytes were common on moist upper channel bars at Gaspé and apparently germinated and grew rapidly on freshly deposited flood muds (Griffing et al. 2000; an inference from facies). In situ erect axes of cf. *P. forbesii* occur in the Trout Valley Formation (Gastaldo 2016). Plants carried wounds suggestive of animal (perhaps arthropod) feeding, wound periderm, presumed coprolites and zygomycete spores (Banks & Colthart 1993; Banks 1981), cortical fungal bodies to 175 µm (Stubblefield & Banks 1983), and in *P. diakanthon* two spine types and a discontinuous cortical fibre layer (Colston et al. 2023). No roots are known: *P. crenulatum* had rhizomatous parts and 2,090 emergences on 28 axes, 62% on lower sides, with a probable rooting role (Doran & Tomescu 2025). Reproduction was apparently homosporous with trilete spores; the gametophyte is unknown.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Architecture rules.** Branch counts (2-7 dichotomies vegetative, 5-7 fertile), segment lengths (2 mm to 8.3 cm) and double dichotomies are L-system parameters from one species (Doran 1980). The genus spans up to about 25 Myr (Pragian to Eifelian, data/timescale.csv), so avoid a single genus-wide body plan.
- **Apical dominance as a continuous trait.** Species span near-isotomous (*P. crenulatum*) to reportedly main-axis-dominated forms. Only overtopping is observable; hormonal control is not.
- **Hydraulic segmentation.** Xylem constrictions occurred at 4 of 6 anisotomous minor-axis bases and 5 of 7 fertile-to-main-axis junctions, none at isotomous junctions (Niklas & Banks 1985); a vulnerability-segmentation hypothesis, untested. Maximum tracheid diameter rose through the Late Silurian-Early Devonian (rho = 0.696, n = 41; Niklas 1985).
- **Size and support.** No secondary xylem is reported for *Psilophyton*, whereas small coeval euphyllophytes had it (*Franhueberia*, Battery Point Formation: Hoffman & Tomescu 2013; 407-397 Ma plants: Gerrienne et al. 2011, who argue hydraulic rather than mechanical drivers). *Wilhowia* reached 1 cm x 30 cm axes without wood (Gensel 2022). Compilations give *Psilophyton* heights of 30-60 cm.
- **Disturbance niche and herbivory.** Early-successional colonisation of flood muds, arthropod damage and two spine types by about 400 Ma give a defence-cost and herbivory-pressure calibration point; no growth rates exist.
- **Rooting.** Uptake and anchorage used rhizomatous axes and emergences; unequivocal euphyllophyte roots date from the Frasnian (Doran & Tomescu 2025).
- **Shape optimisation.** Niklas & Kerchner (1984) give a photosynthesis versus moment-arm model against which these architectures can be scored.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast leafless branching architectures, hydraulic segmentation and rhizomatous anchorage using species-specific anatomy and fertile units rather than a single genus-wide body plan.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Vegetative architecture: P. crenulatum from Atholville, New Brunswick, has branches 2 mm to 8.3 cm long with 2-7 dichotomies; use these species-specific geometric rules (Doran 1980).
  - Fertile architecture: Atholville P. crenulatum fertile units divide 5-7 times and bear sporangia 3-5 x 1-2 mm containing spores 48-102 µm; preserve the linked measurements (Doran 1980).
  - Xylem segmentation: P. dawsonii has constrictions at 4 of 6 anisotomous minor-axis bases and 5 of 7 fertile junctions, absent at isotomous junctions; this card gives no specimen identifiers (Niklas & Banks 1985).
  - Rhizomatous architecture: Atholville P. crenulatum has 2,090 emergences on 28 axes, 62% on lower sides; use positional asymmetry and rhizomatous parts, retaining rooting function as an interpretation (Doran & Tomescu 2025).
  - Conducting-cell structure: Gaspé P. dawsonii has P-type tracheids with scalariform to circular bordered, multiaperturate pits; use this anatomical state without importing conductance values (Hartman & Banks 1980).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Standing-height priors cannot use P. dapsile and P. forbesii textbook reconstructions as measurements; this card explicitly excludes them pending primary descriptions (Taylor et al. 2009, relayed by Wikipedia).
  - Regular spiral laterals, strong apical dominance and one genus-wide architecture cannot be assumed: species descriptions and the genus-level scoring conflict, and hormonal control is unobserved (Tomescu & Whitewoods 2024).
  - Measured hydraulic vulnerability or conductance cannot be derived directly from P. dawsonii constrictions; vulnerability segmentation is an untested functional hypothesis (Niklas & Banks 1985).
  - True roots, quantified uptake and clonal spread rates cannot be assigned to P. crenulatum emergences: rhizomatous parts are observed, but their rooting role is probable (Doran & Tomescu 2025).
  - Rapid growth and disturbance-recovery rates cannot be calibrated from moist flood-mud occurrences; the ecological interpretation comes from facies and no growth rates exist (Griffing et al. 2000).

## Open questions

- Primary height and axis-diameter ranges per species.
- Whether *P. forbesii* and *P. princeps* have regular lateral arrangement.
- Stomatal density and photosynthetic rate.
- Generic limits of *Psilophyton* and placement relative to the radiatopsid plexus.
