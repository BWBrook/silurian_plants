## In brief

*Marchantia polymorpha* is the model liverwort: a gametophyte-dominant, dioicous colonist with a small matrotrophic sporophyte, a 225.8 Mb genome with no ancient duplications and unusually low regulatory redundancy, and a full set of molecular tools (Bowman et al. 2017; Kohchi et al. 2021). It supplies the best-measured bryophyte life history for F1 (sporophyte takeover): spore output, fertilisation distance, timing in the field, and the genetic basis of sporophyte traits. It is also a derived weed, not an ancestral land plant: it lacks stomata and, unlike related species, arbuscular mycorrhiza.

## Morphology and anatomy

The thallus is a flat, dichotomously branching body 2-10 cm long and 7-20 mm broad (US Forest Service review), with dorsal air chambers and pores, ventral scales, and smooth and pegged rhizoids; stalked antheridiophores (eight lobes) and archegoniophores (nine lobes) elevate the gametangia. The sporophyte has foot, seta and capsule, no apical meristem, and a placental junction through which the gametophyte supplies nutrients and water; the seta elongates only when the capsule is mature, and the capsule holds spore mother cells and elaters at about 32:1 (Bowman et al. 2022). Spores are about 10 micrometres across (preprint of Wu et al.).

## Age and distribution

Cosmopolitan; the three subspecies diverged about 5 Ma (Bowman et al. 2022). The fossil record of Marchantiales starts in the Permo-Triassic (see range note). Setaphyta date to 419-447 Ma (Bechteler et al. 2023) and crown bryophytes to 445-429 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026), so the living species is a young member of an old lineage.

## Affinity and controversies

Bryophyte monophyly makes the liverwort-first view of ancestral traits unreliable: both bryophytes and tracheophytes are derived from a more complex ancestor, and liverworts lost stomata (Harris et al. 2020, 2022). Bowman et al. (2017) argued, whatever the position of liverworts, that the last common ancestor of living land plants probably had a regulatory genome as non-redundant as that of *Marchantia*; Harris et al. (2022) infer an ancestor more complex than any bryophyte, so whether low redundancy is ancestral or derived is open. *M. polymorpha* subsp. *ruderalis* does not form mycorrhizas, whereas related *Marchantia* species do, and the loss is recent (Bowman et al. 2022).

## Ecology and physiology

Spores colonise bare ground by air: Duckett et al. (2024) saw establishment about 100 days after a heathland fire, with first gametangiophores at 186 days, and populations nearly vanished in a severe 2022 drought. Gemmae splash no more than 1.2 m (cited there). Antheridiophores appear 2-3 months before archegoniophores; archegoniophore initiation to spore dispersal takes about 60 days in summer and over twice that in other seasons. An antheridium holds over 200,000-250,000 sperm; fertilisation reached about 100% in females up to 19 m from the nearest male, with sperm carried on surface lipid films (Pressel & Duckett 2019; Duckett et al. 2024). A capsule holds up to 300,000 spores (O'Hanlon 1926, as cited by Bowman et al. 2022), and up to 7 million spores per plant are reported (US Forest Service review). The thallus phase lasts months to years. A survey of 209 accessions from 37 European and Japanese sites found 78 genetically distinct individuals and no structure among European populations (Wu et al. 2025). No gas-exchange or hydraulic measurements were found. Field et al. (2012) used *M. paleacea*, a mycorrhizal species, not *M. polymorpha*.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Fertilisation limit (F1).** In wet heathland populations this species fertilised about 100% of females at up to 19 m, so water-limited fertilisation, Bower's premise (see `docs/framings.md`), need not limit a motile-sperm bryophyte. The result rests on sperm number and film transport (Pressel & Duckett 2019); parameterise sperm kernels from architecture, not taxonomy.
- **Fecundity and generation time.** Up to 3 x 10^5 spores of about 10 micrometres per capsule, against about 4 x 10^5 spores of about 65 micrometres per *Aglaophyton* sporangium ([Aglaophyton](aglaophyton-majus.md)): a roughly 270-fold gap in spore volume (derived) at similar numbers. In the field, about 60 days or more from archegoniophore initiation to spore release, after months to years of thallus growth.
- **Haploid versus diploid selection.** In a preprint, genes with diploid-biased expression show higher diversity and divergence at non-degenerate sites, which the authors read as weaker purifying selection in diploids and not as masking (Shen et al. 2024). It bears on explanations of sporophyte elaboration that invoke masking of deleterious mutations, but it is unreviewed.
- **Gametophyte-supported sporophyte.** The gametophyte elevates the sporophyte on a stalk, so height for dispersal comes from the haploid generation. Dispersal-height rules for F2 should allow that.
- **Sporophyte genetics.** A class II bHLH gene regulates sporopollenin and the secondary walls of capsule wall, elaters and exine, and a class II KNOX-BELL pair controls capsule-wall secondary walls and spore pectin, expressed in the maternal calyptra (Levins et al. 2024; Dierschke et al. 2024). These are candidate ancestral spore-wall mechanisms.
- **Limits.** A weedy, derived, non-mycorrhizal thalloid species is a poor analogue for Silurian cryptospore plants or Rhynie gametophytes ([Rhynie gametophytes](rhynie-chert-gametophytes.md)).

## Open questions

Net photosynthesis, hydraulic and desiccation parameters of the thallus; spore aperture; the time from fertilisation to spore release under controlled conditions; whether Marchantiales fossils exist before the Permian; and whether the reported haploid-diploid selection difference holds up in peer review.
