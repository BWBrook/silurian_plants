## In brief

Aglaophyton majus (originally Rhynia major; the epithet was corrected from "major" under ICN Art. 23.5 in Edwards et al. 2017) is a naked, dichotomising plant under 20 cm tall from the Rhynie chert. Its strand of water-conducting cells has no secondary wall thickenings, so D.S. Edwards (1986) removed it from Rhynia and from the tracheophytes. It is the best-documented "protracheophyte": its sporangia, in situ spores, germinating spores, both sexes of gametophyte (Lyonophyton) and arbuscular-mycorrhiza-like fungi are all known from one site.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed: axes lay on the substrate and rose as aerial branches; when upright axes grew too tall for the strand to support them they bent down, rooted by rhizoids and rose again, giving U-shaped growth (Kerp 2017). The conducting strand has a core of thin-walled cells (18 to 44 µm) and a surrounding zone of thick-walled cells (22 to 50 µm) with double-layered walls and wall vesicles but no helical or annular bars; a sheath of tightly packed parenchyma without intercellular spaces lies outside it, in the phloem position but with no sieve areas (Kerp 2017). D.S. Edwards (1986) compared the strand with the hydroids and leptoids of polytrichaceous mosses. Cuticle is up to 5 µm thick. Sporangia, 12 × 2.5 mm with walls to 0.4 mm and about 400,000 spores each, abscised below the point where the strand ended (Kerp 2017). A dark ring in the cortex marks fungal hyphae and vesicles in the intercellular spaces.

## Age and distribution

Rhynie chert: silica sinter of an alkali-chloride hot-spring system, in a small half-graben on the Old Red Sandstone continent, c. 25 to 28° S (Wellman 2017; Edwards et al. 2017; Channing 2017). Two radiometric ages exist. U-Pb on zircon from the Milton of Noth Andesite (which underlies the chert) gives 411.5 ± 1.3 Ma (Parry et al. 2011); 40Ar/39Ar on hydrothermal adularia gives 407.6 ± 2.2 Ma (recalculated from Mark et al. 2011), favoured by Edwards et al. (2017) as dating the hot-spring activity itself. On ICS v2024/12 bases these are Pragian and earliest Emsian. Aglaophyton's spore, Retusotriletes sp. CW-A, occurs throughout the Rhynie sequence but is not identifiable elsewhere (Wellman 2017).

## Affinity and controversies

D.S. Edwards (1986) concluded that the plant showed "characters intermediate between tracheophytes and some mosses" and assigned it to no higher group. Phylogenetic analyses cited by Kerp (2017) place it nearer vascular plants. Kerp also reports wall vesicles coalescing into a continuous layer like that of Rhynia, which would bring it closer to Rhynia than D.S. Edwards allowed; this needs ultrastructural confirmation. Whether the cells were lignified is untested: walls are presumed lignified (Edwards 2003), and bulk geochemistry of the chert finds lignin monomers that cannot be assigned to a species (Holman et al. 2024).

## Ecology and physiology

Stomata with cutinised channels and substomatal chambers suggest high water-use efficiency, as in other Rhynie plants (Edwards 1998). Arbuscules lie in a meristematic cortical zone that renewed itself to supply new host cells (Remy et al. 1994; Taylor et al. 1995, genus Glomites); Strullu-Derrien et al. (2014) argued that Rhynie endophytes include Mucoromycotina as well as Glomeromycota. Nutrient transfer is inferred, not observed. Reproduction was by spores and by vegetative bulbils; germinating spores sit on wet microbial mats (Taylor et al. 2005). Plants colonised fresh sinter and sand surfaces between flood and sinter pulses, and no climax community had time to form (Wellman 2017).

## Why it matters for modelling

- A sprawling, rhizoid-anchored axis with no true roots, whose hydraulic strand is a small fraction of the stem: height is limited by mechanical support (U-shaped growth), not by conduction alone.
- Fecundity: about 4 × 10^5 spores per sporangium, spore diameter about 65 µm; sporangia abscised, so dispersal was by detached sporangia and spore masses.
- Clonal and sexual routes coexist (bulbils plus a free-living, unisexual gametophyte), making local persistence and dispersal separable parameters.
- Environmental envelope: seasonal flooding, saline and alkaline hot-spring water, CO2 about 3100 ppm and O2 26% in the GEOCARBSULF run quoted by Wellman (2017), plant cover about 40% in active hot-spring areas.
- Stomata present on a plant with unthickened conducting cells separates "stomata" from "tracheids" as evolutionary steps.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast hydroid-like conduction, fungal association and free-living gametophytes within one anatomically preserved Rhynie plant whose conducting strand lacks tracheid wall thickenings.

- **Can transfer:**
  - A. majus, Rhynie chert conducting-strand sections: represent water-conducting cells with double-layered walls and wall vesicles but no helical or annular bars (Kerp 2017).
  - A. majus, Rhynie chert cortex: represent arbuscules occupying a renewing meristematic cortical zone as an observed fungal association (Remy et al. 1994; Taylor et al. 1995).
  - A. majus, Rhynie chert sporangia: use about 400,000 spores per sporangium as a calculation by Wellman and colleagues reported by Kerp (2017), retaining its calculated status.
  - A. majus and its Lyonophyton rhyniense gametophyte, Rhynie chert: represent a free-living, unisexual gametophyte alongside vegetative reproduction (Taylor et al. 2005; Kerp 2017).
  - A. majus, Rhynie chert basal axes: represent arrested apices producing bulbil-like propagules and adventitious axes, predominantly at the prostrate base (Kerp 2017).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not assign demonstrated lignification to A. majus conducting cells; their wall chemistry remains untested.
  - Do not assign a measured fungal nutrient-transfer rate or benefit; fungal structures are preserved, but nutrient transfer is inferred.
  - Do not turn stomatal anatomy into a measured water-use-efficiency parameter; the proposed efficiency is an anatomical interpretation.
  - Do not treat 20 cm as a measured whole-plant maximum; the stature comes from reconstruction.

## Open questions

Wall chemistry and ultrastructure of the strand; whether Aglaophyton is closer to Rhynia; mycorrhizal function; and how widespread the plant was: no definitive macrofossil of a Rhynie taxon has been found outside the hot-spring deposits (Wellman 2017).
