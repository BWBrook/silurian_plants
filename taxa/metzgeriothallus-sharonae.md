## In brief

*Metzgeriothallus sharonae* is a strap-shaped, forking thalloid liverwort preserved in large numbers as carbonaceous compressions in a dark shale lens at Cairo, New York, dated to the Givetian of the Middle Devonian (c. 388 Ma in recent papers). Hernick et al. (2008) called it the earliest liverwort, and later work still treats it as the oldest generally accepted liverwort (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026). It lies just outside this programme's window (the Givetian is younger than the 393.47 Ma Eifelian base) and is included because it is the secure lower bound on liverwort megafossils, set against molecular clocks that place the bryophyte crown in the Silurian or earlier.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed in macerated and matrix specimens: a thallus built of a dichotomously branching multistratose midrib, which the preprint version of Tremblay & Mercadal (2026) describes as 2-3 layers of elongate cells, with unistratose wings; fragments preserve at most 2-3 cm of one thallus; small branches arise from the midribs of mature thalli (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026; Mercadal et al. 2026). Scattered dark cells in the wings were first read as possible storage cells (Hernick et al. 2008, as cited by Tremblay & Mercadal 2026). Tremblay & Mercadal (2026) found their frequency and spatial distribution comparable to oil-body cells of extant liverworts and saw evidence of oil-body membranes within them, and argue that the cells are homologous. Mercadal et al. (2026) show a regular spacing and an excess of isolated cells (idioblasts) that fit lateral inhibition: in 55 analysed fossil sections the dark cells were separated by 1-3 cells (mean about 2.7), with regular "salt-and-pepper" patterns at dark-cell frequencies of at least 0.19 in the most regular category (sections of 400-900 cells each). An associated sporophyte capsule is reported in the abstract of Hernick et al. (2008) as relayed to me; I did not see it described.

## Age and distribution

Single locality: Cairo Quarry, Greene County, New York, in a 25 m wide lens of dark-grey shale and mudstone (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026), in the Catskill Delta deposits (Labandeira et al. 2014). The age rests on the authors' Givetian assignment; the sources I read give no biozone, formation or radiometric date, and the quoted "c. 388 Ma" is a rounded figure at the very base of the stage. The Carboniferous *M. metzgerioides* shows the genus is long-ranging, and the Late Devonian *Pallaviciniites devonicus* (Hueber 1961) was the oldest liverwort until 2008 (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026). The Lower Devonian [Riccardiothallus devonicus](riccardiothallus-devonicus.md) from Yunnan would be older but its liverwort status is doubted.

## Affinity and controversies

Thallus form, the oil-body-type cells and a reported sporophyte capsule support liverwort identity, and no source I read disputes it (unlike the Pragian thalloid fossils). Oil bodies are a liverwort synapomorphy (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026), which makes the dark cells the strongest argument, though the homology is a 2026 result from one group and one site. Placement within Jungermanniopsida (Metzgeriales on a Wikipedia page, which I did not check against a primary source) rests on form. The relation to *Pallaviciniites* is that they belong to separate simple thalloid lineages (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026). Tremblay & Mercadal note that at about 388 Ma the main extant lineages may not all have diverged (citing Heinrichs et al. 2007), so the fossil may be stem-group (my inference).

## Ecology and physiology

A free-living, photosynthetic gametophyte thallus on a wet delta surface (inference from habit and facies). Labandeira et al. (2014) documented nine damage types in three functional feeding groups, the earliest external foliage feeding and galling in the terrestrial record, and argued that oil-body cells probably helped defend the thallus against arthropod herbivores; they inferred Devonian arthropod herbivores to be significantly smaller than later Palaeozoic ones. Dark cells clump near the margin (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026), and were more frequent in young tissues, branch apices and bases (Mercadal et al. 2026), consistent with, not proof of, a defensive role.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Lower bound for liverworts.** Givetian (387.95-382.31 Ma) is the secure liverwort megafossil bound; Hooper et al. (2026) put crown bryophytes at 445-429 Ma, and Flores et al. (2023) a Late Silurian to Early Devonian origin for complex thalloid liverworts. The gap of about 40-60 Myr between those clock ages and this fossil is a megafossil sampling gap, not evidence of absence.
- **Sampling.** Only seven Devonian liverwort occurrences, and no Devonian mosses or hornworts, enter the 2025 bryophyte fossil database (Blanco-Moreno et al. 2025). Tomescu et al. (2018) review taphonomic reasons for the rarity, and the Devonian and Carboniferous liverworts were all found in fine shales and mudstones (Tremblay & Mercadal 2026), so for F3 the observation model for bryophyte bodies must be much sparser than for vascular plants and tied to fine-grained lens facies.
- **Dominant gametophyte.** A gametophyte-dominant liverwort (with, apparently, a small sporophyte) persisted alongside sporophyte-dominant vascular plants by 388 Ma, so F1 has to explain divergent outcomes among lineages, not one replacement.
- **Defence and herbivory.** About 388 Ma, thalli carried nine damage types (Labandeira et al. 2014) and putative defensive cells, with dark-cell fractions of 0.19 or more in the most regular sections and clumping at margins; these are candidate parameters for a herbivory term.
- **Developmental rule.** A lateral-inhibition pattern with about 2.7 cells between idioblasts is a quantitative target for a cell-level model.

## Open questions

- Formation, biozone and any radiometric constraint for the Cairo lens, and the capsule's attachment and spores.
- Verified thallus dimensions, and rhizoid features.
- Is *Metzgeriothallus* stem- or crown-group, given the molecular clocks?
- Whether older liverwort megafossils exist in the Silurian, now that trilete spores are placed before the crown (Hooper et al. 2026).
