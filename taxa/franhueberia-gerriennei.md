## In brief

*Franhueberia gerriennei* is known only from permineralised axes with a small centrarch primary xylem strand, a cylinder of secondary xylem produced by a vascular cambium (radial files of tracheids, multiplicative divisions, narrow uniseriate rays) and P-type tracheids, from the Emsian Battery Point Formation of Gaspé. Hoffman & Tomescu (2013) described it as a basal euphyllophyte that predates stem-leaf-root organography and as evidence that the machinery for wood production entered the euphyllophyte clade early. It is one of at least ten Early Devonian plant types with secondary growth, so the question is no longer whether Early Devonian plants made wood but how, and why, it varied.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed (Casselman & Tomescu 2025, emended diagnosis, with the type from Hoffman & Tomescu 2013): protostele about 0.5 mm; protoxylem tracheids 8 µm; metaxylem tracheids 7-15 µm with bordered pits; secondary xylem tracheids 8.4-37 µm wide tangentially and 21.6-42 µm radially, with multiaperturate scalariform bordered pits; narrow rays (9-12 µm wide, 90 to over 150 µm tall). The type has 0.70 mm of secondary xylem and up to 25 tracheids per file; the emended diagnosis, which adds two topotypes, gives xylem reaching over 6 mm in diameter, with a thick parenchymatous inner cortex and a thin sclerified outer cortex with ridges and conical protrusions (outer cortex at least 95-130 µm thick). Phloem, epidermis, branching, leaves, sporangia and roots are not known; the type preserved only xylem.

## Age and distribution

Battery Point Formation, south shore of Gaspé Bay, middle to late Emsian (Hoffman & Tomescu 2013; Casselman & Tomescu 2025), a fluvial succession (the dating basis is not detailed in the sources I read). The related evidence for Early Devonian wood is spread across several units:

| Plant | Unit and place | Age as given by sources |
|---|---|---|
| *Armoricaphyton chateaupannense* | Chalonnes Fm, France | late Pragian, c. 407-408 Ma; oldest known wood (Strullu-Derrien et al. 2014; Casselman & Tomescu 2025); late Pragian or earliest Emsian in Gerrienne et al. (2011) |
| "New Brunswick plant A", plant B | Campbellton Fm, New Brunswick | early Emsian (Casselman & Tomescu 2025); late Emsian in Gerrienne et al. (2011) supplement: the sources disagree |
| *Franhueberia*, *Gmujij*, *Kenrickia*, *Tainioxyla*, *Perplexa*, "*Psilophyton*-type axes" | Battery Point Fm, Gaspé | middle to late Emsian (Casselman & Tomescu 2025) |

The 407-397 Ma plants of Gerrienne et al. (2011) are *Armoricaphyton* and New Brunswick plant A; I treat the Ma values as the authors' and use stages.

## Affinity and controversies

P-type tracheids and the centrarch protostele place it with basal euphyllophytes; Gerrienne et al. (2011) called the two plants they described "close to the genus *Psilophyton*" (supplement) and probable precursors of lignophytes. Hoffman & Tomescu (2013) inferred a single origin of vascular cambial growth in euphyllophytes (as relayed by Casselman & Tomescu 2025), and Toledo et al. (2021) reiterated that possibility. The set is diverse, which argues against a simple single-lineage story: terete centrarch protosteles (*Franhueberia*, *Armoricaphyton*), four-lobed actinosteles (*Gmujij*), a cruciform actinostele with thin secondary xylem (*Kenrickia*, sister to other radiatopsids), and different ray types, so wood may have been explored by several lineages at once (Casselman & Tomescu 2025; Pfeiler & Tomescu 2021). Secondary growth is not found in some large Early Devonian euphyllophytes: *Wilhowia* has axes to 30 cm × 1 cm with no secondary tissue (Gensel 2022), and fossils assigned to *Psilophyton dawsonii* show at most possible multiplicative divisions without rays (Casselman & Tomescu 2025), consistent with the [Psilophyton](psilophyton.md) card. Casselman & Tomescu (2025) assign two new specimens to *Franhueberia* by quantitative tracheid metrics, but the metrics overlap with *Gmujij*, and primary xylem separates them.

## Ecology and physiology

Gerrienne et al. (2011) argue from small plant size and thick-walled cortical cells that early wood was driven by hydraulic constraints, not mechanical support. Strullu-Derrien et al. (2014) measured the hydraulic properties of *Armoricaphyton*, a small plant with narrow axes: specific conductivity of the xylem area 8.7 kg m^-1 s^-1 and a mean tracheid wall thickness-to-span ratio (t/b)^2 of 0.0372, suited to conductive performance with low resistance to tension, with rigidity from low-density wood (this concerns *Armoricaphyton*, not *Franhueberia*). Casselman & Tomescu (2025) note that the largest Emsian woody specimens (up to 3.66 mm of xylem) exceed the 1.7 mm of the largest zygopterid stems, which they argue may imply a bifacial cambium. That is speculation.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Secondary xylem before large size.** Wood appears in axes with xylem cylinders of about 2 mm to over 6 mm across, while the tallest Early Devonian plant, [Pertica](pertica.md) (1-2 m), has unknown anatomy (Gerrienne et al. 2011 supplement). A height-race model (F2) should not assume wood as the support for height in the Emsian.
- **Rates and amounts.** Secondary xylem thickness 0.32-3.66 mm across Early Devonian woody taxa and up to 95 or more tracheids per radial file (Casselman & Tomescu 2025, Table 1; tracheid sizes are measured on published images). These bound how much wood was added in the plants' (unknown) lifetime.
- **Hydraulics.** Strullu-Derrien et al.'s conductivity and wall-thickness ratio for *Armoricaphyton* give a measured hydraulic baseline for the earliest wood.
- **Timing.** First wood is late Pragian, so the trait predates the Battery Point plants; first-appearance models for wood should use *Armoricaphyton*, not this species.
- **Trait independence.** Wood without leaves or roots (organography after wood), and absence of wood in the largest known leafless axes (*Wilhowia*), separate the cambium from size in the trait matrix.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast secondary xylem production with assumptions linking wood to height and mechanical support, using small preserved xylem cylinders whose whole-plant stature and hydraulic performance remain unknown.

- **Can transfer:**
  - For the Franhueberia gerriennei type from the Battery Point Formation, Gaspé, use 0.70 mm secondary xylem thickness and up to 25 tracheids per file as accumulated anatomical amounts (Hoffman & Tomescu 2013; Casselman & Tomescu 2025).
  - For Franhueberia gerriennei in the emended diagnosis incorporating the type and two Battery Point topotypes, use secondary tracheids 8.4-37 µm tangentially and 21.6-42 µm radially, with multiaperturate scalariform bordered pits (Casselman & Tomescu 2025).
  - For Franhueberia gerriennei in the emended type-plus-two-topotype diagnosis from Battery Point, represent xylem cylinders reaching over 6 mm across; this describes xylem rather than complete stem diameter (Casselman & Tomescu 2025).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Specific conductivity 8.7 kg m^-1 s^-1 and wall thickness-to-span ratio 0.0372 cannot be assigned to Franhueberia: Strullu-Derrien et al. (2014) measured Armoricaphyton.
  - Wood thickness and radial cell counts cannot supply annual cambial growth rates: fossil lifetimes are unknown, and the broad ranges pool different woody taxa.
  - Unknown leaves and roots cannot be coded as observed absence or demonstrate that wood preceded those organs in this species.
  - The front-matter maximum of 6 mm cannot be a hard upper limit on stem diameter: it is a lower bound on the maximum xylem-cylinder diameter.
  - The Emsian Franhueberia occurrence cannot calibrate the first appearance of wood: this card identifies older late Pragian Armoricaphyton.

## Open questions

- Is the whole set of Early Devonian wood one origin or several, given the different steles and rays?
- Can unifacial versus bifacial cambium be told apart from the fossils?
- Whole-plant reconstruction: branching, leaves, sporangia and spores of *Franhueberia*.
- Reconcile the early versus late Emsian age of the Campbellton Formation.
