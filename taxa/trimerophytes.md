## In brief

Trimerophytes are the large, leafless, three-dimensionally branched plants of the Pragian to Eifelian whose fusiform sporangia hang in terminal clusters or pairs. Banks proposed the group in 1968 as a subdivision of the old "psilophytes", with *Psilophyton* at its core. It is now treated as a paraphyletic grade at the base of the euphyllophytes, a plexus from which fern-side, lignophyte-side and sphenopsid lineages arose, not a clade. The grade includes metre-scale plants (*Pertica*), the best-documented leafless architectures, and the raw material for the origin of megaphylls. Member cards: [Psilophyton](psilophyton.md) and [Pertica](pertica.md); the leaf-precursor plant [Eophyllophyton bellum](eophyllophyton-bellum.md) is a related basal euphyllophyte.

## Morphology and anatomy

Psilophyton has 2-7 dichotomies per branch, bordered-pitted tracheids and fusiform sporangia 3-5 x 1-2 mm on pendulous fertile units (Doran 1980; see the Psilophyton card). Pertica has a pseudomonopodial main axis with helical, tetrastichous laterals and dense sporangial clusters (Kasper and Andrews 1972; Doran et al. 1978). Trimerophyton, the type genus, was erected by Hopping (1956) for a specimen that Dawson had named *Psilophyton robustius*; the name stresses side branches that divided in three as well as two (I saw Hopping only through a search summary). Dawsonites Halle 1916 (Röragen, Norway) was placed in Psilophyton by Schweitzer (1980), according to the Plant Fossil Names Registry. Andrews et al. (1975) named a "trimerophyte-progymnosperm" intermediate plant, *Oocampsa*, whose Emsian lateral systems are helical (Tomescu and Whitewoods 2024). *Chaleuria* has a main axis with closely spiralled branches, dichotomous branchlets ending in sporangial pairs, and two spore size classes (Andrews et al. 1974). Anatomy is documented in only a few species, so most placements rest on form.

## Age and distribution

The grade appears in the Pragian of Yunnan, is richest in the Emsian of Laurentia and Belgium, and ends around the Eifelian (see range notes). *Psilophyton* alone has at least 14 species across the interval (Doran and Tomescu 2025), and *Pertica* three or more species. In genus-level macrofossil counts, basal euphyllophytes help drive a minor increase in diversity near the end of the Early Devonian, and euphyllophytes overall expand in the Givetian (Capel et al. 2022); the text I read does not separate the trimerophyte curve. Laurussian Emsian assemblages hold most records; South China holds Pragian relatives.

## Affinity and controversies

The concept builds on Dawson's 1859 *Psilophyton* (emended by Hueber and Banks 1967) and Hopping's *Trimerophyton*; Banks set out the subdivision in 1968 and the name was published formally in Banks (1975), formed from Hopping's genus (ICN Art. 16, Ex. 6). The plexus view comes from fossil-inclusive analyses: Kenrick and Crane (1997) proposed that euphyllophyte leaves are partly homologous as lateral branching systems of the kind found in the trimerophyte grade, and Rothwell and Nixon (2006) found seed-free euphyllophytes paraphyletic, with the living psilotopsids, equisetopsids and ferns separated by leafless extinct lineages. Toledo et al. (2021) report that adding the actinostelic *Kenrickia* overturns previously established relationships among the Radiatopses. Tomescu and Whitewoods (2024) place Emsian *Kenrickia*, *Gothanophyton* and *Wilhowia* in the lignophyte-ward plexus, in contrast with *Psilophyton*, whose branches have irregular taxis and radial symmetry; Gensel (2022) instead treats *Wilhowia* as a lineage fitting no recognised group. Claisse et al. (2025) included *Psilophyton crenulatum* and *Polythecophyton* only for comparison with the lycophytes. Capel et al. (2022) group *Psilophyton*, *Pertica*, *Eophyllophyton* and *Polythecophyton* as "basal euphyllophytes" for convenience, because the group is a plexus. Monophyly of *Psilophyton* itself is untested.

## Ecology and physiology

Trimerophytes grew on moist channel bars and freshly deposited flood muds (Griffing et al. 2000), with erect autochthonous axes of cf. *P. forbesii* in the Trout Valley Formation (Gastaldo 2016). They lacked roots; Doran and Tomescu (2025) read the rhizome and emergences of *P. crenulatum* as rooting devices. The Psilophyton card records arthropod damage, wound periderm and fungal bodies.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Size.** Plants of 30 cm to about 3 m, with axes 3-10 mm (textbook heights; use as bounds, not priors). *Pertica* is about a metre tall (*P. quadrifaria*, "perhaps") to about 3 m (*P. varia*, secondary).
- **Architecture.** L-system parameters exist for *P. crenulatum* and *Pertica* (dichotomy counts, helical laterals, 32-128 sporangia per cluster). The grade spans irregular lateral arrangement (*Psilophyton*) to regular, helical taxis (*Pertica*, *Oocampsa*, *Chaleuria*), the one leaf-defining character the last three show; their laterals branch in three dimensions (Tomescu and Whitewoods 2024).
- **Hydraulics.** Maximum tracheid diameter rose through the late Silurian-Early Devonian (rho 0.70, n = 41; Niklas 1985), and xylem constrictions at branch bases suggest vulnerability segmentation (Niklas and Banks 1985).
- **Wood and support.** No secondary xylem is reported for *Psilophyton*, whereas smaller coeval euphyllophytes had it (Gerrienne et al. 2011); *Wilhowia* stood on 1 cm axes without wood (Gensel 2022). Height was reached without wood.
- **Leaf timing.** Lycophyte leaves appear around 415-408 Ma, euphyllophyte leaves only by about 385 Ma (older timescale; Tomescu and Whitewoods 2024): a 10-25 Myr delay between Emsian architectures and Givetian leaves that a model must explain (Pertica card).
- **Heterospory.** *Chaleuria* (apparently heterosporous; Andrews et al. 1974) warrants a sensitivity case.

## Open questions

Whether *Psilophyton* and *Pertica* are on the stem of crown euphyllophytes; anatomy of *Pertica*; whether any trimerophyte is older than the Pragian; the status of *Chaleuria* and *Oocampsa*; and a clade-by-clade diversity curve for basal euphyllophytes.
