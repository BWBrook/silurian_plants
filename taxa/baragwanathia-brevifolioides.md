## In brief

*Baragwanathia brevifolioides* is a single sterile compression from the upper Silurian (Přídolí) of Bohemia, with isotomous axes and short, midrib-bearing, densely arranged leaves, described as a species of the Australian genus *Baragwanathia* (Kraft & Kvaček 2017, 2021). It is the best-dated Silurian record of a lycopsid-type plant with differentiated microphylls, since graptolites in the same beds give the age. It is also a single specimen without anatomy, so its lycophyte status is a morphological judgement. Both facts bear on when microphylls appeared, which the Australian type species ([Baragwanathia longifolia](baragwanathia-longifolia.md)) cannot settle alone given its dating dispute.

## Morphology and anatomy

I could not read the 2017 description (closed access); this card rests on the 2021 diagnosis, the 2017 abstract as relayed by search-engine summaries, and later reviews. Observed in the holotype: slender axes with isotomous branching; linear, moderately long microphylls, uniform in dense arrangement (phyllotaxis not stated in the sources read), truncate or rounded at the tip, each with a median midrib; and an apex enfolded in a tuft of upward-facing leaves (Kraft & Kvaček 2021). The name refers to the short leaves compared with *B. longifolia* (Kraft & Kvaček 2021, etymology). The fossil is a coalified compression: no anatomical detail survives, so the abstract's case for vascular tissue is that midrib-like strands in the microphylls suggest a tissue unknown in algae (summary only; I did not read the paper). No fertile material, spores, stomata or roots are reported.

## Age and distribution

Požáry Formation at Karlštejn, Prague Basin (Barrandian), in the *Neocolonograptus ultimus* Biozone (called *Skalograptus ultimus* in Pšenička et al. 2021), Přídolí; ICS bases 422.7-419.62 Ma. The Prague Basin was in the peri-Gondwanan temperate belt (Pšenička et al. 2021). Pšenička et al. (2021) call it the only lycophyte with differentiated microphylls among the Silurian plants of the Barrandian, which otherwise show rhyniophytoid characters; the associated plants include *Cooksonia*, *Aberlemnia bohemica*, *Tichavekia* and *Fusiformitheca*. A tentative Kazakh *Baragwanathia* sp. is marked with a query in the Přídolí compilation of Bek et al. (2024), and a Thuringian "*B. brevifolia*" of Hundt (1952), from the lower Ludlow, is a different, earlier name: Kraft & Kvaček (2021) judge that specimen poorly preserved, its published figure unreadable, and its identification as a plant dubious. Hundt's name remains valid under the Code, which is why the Bohemian species needed a replacement name.

## Affinity and controversies

Kraft & Kvaček (2017) place it in *Baragwanathia* on morphology and call it a possible aquatic precursor of land lycophytes (abstract, as relayed). Risks: a single compression with midribs but no xylem; no spores to settle lycophyte status; convergent leaf-like organs cannot be excluded without anatomy. A search-engine summary of Libertín et al. (2024) attributes to that paper the remark that the Czech specimen may be a graptolite or an alga (oral communication); I could not access the paper and treat this as unverified. With Earp's (2024) reassignment of most Yea beds to the Early Devonian, this specimen is arguably the only well-dated Silurian *Baragwanathia* candidate (my inference), so doubts about it bear directly on the earliest-lycophyte date.

## Ecology and physiology

Nothing direct. The plant lies in marine mudstone with epibionts on its dead parts, so it either grew in water or was washed into it, and the epibionts do not decide which. Pšenička et al. (2021) note that *Baragwanathia* had been read as typical of warm, humid zones, whereas this occurrence is at higher latitude, and suggest ("it seems") that the spread of *Baragwanathia*-like plants to higher latitudes is associated with increased humidity during glaciation and the warming after the mid-Ludfordian glacial.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Earliest microphylls.** The Silurian record of microphylled plants is one specimen in Bohemia, a tentative one in Kazakhstan and the disputed Victorian beds. For F3, the bound on microphyll origination is Přídolí (422.7-419.62 Ma) with low confidence, not Gorstian.
- **Against the molecular timescale.** Hooper et al. (2026) place crown tracheophytes at 432-428 Ma; docs/primer.md gives their constraint as 431.2-420.7 Ma and Harris et al. (2022) crown lycophytes at 431-411 Ma. The Přídolí (422.7-419.62 Ma) overlaps the young end of the first and lies inside the second. A Gorstian lycopsid (426.7-425.0 Ma) would also fall inside both, so the clocks cannot choose between the dating scenarios.
- **Sampling unit.** A single specimen from a basin whose Přídolí plants are otherwise cooksonioid is a one-count datum; a first-appearance model should treat the lycopsid record as one sighting whose identification is uncertain.
- **Climate belt.** Higher-latitude occurrence is a constraint for spatial models: if correctly identified, lycopsid-grade plants occurred in a temperate peri-Gondwanan belt in the Přídolí.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Test a Pridoli minimum for microphyll-like differentiation against later or disputed records, using a graptolite-dated holotype whose lycophyte identification remains morphological.

- **Can transfer:**
  - B. brevifolioides, holotype KR 1 from Karlštejn: represent slender axes with isotomous branching as observed gross architecture (Kraft & Kvaček 2021).
  - B. brevifolioides, holotype KR 1 from Karlštejn: represent median-midrib-bearing appendages and an apex enfolded by upward-facing leaves as compression morphology (Kraft & Kvaček 2021).
  - B. brevifolioides, holotype KR 1, Požáry Formation at Karlštejn: use a single Pridoli candidate occurrence dated by co-occurring graptolites, retaining identification uncertainty (Kraft & Kvaček 2017, abstract summary; 2021).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not assign demonstrated xylem, hydraulic properties or secure lycophyte affinity from median midribs alone; the single compression lacks xylem and diagnostic spores.
  - Do not parameterise reproductive output, spore traits, stomatal behaviour or rooting from this sterile specimen; the relevant organs are unreported.
  - Do not assign whole-plant height or measured axis diameter; the incomplete specimen lacks a height reconstruction and this card supplies no axis measurement.
  - Do not infer an aquatic growth habit from marine epibionts; they cannot distinguish growth in water from transport after death.

## Open questions

- Re-examine the holotype for cuticle, any vascular strand, leaf attachment and the epibiont identification, and look for additional specimens.
- Is the Bohemian plant congeneric with *B. longifolia*, or only convergent in leaf form?
- Verify the reported doubts about its identity in Libertín et al. (2024) and whether they were published or oral.
- Are there fertile Prague Basin lycophytes or *Baragwanathia*-type in situ spores?
