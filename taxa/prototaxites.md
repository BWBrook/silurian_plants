## In brief

Prototaxites is a genus of columnar bodies, mostly unbranched and up to about 8 m long, built of interwoven tubes. It was the largest terrestrial organism of the Late Silurian to Late Devonian, standing far above contemporaneous vascular plants (Rhynie vascular plants reach about 20 cm; Berbee et al. 2020). Its affinity is the unsolved core. A fungal reading was the working majority view, but Loron et al. (2026) argue from anatomy and infrared chemistry that P. taiti belongs to an extinct lineage outside Fungi. On either reading it was probably a non-photosynthetic heterotroph and a large standing pool of organic carbon.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed, P. taiti, Rhynie chert (Loron et al. 2026). The largest specimen (NSC.36, a fragment 5.6 cm wide and 6.9 cm long, incomplete at both ends) has three tube types. Type 1: about 10 µm wide, walls under 1 µm, septate with pores, about 75% of volume. Type 2: 20-40 µm, double-layered walls of about 2 µm, aseptate, about 20%. Type 3: about 40 µm, annular thickenings ("banded"), aseptate, unbranched, about 5%. Medullary spots, 200-600 µm across, are dense three-dimensional networks of all three types plus tubes under 1 µm, with no discernible branching order. No reproductive structures, photobionts or lichen-style organisation were found. Growth rings occur only in P. loganii.

Large forms. Retallack & Landing (2014) describe an 8.83 m trunk from the Givetian Bellvale Sandstone of New York with six branches, each about 1 m long and 9 cm across, on its upper 1.2 m.

Borings. Three silicified German stems carry tunnels with chamber-like expansions filled with hexagonal prismatic coprolites (new ichnogenus Hexacoprus); partial healing shows the tissue was alive (Laaß et al. 2026).

## Age and distribution

Late Silurian to Late Devonian (see range notes); Rhynie P. taiti is Pragian (U-Pb 411.5 ± 1.3 Ma; Parry et al. 2011). Records span Laurentia (New York, Quebec, Scotland), Avalonia (Welsh Borderland), Germany and Baltica. In Baltica the genus appears only from the middle Givetian, with the onset of subaerial delta-plain deposition; Vinn et al. (2026) attribute the earlier absence to preservation, not biogeography. Retallack (2019, 2022) reports Darriwilian "P. honeggeri" from Tennessee; I treat it as unconfirmed.

## Affinity and controversies

1. Fungus. Hueber (2001) reconstructed P. loganii as a giant basidiomycote sporophore. Selosse (2002) argued that surrounding producers could not feed so large a saprotroph and suggested lichenisation (as summarised by Berbee et al. 2020). Honegger et al. (2018) placed P. taiti in the Ascomycota on a superficial hymenium with polysporic asci. Vajda et al. (2023) read the bodies as mega-rhizomorphs that may have grown horizontally. Edwards & Axe (2012) found cord-like construction in the close ally Nematasketum.
2. Rolled liverwort mats (Graham et al. 2010), rebutted anatomically by Taylor et al. (2010).
3. Extinct non-fungal lineage (Loron et al. 2026). Infrared spectra of P. taiti separated it from fungi and chitinous arthropods with 91-93% classification accuracy, lacked the sugar-protein alteration products of fungi, and suggest a polyphenolic, lignin-like wall. The fungal biomarker perylene was absent from the fossil (present in the matrix), and the authors find no fungal parallel for the tube organisation. Loron et al. (2023) had already found Rhynie nematophytes plant-like in composition.

Publication status. Loron et al. appeared in Science Advances 12(4): eaec6277 (January 2026), after a bioRxiv preprint (v1, 17 March 2025, different title, which said recent studies had converged on a fungal affinity). The journal version frames the choice as fungus versus extinct lineage. The authors acknowledge one species from one locality, wall chemistry inferred from spectra and possible differential preservation. Press coverage (read only as search summaries) quotes M.-A. Selosse noting that only one of about 25 species was analysed and that lichen-like function remains possible. I found no published rebuttal. Retallack (2022) holds a further minority view: branched, lichen-like nematophyte canopies, inferred from palaeosol trunk spacing.

## Ecology and physiology

Boyce et al. (2007) measured δ13C spanning up to 13‰ across five localities, against narrow ranges in co-occurring vascular plants, and read heterotrophy on isotopically varied substrates; the heaviest values may reflect feeding on organisms with carbon-concentrating mechanisms (soil crusts, bryophytes). Hobbie & Boyce (2010) found comparable variability in the modern saprotroph Arrhenia obscurata beside glacial meltwater pools (soil up to 5‰ heavier) and noted that Prototaxites values do not track tracheophytes. Retallack & Landing (2014) report narrow values for the coalified outer layer (trunk -25.0 ± 0.13‰, branches -26.2 ± 0.69‰) and interpret the histology as lichen-like, with coccoid chlorophyte photobionts. Nutrition is unsettled, though Loron et al. cite a growing consensus for saprotrophy. Growth rate and lifespan are unknown.

## Why it matters for modelling

- Vertical structure: 8-9 m against about 0.2 m for Rhynie vascular plants. If heterotrophic it does not compete for light; if Retallack's photobiont model holds it dominated the canopy. In my reading, the δ13C spread (13‰ versus a narrow plant range) is the available discriminator.
- Carbon: a heterotrophic pool fed by non-vascular organic matter (mats, algae, crusts), decoupled from vascular net primary production and found in wet delta-plain settings.
- Trophic links: living tissue was bored by an endophagous animal, and the close ally Nematasketum is the main content of selective-feeder coprolites (Edwards et al. 2012). It is a food resource as well as a carbon sink.
- No growth-rate, longevity or standing-biomass data exist; treat them as free parameters.

## Open questions

- Do the Rhynie results hold for P. loganii and the other species?
- Are the asci of Honegger et al. part of P. taiti?
- Saprotroph, lichen-like or symbiotic nutrition?
- Growth rate, longevity and the cause of the Late Devonian disappearance.
