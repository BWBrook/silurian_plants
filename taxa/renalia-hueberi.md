## In brief

*Renalia hueberi* is a small, slender, leafless plant from the Emsian of Gaspé whose reniform, bivalved sporangia sit at the branch tips, a mix of zosterophyll-like sporangium shape and rhyniophytoid-like position. Whether it belongs with the rhyniophytoids, the zosterophylls or the stem of the lycophytes is unresolved, largely because anatomy is poorly known. It tests how sporangium position, dehiscence and spore-wall features were assembled on the way to the lycophytes, and shows that a morphologically intermediate plant can post-date the groups it is intermediate between.

## Morphology and anatomy

Compressions only. An abstract-level summary of Gensel (1976), which I could not read in full, gives axes about 1 mm wide and at least 11 cm long, lateral branches that dichotomise several times, and round to reniform sporangia at branch tips that open along the outer margin through thick-walled rectangular cells. A compilation estimates the plant at about 30 cm tall with axes up to 1.5 mm. The sporangia are bivalvate and seemingly paired (Nibbelink & Tomescu 2022).

Anatomy is the weak point: only cellular patterns from cleared specimens and short tracheid strands are known (Gensel 1976, per Nibbelink & Tomescu 2022), so xylem maturation, the character that separates zosterophyll exarch protosteles from other protosteles, is unobserved. Spores are trilete; TEM shows a single homogeneous wall with some lamellae, no perispore or paraexospore, surface globules, and a raised trilete mark with a loosely granular inner layer beneath it, features otherwise known only in Lycophytina (Gensel et al. 2013). Whole-plant form is reconstructed from fragments.

## Age and distribution

Known from the Battery Point Formation at Gaspé Bay, Quebec, which is Emsian (Griffing et al. 2000; Gensel 2022). Further species, *R. grabertii* and *R. major*, are reported from the Lower Devonian of the Rhineland (Schweitzer 1980; secondary record). *R. major* is described with lateral sporangia on short stalks, which would blur the line with zosterophylls; I have not checked this.

## Affinity and controversies

Gensel (1976) compared the plant with cooksonioid rhyniophytoids and zosterophylls, chose the former, and allowed an intermediate position. Gensel (1992) stressed its mixture of zosterophyll and rhyniopsid features. Hueber (1992) separated terminal, globular or reniform, dehiscent sporangia of cooksonioids from rhyniophytoid ones, placed the zosterophyll and lycopsid split in the ontogeny of the sporangium relative to the apical meristem, and judged Renalia closer to the zosterophylls. Kenrick & Crane (1997) left its affinity uncertain. Hao & Xue (2013) recovered it as sister to a clade of zosterophylls, lycopsids, barinophytes and euphyllophytes. Gensel et al. (2013) treat it as a putative basal lycophyte on spore-wall evidence. Nibbelink & Tomescu (2022) nest it among zosterophylls in some analyses and in phenetic comparisons, while noting that anatomy is missing. Terminal versus lateral sporangia remains the character that most separates it from zosterophylls; xylem maturation, sporangium attachment and further material would settle it.

Timing complicates "intermediate": zosterophylls already dominated a late Ludlow (Ludfordian) flora (Kotyk et al. 2002), 12-31 Myr before the Emsian of *Renalia* on data/timescale.csv.

## Ecology and physiology

A small herb in a coastal-fluvial basin where zosterophylls formed extensive, long-lived stands in backswamps and marshes (Griffing et al. 2000). Homosporous. Thick-walled cells along the sporangial margin suggest active opening (inference). Stomata, roots and gametophyte are unreported in the sources read.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Ancestral-state reconstruction.** Sporangium position (terminal, lateral), dehiscence (indehiscent, bivalved) and spore-wall type are scored on Renalia; its placement changes the number and direction of inferred transitions. Treat it as an unresolved lineage, not a node.
- **Rates.** Ludfordian zosterophylls and Emsian Renalia imply a ghost lineage of at least 12 Myr, up to about 31 Myr; rate estimates must treat Renalia as a late sample.
- **Stature.** About 1 mm axes and 11-30 cm height define a small-plant end member in an Emsian community that also held much larger euphyllophytes (e.g. *Wilhowia*, axes 1 cm x 30 cm; Gensel 2022), constraining size-structure assumptions.
- **Spore-wall evolution.** Gensel et al. (2013) describe a shift from homogeneous or layered walls in rhyniophytoid-grade plants to single-layered walls, partly lamellate, in lycophyte-related plants; globules are more regular in Emsian lycophyte-related plants.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast terminal reniform bivalved sporangia and lycophyte-like spore walls with alternative ancestral-state reconstructions, using a small Emsian plant that post-dates older zosterophylls.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Preserved-axis geometry, provisionally: R. hueberi Gaspé compressions have axes about 1 mm wide and at least 11 cm long; use fragment dimensions rather than whole-plant height (Gensel 1976, abstract-level summary).
  - Reproductive architecture: R. hueberi from the Battery Point Formation, Gaspé, has terminal reniform bivalvate sporangia, seemingly paired; preserve uncertainty about pairing (Nibbelink & Tomescu 2022).
  - Spore-wall structure: R. hueberi Gaspé material has trilete spores with a single-layered wall, no perispore or paraexospore, and surface globules; use these character states independently of phylogenetic placement (Gensel et al. 2013).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - A measured standing-height range cannot use 11-30 cm: the lower value is preserved-axis length and the upper value is a secondary whole-plant estimate, unverified against Gensel (1976).
  - Exarch xylem maturation or a detailed hydraulic model cannot be assigned: R. hueberi preserves only cleared cellular patterns and short tracheid strands (Gensel 1976, per Nibbelink & Tomescu 2022).
  - Lateral sporangia cannot be transferred from R. major to R. hueberi; the Rhineland report concerns another species and is unchecked (Schweitzer 1980, secondary record).
  - An ancestral node date cannot be assigned from morphological intermediacy: Emsian Renalia post-dates Ludfordian zosterophylls and has unresolved placement (Kotyk et al. 2002; Nibbelink & Tomescu 2022).
  - Backswamp or marsh stand structure cannot be assigned to R. hueberi from other Gaspé zosterophylls: its own facies is unstated (Griffing et al. 2000).

## Open questions

- Xylem maturation and any anatomy, ideally permineralised material.
- Whether sporangia are strictly terminal and paired, and how *R. major* attaches them.
- Exact horizon and facies within the Battery Point Formation.
- Primary measurements, which I took from abstract-level summaries.
