## In brief

The Posongchong Formation of Wenshan, south-east Yunnan, yields the most diverse Early Devonian macroflora known: 28 genera and about 33 to 37 species of compressions, most of them endemic to South China (Cascales-Minana et al. 2018; Wang et al. 2022). It is dated to the middle to late Pragian by spores, fish and the overlying marine beds, with no radiometric age. For a record-and-timescale model it is a large, well-sampled, isolated sampling unit whose composition differs from every contemporary Laurussian flora.

## Morphology and anatomy

Composition, grouped as in Cascales-Minana et al. (2018, table 4, from Hao & Xue 2013; counts derived by me, 33 taxa including open nomenclature):

| Group | Taxa (count) |
|---|---|
| Zosterophylls | 15: *Zosterophyllum* (four species including *Z. australianum*; see [*Zosterophyllum*](zosterophyllum.md)), *Ramoferis* (two), *Yunia* (two), *Guangnania*, *Gumuia*, *Discalis*, *Wenshania*, *Oricilla*, *Distichophytum*, cf. *Hicklingia* |
| Incertae sedis | 6: *Adoketophyton* (two), *Catenalis*, *Celatheca*, *Demersatheca*, *Polythecophyton* |
| Euphyllophytes | 4: [*Eophyllophyton bellum*](eophyllophyton-bellum.md), [*Psilophyton primitivum*](psilophyton.md), *Pauthecophyton gracile*, *Hedeia sinica* |
| Lycopsids | 4: *Hueberia*, *Zhenglia*, *Baragwanathia* sp., cf. *Halleophyton* |
| Barinophyte-like | 2: *Dibracophyton*, *Stachyophyton* |
| Others | *Estinnophyton* (sphenopsid lineage), *Huia* (rhyniopsid-related) |

The thalloid [*Riccardiothallus*](riccardiothallus-devonicus.md) comes from the same formation but is not in that table. The authors note absent paratracheophytes (S-type tracheid plants) and no *Cooksonia*-type taxa in the table. Dispersed spores: 18 genera and 32 species from 12 Posongchong samples, nine productive (Cascales-Minana et al. 2018).

## Age and distribution

**Age.** See range notes. Biostratigraphic, with a spore zonation the authors call preliminary for China.

**Setting.** Continental clastics lying unconformably on older marine rocks and overlain conformably by marine Pojiao beds (Cascales-Minana et al. 2018); the Feigu section is 446 m thick (Hu et al. 2025). South China is read as an isolated, low-latitude block in the north-eastern Gondwanan phytogeographic unit (Xue et al. 2018, title; Cascales-Minana et al. 2018). PBDB places the formation at about 0.6 degrees S (gplates) or 6 degrees N (Scotese); no source read names a plate model for a published latitude.

**Taphonomy.** Carbonaceous films and impressions in fine sediment; autochthonous or short-transport burial inferred for *Hueberia* at Zhichang and Feigu (Hu et al. 2025, citing Yang 2009).

**Sampling.** First described in 1988 (*Eophyllophyton*); monograph in 2013; at least 17 numbered fossil sites at Zhichang; a new locality in 2025. PBDB holds three collections and five of the 33 taxa, so it cannot stand in for the monograph data.

## Affinity and controversies

Endemism: no Posongchong species is shared with the Old Red Sandstone continent or other Laurussian floras; three are shared with the Xujiachong flora (*Guangnania cuneata*, *Hedeia sinica*, *Zosterophyllum australianum*) and only *Z. australianum* with the Victorian Baragwanathia flora (Cascales-Minana et al. 2018). Over 70% of genera (20 of 28) are endemic (Wang et al. 2022, citing Hao & Xue 2013 and Xue et al. 2018). Cascales-Minana et al. (2018) find the megafossil differences (no paratracheophytes, many euphyllophytes) hard to reconcile with broadly similar spore morphotypes in Posongchong, Xujiachong, Anglo-Welsh and Rhynie assemblages, while species-level spore differences also point to an isolated block. Species counts differ between sources (about 33 against 37).

## Ecology and physiology

Little is known of the biology of individual plants. *Hueberia* is described as autochthonous or short-transport stems with leaves about 2 mm long (Hu et al. 2025). Cryptospore dyads typical of Euramerican assemblages were not found, and the rare tetrads (0.5-2%) cannot be tied to parent plants (Cascales-Minana et al. 2018).

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Sampling unit.** One formation holds about 33 species, and Capel et al. (2022) report a sampling-corrected rise in richness in the Pragian (and Givetian). Whether this flora drives the Pragian signal can be tested by dropping it (my inference; not tested in the sources read).
- **Province structure.** More than 70% generic endemism and no shared Laurussian species: first-appearance data from this block need their own preservation and origination terms, not pooling with Laurussia.
- **Spore channel.** South China spores differ in abundance, with few *Camarozonotriletes*, *Emphanisporites* and *Verrucosisporites* and no *Clivosispora* or *Breconisporites* (Cascales-Minana et al. 2018); spore-based dating of a megafossil unit is not transferable between provinces (F4).
- **Time resolution.** Age bracket of about one stage (2.4 Myr Pragian), capped by marine beds of late Pragian to earliest Emsian age; use stage-level, not Ma-level, timing.
- **Trait snapshot.** At roughly one time slice the flora contains microphyll lycopsids, lateral-sporangium zosterophylls, terminal-sporangium euphyllophytes and a disputed leaf precursor. Trait frequencies need the species-level data in Hao & Xue (2013), not read.

## Open questions

Can a radiometric age be found (ash beds, detrital zircon)? How many of the 32 dispersed spore species belong to compression taxa? How much of the Pragian peak in richness depends on this one formation? Are Hao & Xue's species-level counts stable under taxonomic revision?
