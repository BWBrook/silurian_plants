## In brief

*Ceratopteris richardii*, the C-fern, is a semi-aquatic homosporous fern and the standard laboratory model for a free-living gametophyte and an independently establishing sporophyte. It is a derived leptosporangiate fern in the Pteridaceae, nested in the leptosporangiates (crown dated at about 393 Ma, around the Early-Middle Devonian boundary, on one clock), with an unusually fast life cycle for a fern, 16-spored sporangia, and a large genome of about 9.6 Gb that is typical of homosporous ferns. It is not an analogue for a Silurian or Devonian plant. It earns a card because it shows, in one tractable organism, how a homosporous plant with no heterospory handles two problems that bear on early land plants: how sex and outcrossing are regulated when every spore is alike (a pheromone, antheridiogen), and how fast an embryo can build a leaf-root axis on a gametophyte that nourishes it only temporarily. Independence itself has not been timed.

## Morphology and anatomy

**Gametophyte.** Spores germinate to a photosynthetic thallus that grows first from a triangular apical cell. Sex is expressed in one of two forms. The hermaphrodite builds a lateral multicellular notch meristem, becomes cordate, and forms archegonia next to the notch and a few antheridia at its margins. The male is smaller, uniform or spatulate, ameristic, and covered in antheridia (Plackett et al. 2018; Lai and Zhou 2025; McConnell et al. 2026).

**Embryo.** From the abstract of Johnson and Renzaglia (2008; full text not read): the first zygotic division is perpendicular to the gametophyte plane and its anterior-posterior axis, giving a "prone" embryo habit that, in the authors' words, maximises rapid independent establishment of a leaf-root axis in a cordate gametophyte. After a globular eight-celled stage the first leaf, the root and the shoot meristems are defined early. The foot expands and differentiates to transport nutrients from the gametophyte, and wall-ingrowth deposition in transfer cells begins in the gametophyte placental cells before the adjacent sporophyte cells, just after the eight-celled stage. Plackett et al. (2018) put the eight-celled stage at about 2 days after fertilisation (DAF) in their conditions, with embryogenesis complete at 6 DAF and fronds then arising from the shoot apex. Renzaglia and Whittier (2013, relaying Renzaglia et al. 1995 and Johnson and Renzaglia 2008; read through a page extractor) state that the embryo is photosynthetic within 1 day of fertilisation and emerges in under 2 weeks. That is photosynthetic capacity and emergence, not measured carbon autonomy or the end of foot transfer. A 2025 preprint (Woudenberg et al.; read through a page extractor) adds that the embryo's ventral foot is a placenta-like structure on the archegonium basal cells and that maternal tissue steers the orientation of the early body axis, aligning the root pole with the maternal rhizoids; it does not measure nutrient provisioning.

**Sporophyte.** A single apical cell makes the shoot, and fronds and roots arise from their own apical cells (Plackett et al. 2018). Fronds change from simple spade-shaped blades to bipinnate and tripinnate fronds; sporophylls are bi- or tripinnate, with sporangia on the abaxial side (McConnell et al. 2026). Sporangia hold 16 spores (Kinosian and Wolf 2022). The genome is 1C about 9.6 Gb by k-mer count (about 15% below earlier flow-cytometry estimates) with n = 39 (Marchant et al. 2022; Kinosian and Wolf and Burow et al. give about 11 Gb). Marchant et al. call a large genome with many chromosomes typical of most ferns, and Kinosian and Wolf call this genome relatively small for a homosporous fern.

## Age and distribution

The species originates in the Caribbean and western Africa and grows rooted or floating in shallow fresh water in seasonally inundated sites; the genus has about ten species across the tropics (Kinosian and Wolf 2022). The Hn-n strain comes from a Cuban collection. Kinosian et al. did not find distinct wild populations of *C. richardii*, and Kinosian and Wolf raise misidentification, poor knowledge of its range or extinction in the wild as explanations. No fossil is given in the sources read. The most recent genome duplication on the lineage, about 62 Ma, post-dates its split from *Acrostichum*, so that split is older than about 62 Ma (Marchant et al. 2022); the genus itself is undated in these sources. On the fern tree of Nitta et al. (2022) the fern crown is 423.2 Ma and the leptosporangiate crown 392.8 Ma. A genus nested inside the leptosporangiates cannot be older than that crown (inference). These ages depend on fossil constraints and a fixed land-plant root and sit on the source's timescale.

## Affinity and controversies

*Ceratopteris* belongs to Pteridaceae (Kinosian and Wolf 2022), one of the largest fern families, in the leptosporangiate clade. Three cautions govern its use.
- **Derived, not basal.** Kinosian and Wolf list several unusual traits: one of a handful of semi-aquatic species among about 12,000 extant ferns, a life cycle that is "incredibly short" for a fern, and 16 or 32 spores per sporangium (16 in *C. richardii*) where most leptosporangiate ferns make 64. They call it a good model but not necessarily a representative fern. Its large genome is not on that list: Marchant et al. (2022) describe large genomes as typical of ferns.
- **Species identity.** Hybridisation and cryptic species are frequent in *Ceratopteris*, and wild material identified as *C. richardii* is genomically more similar to other species than to one another (Kinosian and Wolf 2022). The laboratory results rest on one inbred strain.
- **Antheridiogen is unidentified.** Fern antheridiogens are gibberellin-related, but the structure of the *Ceratopteris* antheridiogen is unknown; gibberellin-pathway involvement is proposed from indirect evidence (Lai and Zhou 2025).

## Ecology and physiology

**Life cycle and timing.** The cycle is about 120 days, much shorter than almost all ferns' annual or multi-year cycles (Kinosian and Wolf 2022). All times below are for Hn-n plants in culture at about 28 degrees C with 16 h light at 80-100 micromol per square metre per second (conditions differ between studies); they are laboratory values, not field values.

| Stage | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Gametophyte sexual maturity | about 6-7 days on 1/2 MS medium with 1% sucrose at 100 micromol; 13-15 days after sowing on C-fern medium at 80 micromol (males 14 days) | Woudenberg et al. 2024; McConnell et al. 2026 |
| Fertilisation | induced by flooding mature gametophytes with water | Woudenberg et al. 2024; McConnell et al. 2026 |

Some culture media contain sugar (1% sucrose in Woudenberg et al.; the C-fern medium composition was not stated in the text of McConnell et al. that I read), and none of these studies tested carbon autonomy, so time to nutritional independence cannot be read from these figures. The sources read do not report when the foot stops transferring nutrients or when the sporophyte first feeds itself. Kinosian and Wolf state only that the zygote is temporarily reliant on the gametophyte and becomes self-sufficient over time.

**Sex determination and mating.** Spores that germinate in an antheridiogen-free environment, or early in a population before antheridiogen accumulates, become hermaphrodites, which secrete antheridiogen; antheridiogen induces later-germinating spores to develop as males, and males hold that identity only while it is present (Lai and Zhou 2025). Germination order is therefore a proxy for exposure to antheridiogen during a responsive period, and hermaphroditic mutants (her) stay hermaphrodite even with antheridiogen present; one such gene, HER7, encodes a brassinosteroid-receptor-like kinase (Burow et al. 2025, abstract only). Once specified, hermaphrodites are insensitive to antheridiogen. Removing it makes males start a new meristem and become hermaphrodites, a conversion also induced by abscisic acid (Lai and Zhou 2025; abscisic-acid work not read). A solitary gametophyte can become bisexual and self-fertilise, so one spore can found a population, and selfing yields a homozygous sporophyte in one generation (Kinosian and Wolf 2022). Outcrossing within and among species is documented (Kinosian and Wolf 2022). Flagellate sperm need liquid water (Sorojsrisom et al. 2022, a general statement for homosporous pteridophytes).

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Two free-living generations with temporary matrotrophy.** The embryo's foot and placental transfer cells are structural evidence for gametophyte-to-embryo transfer; the rates are unmeasured. Leaf, root and shoot meristems are laid down by the eight-celled stage, and the embryo is photosynthetic within a day (relayed), but photosynthesis is not carbon autonomy.
- **Homospory without heterospory.** Sex is set by the neighbourhood: spores that germinate before antheridiogen accumulates become hermaphrodites, later spores males. This is a candidate rule for outcrossing in a homosporous Palaeozoic plant, but no evidence that any Palaeozoic plant used it exists in the sources read.
- **Flood-linked recruitment.** Motile sperm and flooding-induced fertilisation tie reproduction to water films, which matters for habitat rules in a model of early land plants.
- **A fast-cycling fern.** About 120 days is exceptional for a fern; treat it as an outlier, not a typical homosporous value.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** sporophyte-independence and reproductive-strategy, because *C. richardii* is the standard experimental model of a homosporous vascular plant with a free-living gametophyte (the only vascular spore-bearing homosporous model species, per Kinosian and Wolf) and a placental embryo. It sets the homosporous, free-living-gametophyte end against heterosporous [Selaginella](selaginella.md) and against the staged fungal dependence of [Lycopodiella inundata](lycopodiella-inundata.md). No species-specific duration of embryo dependence exists for any of the three.

- **Can transfer:**
  - A rule set for sex expression in a homosporous population, as an exposure-dependent rule: spores germinating before antheridiogen accumulates become hermaphrodites, later ones become males under antheridiogen, hermaphrodites are insensitive, males are reversible, and solitary gametophytes can self (*C. richardii* Hn-n and its mutant lines, Lai and Zhou 2025 and Burow et al. 2025 abstract; review-level statements for the genus, Kinosian and Wolf 2022).
  - The ordering of events from spore to spore in 28 degree C, 16 h light, humid culture at 80-100 micromol: gametophyte sexual maturity in about 1-2 weeks, flooding-dependent fertilisation, embryogenesis in about 6 days, simple fronds at 20 DAF, dissected fronds at 60 DAF, sporangia on sporophylls by 72 DAF (a sampling age, not a measured first appearance), and spores collected after at least 3 months of drying in the protocol (Plackett et al. 2018; McConnell et al. 2026; Woudenberg et al. 2024). These are observation ages from different protocols, not one calibrated life-cycle distribution.
  - A two-step structure for embryo support: a placental foot with transfer cells carries the embryo while the leaf-root axis is built, then the sporophyte stands alone (Johnson and Renzaglia 2008, abstract; timing unmeasured). Keep organ specification, photosynthetic capacity, net carbon autonomy and the end of placental transfer as separate variables.
  - A spore number per sporangium of 16 in this species (Kinosian and Wolf 2022).
  - Qualified functional comparisons across lineages: auxin response in Ceratopteris gametophytes resembles that of a thalloid bryophyte and in the sporophyte that of flowering plants, so response properties follow developmental phase more than phylogeny (Woudenberg et al. 2024, abstract and text).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - The 120-day cycle and the 16-spore sporangium as typical homosporous values: both are unusual for ferns (Kinosian and Wolf 2022). The 9.6 Gb genome is large, but that is typical of ferns, so it is not a Ceratopteris peculiarity (Marchant et al. 2022); it still does not transfer to a Devonian plant.
  - Time to nutritional independence: not reported in the sources read, and no study tested carbon autonomy.
  - Photosynthesis within a day or emergence within two weeks (relayed) as the end of matrotrophy.
  - Antheridiogen or any pheromone system to a Silurian or Devonian plant: it is unidentified chemically and unsupported by fossils.
  - Gametophyte form: a cordate thallus with a notch meristem is a fern condition; compare the Rhynie gametophytes only on traits, not on shape ([Rhynie gametophytes](rhynie-chert-gametophytes.md)).
  - Frond and root anatomy as homologous to lycopsid microphylls and root-bearing axes: fronds are treated as an origin independent of microphylls and of seed-plant leaves (Plackett et al. 2015). Anatomical caution does not rule out the functional comparisons above.
  - Habitat: semi-aquatic with an uncertain wild range, and most results from one inbred strain (Hn-n). Flooding is a fertilisation protocol in culture; field recruitment has not been measured, and Kinosian and Wolf say gametophyte establishment in the wild is almost unknown.

## Open questions

Time to nutritional independence and the end of foot transfer in this species (the full text of Conway and Di Stilio 2020 would give staged times; I read only its abstract); transfer rates across the placenta; spore aperture and size; wild gametophyte establishment, which Kinosian and Wolf say is almost unknown; the antheridiogen structure; whether antheridiogen systems occur in lineages that were homosporous in the Devonian (Hornych et al. 2021, cited by Lai and Zhou, not read); the original basis of the 2-month spore dormancy (Jiang et al. 2024, supplement); and the fossil record (Eocene Ceratopteris-type spores, a lead from the audit, not read).
