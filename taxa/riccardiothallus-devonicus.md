## In brief

*Riccardiothallus devonicus* is a flat, regularly forking, midrib-less thallus from the Pragian Posongchong Formation of Yunnan. Guo et al. (2012) assigned it to Aneuraceae, the simple thalloid liverworts, near living *Riccardia*, and called it the earliest unequivocal liverwort megafossil. It is the Early Devonian bryophyte that Wellman (2014) cites as the earliest fossil bryophyte. Its significance is double: it would push simple thalloid liverworts back to the Early Devonian, and it shows how thin the bryophyte megafossil record is before the Middle Devonian. A 2025 compilation excludes it as not empirically justified.

## Morphology and anatomy

Observed (abstract of Guo et al. 2012; I could not access the full text): a partially permineralised compression in grey arenaceous mudstone; a regularly bifurcating, flat, ecostate, multilayered thallus with entire margins. Compared with gametophytes of living and fossil liverworts, it was placed in a new genus of Aneuraceae. Thallus size, rhizoids, cell detail and any reproductive structures are not stated in the abstract. Interpreted: the identification as a liverwort gametophyte and the Riccardia comparison rest on gross form.

## Age and distribution

Pragian, Posongchong Formation, south-east Yunnan, South China (authors' 407-411 Ma). The formation yields a diverse flora of early vascular plants; I took the genus list from a secondary summary. The age places the thallus some 20 to 25 Myr before the next-oldest liverworts (stage ages from data/timescale.csv): *Metzgeriothallus sharonae* from the Givetian of New York (Hernick et al. 2008) and *Pallaviciniites* (originally *Hepaticites*) *devonicus*, described by Hueber (1961) and reported as lowermost Upper Devonian in secondary sources.

## Affinity and controversies

Guo et al. (2012) argued that the Early Devonian age of *Riccardiothallus* puts the differentiation of Jungermanniopsida and Metzgeriidae well before molecular estimates (Late Devonian, 359-385 Ma, or Late Carboniferous, 299-307 Ma). Wellman (2014) called the Early Devonian bryophytes "strangely modern", unexpected under the accepted phylogeny.

Doubts: Blanco-Moreno et al. (2025) excluded it from their pre-Miocene bryophyte database under the criteria of Tomescu et al. (2018), because its placement among liverworts "has not been justified empirically". Gross thallus form is simple and could be convergent. At a Lochkovian site in the Welsh Borderland, Edwards et al. (2021b) show thalloid fossils built of transfer cells that may be gametophytes of eophytes, so Devonian thalloid compressions need not be liverworts. Resolving it requires cell-level anatomy, rhizoids, or a sporophyte.

## Ecology and physiology

Aneuraceae are moist-habitat thalloid plants with a dominant gametophyte; the authors extend that to a warm, humid, riverine setting for the fossil (inference). Cells and ecophysiology of the fossil are unknown. Devonian liverwort thalli from the Givetian preserve oil-body-type cells interpreted as antiherbivore defence (Labandeira et al. 2013), which is evidence for bryophyte chemistry later in the Devonian, not for this fossil.

## Why it matters for modelling

- Bryophyte megafossils before the Middle Devonian are scarce. Wellman (2014): no clear bryophytes among 24 Silurian localities, none in the Rhynie chert; Hooper et al. (2026) note a general paucity before the Devonian. Blanco-Moreno et al. (2025) record seven Devonian liverwort occurrences and no Devonian mosses or hornworts, against 207 Cretaceous occurrences, with the first mosses in the Carboniferous.
- Do not read that scarcity as absence. Candidate causes: minute, poorly preservable plants (Edwards et al. 2021a; Tomescu et al. 2018), few rocks, and research geography (Blanco-Moreno et al. 2025). A model should use fossil counts only as a lower bound on bryophyte cover.
- Clocks: crown bryophytes diverged 445-429 Ma (Hooper et al. 2026), early Silurian; complex thalloid liverworts radiated in the Late Silurian-Early Devonian (Flores et al. 2023); most bryophyte orders originated in the Jurassic or later (Bechteler et al. 2023). If those clocks hold, a Pragian thalloid liverwort would sit on a stem lineage or in an extinct clade.
- Gametophyte-dominant, multilayered thalloid plants offer a competitor and soil-cover type for moist Early Devonian habitats; treat their abundance as unknown, not zero.
- Give this fossil low weight; use Givetian *Metzgeriothallus* as the secure liverwort bound.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Test bryophyte first-appearance constraints against a disputed Pragian thalloid fossil whose gross morphology is reported but whose liverwort identity lacks diagnostic support.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Riccardiothallus devonicus, Posongchong Formation compression in Yunnan: regularly bifurcating growth can define a qualitative branching rule, without calibrated branch lengths or rates (Guo et al. 2012, abstract).
  - Riccardiothallus devonicus, partially permineralised Posongchong compression: a flat, ecostate, multilayered thallus with entire margins can define a morphological cover type, independent of liverwort assignment (Guo et al. 2012, abstract).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Do not use this fossil as a secure liverwort calibration or evidence of Aneuraceae physiology: the liverwort placement was excluded as empirically unjustified (Blanco-Moreno et al. 2025).
  - Do not assign gametophyte dominance, sporophyte dependence or matrotrophy as observed traits; identifying the thallus as a gametophyte depends on gross-form affinity.
  - Do not supply thallus dimensions, rhizoid architecture or reproductive output: none is available in the abstract used by this card.
  - Do not assign oil-body chemistry or antiherbivore defence from Givetian liverworts to Riccardiothallus; those observations concern later fossils.

## Open questions

- Does Riccardiothallus show liverwort-diagnostic features (oil bodies, rhizoid types, sporophyte)?
- Are other Pragian thalloid compressions in the Posongchong flora bryophytes, eophyte gametophytes or algae?
- How much of the pre-Middle Devonian bryophyte gap is preservation, and how much is true scarcity?
