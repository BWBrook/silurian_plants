## In brief

The Silurian-Devonian rocks of the Welsh Borderland and South Wales hold the most complete regional record of early land vegetation, and from the Pridoli and Lochkovian they yield minute, charred, three-dimensional plant fragments (mesofossils) that preserve anatomy and spores (Edwards & Richardson 2004). They give the only parent plants of the Ordovician-style cryptospores (eophytes), anatomy of the earliest Cooksonia, nematophytes and lichens, and are read as wildfire products. They are allochthonous, so they sample the regional flora through a filter of fire, transport and rock type.

## Morphology and anatomy

|---|---|

| Eophytes | [*Partitatheca*](partitatheca.md) and other minute forms with forking striated axes, valvate sporangia and permanent dyads or tetrads (Edwards et al. 2014, 2021a, 2022) |
| Nematophytes | [*Nematothallus*](nematothallus.md), *Nematasketum*, *Tristratothallus ludfordensis*, fragments of [*Prototaxites*](prototaxites.md) (Edwards et al. 2018) |
| Fungi, lichens | Stratified lichen thalli, a pycnidium-bearing cyanolichen, an apothecium fragment (Honegger et al. 2013; Edwards et al. 2018); coprolites with digested hyphae |
| Others | Thalloid fossils with transfer cells (Edwards et al. 2021b); banded tubes, spore masses and sterile axes |

Preservation types are charcoal (HF extraction, SEM), coalified compressions and pyrite permineralisations that revealed tracheid ultrastructure (Edwards & Richardson 2004).

## Age and distribution

**Age.** See range notes: upper Ludlow or basal Pridoli to early Lochkovian for the mesofossils, with compressions of Pridoli to Pragian age in the wider Anglo-Welsh Basin (Edwards & Richardson 2004). Biostratigraphic only.

**Setting.** Avalonian part of Laurussia (as on the Nematothallus card). Edwards & Kenrick (2015) report that Lang's fossils were transported into shallow near-shore marine (Upper Silurian) or river (Lower Devonian) deposits; the 2004 synthesis adds that habitats are uncertain and proposes seasonally dry interfluves with calcretes as refugia for cryptospore plants while vascular plants lived along rivers. No source read states a plate model or latitude for these sites; PBDB's two models give about 5-11 degrees S (gplates) or 18-29 degrees S (Scotese) across its UK Pridoli and Lochkovian collections.

**Taphonomy.** Charred by low-temperature fire, with exceptional three-dimensional cellular detail and volatile, non-mineralised tissues; the North Brown Clee section is proposed as the first wildfire-derived Konservat-Lagerstatte, read as instantaneous fossilisation (Glasspool et al. 2006, abstract as returned by search). Edwards & Axe (2004) conclude that smouldering surface fires burned low vegetation of small plants, dated there to about 405 Ma (Lochkovian, on their scale; abstract as returned by search).

**Sampling.** Lang collected from the 1920s; intensive re-collecting at his localities then found far more *Cooksonia*-like diversity, but charcoalified types are fragmentary and often single specimens (Edwards & Kenrick 2015). One Lochkovian site yielded several hundred eophyte mesofossils (Edwards et al. 2022); only seven *Partitatheca* sporangia were selected from several hundred (Partitatheca card). PBDB lumps Lang's 1937 material into two collections (39 occurrences) and holds none of the eophytes, *Tristratothallus* or the lichens.

## Affinity and controversies

- **Eophyte placement.** Stem-embryophyte, stem-tracheophyte or bryophyte-like: unresolved (see Partitatheca card; Edwards et al. 2014, 2021a).
- **Coalified or charcoal.** Edwards & Richardson (2004) called the mesofossils coalified and hypothesised wildfire; Glasspool et al. (2006) showed quantitatively that those from the Lochkovian stream section are charcoal (abstract as returned by search).
- **Earliest wildfire.** The Pridoli record (Glasspool et al. 2004) is no longer the oldest: charcoal dominated by nematophytes occurs in the early mid-Silurian of Wales (Glasspool & Gastaldo 2022).
- **Replacement.** Cryptophytes were competitively replaced by early tracheophytes (Edwards et al. 2014); Morris & Edwards (2014; abstract as returned by search, full text not read) link rising branch size across the Lochkovian to competition for light and space and the local loss of rhyniophytes.

## Ecology and physiology

Eophytes were probably poikilohydric, with thick-walled epidermal cells and leptoid-like conducting cells (Edwards et al. 2021a). Thalloid fossils with transfer cells may be gametophytes of this group (Edwards et al. 2021b). The vegetation that burned is read as low-growing and of low diversity (Glasspool et al. 2004). Lochkovian coprolites with fungal tubes, and later ones with digested hyphae, show fungivory, probably by millipedes (Nematothallus card).

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Spore-body mapping.** The only deposit in which parent plants of permanent cryptospores are known. Cryptospores dominated assemblages for about 60 Myr and fell sharply near 410 Ma, 'latest Lochkovian' on the authors' scale (Edwards et al. 2021a, via the Partitatheca card); on ICS v2024/12 the latest Lochkovian lies just below 413 Ma, so record the stage, not the number.
- **Preservation filter.** Anatomy in small plants is preserved only where fire, fine rock and HF extraction coincide. Sustained burns need about 16% O2, first exceeded in the mid-Silurian (Glasspool & Gastaldo 2022). Treat preservation probability as a function of fuel, O2 and rock type.
- **Size spectrum.** Eophyte axes under about 2 mm and heights of millimetres to centimetres, Cooksonia to about 6 cm, set the small end of the early size distribution in a record that also contains much larger nematophytes.
- **Sampling frame.** Allochthonous bodies from a few streams and sections, with typically one or a few specimens per taxon: use as presence data with large unobserved-habitat uncertainty, not as abundance data.
- **Regional bias.** Edwards & Richardson (2004) state that major evolutionary innovation probably occurred elsewhere, so first appearances here are minimum-age constraints for the region, not for the clade.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast trilete-spore plants with permanent-cryptospore eophytes and their different conducting tissues, using charcoalified fragments that directly associate anatomy with in situ spores.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Annularly thickened tracheids in charcoalified Cooksonia pertoni from the Welsh Borderland assemblages can define a conducting-tissue morphotype (Edwards & Kenrick 2015).
  - Permanent dyads or tetrads in eophytes, including Partitatheca from North Brown Clee Hill, provide alternative cryptospore states rather than trilete-spore states (Edwards et al. 2014).
  - Preserved lengths of Cooksonia, species not separated, in Lang's Downtonian compressions from England and Wales: about 1-6 cm; use as fragment lengths (Edwards & Kenrick 2015).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Tracheids cannot be assigned to eophytes from the assemblage-wide front-matter state; their reported conducting cells are leptoid-like food conductors (Edwards et al. 2021a).
  - Gametophyte–sporophyte dependence cannot be established from North Brown Clee Hill transfer-cell thalli: their gametophyte identity is hypothesised and no sporophyte is matched (Edwards et al. 2021b).
  - Population abundance and relative taxon frequencies cannot be inferred from these transported, selectively preserved fragments; this card recommends presence data.
  - Global lineage-origination dates cannot be assigned from regional first appearances; major innovations may have occurred elsewhere (Edwards & Richardson 2004).

## Open questions

What did the living vegetation look like, and where did it grow? Do the basal Pridoli charcoal horizon and the upper-Ludlow Downton Castle Sandstone base coincide? Which sporophytes go with the thalloid transfer-cell fossils? How many specimens underlie each mesofossil taxon, and how do they compare across horizons?
