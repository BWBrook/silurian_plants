## In brief

Sporogonites is a Lower Devonian form genus of slender, unbranched, smooth axes, each ending in one large capsule-like sporangium; Halle (1916) described it from Røragen, Norway, and it has since been reported from Belgium, Wales, France, South Africa, Brazil, Yunnan and Victoria. It is conventionally placed with the bryophytes, but little anatomy is known (one sectioned Røragen capsule and epidermal cell patterns on compressions), no vascular tissue has been shown in the sources I read, and Lang (1937) already treated a simple tracheophyte as an equal possibility. It earns a card as the unbranched, one-capsule contrast to branched polysporangiophytes and because axes have been reported attached to a gametophyte three ways: a thalloid structure (Andrews 1958), several sporophytes on one gametophyte (a Belgian specimen re-illustrated by Gerrienne & Gonez 2011), and organic connection to an axial gametophyte (a September 2026 conference abstract, Capel et al.). These accounts disagree on the form of the gametophyte, and the newest has no published description yet.

Observed: one axis, one terminal capsule, smooth surface, no appendages; spores in two flattened Røragen incrustations (Lang 1937) and none reported in the Belgian, Rebreuve, Impofu or Lilydale compressions; an epidermal cell pattern on the Belgian specimen (Lang 1937). Capsule lengths are not defined alike: Lang's 6-7 mm includes the basal region, and the Rebreuve and Impofu values do not state how they treat it, so compare like with like. Andrews (1958) is reported to describe Belgian specimens with a presumed thallus (14 x 3 cm on specimen 23480) and basal attachments, to cite Croft & Lang for stomata on the upper stalk of Welsh specimens, and to give spore sizes of about 20 um (Welsh) and 20-25 um (Norwegian); I could read only its abstract, so none of this is verified here. Halle's anatomy comes from a single semi-petrified Røragen capsule. A search summary says Halle (1936) later queried the columella and the abstract of Bharadwaj (1981) says the capsule lacks one; I could read neither source in full, so the capsule structure of the type species is not settled here. Interpreted: Gess & Prestianni (2021) read the small hemispherical tip in sp. A and sp. B as an operculum, a reading that holds only if the plant is a bryophyte, and they cite the same structure in Halle's type material and in Belgian S. exuberans (the second author, Prestianni, confirmed it in Belgian specimens). Unbranched is an observation on fragments: Gess & Prestianni's sp. B base is missing, and Edwards (2000, cited by Ligrone et al. 2012) pointed out that the absence of sporophyte branching is hard to demonstrate in fossils.

## Age and distribution

Røragen (type, Lower Devonian, stage unresolved) and the Belgian Grès de Wépion were the northern records known to Lang (1937). Later material extends the Laurussian record to the Senni Beds of Wales (Pragian to Emsian) and the Artois of France, where Rebreuve is early Emsian by correlation, with palynology failing there (Capel et al. 2022). Gondwanan occurrences are Lochkovian (Impofu Dam; the Furnas Formation in Brazil) and Victorian; South China has S. yunnanense (Hsü 1966, not read). The oldest claimed record, in the Late Silurian of Vietnam, comes from an unpublished-thesis source (Gonez 2012, relayed by Gess & Prestianni 2021) and is not used.

Victoria. The Victorian species is S. chapmanii Lang & Cookson (1930), from two localities in the Centennial beds at Walhalla (Cookson 1949). Lang (1937) already treated it as a distinct species from S. exuberans, with larger forms comparable to the smallest Norwegian capsules and a more pointed tip, and Gess & Prestianni (2021) exclude their rounded-tipped Impofu specimens from it for that reason. At the genus level it is the same form genus as the type, assigned on capsule shape; no anatomy has been shown for S. chapmanii. The Centennial beds were first thought Upper Silurian or possibly Lower Devonian (Skeats 1928) and are regarded as Lower Devonian by Cookson (1949, citing Thomas 1937 and Gill 1942); I did not map them to the modern formations used in [the Baragwanathia floras](baragwanathia-flora.md), and S. chapmanii comes from Walhalla, not from the disputed Yea Lower Plant Assemblage. The Lilydale 'cf. Sporogonites' that Cookson (1945) first named S. chapmani was withdrawn from that species by Cookson (1949) and later reassigned to Uskiella spargens, a bivalved rhyniopsid, by Shute & Edwards (1989, as relayed by Gess & Prestianni 2021). The same reassignment applies to Croft & Lang's Welsh 'Cooksonia sp.' that Cookson had compared to Sporogonites. Single-capsule, unbranched fragments can therefore belong to branched, bivalved plants.

## Affinity and controversies

Bryophyte or not. Halle (1916) titled his note 'a fossil sporogonium'. Lang (1937) judged that Sporogonites might be an early bryophyte sporogonium or a simple pteridophyte allied to the Rhyniaceae, and that two facts would decide it: how the stalks were borne, and whether the axes were vascular. Andrews (1958; abstract only) concluded that general habit and the lack of vascular tissue suggest bryophytic over psilophytic relationships, and Bharadwaj (1981; abstract only) called Sporogonites the earliest known bryophyte, with capsule features of two primitive moss subclasses and a thallus-like gametophyte marked as uncertain. Later accounts keep it as a possible moss (Ligrone et al. 2012), a possible thalloid moss or early hornwort (Poli et al. 2003, via Gerrienne & Gonez 2011), or incertae sedis Bryophyta (Gess & Prestianni 2021). All rest largely on gross form. The sources I read show only epidermal cell patterns on compressions (Lang 1937) and Halle's one sectioned capsule, with a columella reported by Halle and later questioned, and no vascular tissue; Andrews (1958) is reported to record more (Welsh stomata and spore sizes), unverified here. Edwards and Shute's Sporogonites project was never completed (Morris et al. 2024), and the 2024 result for Tortilicaulis, a taxon that Edwards (1979) compared with Sporogonites for similar morphological variation (Morris et al. 2024), shows how such a reading can fail once anatomy is found (see [Tortilicaulis offaeus](tortilicaulis-offaeus.md)). I treat bryophyte affinity as a hypothesis and the vascular question as untested.

Attachment. Claims and non-observations differ.

- Lang (1937): numerous parallel axes on one block, none complete below, 'no evidence of any structure from which they arose'. Gess & Prestianni (2021): bases not preserved in either species.
- Andrews (1958; abstract read, full text unavailable to me): Belgian specimens appear borne on a presumed thallus-like structure; the full text is reported to describe basal attachments and the thallus dimensions above. Gerrienne & Gonez (2011) relay this as axes attached to a thalloid structure read as a gametophyte, and re-illustrate a large Estinnes-au-Mont specimen (Stockmans 1940, plate IV fig. 6) showing several undivided sporophytes apparently on one gametophyte.
- Capel et al. (2026 abstract): at Impofu Dam, gametophytes preserved in organic connection with sporophytes; Sporogonites reconstructed as a polysetous organism, an axial unbranched gametophyte bearing multiple unbranched sporophytes at its distal end. They say this refutes the thalloid interpretation of the gametophytes of Sporogonites (and of Cooksonia) and shows early sporophytes remained nutritionally dependent on the gametophyte. This is a conference abstract: I found no paper, figures or measurements, and the abstract does not state the basis for the nutritional-dependence conclusion.

If the 2026 reconstruction is right, the thalloid reading of Andrews and of Bharadwaj is wrong in form, and any dependence argument rests on axial attachment instead. If it is wrong, the Belgian thalloid reading stands only on a presumed structure. I read no measurements of an attached structure in the sources available to me: the Estinnes figure in Gerrienne & Gonez (2011) carries a 5 mm scale bar but no stated dimensions, and Andrews (1958) is reported to give 14 x 3 cm for a presumed thallus on specimen 23480, with incomplete preservation, which I could not check.

Species and genus limits. Four species are recognised (S. exuberans, S. chapmanii, S. excellens Frenguelli 1951, S. yunnanense Hsü 1966), plus the invalid S. 'punctatus' of Gonez's thesis (Gess & Prestianni 2021). Lang (1937) named Belgian material S. exuberans forma belgica; Bharadwaj (1981) raised it to S. belgica, which later authors ignore (Capel et al. 2022). Impofu Dam material is left unnamed because a diagnostic sporangial ornament cannot be checked in the phyllosilicate preservation.

## Ecology and physiology

Probably gregarious: Impofu Dam sp. A is very abundant in some layers, its stems covering whole bedding planes, in horizons where plant fossils sometimes form vegetation mats, and the Belgian block carries 10 to 15 distinguishable sporangia and many axes in one layer with parallel orientation, which Lang (1937) judged against chance transport of isolated specimens, while noting that nothing showed the plant's mode of growth. Little is known directly of water relations, photosynthesis or nutrition; stomata on Welsh stalks are known to me only at second hand (Andrews 1958 after Croft & Lang, unverified). Epidermal cells on axes and capsules were probably firm-walled (Lang 1937). Spore release has not been observed, and no dehiscence line has been described. Whether the sporophytes were photosynthetic, and whether a gametophyte supplied them, is unobserved; the dependence claim exists only in the 2026 abstract. Fertilisation mode is unknown.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Sporophyte form.** One capsule per preserved unbranched axis. Whether an axis is a whole sporophyte or part of a larger one is not established, because no base is preserved in the specimens I read; if each axis is a whole sporophyte, sporangium number per sporophyte is one, and fecundity depends on the number of axes, and on how many axes a gametophyte carries if the polysetous reconstruction is right. This contrasts with the several to many sporangia per sporophyte of branched polysporangiophytes (see [the rhyniophytoids](rhyniophytoids.md) and [Cooksonia](cooksonia.md)).
- **Size.** Axes at least 10-11.5 cm long and 0.9-1.3 mm wide at Impofu Dam, carrying capsules of 7-7.5 mm, show that an unbranched sporophyte axis could reach about 10 cm. If the dependence claim holds, that length was reached with a gametophyte attached.
- **Dependence as a scenario.** A model of sporophyte independence can carry two scenarios, a dependent sporophyte on an axial gametophyte (Capel et al. 2026 abstract) and an unresolved alternative with a thalloid or unattached gametophyte, and should not collapse them into one parameter.
- **Record.** A Lochkovian to Emsian bryophyte-like sporophyte record only; no dispersed-spore equivalent is established, the South African Lochkovian age depends on assemblage comparison, and I did not check the basis of the Brazilian early Lochkovian age.

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** two separate contrasts. Reproductive-strategy: capsule-bearing axes that are unbranched in all the material I read about, with one capsule per preserved axis, against the several sporangia on a branched sporophyte of [Cooksonia](cooksonia.md). Sporophyte-independence: a reported attachment of these axes to a gametophyte, against the Rhynie sporophytes and gametophytes in [the Rhynie chert gametophytes](rhynie-chert-gametophytes.md), whose matches rest on tissue similarity and co-occurrence and not on connection. Branching and independence are different axes; Gerrienne & Gonez (2011) also raise a possible gametophyte dependence for small Cooksonia, as a hypothesis.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Sporogonites sp. A, Impofu Dam (AM 7944, AM 7927): axes 10-11.5 cm long, 0.9-1.3 mm wide, capsules 7-7.5 x 3-3.5 mm, one capsule per axis, preserved in parallel bunches (Gess & Prestianni 2021). Treat lengths as minima and capsule length as measured without a stated treatment of any basal region.
  - Sporogonites sp. B, Impofu Dam (AM 7953): a 66 mm axis 1.9-2.1 mm wide with a 14.3 x 6.1 mm capsule, one specimen with the base missing (Gess & Prestianni 2021). Use it as an observed large capsule, not a biological maximum or a mean.
  - S. exuberans f. belgica, Fooz-Wépion (no. 17012): axes about 0.5 mm wide, pieces to 4 cm, capsules 6-7 mm long in total (basal region up to 2 mm, sporangium proper 4-5 mm) x about 2 mm, 10-15 distinguished on one block (Lang 1937). Use for Belgian capsule size and axis calibre; the count on one block is not a stand density.
  - Unbranched single-capsule architecture across the Belgian, Rebreuve and Impofu sp. A material (Lang 1937; Capel et al. 2022; Gess & Prestianni 2021), as capsule number per preserved axis and not per complete sporophyte.
  - Attachment claims as separate scenarios, each with its source: thalloid (Andrews 1958, abstract read), several sporophytes on one gametophyte (a Belgian specimen, Stockmans 1940, as re-illustrated by Gerrienne & Gonez 2011), and axial with several sporophytes per gametophyte (Capel et al. 2026, abstract read in full, a conference source with no stated evidence).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Any gametophyte size, form, sex or lifespan, or any sporophyte-to-gametophyte ratio, until Andrews (1958) is read. The claims conflict and the identity of the attached structure is interpretive. Andrews is reported to give 14 x 3 cm for a presumed thallus on Belgian specimen 23480 (incompletely preserved), and the Estinnes figure in Gerrienne & Gonez (2011) has a 5 mm scale bar; neither is verified, and neither fixes a generation ratio.
  - Nutritional dependence of the sporophyte as a fact: it is asserted in one conference abstract, which gives no evidence for it beyond the connection it describes.
  - Bryophyte physiology by analogy with living mosses or hornworts, or vascular status: affinity is unresolved. Stomata and spore size are known to me only at second hand for Welsh and Norwegian specimens (Andrews 1958, reported, unverified), and spore type is not established; restrict any use to the specimen and identification concerned.
  - Whole-plant height: no complete plant is documented in the sources I read. Andrews is reported to describe basal attachment structures, which would not by themselves give whole-plant height.
  - Capsule lengths pooled across material without a common definition: Lang's total includes the basal region, and the Rebreuve (4.5 mm) and Impofu values may not.
  - Values between species or localities: S. chapmanii (pointed capsules, Victoria), S. yunnanense (3.2-4.5 mm capsules, as quoted by Gess & Prestianni 2021), S. excellens and the unnamed Impofu forms are not interchangeable. Gess & Prestianni compare the Brazilian sp. A with Gonez's invalid S. 'punctatus', which differs from S. exuberans by a minute conical ornament on the upper sporangium; the Impofu preservation cannot show that ornament, so Brazilian and Impofu forms are not assumed equivalent. The Lilydale cf. Sporogonites was reassigned to Uskiella spargens.
  - A Silurian origin of the genus: the Vietnamese record is an unpublished thesis claim, and the South African Lochkovian age depends on assemblage comparison.

## Open questions

What anatomy, if any, do Sporogonites axes and capsules hold, and was a columella present (Halle 1916 against Halle 1936 and Bharadwaj 1981)? What does the full text of Andrews (1958) show: the thallus dimensions and structure, basal attachments, Welsh stomata and spore sizes? What do Capel et al.'s Impofu Dam connections look like when published: is the gametophyte axial, how many sporophytes does it carry, and what supports the dependence claim? Does the Belgian Estinnes-au-Mont specimen (Stockmans 1940, plate IV fig. 6) show a thallus or an axis? Is S. chapmanii a Sporogonites on anatomy, and what is its stratigraphic position among Victorian units? Which Lochkovian and Silurian records survive a check on dating, and what is the basis of the Brazilian Furnas age?
