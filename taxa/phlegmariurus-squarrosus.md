## In brief

*Phlegmariurus squarrosus*, the rock tassel fern, is a Palaeotropical lycophyte with tufted, pendulous or arching branches clad in crowded linear leaves and ending in tassel-like fertile zones. It is the stock living comparison for *Baragwanathia*: dense microphyll-clad axes, sporangia at the bases of sporophylls, and a gradual change from sterile to fertile shoot. The comparison holds for organ organisation and fails for almost everything else. The species belongs to a lineage that radiated in the Mesozoic and Cenozoic, it is a mesic epiphyte or lithophyte with derived water relations, and its gametophyte is unknown. Measured traits exist only for congeners.

## Morphology and anatomy

Branches are 30-75 cm long; leaves 10-20 x 1-2 mm, angled 60-90 degrees from the axis and twisted near the base; sporophylls 5-7 x 1.5-2 mm, whorled, imbricate, with sporangia filling about one-sixth of their length; the fertile zone is 10-30 cm long and 7-10 mm across, and the sterile-to-fertile change is gradual (Australian online key). The genus has isotomous shoots, no bulbils on sporophyllous shoots, and spores with foveolate-fossulate distal sculpture (Field & Bostock 2013). Sporangia sit at the sporophyll bases, the family condition (Wagner & Beitel 1992; I read a poor OCR copy, so this is unchecked for the species).

## Age and distribution

Palaeotropical, from the western Indian Ocean through tropical Asia to eastern Australia and the Pacific; the type is from Tahiti. The Australian key describes it as widespread but scarce. No fossils were found. Testo et al. (2018) estimate the Lycopodiaceae crown at 368.4 Ma and the Huperzioideae crown at 199.5 Ma; the Neotropical and Palaeotropical *Phlegmariurus* clades split about 102 Ma, and most species-level divergences are under 15 Myr old.

## Affinity and controversies

Accepted name here: *Phlegmariurus squarrosus* (G.Forst.) Á.Löve & D.Löve, Taxon 26: 324 (1977), basionym *Lycopodium squarrosum* G.Forst. Synonyms listed by Field & Bostock (2013): *Huperzia squarrosa* (G.Forst.) Trevis. (1874); *Urostachys squarrosus* (G.Forst.) Herter (1923); and as heterotypic synonyms *Lycopodium acutifolium* Desv. ex Poir., *L. epiceifolium* Desv. ex Poir., *Urostachys madagascariensis* (Nessel) Herter and *L. pseudosquarrosum* Pamp. *Phlegmariurus* is the PPG I (2016) genus and is supported by Field et al. (2016) and by Chen et al. (2022), who retain it to keep *Huperzia* monophyletic. Field et al. also found that one genus (*Huperzia* s.l.) or three are equally supported, so this is a classification choice. A search result for Kew's Plants of the World Online listed *Huperzia squarrosa* as the accepted name; I could not open that site, and the GBIF backbone accepts *Phlegmariurus squarrosus*.

Phylloglossum is sister to Phlegmariurus in the maximum-likelihood and parsimony analyses of Chen et al. (2022) but not in the Bayesian one. The family crown is Late Devonian (368.4 Ma) but the genus is Mesozoic, so living *Phlegmariurus* says little about Silurian-Devonian lycopsids beyond the deep lycophyte-euphyllophyte split at 431.5 Ma (426-440; Testo et al. 2018). Epiphytism is inferred to be the ancestral habit of the genus (Testo et al. 2018, citing Field et al. 2016), so it dates from the genus, not from the Devonian.

## Ecology and physiology

Not measured in this species. Congeners show what to expect: the epiphytic *P. nummularifolius* had the lowest stomatal density among 38 ferns and lycophytes (17.1 per mm2) and *Huperzia phlegmaria* the lowest vein density (0.37 mm mm-2); epiphytes had lower vein density (1.07 +/- 0.49) than terrestrial species (2.00 +/- 0.70 mm per mm2) and larger stomata (Prats et al. 2024). *Huperzia* and *Lycopodium* sporophytes and *Huperzia* gametophytes in Ecuadorian páramo shared glomeromycotean *Glomus* A phylotypes, from which Winther & Friedman (2007) raise the possibility of carbon flow from sporophyte to gametophyte; *P. saururus* from Argentine rock crevices carries arbuscular and ectomycorrhizas plus septate endophytes (Romagnoli et al. 2025). Subterranean club-moss gametophytes are long-lived, and bisexual club-moss gametophytes reach maturity after about five to ten years (Rimgaile-Voicik & Naujalis 2022, a review of *Lycopodium* and *Diphasiastrum* citing older work); no such figure exists for *Phlegmariurus*.

## Why it matters for modelling

- **Where the analogy holds.** A crowded microphyll canopy (single vein assumed from the genus) on a dichotomous axis, with sporangia at sporophyll bases and fertile zones that grade out of the vegetative shoot. These support rules for microphyll area per axis and for zonal fertility in [Baragwanathia](baragwanathia-longifolia.md) and [Drepanophycus](drepanophycus-spinaeformis.md).
- **Where it fails.** Lineage age (Huperzioideae crown about 200 Ma and *Phlegmariurus* crown about 102 Ma, against Early Devonian lycopsids at about 410 Ma), true roots, mesic epiphytic water relations (low vein and stomatal density in congeners), and, in related club mosses, a subterranean gametophyte phase of years (unknown here). Do not transfer congener stomatal or vein values to a Devonian lycopsid.
- **Numbers to use as ranges.** Leaf 10-20 x 1-2 mm; sporophyll 5-7 mm; trilete spores of about 31-61 micrometres in huperzioids; fertile zone 10-30 cm.
- **Genomic traits.** Two whole-genome duplications are inferred in Lycopodiaceae (206-214 Ma, which sits uneasily with a 368 Ma crown age from a different clock) and in the ancestor of extant *Phlegmariurus* (22-23 Ma; Xia et al. 2022), and the mitochondrial genome of *H. squarrosa* is 413,530 bp with 66 genes and an ancestral gene order (Liu et al. 2012).

## Model transfer

**Chosen to contrast:** Contrast crowded microphyll axes and graded fertile zones with Devonian lycopsid organisation, while using derived mesic epiphytism to delimit the hydraulic analogy.

- **Can transfer:**
  - Shoot length: the north-eastern Queensland account of P. squarrosus gives branches 30-75 cm long, hanging, erect or arched; use length rather than standing height (Australian online key, accessed 2026-10-27).
  - Leaf geometry: P. squarrosus in the Australian key has leaves 10-20 x 1-2 mm, angled 60-90 degrees from the axis and twisted basally; use as an organ-geometry analogue (Australian online key, accessed 2026-10-27).
  - Fertile-zone geometry: P. squarrosus in the Australian key has fertile zones 10-30 cm long and 7-10 mm across, grading gradually from sterile shoots (Australian online key, accessed 2026-10-27).
- **Cannot transfer:**
  - Stomatal density 17.1 per mm2 belongs to the epiphytic congener P. nummularifolius, not P. squarrosus or a Devonian lycopsid (Prats et al. 2024).
  - Vein density 0.37 mm per mm2 belongs to epiphytic Huperzia phlegmaria, not P. squarrosus; it cannot supply a species-specific or Devonian hydraulic parameter (Prats et al. 2024).
  - Spore diameters 31-61 micrometres describe Neotropical huperzioids, not measured P. squarrosus spores; retain the original taxonomic scope (Rincon Baron et al. 2014).
  - Gametophyte maturation times cannot be assigned to P. squarrosus: the cited review concerns Lycopodium and Diphasiastrum, and its own gametophyte is unknown (Rimgaile-Voicik & Naujalis 2022).
  - Mesic epiphytism cannot represent ancestral Devonian lycopsid ecology: this card assigns it to the much younger Phlegmariurus lineage (Testo et al. 2018, citing Field et al. 2016).

## Open questions

Gas exchange, CAM status (nothing found), growth rate and shoot lifespan of this species; spore output and size; its gametophyte and the time to first sporophyte; whether the single vein and sporophyll form match those of *Baragwanathia*; and a Palaeozoic or Mesozoic fossil that bears on the lineage.
